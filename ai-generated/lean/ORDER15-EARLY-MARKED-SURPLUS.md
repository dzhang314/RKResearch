# The anchored twenty-row order-15 early-marked-surplus branch is impossible

**Exact assertion.** Let the coefficient field have characteristic zero. Suppose a lower-triangular 20-stage Runge–Kutta tableau satisfies the genuine rooted-tree equations through order 15, its ordinary quadratic stage defect is zero (C(2)), and its full marked defect space M4 has dimension at least 3. These hypotheses are contradictory. Neither an order on the field nor a sign, clock distinctness, diagonal, or weight nonvanishing condition on the array is imposed. This statement alone does not decide the non-C(2) and minimal marked alternatives and is not a numerical order-15 floor by itself.

Let C denote diagonal multiplication by the actual ordinary node vector c, and E_j,F_j the full and pure ordinary tree spaces of grade j. Standard anchored equality gives an actual zero first row and f3=1, f6=5, f7=6, dim E7=14. The early marked surplus makes M4…M7 attain ranks 3,4,5,6, so their annihilators E10,…,E7 have ranks 17,16,15,14. The marked clock escapes imply the dual ordinary escapes: the two clock operators are reflected and self-transpose under the physical pairing. Origin-anchored delayed moments separate degrees through seven from F8. Strict forward gain then gives f8=7 and the true decomposition E8 = P7(c) ⊕ F8, where P7 denotes polynomials of degree at most seven.

The actual grades six through eight have two unit clock gains. Their common maximal invariant core U and normalized head w give F_(6+i)=U+span(w,Cw,…,C^(r+i−1)w), i=0,1,2. We check dim U≤2 as follows. A clock-invariant subspace in F7 has rank at most 4 by its outgoing gain to F8. If it is not in F6, its sum with F6 is all F7, which would be invariant since C F6⊂F7. The established ordinary-core rank dispatch, now usable on this same maximal core in F7, gives dim U≤3. Equality would make it an invariant hyperplane in F5 with unit gains through F7, which the kernel-checked general weighted fifth-debut exclusion rules out even without product closure. Thus r=5−dim U≥3. This avoids a separate marked projection of the cubic.

## Closing the branch

All of F4 is contained in U. Strict growth from f3=1 and dim U≤2 give F4=U and dim U=2. Its outgoing gain forces f5≥4, and f6=5 gives f5=4. Use the same core to start the ordinary one-head flag at grade five: both the new head w' and C w' are genuinely cheap. Recompute the same grade-ten cut with r=3 at grade six. Apply the preceding projection and multiplier argument now to v=w': the three paid products are w'², w'(Cw'), and (Cw')². The local product theorem shows U is stable under multiplication by w' too, since U=F4 and w'∈F5. The conclusion is w'∈U, contradicting the actual grade-five head escape. This eliminates the entire early-surplus case without first making a separate f5=3 versus f5=4 dispatcher.
