# Coefficient fields: algebraic cut lemmas, the order-twelve transfer, and where reality really enters

A coefficient field K has characteristic zero. Formal integration throughout is the Q-linear monomial rule x^j→x^(j+1)/(j+1), evaluated in K; no path of integration in K is required. In particular these statements apply over C or any subfield. For ordered fields there is, separately, logical transfer: a given p,s feasibility assertion is existential for finitely many rational-polynomial RKOC and triangular zero constraints. Any ordered field embeds into a real closure, all real closed fields have the same parameter-free first-order theory as R, and hence nonexistence over R implies nonexistence over every ordered field. A real construction, conversely, need not be defined over its smaller ordered subfields. Logical transfer does not make a proof based on real ordering literally algebraic over C, or guarantee existence in every ordered field.

## Lemma-by-lemma, including real-only looking steps that are not genuine obstacles

1. The ordinary/one-mark rooted-feature operations, their grade pairings, and R=CA−AC−A² (diagonal −a_ii²) are rational identities. The chronological invariant quotients and their no-stall witnesses in dirk_reverse_quotient_independent_audit.md compare the first effective diagonal to t/2 and t/3, with nonzero t. Spectral node projectors exist by ordinary interpolation because C is already split diagonal. These arguments require no ordered spectrum or signed-root count.
2. Instead of deducing H=0 from a single integral of its square, take every test of the permitted degree. If H=x^a(1−x)^b G for a,b=0 or 1 and ∫H R=0 for every R of degree ≤deg G, then G=0 by nonsingularity of the finite rational moment matrix of x^a(1−x)^b on [0,1]. Its determinant is a nonzero rational (its real positive definiteness is a perfectly good proof of this fixed rational number). Hermitian squaring unknown K-coefficients is unnecessary. At a nonzero upper limit z, scale x=zy; for weight x(x−z) the remaining matrix is, up to units on its rows and columns, that of y(1−y). This also handles enough active quadrature sites: if all nonzero ones are roots of V, test ALL xV R, deg R≤deg V. When the unknown node u appears in the proposed mask and a positivity proof tested x(x−u)²P², place x(x−u)P in the profile and use all free test polynomials of degree up to deg[(x−u)P]; the fixed moment measure is then x dx, not a potentially singular complex-parameter weight (x−u)² dx.
3. The inclusive incoming/outgoing r-detector Hermite matrix in dirk_incoming_hermite_unconditional_dyadic_gain_20261123.md and dirk_two_surpluses_even_horizon_20261123.md is algebraically nonsingular. There are 2r+2 zeros counted with multiplicity against degree at most 2r+1. This replaces the sorted-interval sign proof exactly. Once enough detectors have been supplied by item 2, all rank gains used in the order-12 proof are thus characteristic zero. This is the inclusive last-node lemma, not an assumption that any older unrefunded odd-horizon Hermite test tolerates an implicit last diagonal.
4. The symmetric one-escape compression has an entirely algebraic proof. For F with dim(F+CF)=dim F+1 put W_j={v∈F:C^i v∈F for 0≤i≤j}. The map W_(j−1)→(F+CF)/F induced by C^j has kernel W_j, so the descending chain drops by one until it stabilizes at its largest invariant U; a stall makes that W_j invariant. If r=dimF−dimU, take w∈W_(r−1) not in U. Then C^r w is outside F and the powers w,…,C^(r−1)w have distinct filtration orders modulo U, proving F=U⊕wP_(r−1)(C). Localize U at each numerical site and choose ANY linear complement there; subtracting the projections of w into the respective U_z gives a valid generator whose nonzero z-component is outside U_z. This replaces Euclidean orthogonality in the head-polynomial zero/count arguments. An isotropic bilinear vector cannot be discarded merely because its squared norm is zero.

## Full characteristic-zero path for s≥15 at p=12

This is a separate, complete transfer claim, not an inference that all previous real DIRK endpoints transfer. Suppose K admits a counterexample with s≤14, choose the smallest such s and trim trailing zero outputs. At m=5 the ordinary no-stall rank gives f₅≥3. Scalar separation gives dim E₅≥9 (fixed rational moment Gram), and the four strict reverse gains through E₉ give s≥13. At equality nonzero g would already give f₅≥4 and s≥14. Thus g=0 at s=13, the first row is the true zero row, and saturation of the reverse ranks gives D1=D2=0, c_s=1, Ae_s=0 and e_s∈E₅ (cited quotient proof, no real-pole input). A representation e_s=H(c)+f forces H(0)=0. Testing against (1−x) times every polynomial through degree four gives the nonsingular x(1−x) Gram for H/x, so H=0, contrary to b_s≠0. This proves s≥14 over K. At s=14, if g≠0 then f₅≥4 and the same rank saturation from E₅≥10 gives e_s∈E₅. Without any origin assumption, test against (1−x) times ALL polynomials through degree five at budget 5+6=11. The fixed 1−x Gram forces H=0 again (dirk_even_nonzero_g_reverse_endpoint.md). Hence C(2).

Now f₆≥4, the origin-separation argument through degree six at 6+5=11 gives E₆≥11, and three later gains through E₉ saturate 14. Consequently f_j=j−2 for j=3,…,6 and E_j has dimension j+5 through j=9, with terminal structure e_s∈E₆ and both endpoints actual explicit. Everything through the first paragraph of dirk_order12_single_cubic_support_at_fourteen.md now transfers by items 1–3. In particular F₆ equals the C-extension of F₅, all F₆ vanish at both endpoints, E₇=P₆⊕(F₆+CF₆) and E₇ is noninvariant, using the ordinary two/three-detector and the reflected two-detector ranks. One can use the incoming F₆ test directly for noninvariance at its unit increment.

If h=Ac²−c³/3 is nonlocal, the cited order-12 proof gives F_j=hP_(j−3) through grade six, E₈=P₆⊕hP₅, at least six distinct h knots, and h=P(c) on its support for a nonzero polynomial deg P≤3. The terminal scalar cardinal G=xL₆′(x)/42 is the solution of fixed rational moment equations for x(1−x)dx; no assumed location of unknown c is involved. If ≥7 h knots occur G=−P Q identically, so P is proportional to a monic cubic factor of the fixed, squarefree rational G. The mask x(P−h) is nonzero using all scalar tests through degree three with fixed x dx; its first nonzero clean location u is one of the listed roots omitted by P, or the terminal 1. The genuine diagonal refund forces J[xP](u)=0, excluded by the existing 70 algebraic factor/pivot pairs modulo 181. In the exactly-six-site branch all h-site b-aggregates vanish by Vandermonde. The seven possible clean Lobatto sites must all occur because their unique degree-six cardinal weights are nonzero fixed algebraic numbers (in the real algebraic splitting field one can prove this once by integrating nonnegative isolators; a nonzero algebraic constant stays nonzero after every embedding). The first three nonzero clean debuts are distinct roots of L₆′; the grade-seven/eight scalar restrictions and 60 five-row full-rank tests modulo 181 apply verbatim to these fixed algebraic knots. The ordered-root finite-free/minors argument in the cited source excludes all characteristic-zero specializations, and never reduces unknown tableau coefficients mod 181. Thus even over K the cubic h must be local. Working in an algebraic closure if necessary is legitimate; all zeros of the fixed rational Lobatto polynomial are accounted for there, regardless of whether the coefficient field originally named an ordering.

With h local the source and its p12 dispatch in the grade-four note yield F₄,F₅,F₆=span(h)+wP_(j−4) and E₈=P₆+span(h)+wP₄, hence ≥5 distinct w knots. The class constancy tests h²∈span(h) and hw∈span(h) use only polynomial root counts and the anchored scalar separation of H/[x(1−x)] in E₇ (item 2). Therefore h is a nonzero constant on T=supp h and w is constant there; subtract that multiple of the true indicator from w rather than invoking a squared norm. This yields the hypotheses of the characteristic-zero grade-four four-knot theorem. The order-12 quadrature supplies four outside nonzero sites as above; contradiction. This proves the full lower bound 15 over K. No Nørsett–Wolfbrandt unrestricted-real scalar-pole bound was needed anywhere along this path.

## Genuine ordered/real dependencies elsewhere; no automatic complex p16 or p17 transfer

The classical scalar real-pole barrier in dirk_saturation_barrier.md uses real-root preservation of Q→Q+Q′ and the impossibility of certain double roots after iterating; that is not the forward/reverse rank no-stall proof. Over C the scalar [2/2] Padé approximation to the exponential, for example, has conjugate nonreal poles and order four at rational degree two. This is a counterexample to the corresponding complex scalar d+1 bound, not a claimed full ordinary DIRK order-four tableau.

The short real-only quartic two-pivot determinant used by dirk_grade_eight_commutator_pivot_refund.md (and again in the projected reverse quartic of dirk_order19_terminal_zero_kernel_barrier_20261123.md) is ρ⁶(ρ−1)⁶(15ρ²−24ρ+10)/8. It is genuinely zero at ρ=4/5±i√6/15, so the stated inference from the five moments to Q=0 cannot be transported unchanged; admissible complex spatial ratios are not excluded by that determinant. The related older grade-nine primitive test has a pure unit-times-ρ⁸(ρ−1)⁸ factor and is field-algebraic when used under its own grade budget; it cannot be substituted one grade earlier for free. The new universal f₇ bound bypasses the grade-eight step when excluding g≠0 at order 16/s21: f₃≥2, f₇≥7, E₇≥15, six further gains saturate s=21 and the unanchored terminal test at rank E₇ is contradictory. But the surviving f₇=6 branch still invokes the projected reverse quartic in its established reduction, so this note does NOT claim global p16≥22 or p17≥24 for general complex coefficients. Similarly any argument that every member of a Jacobi pencil has only simple real roots requires a real pencil parameter or a separate multiplicity-aware algebraic proof. Do not conflate these upstream dependencies with the field-robust grade-four/five no-head theorems.
