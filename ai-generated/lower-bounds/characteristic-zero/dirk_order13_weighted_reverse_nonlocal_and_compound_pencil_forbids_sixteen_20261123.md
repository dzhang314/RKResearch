# Order 13 requires at least 17 stages, via the weighted reverse cut and a continuous Jacobi pencil

Candidate lower endpoint pending independent audit; no ledger change in this note. The proof works for ordinary lower triangular RK with any coefficient field K of characteristic zero, in particular arbitrary real diagonal, repeated/exterior nodes, and signed/zero b. For real coefficients the previously proved g≠0 exclusion in dirk_low_odd_cubic_mask_rank.md is an alternative; we include a separate characteristic-zero proof so its field-sensitive subcases are not assumed away. There is no fixed collocation mesh hypothesis.

## Starting with an unrestricted counterexample

Use forward E_j,F_j and genuine one-mark row M_j, with grade=tree order minus one, A corresponding to J:x^k↦x^(k+1)/(k+1), C corresponding to multiplication by x. The following sources (all in this folder) spell out the lemmas:

If a minimal s has E_(p−3) full and every intervening increase from E_m to there is one, then D₁=D₂=0, c_s=1, A e_s=0, and e_s∈E_m. Indeed b_s≠0 by minimality/trailing trim, D₃≠0 and its terminal-zero N=Aᵀ,Y=1−C words have independently growing ranks; by saturation they fill the appropriate annihilator. No real-pole stability bound is needed.
* dirk_universal_grade_seven_surplus_and_grade_six_terminal_head_20261123.md and the varying-inside-masks extension therein: p≥10 gives f₇≥f₃+5. At p≥8, f₆=f₃+3 forces F₃=U⊕span(w), F_j=U⊕wP_(j−3) through grade six, where U is a genuine F₃ site-class mask algebra, w vanishes on its support T, and w=P(c) on S=supp w for nonzero P of degree 2 or 3 with P(0)≠0. If U=0 there are no masked classes. This is an ordinary forward theorem.
* dirk_multigrade_confluent_cut_rank_and_weighted_reflection_20261123.md: for a node-local D₃, the genuine one-mark minimal τ-head through M₆ or through its terminal-zero subflag M₆⁰ is impossible. The projected N and commutator errors have true grades ≤4 and ≤5, respectively. See the detailed application there as well.
* dirk_field_audit_refunded_cuts_and_order12_over_characteristic_zero_20261123.md: strict quotient, scalar moment separation, one-escape compression and inclusive two/three-detector Hermite gains over characteristic zero. In particular all scalar zero-square uses below are shorthand for testing against the whole same-budget polynomial space and a nonsingular fixed rational Hilbert/Jacobi Gram, not anisotropy of a complex square.

Assume a smallest counterexample with s≤16, trim trailing zero b. At m=6, f₆≥4 and P₆ separates from F₆ at 6+6=12, so dim E₆≥11. Four strict increases through E₁₀ give s≥15. Equality 15 needs g=0 (otherwise f₆≥5), so the origin is a true zero row. Saturation gives e_s∈E₆, with scalar part H satisfying H(0)=0. Testing e_s against (1−x) times every polynomial through degree five makes H/x orthogonal to P₅ for x(1−x)dx. Thus H=0, contradicting the nonzero b_s. Therefore s=16.

If g≠0, f₃≥2 and f₆≥5. The same E₆ count and four increases to E₁₀ force f₃=2, f₆=5, e_s∈E₆ and terminal zero of all F₆ (pair with e_s at 6+6=12). Write e_s=G(c)+f, f∈F₆. Then G(1)=1 and ∫(1−x)G R=0 for all R∈P₅. G(0) cannot be zero, by the same fixed x(1−x) Gram. The grade-six equality profile above now gives U,w,P,T,S. Call the complement of T and S clean. All clean clocks are nonzero: at a clean zero the cardinal would give e_s=G(0)≠0, even though only the physical terminal carries e_s and has clock 1. The contrasts
    θ=P(c)−Σ_(T_a)P(a)1_(T_a)−w     and     (c−u)θ
have respective grades ≤3,4, and vanish on T and S. They are nonzero: otherwise their profiles P or (x−u)P would pair to zero against all degree ≤3 or 4 polynomials, impossible for the nonsingular rational ordinary moment Gram. Take u to be the first numerical nonzero coordinate's clock of θ and v the first of the second contrast; both are clean, nonzero and v≠u. At each pivot R=CA−AC−A² has pure grade ≤6 and vanishes on clean rows, hence its physical diagonal is zero. Applying A after permissible C powers gives ∫₀ᵘ x^j P=0 for j=0,1,2 and ∫₀ᵛ x^j(x−u)P=0 for j=0,1. The first three conditions imply P(ux) is proportional to Z=20x³−30x²+12x−1 (a quadratic is already impossible). For t=v/u, direct integration gives
    2J[(x−1)Z](t)=t(t−1)²(8t²−9t+2),
    6J[x(x−1)Z](t)=t²(t−1)²(20t²−20t+3).
Five times the first residual quadratic minus twice the second is 4−5t, and the first at 4/5 is −2/25. Thus no such t exists. This rules out g≠0 without any reality assumption or extra rank-seven square tests.

## Origin anchor, minimal adjoint and the sole free cubic head

Assume g=0, so row one is the true origin. If f₆≥5 the same E₆ saturation puts e_s∈E₆, now immediately impossible by the anchored x(1−x) test above. Hence f₆=4=f₃+3 and f₃=1. In its grade-six equality profile U=0, F_j=hP_(j−3), 3≤j≤6, with h=Ac²−c³/3 nonlocal. Up to global scaling h=P(c) on S=supp h, deg P 2 or 3, P(0)≠0. Every clean row outside S therefore annihilates F₆. The universal f₇≥6 and degree-six separation from F₇ at 7+5=12 (H/x using the origin) give dim E₇≥13. The three strict increases to E₁₀ force equality throughout: E_j has dimension j+6 for j=7,8,9,10 and f₇=6. Thus D₁=D₂=0 and e_s∈E₇, c_s=1, a_ss=0. Also F₅ annihilates the terminal by pairing with the true e_s (5+7=12), whence F₆ and Z₇=hP₄=F₆+CF₆ do too. F₆ is noninvariant by its unit incoming gain and Hermite, so Z₇ really has rank five. P₇(c) separates from Z₇ by the two endpoint test: if H(c)∈Z₇ then H vanishes at 0 and 1, and H/[x(1−x)] has degree ≤5 and tests at 7+5=12. It follows directly that
    E₇=P₇(c)⊕hP₄(c).

The D₃-word flag B_j of rank ≥j−2 is terminal-zero. The saturated annihilators give the full M_j=B_j=Ann E_(12−j) for 3≤j≤5. At grade six use the 14-coordinate interior cut: F₆ restricts injectively since it vanishes at both endpoints, and M₆⁰ restricts injectively since it vanishes at the terminal and annihilates 1. Opposite scalar bands xP₅ and b(1−x)P₅ have Gram rank 6 by x(1−x)dx, with products of grade 6+6=12. They and the two pure spaces give 14≥4+6+dim M₆⁰. Consequently M₆⁰=B₆ has its minimal rank four. The two-detector Hermite gain forbids invariance of B₄ and B₅ against their next unit steps (all requisite primitive discrepancies are terminal-zero, since the first reversed row of N is zero and y_s=0). If D₃ were node-local, B_j=span D₃⊕τP_(j−4) at j=4,5,6 and the weighted projected four-knot theorem would contradict order ≥8. Thus D₃ is NONLOCAL. This uses only true exterior contexts, including when individual b vanish; no multiplication of two arbitrary marked rows is used. In particular Ann E₉=span D₃ is not invariant. Ann E₇=B₅ and Ann E₈=B₄ are likewise noninvariant.

## Every remaining node set forces a factor of one continuous pencil

Write e_s=G(c)+hQ(c) in E₇, deg G≤7, deg Q≤4. Since h at both endpoints is zero, G(0)=0,G(1)=1. Testing against (1−x)R for deg R≤4 at 7+5=12 forces G/x orthogonal to P₄ for x(1−x) dx. Write D_n=L_(n+1)′, L the shifted Legendre polynomial normalized at 1. Then G/x belongs to span(D₆,D₅), of degree exactly five or six. Consecutive D₆,D₅ have no common zeros.

If deg G=7, replace the top scalar power of the E₇ decomposition by the actual eigenvector e_s, obtaining E₇=P₆⊕span(e_s)⊕hP₄. For j=7,8,9, E_j is noninvariant since its annihilator just proved noninvariant. CP₆⊂E₇ and Ce_s=e_s; each of these three exact one-dimensional extensions must add the next head power. Therefore E₁₀=P₆⊕span(e_s)⊕hP₇, and S has at least eight DISTINCT numerical knots. If deg G=6 then G is proportional to xD₅. With ≤10 distinct h knots, b·c^j h=0 (0≤j≤9) and h=P(c)≠0 on S force zero aggregate S-weights at each. All remaining quadrature weight would be on the clean grid {0,1}∪roots D₅, impossible at degree twelve: x(1−x)D₅² vanishes on that entire grid and has a nonzero FIXED rational integral. Thus in this degree drop there are at least eleven h knots. Either way G=−P Q is valid as a polynomial identity (both sides degree ≤7, coincide at ≥8 knots; h_s=0). Since P(0)≠0, P divides G/x with no origin-factor ambiguity.

Consider x(P(c)−h), a true grade-four contrast zero on S. Its first nonzero coordinate is at a nonterminal nonzero clean site u with P(u)≠0. Indeed the contrast is nonzero by the fixed x-weighted moment Gram, and if its first nonzero coordinate were terminal, the refund at that row would give J[xP](1)=0 while testing the contrast against b gives b_s P(1)≠0. At the first nonzero coordinate the same R refund (grade six) and A after 1,x give
    J[xP](u)=J[x²P](u)=0.
The cardinal has G(u)=0, so G/x vanishes at u. In particular P is, up to nonzero scalar, a monic factor of degree k=2 or 3 of the divided pencil
    q(X,u)=[D₆(X)D₅(u)−D₅(X)D₆(u)]/(X−u).
This formula is valid since the consecutive D's have no common root, including D₅(u)=0 (in which case q is proportional to D₅(X)/(X−u)). We need not presume that a complex member of the pencil is simple or real-rooted.

## Uniform characteristic-zero certificate, not a chosen-grid search

View q=Σ_(j=0)^5 q_j(u) X^j; all coefficients are polynomials in u of degree ≤5 by symmetry of the divided alternant. Let A be the 5×5 companion numerator with subdiagonal ones and last column −q₀,…,−q₄, and B=diag(1,1,1,1,q₅). For k=2 or 3 and a=1,2 form
    M_(k,a)(u)=∫₀¹ t^a ∧^k(utB−A)dt,   N_(k,a)=det M_(k,a).
At q₅≠0, ∧^k(utB−A)=∧^k(ut I−AB⁻¹) ∧^k B. The matrix AB⁻¹ has characteristic polynomial q/q₅; upper triangularize it in an algebraic closure. The exterior moment matrix is triangular in the induced basis before its fixed invertible right factor ∧^k B; its diagonal elements are exactly the k-root-slot moment products ∫t^a∏(ut−r_i)dt, allowing repeated roots or Jordan blocks. A factor P with the required zero moment forces det M=0 (u≠0). At q₅=0, D₅(u)=0, D₆(u)≠0, and q∝D₅(X)/(X−u) has four DISTINCT finite roots (D₅ is fixed squarefree). For each selected root r, y=(1,r,…,r⁴) obeys yA=r yB even in this singular case; wedging k such Vandermonde rows gives a nonzero left vector annihilating M if its selected factor moment vanishes. This includes the dropped-degree pencil without a nonunit division. If u itself is a multiple root of a complex pencil, q simply retains its remaining multiplicity; P(u)≠0 and the same repeated-slot argument still applies.

A column of the exterior compound not using original column 5 has degree ≤k in u; columns using it have degree ≤k+5 since q_j have degree ≤5. Therefore deg N_(k,a)≤k binom(5,k)+5 binom(4,k−1)=2k binom(5,k), i.e. 40 or 60. Exact modular interpolation and Euclidean gcd give:
    prime 1009:  k=2 degs (40,40), LCs (346,363), monic gcd 1;
                 k=3 degs (60,60), LCs (721,334), monic gcd 1.
    prime 1013:  k=2 degs (40,40), LCs (92,1003), monic gcd 1;
                 k=3 degs (60,60), LCs (795,287), monic gcd 1.
The determinants are over Z localized away from the small integration denominators. Reaching the proved degree bounds ensures the fixed-degree Sylvester resultants reduce to nonzero elements, so each pair has no common root even over an algebraic closure of Q (hence over any characteristic-zero field). This contradicts the two required primitive moments for all continuous pencil parameters. It excludes the last possible s=16 branch, giving s≥17 at order 13.
