# Two opposite first-pivot Gram determinants exclude one free affine factor

Let E_a=J_(n+1)+aJ_n with J_i the derivative of the shifted Legendre polynomial of degree i+1, n=5 or 6, so E_a(1-y) is a constant sign times E_(-a)(y). Suppose P=S T, S a nonzero polynomial of degree at most one and monic T of degree at most three dividing xE_a. For a forward pivot u≠0 with E_a(u)=0 and P(u)≠0, and reverse pivot y=1-t≠0 with t=0 or E_a(t)=0 and P(t)≠0, the following cannot all hold: J[xP](u)=J[x²P](u)=0 and J[yP(1-y)](1-t)=J[y²P(1-y)](1-t)=0. The pivots may be at equal or different physical clocks. No hypothesis about real coefficients or real roots is used. This is a bidirectional fixed-coefficient calculation with a FREE Jacobi pencil parameter, not a finite scan of possible RK arrays.

Write T=x^e V, e=0 or 1, V monic of degree k<=3-e dividing E_a (if x divides T remove one x only). At the forward root V divides the n-slot Christoffel-Darboux q(x,u)=[J_(n+1)(x)J_n(u)-J_n(x)J_(n+1)(u)]/(x-u), and J_n(u)≠0. Set I_j:=u^(-j-1) J[x^j V](u). Because S is affine, the first two moments of P force the determinant I_(1+e) I_(3+e)-I_(2+e)^2=0; the powers of u cancel. At a reflected pencil root run the same determinant with j=1 and integrand (1-y)^e V(1-y), using the reflected V as a monic factor up to sign. At a fixed reverse endpoint t=0, e=1 is forbidden since P(0)≠0, and one runs the two-by-two determinant at u=1 on the k-root factors of the pencil of parameter -a itself. For k=0 the forward determinant is a nonzero rational fixed Hilbert determinant.

For a root-subset factor V with a possible extra reflected (1-x)^w, w=0 or 1, form the k-th compound matrix of xI-M, M the companion of q. Integrate its entries as above to obtain three normalized matrices A,B,C of adjacent moments. Under simultaneous triangularization of the induced compound matrices (triangularize M first), det(AC-B²) is the product of all the required factor Gram determinants. This needs only triangularity, not eigenvalue simplicity or mutual diagonalizability. For q_n=[x^n]q, the product is cleared by q_n^(2h'), h'=binom(n-1,k-1): as a symmetric polynomial in the n roots, each root has exponent at most 2h' since it appears in h' subsets and a determinant is at most quadratic in each root of its factor. The symmetric-polynomial theorem with this exponent bound uses at most 2h' elementary factors per term (successively cancel leading sorted monomials, or use the elementary basis indexed by conjugate partitions). Substituting the elementary ratios q_i/q_n proves the numerator polynomial claim after this clearing. Its rational degree in u is exactly 2(k+w)h+2n h', h=binom(n,k): in each normalized moment only the x^k term of V and the top term of the optional (1-x) can achieve its top degree, and the 2-by-2 Gram constant there is 1/[z(z+2)]-1/(z+1)^2≠0, z=j+k+w+1. All q_i have degree <=n. The script verifies this leading coefficient after reduction.

If F(u) is such a norm numerator, the parameter polynomial is Res_u(F(u),E_a(u)); its degree in a is <=deg F since E_a is affine in a and the Sylvester determinant is homogeneous in E_a coefficients of degree deg F. Every such parameter polynomial in this replay ATTAINS its bound modulo 1009. In particular its rational degree is preserved. For a reflected endpoint norm, the degree bound in the pencil parameter is 2*binom(m-1,k-1), m=degree E; it follows from the same symmetric-root exponent bound and the fact that its coefficients are affine while its leading coefficient is constant. Its full degree is likewise checked. Taking the univariate gcd of the forward parameter norm and the reflected polynomial at -a excludes simultaneous zeros even if the two norms use different factors, a stronger test than required. These gcds all equal 1 modulo 1009, with both rational degrees retained. The resultants over Q therefore cannot vanish. No inference is drawn merely from checking individual values of the parameter in a prime field.
