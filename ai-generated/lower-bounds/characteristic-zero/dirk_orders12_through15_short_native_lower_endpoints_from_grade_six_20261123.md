# Four known DIRK lower endpoints from one ordinary rank theorem

For unrestricted lower triangular Runge–Kutta methods, the all-rooted-tree order conditions alone imply
    p = 12, 13, 14, 15:       s >= 15, 17, 18, 20, respectively.
All four lower bounds, and every entry in the split table below, hold over **every characteristic-zero coefficient field**, including the complex numbers. No real-rooted stability-function inequality, ordering of the nodes or positivity of any RK weight is used. These numbers are existing real lower bounds, not improved real minima or existence results. The point is one short uniform proof; in particular the order-fifteen argument does not need the reality of a quasi-Lobatto pencil. At p=14 any hypothetical eighteen-stage solution must satisfy C(2).

**Native growth, with its arithmetic scope.** A plateau F_j=F_(j+1), j>=2 in the paid range, gives invariance under A and C. The quotient by this subspace inherits a triangular chronological flag with C still diagonal: split the invariant subspace by clock and eliminate a basis with distinct first pivots within each clock; the pivot is transferred only later within that clock. **Scalar dimensions.** If a genuine pure of grade m equals H(c), testing it by every available b q(c) gives integral H q =0. The rational ordinary moment matrix on P_d is nonsingular, so this separates polynomials of degree at most d<=min(m,p-1-m). With the true zero first row, H(0)=0 and the equally nonsingular x dx moment matrix separates through d<=min(m,p-m) by testing every q of degree at most d-1. All scalar quadratures used here have degree within p-1 (in particular for the anchored test, 2d-1<=p-1). This proves the scalar dimensions in the table, so before the equality test the lower count is f_m plus scalar dimension plus p-3-m.

**The single equality test.** At equality in any marked row of the table, E_(p-3) is full and all increases from E_m to there are single. Work at the putative minimum with no trailing zero output. The only class-sensitive trim is p=14: rule out a seventeen-stage array in both classes first, then trim an eighteen-stage g!=0 candidate only if that would leave a previously excluded seventeen stages. The same unrestricted baseline justification applies at p=13 and 15, so an arbitrary unused final stage never creates an extra premise.
