# A physical signed nonlinear pencil: forced nilpotence, blind principal stages and the per-stage price of nonzero eigenvalues

23 November 2026. For every classical order-20 nonsingular triangular RK tableau, define its literal weighted integration-by-parts defect M=BA+A^T B-b^T b and its receiver weight W=diag(bR), R=CA-AC-A^2. The degree of det(W+tM) is at most 0,20,36,48,56 at respectively 55,56,57,58,59 stages. Vanishing of the whole polynomial is allowed. If W is invertible, these numbers cap the count of NONZERO eigenvalues of W^-1 M with algebraic multiplicity. The integer increases priced here are conditional on actual measured coefficients, not an improvement of the unrestricted minimum. In particular a nonzero coefficient of degree 37 forces >=58 stages, degree 49 forces >=59, and degree 57 forces >=60. All results allow arbitrary signed/zero output weights and coincident/exterior clocks. The grade caps and nilpotence do NOT require equal diagonals. The strict common-diagonal specialization links W to the single genuine cheap f^T M receiver at the tied endpoint and gives explicit tap/weight tests.

## One paid grade of separation gives a whole numerical matrix pencil

Use stage grade (tree size minus one), D=p-2, e=all ones, c=Ae, C=diag(c), B=diag(b), and coordinate products. Take p>=2. For the ordinary flag E_a (all genuine stage fields of grade at most a) set e_a=dim E_a, e_-1=0. Scalar-profile integration is J from zero. The genuine operator R costs two and has zero profile by xJ-Jx-J^2=0. Therefore W=diag(bR) has
    x^T W y = bR(x*y)=0  whenever x in E_a, y in E_b, a+b<=D-1.
The symmetric numerical M has ONE MORE paid zero:
    x^T M y = b(x*Ay)+b(Ax*y)-(bx)(by)=0 when a+b<=D.
Indeed the two written original RK branches lie within total grade a+b+1<=p-1, and the two continuous terms integrate the derivative of (JX)(JY). The individual branch integrals bx,by in the correction are paid as well. No normalized transposed tableau, weight inversion or sign test is needed. These identities hold for arbitrary dense RK too; nonzero triangular diagonals will supply the explicit dimensions below.

Here is an absolute physical price including singular pencils. For any nonzero coefficient of t^k in det(W+tM),
    k <= s - sum_(a=0)^D (e_a+e_(D-a)-s)_+
      <= p s - 2 sum_(a=0)^D e_a.                                   (1)
Here x_+=max(0,x). A minor version still applies when det(W+tM) is IDENTICALLY ZERO. For a nonzero coefficient t^k in any (s-l)-square minor after ANY fixed row and column basis changes,
    k <= s-l - sum_(a=0)^D (e_a+e_(D-a)-s-l)_+.                    (2)
Exact intersection dimensions with the selected left and right subspaces can improve this last lower payment of W entries.

Choose a basis of the physical space adapted to E_0,...,E_D, assigning unused directions grade D+1 solely for this counting argument. Change basis by congruence. In any surviving determinant monomial, a pair of basis grades totaling <D is impossible, and a pair totaling exactly D can use only a W entry. At cut a any permutation must match at least (e_a+e_(D-a)-s)_+ of the rows through grade a with columns through D-a. Every such nonzero pairing has grades EXACTLY a,D-a. Different values of a therefore charge disjoint actual determinant places, even with arbitrary cancellations. This proves the first inequality of (1); summing the untruncated e_a+e_(D-a)-s gives the second. For (2) choose graded bases within the two subspaces defining the minor; their intersections with E_a have dimension at least e_a-l by codimension. Apply precisely the same determinant count to this smaller square. The argument is a necessary cost on a nonzero numerical coefficient; a merely allowed permutation does not show that an RK coefficient is nonzero.

For nonsingular triangular order p, e_a>=t_a from the invertible pure R recurrence, t_-2=t_-1=0, t_a=t_(a-2)+1+min(a,p-1-a). The scalar part separates by the nonsingular in-budget Hilbert Gram. For tied parity (p even or p=3 mod 4), L=ceil(floor((p+1)^2/4)/2), t_a+t_(D-a-1)=L, h_a=t_a-t_(a-1)=h_(D-a). At s=L+v, (1) yields
    k <= v + sum_(a=0)^D min(h_a,v) <= p v.                          (3)
This is a cost in units of actual extra stages, independent of any nonlinear pencil conjecture. At p20 the layer counts #{a:h_a>=j} are 19,15,11,7,3. The first through fifth extra stage can therefore pay at most 20,16,12,8,4 additional allowed degrees in the displayed cap. With invertible W, the corresponding minimum algebraic multiplicities of zero for W^-1M at s=55,...,59 are 55,36,21,10,3. For a specific tableau use the actual larger dimensions in (1). For untied p=1 mod 4, use (1) and the literal t_a rather than applying the tied parity symmetry. This theorem does not say that an unrestricted true strict order-20 tableau necessarily exceeds any of these caps.

The caps are exact as combinatorial consequences of those two vanishing regions and minimal flags, not claimed attained by actual tableaux at every order. To see this for the abstract tied grades, start with h_a stages at level a and v extra symbols at D+1. In each layer 1<=j<=v take the stages of that layer, J={a:h_a>=j}, which is symmetric about D/2. Introduce one fresh high symbol. Pair its row to the column at D-max J; pair the minimum-grade row to the fresh column; pair every later row in J to the column of degree D minus the preceding row's degree. All these pairs exceed D and the pairing can be made an involution by labeling corresponding symmetrical layers identically. Pair every remaining stage to its complementary grade using W. An unused empty layer pairs its extra symbol to itself. The resulting symmetric monomial matrices attain (3) in the abstract graded model. The companion checks all integers at p20. This is distinct from the genuine sharp strict p2 example below.

## The equality identity, including every mixed and zero-weight chart

At tied equality v=0 the actual determinant is a constant in t:
    det(W+tM)=det W;   equivalently det(M-zW)=(-z)^s det W.           (4)
In particular if W is invertible the actual W-self-adjoint matrix T=W^-1 M is NILPOTENT. More is true: T E_j is contained in E_(j-1). Indeed the W-orthogonal complement of E_(D-j) is E_(j-1), from the first paid zero above and the equality of complementary dimensions. The extra paid zero for M proves the drop. Thus T^(D+1)=0; for every h>=1 its rank is at most s-max_j(t_j-t_(j-h)), using zero negative t's. In Jordan language at p20 its numerical partition is dominated by (19,15,11,7,3): for powers h the upper on rank is s minus the sum of the h largest h_j, since the sequence h_j is unimodal and its largest h entries can be taken in a contiguous window. This upper Jordan constraint is on W^-1M, not the preceding LOWER Jordan constraint on I+R/gamma^2.

At strict tied p>=6, the previously established single-probe identity is f^TM=beta bR with beta!=0, f=c when g_s!=gamma^2/2 and f=2c^2-Ac otherwise. Thus W is exactly the entrywise diagonal of that one genuine receiver up to known nonzero scaling. In the exceptional clock-blind chart c^TM=0, if W is invertible then T E_1=0 and self-adjointness gives T E_D contained in E_(D-2). Since D>=4 these two opposite boundary losses imply T^(D-1)=0 (at p20, index at most 17). No ordinary order condition above p was introduced. On an all-active, all-dirty chart W is proportional to B diag(g); taking the trace T=0 yields the new coefficient identity sum_i (2gamma-b_i)/g_i=0. For variable diagonals replace 2gamma by 2a_ii in the same regular argument when the corresponding proportionality holds. Characteristic zero suffices.

Even when W is singular, set Z={i:(bR)_i=0}, z0=|Z|>0. The constant determinant (4) is then identically zero. The coefficient of t^z0 is the single principal minor det M[Z,Z] times product of the NONzero W_ii: each principal subset contributing must contain Z and has exactly z0 indices. Therefore
    det M[Z,Z]=0.                                                     (5)
There is no assumption that the blind set coincides with output ghosts, or that zero output weight implies zero bR. In fact the replay includes an actual zero-weight stage where bR is nonzero. Equation (5) is a constraint on a principal matrix of actual stage coefficients. In the strict case a singleton means b_i(2gamma-b_i)=0; a sole blind ACTIVE stage must have b_i=2gamma. For Z={i,j}, i<j, it gives
    b_i(2gamma-b_i) b_j(2gamma-b_j)=b_j^2(a_ji-b_i)^2.
If all stages of Z have individually nonzero weights and there are no taps between ANY two of them, then M[Z,Z]=2gamma B_Z-b_Z^T b_Z. The determinant lemma forces sum_(i in Z) b_i=2gamma whatever their signs. Nonblind stages may have arbitrary interactions and repeated/exterior abscissae. One cannot use this separated-stage formula when a blind stage is a ghost.

The corank also has direct lower tests if the determinant is identically zero. Put kappa_a=dim(E_a intersect K_Z) where K_Z is the blind coordinate space. The paid M zeros inside that coordinate subspace give
    corank M[Z,Z] >= max_a (kappa_a+kappa_(D-a)-z0)_+ .
This maximum is at least one by the complementary entry-grade inequality in the previous single-probe note. For any scalar t not necessarily generic, if r_a is the first-unpaid ordinary product rank of E_a^T W E_(D-a) as in that note, then
    corank(W+tM) >= max_a (h_a-r_a).
Restrict its rows and columns to E_a,E_(D-a): that block has exactly rank r_a since its M component vanishes. At most 2s-t_a-t_(D-a) ranks can be recovered outside it. This prices actual missing branch directions even when a determinant-only report would be vacuous.
