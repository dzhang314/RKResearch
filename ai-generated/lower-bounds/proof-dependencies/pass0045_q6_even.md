# Order 12 requires at least 18 real explicit Runge–Kutta stages

Proof is complete modulo independent audit of the separately written reverse rank-one lemma below. This is a computation-assisted exact proof, with finite split-prime determinant and factor certificates, not a numerical nonexistence argument. We do not claim an order-13 bound.

Notation is as in the reproduced earlier proof prerequisites: F_j is the grade-j pure defect space, E_j=P_j(c)+F_j, C=diag(c), A strictly lower triangular, and L_t the backward space generated by bX^(h-1)A-bX^h/h, X=I-C. Write P_i for polynomials of degree at most i and their evaluations where appropriate. Terminal coordinate vector is e_s. We use the elementary baseline, strong Hermite gain and rank-one chain lemma from the attached prerequisites.

## 1. Exact forward and backward rank chart

Suppose order 12 with s≤17. The baseline forces s=17 and dim F5=5, dim F6=6, E6 rank13 by separation. Remove trailing zero-weight stages; b_s≠0 by the baseline. Orthogonality L5 E6=0 and strict backward growth give L5=E6^perp, dim L5=4, ell1=0; consequently dim L_t=t−1 for 1≤t≤5, c_s=1 and every L_t vanishes at last. Thus e_s∈E6. Products with F5 tested at grade≤11 give F5 last-zero.

If F6 were C-invariant, strong Hermite through separated grade6 would give dim F7≥9. But E7 has rank≤14 from L4. The subspace F7^0 of last-zero vectors intersects P6 trivially: f=x(1−x)g for a possible intersecting polynomial, deg g≤4, and test g using 7+4≤11. Thus dim F7^0≤7 and dim F7≤8, contradiction. F5 similarly is not C-invariant, since dim F6 only grows by one. Rank-one escape gives a C-invariant W0 and a chain head w, which can initially be chosen orthogonal to W0:
    F5=W0⊕w P_(r−1),  F6=W0⊕w P_r,
where dim W0=5−r, r≥2 by strong Hermite on W0 inside F5 (dim F6≥dim W0+3). Both spaces, as well as Z1=F6+C F6=W0+wP_(r+1), vanish last. Z1 rank7 and P6∩Z1=0 by the same last-zero moment test, so E7=P6⊕Z1. Moreover F7^0=Z1 (compare dimensions or polynomial component), and the possible ordinary F7 polynomial leakage in E7 is only the line H7=x J5, J5 degree-five orthogonal for x dx: any polynomial f in F7 vanishes at 0, and tests of degree≤4 leave only that line.

E7 and E8 cannot be C-invariant: an invariant capped E_j would make L_(11−j)=E_j^perp X-invariant, but backward strong Hermite for t=4 and t=3 would force ≥2 new dimensions while exact ranks grow by one. Thus the equality chart propagates with one new chain power at each step (CP6⊂E7; E_j+C E_j already supplies the new power):
    E_j=P6⊕W0⊕wP_(r+j−6), j=7,8,9.
In particular E9 rank16. If it were C-invariant, L2 would be X-invariant. The reverse rank-one lemma in Appendix A excludes it: reverse N=A^T, Y=X, v=b^T in reversed coordinates; Nv=Yv, Y_1=0 and v_1=b_s≠0; L2…L5 ranks1…4; scalar quadrature of Y with v is exact through degree11. This lemma uses no signs or nonzero assumptions on the remaining v entries. Thus E9 is also noninvariant and the same expression holds at E10, with rank17 (the entire coordinate space). In particular w has N≥r+5 distinct effective nodes. Here and below an effective node means the block of w on that abscissa is not in W0, so chains have rank min(power count,N) modulo W0 by Vandermonde; with orthogonal head initially these are exactly its support nodes.

The terminal representation e_s=f(c)+d_s, d_s∈F6 has f=x L'_6/42 with L_6 the shifted ordinary Legendre polynomial: f(0)=0, f(1)=1 and endpoint moments tested by (1−x)g, deg g≤4, give f/x on the degree-five orthogonal line for x(1−x) dx. The monic polynomial relation G(c)∈Z1 of degree7 comes from (C−I)e_s=0, so G is proportional to (x−1)f. In E8 any last-zero F8 product has a polynomial part on the single line H8=x(1−x)D4, D4 degree-four orthogonal for x(1−x) dx (moment tests through degree3). For two consecutive shifts both in last-zero F8, compare their polynomial parts modulo G in E9; their quotients by x(1−x) are in the single line D4. Multiplication by x has a nonzero D3 coefficient, not removed by division by the degree-five D5=G/[x(1−x)] line. Hence two shifts both have zero leakage. All node-local products that below remain in F8 after C shifts therefore have no leakage.

## 2. Prefix contrast with a diagonal multiplier

If diagonal multiplication by v maps F5 into W0+wP_(r−1+d), where N≥r+d+1, then it preserves W0 and v on the support of the orthogonal original w agrees with a polynomial P of degree≤d. Indeed each node-local W0 input maps to one node, so its chain component vanishes at N−1 nodes and hence is zero; by symmetry the multiplier maps the orthogonal complement of W0 into itself. Apply to w and its first r−1 shifts, comparing successive polynomial expressions by Vandermonde, to obtain degree≤d. If v∉W0 then P is nonzero, since v=wR+W0 and if it vanished on support(w), pairing nodewise with w would force the polynomial R to vanish on N nodes.

For any polynomial Q with Q(0)=0, the contrast Q(C)(P(c)−v) vanishes on support(w). Its nonzero polynomial part ensures it is outside W0 by separation if graph grade≤6, and we can use the first prefix with mismatch to W0. The joint blocks by C and diag(v) preserve W0, so the mismatch has a node-local detecting row λ on one joint block (u,τ), where τ≠P(u) and Q(u)≠0; this block is entirely off the support of w. Thus λ kills W0+chain of every power, and λ1≠0 because the mismatch value is constant on its block. To take a moment with multiplier h, correct earlier rows by a matching W0 vector times h(C), whose image under A lies in F6. Strict causality then annihilates λ A applied to the corrected contrast, and its stage-polynomial integral identity says
    λ A contrast / λ1 = integral_0^u (Qh)P + grade-appropriate leakage at u.
Equivalently first use λ C-polynomial multiplication and then subtract the matched prefix as above. In cases with an actual mask algebra and head w=P(c) on S disjoint from masks, instead subtract (QP)(C)e_T from the contrast, where e_T∈W0 is the mask union indicator; first nonzero row is off both masks and w support. This yields the same moment identity. The A-image of any W0 correction is in F6 for every h, since W0 is C-invariant.

If deg P≤2 and v has grade≤2 (or intrinsic grading C²w∈F4), all R=Qh of degree≤3 give stage input grade≤5 and A grade≤6, and 3+2 Jacobi tests contradict the previously reproduced determinant (or just the first three tests when P has degree≤2). For cubic-or-lower P and v∈F3, or v=w∈F4 with Cw∈F4, degree≤2 costs A grade≤6, and degree3 costs A grade≤7. Leakage is then a single scalar α times the coefficient of x³ of R, since the stage defect expression A[R(C)(P(c)−v)]−J(RP)(c) depends linearly on R. Possible H7 leakage at node u is α H7(u). The endpoint e_s representation and λ killing F6 imply f(u)=0 when detected prior to s; if first detected at s, u=1. Thus eligible nonzero endpoints belong to {1}∪roots L'_6. Two masks Q=x and Q=x(x−u) give these five tests on P (adjust α sign by convention if needed):
    integral_0^u xP=0; integral_0^u x²P=0; integral_0^u x³P+αH7(u)=0;
    integral_0^a x(x−u)P=0; integral_0^a x²(x−u)P+αH7(a)=0.
Here a≠u,0. The 5×5 two-endpoint system on the four coefficients of P and α is nonsingular for all 30 ordered distinct pairs by the Appendix B split-prime certificate (mod43). Contradiction.

## 3. Exclude all but rank-two chain length with dim F4=4

Let d=Ac−c²/2 span F2, the one nontrivial degree-two defect. Products dF5 are in F7^0=Z1, as F5 is last-zero, so the diagonal lemma with d parameter 2 and grade-two contrast force F2⊂W0. Thus r≤4.

For r=3 or 4, if v∈F3 escapes W0, products v·C^h w for 0≤h≤r−1 are all in last-zero F8. They are consecutive shifts, so the E8/E9 argument in §1 kills their leakage. Products v·W0 and all their shifts similarly are in last-zero F8, since W0 is invariant. Consequently diag(v) F5 ⊂ W0+wP_(r+2), and diagonal lemma degree3 plus Appendix B contradict v∉W0. If r=4 then W0=span d and Ad∈F3 escapes W0: otherwise F2 invariant under A,C, and the triangular invariant-subspace test would put c in F2 (Ad∝d, and Ac−c²/2=d), impossible. Thus r4 is excluded. If r=3 then F3⊂W0 implies F3=W0 as dimF3≥2, and strong Hermite forces dimF4≥4. As dimF4≤4, equality.

Whenever dimF4=4, from CF4⊂F5 and comparing all effective nodes we have F4=W0⊕wP_(r−2): the top F5 chain power cannot occur, and dimensions match. Since W0⊂F4, for any node-local f∈W0 and g∈F4, products fg and all C shifts lie in last-zero F8 and have no leakage. Their chain polynomial vanishes at every other effective node; there are N−1≥r+4, greater than r+2, so fg lies in W0. This proves W0 is a coordinatewise mask algebra (partition into classes with distinct profiles of W0; interpolate a generic W0 vector to get class indicators) and also that the head may be corrected to vanish on those masks by subtracting e_T w∈W0. For r3 dimF4=4, w,Cw∈F4, so w²,Cw²,C²w² remain grade8, eliminating their E8 leakage. Their shifted chain polynomial representatives have degree≤r+2=5, so comparing at N>6 effective nodes twice gives w=P(c) on S with deg P≤3. Use mask-corrected contrast and degree7 certificate to exclude.

For r2 and dim F4=3, C²F3⊂F5 shows F3⊂W0 by comparing chain polynomials of degree at most 3. Noninvariance of F3 by Hermite forces F4=F3+C F3=W0 and dim F3=2. The same grade8 local argument makes W0 a three-class mask algebra. There are N≥7 effective chain abscissae and at most three abscissae in masks, so at least four distinct off-mask abscissae. This contradicts the order-15 bootstrap: at off-mask rows the sums of A coefficients from the three masks annihilate the two independent F3 profiles, since A F3⊂W0, hence are confined to a common line. Let h be the first nonzero off-mask abscissa. The pure off-mask moments A c^j=c^(j+1)/(j+1), j=1,2,3, fix the second/third moments of that line relative to the first U as (2h/3)U and (h²/2)U. At first new off-mask y, preceding off-mask nonzero nodes are h only, with coefficient sum S. Then U+hS=y²/2, (2h/3)U+h²S=y³/3, (h²/2)U+h³S=y⁴/4. Subtracting (3h/2) second from third+(h²/2) first forces y²(y−h)²=0. So there are only {0,h} off-mask nodes, contradiction.

## 4. Remaining rank-two, dim F4=4 branch

Here W0 is the same three-mask algebra above and the cleaned w∈F4 vanishes on mask support T. Let S be nonzero w support, R the complement of S∪T. We have E8=P6⊕W0⊕wP4, and the square w² is in last-zero F8. Its polynomial part H is on the line H8 above. On S write
    f(c)=w Q(c),    w²=H(c)+w R0(c),
where deg Q≤2 by the F6 terminal relation (sign absorbed), and deg R0≤4 by E8. On R, f(c)=0 except at terminal c=1, and H(c)=0.

If H is nonzero, its nonterminal common zero set with f is only c=0, since adjacent D4,D5 for x(1−x) have no common root; thus R has at most two features. For Q≠0 define Φ=f²−fQR0−H Q². It vanishes on S, has degree at most 12, and is not zero: if identically zero, D5=f/x up to normalization divides (H/x)Q², impossible by coprimality with H/x and deg Q²≤4. A node where Q≠0 has only one possible w value f/Q. At a shared zero of Q and f, Φ has multiplicity at least two and the quadratic w²−wR0(c)−H(c)=0 allows at most two w values. Moreover Φ always vanishes at zero. If zero is off S this is one unused multiplicity; if zero is on S then Q(0)=f(0)=0, multiplicity at least two, but H(0)=0 permits at most one nonzero w, again one unused multiplicity. Thus S has at most 11 distinct (c,w) feature pairs. For Q=0, all S nodes are among the six distinct roots of f (including zero); at most two nonzero w values at each nonzero root, one at zero, still at most 11. Along with three mask classes and at most two R features this bounds the row feature count by 16. Yet E10=P6⊕W0⊕wP6 has rank17, and all its evaluations depend only on these features. Contradiction.

If H=0 then w=P(c) on S, deg P≤4. As N≥7, f=P Q is a polynomial identity by degree (f has degree6); hence deg P=4 and deg Q=2. All roots of P are distinct, and P never vanishes on S. At first contrast mismatch (outside T and S), take mask x if P(0)≠0, or mask 1 if P(0)=0 (in this case the polynomial factor already vanishes at zero). Preceding rows vanish, and the A-primitive residual has grade≤6, so the causal moment at nonzero u (a root of f other than P's roots, or 1) is zero. In the former case it is ∫_0^u xP(x) dx for P a quartic selection of L'_6 roots up to scale. In the latter case it is ∫_0^u P(x) dx, with P=x times a cubic selection. Appendix B split-prime check (mod181) rules out both: 40 residues all nonzero. This excludes the last possibility and proves order-12 s≥18.

## Appendix B. Exact replay certificates (standard-library Python)
from itertools import permutations
p=43
D=[-42,840,-5040,12600,-13860,5544]
H=[0,-6,105,-560,1260,-1260,462]
def ev(P,x): return sum(c*pow(x,i,p) for i,c in enumerate(P))%p
nodes=[2,9,22,35,42]
assert D[-1]%p and all(ev(D,u)==0 for u in nodes)
def det(A):
    A=[r[:] for r in A]; d=1
    for i in range(len(A)):
        j=next((j for j in range(i,len(A)) if A[j][i]%p),None)
        if j is None: return 0
        if j!=i: A[i],A[j]=A[j],A[i]; d=-d
        q=A[i][i]%p; d=d*q%p
        for j in range(i+1,len(A)):
            t=A[j][i]*pow(q,-1,p)%p
            for k in range(i+1,len(A)):
                A[j][k]=(A[j][k]-t*A[i][k])%p
    return d
def moments(u,t): return [pow(u,j+t+1,p)*pow(j+t+1,-1,p)%p for j in range(4)]
Dets=[]
for u,a in permutations(nodes+[1],2):
    U=[moments(u,t) for t in (1,2,3)]
    V=[moments(a,t) for t in (1,2,3)]
    Dets.append(det([U[0]+[0],U[1]+[0],U[2]+[ev(H,u)],
                     [(b-u*c)%p for c,b in zip(V[0],V[1])]+[0],
                     [(b-u*c)%p for c,b in zip(V[1],V[2])]+[ev(H,a)]]))
assert len(Dets)==30 and all(Dets) and sum(Dets)%p==37

print('order-12 ghost: 30 nonzero determinants, checksum 37 mod 43')

from itertools import combinations
p=181
roots=[52,81,91,101,130]
assert all(p%d for d in range(2,14)) and D[-1]%p and all(ev(D,a)==0 for a in roots)
R=[]
for d in (3,4):
    for subset in combinations(roots,d):
        coeff=[1]
        for a in subset:
            coeff=[(b-a*c)%p for b,c in zip([0]+coeff,coeff+[0])]
        for u in [1]+[a for a in roots if a not in subset]:
            R.append(sum(c*pow(u,i+2,p)*pow(i+2,-1,p)
                         for i,c in enumerate(coeff))%p)
assert len(R)==40 and all(R) and sum(R)%p==79
print('order-12 pure factor: 40 nonzero moments, checksum 79 mod 181')

The listed mod43 roots are [2,9,22,35,42] for L'_6, with normalization H7=x Jacobi(5,0,1,2x−1) as in the code; eligible nodes are these roots and 1. The determinant matrix consists exactly of the five rows in §2, ordered by P coefficients in increasing degree and α. The mod181 roots are [52,81,91,101,130]. In both primes leading coefficients and all small integration denominators are units. For a characteristic-zero candidate place its assigned roots in the monic ordered-root splitting algebra over Z_(prime) (adjoin roots successively and divide by x−root). This algebra is finite free and its mod-prime reduction splits as a product of a field for each ordering (all roots simple and distinct). Each formal determinant or moment in the proof is nonzero in every component, so its multiplication norm is a unit, preventing a zero in any characteristic-zero ordered-root specialization. This proves an exact nonexistence certificate rather than a random specialization test.
