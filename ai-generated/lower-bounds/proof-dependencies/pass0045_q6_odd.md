# Backward localized-L2 chronology at q=6: second ordering removed, third still open

This extends pass2345_q7analytic.md. The even order-12 and odd prefix order-13 equality cases have ℓ1=0 and exact ranks dim L_j=j−1 for 1≤j≤5. Given localized reverse L2≠0 (the only possibility if L2 is diagonal invariant), the proof below excludes the ordering in which the first off active nonzero node y occurs before the second independent local feature at the L2 node a, **but the second independent feature comes before any second off active nonzero node**. The earlier note already excludes the ordering with a's second feature first. A chronology with two distinct off active nodes before the second a feature is not excluded; cancellation can even leave the L2 localized vectors proportional to v throughout that node, in which case the second feature never exists. No full order-13 numerical result claimed in this note.

## Notation and first two features

Work in reverse with N=Aᵀ and Y=diag(1−c) (both reversed). Put v=bᵀ reversed. Then Nv=Yv and first coordinate has Y=0 and v≠0. Let d=NYv−Y²v/2 (L2 generator), g=Nd. Scale N,Y by the nonzero L2 support node so a=1 (up to the naturally induced scaling of v,d,g and all ℓ_j). The first nonzero Y_i v_i must be at node 1, since NYv=0 at that first row and d=−v/2 there. Until the second independent feature at that node, d=−v/2 there, and d=0 elsewhere. Before first off active node y∉{0,1}, this relation makes g=0: for all preceding inputs Nd=−NYv/2 and NYv=Y²v/2+d vanishes on all existing rows including ghosts. At first y, g_y=−y²v_y/4≠0. Consequently
    ℓ3=NY²v−Y³v/3=(2/3)d+κ g,   κ=4y/3−2,
as seen at the first nonzero d (g=0, ℓ3=(2/3)d) and first y (NY²v=NYv).

Strong backward Hermite amplification forces L3 and L4 not Y-invariant, because they have successors of rank only one more while floor((j+1)/2)≥2 for j=3,4. Thus L4=span(d,g,Yg) and L5=span(d,g,Yg,Y²g). On the first nonzero d and first y rows one has Ng=0 by chronology (all preceding g were zero); also ℓ4=(1/2)d at the first d row and ℓ4(y)=(y²−2)g_y. Hence the forms
    Ng=k(Y−yI)g,
    ℓ4=NY³v−Y⁴v/4=d/2+[y²−2+l(Y−yI)]g
hold for some real k,l.

## The second a feature before the second off active node

Suppose that, after first y, a row r at node 1 with v+2d≠0 occurs before any second distinct nonzero off active node. Before r, the only active nodes are 0,1,y and d=−v/2 at node1, hence S1=−2g for the incoming weighted sum S1 of v at node1. Let Sy be the corresponding sum at y. The second and third stage moments give
    −2g+y Sy=x²v/2+d,
    −2g+y² Sy=x³v/3+(2/3)d+κg
at a new row of node x. Eliminating Sy gives
    g=x²(2x−3y)v/(4y)+(2−3y)d/(2y).
So all earlier g are zero off y and are −y²v/4 at y, including arbitrary v=0 ghost rows; there is no assumption of distinct stages. At r, set ζ=(2−3y)/(4y), η=−y²/4. Then g_r=ζ(v_r+2d_r). If y=2/3, this vanishes but Sy=(v_r+2d_r)/(2y) is nonzero, contradicting Ng=k(Y−y)g at r because Ng_r=η Sy. Otherwise substitute these values at r in Ng and ℓ4 to obtain
    k=y/(3y−2),   l=−k(y−1).

## Rigorous continuation and exceptional repeats

After r, maintain by induction that all nonzero v are at 0,1,y, with g=ζ(v+2d) at 1, g=ηv at y, and g=0 elsewhere. For the next row x, add incoming weighted sums S1,Sy from preceding rows; d inputs at node1 sum to g_x by construction. The four relations are
    S1+y Sy=x²v/2+d,
    S1+y² Sy=x³v/3+(2/3)d+κg,
    S1+y³ Sy=x⁴v/4+d/2+[y²−2−k(y−1)(x−y)]g,
    ζ S1+2ζ g+η Sy=k(x−y)g.
Eliminate S1 and form the augmented determinant for the three resulting equations in g,Sy. It is
   −y(x−1)(y−1)[(12y²−14y+4)d+3v x² y(x−1)(x−y)]/[12(3y−2)] .
At x≠1 one has d=0, so a distinct nonzero x∉{1,y} cannot have v≠0. The first and second pair minors after eliminating S1 are, up to nonzero factors, 3xy−3x−5y+4 and 3xy+y−2. Simultaneous vanishing forces y=1/2,x=1 (the other y=2/3 was excluded). At every nonspecial row the induction values solve, and hence uniquely solve, the system; at x=1 the accompanying values are S1=−2g and Sy=(v/2+d+2g)/y, and at x=y or x=0 the solution has Sy=0.

For the exceptional y=1/2,x=1, use N(Y−yI)g∈L5. Its d coefficient is zero by evaluation at the first nonzero d. Its remaining quadratic coefficient on g vanishes at first y (all earlier g zero), and at r (all earlier nonzero g supported at y and g_r≠0). At any repeated node1 row its left side is thus zero; by earlier induction it equals (1−y)ζ(S1+2g). Hence S1=−2g, and the first two moment equations now give g=ζ(v+2d), restoring the required feature. This closes the induction. Only three active Y nodes would remain, contrary to exactness of scalar quadrature on the square of their cubic vanishing polynomial (degree six).

## Unresolved order and caution

If y then z (both distinct active off nodes) precede the second independent feature r at node1, at first z the same calculation gives g_z=z²(2z−3y)v_z/(4y), requiring 2z≠3y because Ng_z=−z²(z−y)v_z/4≠0. The affine parameters now become k=y/(3y−2z) and l=−k(y−z), not those above. Incoming active support now includes 1,y,z, so after eliminating S1 the four primary equations give three equations in three unknowns g,Sy,Sz and do not give the augmented determinant obstruction above. Additional degree-five relations (N(Y−y)g quadratic coefficient has roots y,z; ℓ5 exact) are the next credible route but are not proved to rule out this chronology. Do not report full L2 nonsaturation.

## Completion: second off node precedes completion of a (or a never completes)

This closes the remaining reverse chronology, so together with the preceding section and the first chronology from pass2345_q7analytic.md it proves **backward L2 is not Y-invariant** under ℓ1=0, exact dims 1,2,3,4, and scalar quadrature exact through degree 8 or higher. The q6 equal-stage cases with ℓ1=0 satisfy these conditions.

Assume first off active y, then second distinct off active z∉{0,1,y}, both before the second independent feature at node 1. In the preceding derivation before that independent feature, S1=−2g, and with only 0,1,y all rows satisfy
    g=x²(2x−3y)v/(4y)+(2−3y)d/(2y).
At first z, d=0 and this yields γ_z:=g_z/v_z=z²(2z−3y)/(4y). The weighted sum of previously incoming y nodes is Sy=z²(z−y)v_z/y², by the first moment and S1=−2g. Consequently Ng_z=−(y²/4)Sy=−z²(z−y)v_z/4≠0. Thus 2z≠3y; comparison of Ng and ℓ4 at this row gives
    k=y/(3y−2z),   l=−k(y−z),
with the same forms Ng=k(Y−yI)g and ℓ4=d/2+[y²−2+l(Y−yI)]g.

We now induct that the only active nodes are 0,1,y,z; node 1 continues to satisfy d=−v/2 and g=0; g=ηv at y for η=−y²/4, g=γ_z v at z, and g=0 at all other rows. Let S1,Sy,Sz be incoming weighted sums at the next row x. The induction makes S1=−2g. The four equations read
    −2g+y Sy+z Sz=x²v/2+d,
    −2g+y² Sy+z² Sz=x³v/3+(2/3)d+κg,
    −2g+y³ Sy+z³ Sz=x⁴v/4+d/2+[y²−2+l(x−y)]g,
    η Sy+γ_z Sz=k(x−y)g.
Eliminate Sy by the first equation and consider the last three equations in g,Sz. In the augmented determinant, the fixed nonzero prefactor is z(y−z)/[12(3y−2z)]. The remaining factor has these useful evaluations (verify by substitution and expansion):
    d=0:     3 v x² y (x−y)(x−z)²;
    x=1:     3 y (1−y)(1−z)² (v+2d).
Both must vanish. Thus x outside {0,1,y,z} cannot have v≠0, and at x=1 the earlier proportional relation d=−v/2 remains. The first/second and first/third coefficient minors are, up to nonzero factors,
    3xy−3xz−5yz+4z²,      3xy+yz−2z².
Divide by z² and solve just as in the preceding section: simultaneous vanishing gives y=z/2,x=z (the other y=2z/3 is excluded). At all nonexceptional rows the candidates with Sz=0 and the previous g formula satisfy the three equations (at x=1 they have v+2d=0). They are therefore unique and preserve all stated invariants, even at ghost nodes where v=0 but x might be arbitrary.

The exceptional repeat x=z,y=z/2 is discharged by N(Y−yI)g∈L5. Its d coefficient is zero at the first nonzero d row; its quadratic coefficient on g vanishes at the first y and first z, since before z all nonzero g are on y and at both rows g is nonzero. Thus the left-hand side vanishes at x=z. By the induction hypothesis the left-hand side is (z−y)γ_z Sz (g at 1 vanishes, and y contributes zero), so Sz=0. The first two moment equations now give the required g formula. This completes induction. There are at most four active Y nodes, contradicting scalar exactness for the square of their monic vanishing polynomial (degree at most eight). No v_i sign, distinctness, or nonzero assumptions were needed.

Consequence for q6: in the even order-12 equality setup and odd order-13 prefix (ℓ1=0) setup, E_(p−3) already attains its upper cap and its annihilator is L2, a one-dimensional row space (in the odd case use the prefix because all backward rows have zero last original coordinate). Hence E_(p−3) is not C-invariant and the chain extension advances one more grade to E_(p−2), saturating full dimension of the retained space. This lemma is not itself a full p12 or p13 exclusion.

## Order 13 prefix: early-terminal genuine leakage, r=2 and dim F4=4, excluded

Assume the q6 odd prefix equality case (s=19, ℓ1=0), r=2, dim F4=4 and e_s∈E7. A grade-eight multiplication argument already in pass2345_q6.md gives the mask algebra W0 (three mask classes at fixed abscissae) and mask-corrected head w∈F4, Cw∈F5, supported away from the masks. Write S for occupied chain stages and R for the complement of S and T (mask support). The completed backward L2 lemma makes retained E11 have dimension 18. This section excludes a nonzero polynomial leakage in w² at grade eight for every such early terminal case, independent of aliasing.

Since grade(e_s)+grade(F5)≤7+5=12, testing bᵀ times the product implies F5 vanishes at the last stage. In particular W0,w, and their pure C-shifts vanish there. In the grade-eight decomposition on the retained prefix write
    w²=H(c)+w R0(c)+h0 ,    h0∈W0,
where H is nonzero, deg R0≤4 (no alias) or ≤5 (alias). The lower projected chain gives a polynomial G with G(c)=w Q(c)+g0 on the retained stages, g0∈W0 and deg Q≤3. In no alias G=x(D6+θD5), in alias G=xD5, with each D_i monic orthogonal polynomial for x(1−x) on [0,1]. In no alias the lift of G has grade seven; in alias it also has grade seven because the advanced chain starts with the Z1 relation. For H the lift is grade eight no alias or nine alias, so moment tests through degree three (after factoring x(1−x) in no alias) or degree two (alias) show H/x=A D5+B D4 (no alias) or H/x=A D4+B D3 (alias). The equality in full coordinates adds only a multiple of e_s, and all chain terms and w² vanish last. Hence
    ∫G=b_s G(1),     ∫H=b_s H(1).
G(1)≠0 in alias; in no alias, if G(1)=0 its grade-seven lift would hold also at the last stage, implying G/x is the monic degree-six polynomial orthogonal for x on [0,1]. It cannot then vanish at 1, by interior simplicity of roots. Thus b_s=∫G/G(1).

Put r_m=∫xD_m/D_m(1)=1/[(m+1)(m+2)], from integration by parts on shifted Legendre L_{m+1}. In alias b_s=r_5=1/42. Since D_4(1)=1/42, D_3(1)=1/14, the last integral relation forces A/B=−33/4 (B≠0 for genuine H). At any common nonzero root u of G and H, D5=(u−1/2)D4−(2/33)D3 gives u=1/2−(2/33)A/B=1, contradiction. In no alias D6(1)=1/429 and D5(1)=1/132 give
    b_s=(13θ+3)/[42(13θ+4)],     A/B=−286(2θ+1)/35.
Here B≠0 because r_5−b_s≠0 (its difference is due to the nonzero D6 term). At any common nonzero root u the recurrence D6=(u−1/2)D5−(35/572)D4 gives u=1/2−θ−(35/572)A/B=1, again impossible. Adjacent orthogonal polynomial values in each recurrence cannot simultaneously vanish at a root, so these deductions are valid also at complex roots. Hence R has only the zero abscissa (because both relations vanish there on R, and any R point must be a common root).

For Q not the zero polynomial define Φ=G²−G Q R0−H Q², vanishing at every retained S abscissa and of degree≤14 in both cases. It is nonzero as polynomial: otherwise G/x must divide (H/x)Q². Consecutive monic orthogonal polynomials and their real pencil have simple roots (the pencil is the characteristic polynomial of an irreducible real symmetric Jacobi matrix with final diagonal shifted), and G/x has degree 5 or 6 and shares no roots with H/x except possibly zero. In these particular two H combinations the ratio at zero also proves they cannot share zero; alternatively allow the one potential common zero, still leaving at least four distinct required Q roots. Since deg Q≤3 this is impossible. A nonzero S node with Q≠0 determines only one head value w; with Q=0 it forces G=0, giving double Φ root and at most two possible w values from the quadratic square relation. At zero Φ always vanishes. If zero belongs S, w≠0 forces Q(0)=0 and Φ has multiplicity≥2 there, while the square relation only allows one nonzero w=R0(0), saving at least one degree. If zero not in S its factor also saves one degree. Thus at most 13 different (c,w) S-feature rows. If Q=0 identically, S abscissae are among roots of G, at most seven including zero, and the same quadratic allows at most 13 features including the zero saving. T contributes three classes; R only one. Every E11 row depends only on those features (prefix P_d⊕W0⊕head powers), so retained E11 has rank at most 17, contradiction.

This excludes genuine H only under e_s∈E7, r=2, dim F4=4 in the odd prefix. If H=0, the exact-factor branch remains, as do later terminal-entry grades and other ranks. Do not claim full order-13 count from this section.
