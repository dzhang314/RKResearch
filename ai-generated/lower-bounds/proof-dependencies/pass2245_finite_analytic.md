# Elementary finite-order threshold: every real explicit order p≥16 needs at least ceil(3p/2) stages

This supplement proves p=16,17,18,19 uniformly. For p≥20 use the prior elementary two-contrast theorem. No modular computations, stage-weight nonzero assumptions (except trim trailing zeros), sign assumptions, or restrictions on abscissae. The proof uses the audited graded basic lemmas and the strong Hermite lemma, restated as needed. It discards the old order-18/19 modular certificates.

## Graded setup and the chain

Let A strictly lower triangular, c=A1, C=diag(c), b the output row. P_j(c) are evaluations of degree≤j real polynomials; F_j are pure stage defects of grade≤j; E_j=P_j(c)+F_j. For stage–polynomial pairs, products grade-add and unary application A raises grade by one while integrating the polynomial from 0. All defects vanish at stage one, c_1=0, and b evaluates the integral of the representing polynomial through grade p−1. In particular AF_j,CF_j⊂F_(j+1), and products F_aF_b⊂F_(a+b). Forward separation P_j⊕F_j holds through q=floor(p/2), with the polynomial evaluation injective. Backward rows L_t are generated by residuals ℓ_i=bX^(i−1)A−bX^i/i (X=I−C), and right A,X at unit grade, with L_t E_j=0 for t+j≤p−1. Backward ranks grow at least one at each grade t≥2; L1 has dimension 0 or 1. The elementary surplus inequality from the audited baseline is s≥ceil(3p/2)−2+dim F_q−(q−1), with dim F5≥5 and strict forward growth, giving s≥ceil(3p/2)−1. The weaker elementary bound s_min(j)≥ceil(3j/2)−2 holds for j≥5.

Suppose s=ceil(3p/2)−1 for p=16,…,19, so q=8 or 9 and dim F_j=j for 5≤j≤q. Trim any trailing zero weights; equality makes b_s≠0. The strong Hermite amplification says if U⊂F_j is C-invariant then dim F_(j+1)≥dim U+floor((j+1)/2). In particular F5,…,F_(q−1) are noninvariant. F_q is also noninvariant: invariant completion forces dim F_q≥s_min(q)−1>q by the elementary baseline (since q≥8), with no order-nine computation. Hence rank-one C-escape from F5 gives C-invariant W0 and a cyclic head w, with r=5−dim W0, such that
    F_j=W0⊕w P_(r+j−6)(c)       (5≤j≤q).
The powers C^a w for 0≤a<r belong to F5. Strong amplification with U=W0 at j=5 gives 6≥(5−r)+3, hence r≥2. We use no case split on dim F4.

For clarity, the strong Hermite lemma also holds for arbitrary backward starting vector v=bᵀ in reversed coordinates, diagonal Y=X, and triangular N=Aᵀ in those coordinates. Here are its exact hypotheses and proof, covering the forward lemma too. The columns v,Yv,…,Y^l v are independent modulo an invariant U contained in the grade-l defect space; N U is in the grade-(l+1) defect space; and N Y^a v−Y^(a+1)v/(a+1) is in that same latter space for 0≤a≤l. Chronologically compress the span of the columns and U: retain successive independent coordinate evaluations, substitute each omitted evaluation by earlier retained ones in N, and preserve Y by coordinate restriction. The annihilator of U in the compressed space decomposes into l+1 node-local rows, one per distinct scalar node, each normalized to evaluate v as 1. Indeed node-local rows annihilating v as well annihilate the entire compressed space. Take the first floor((l+1)/2) nonzero node rows, ordered by support completion time. If a nonzero linear combination λ killed all grade-(l+1) defects, its latest contributing row u_i sees λN supported strictly before its completion and annihilating U. Thus λN is a combination of earlier node rows. Choose P=x²∏_(earlier nonzero u)(x−u)² if a zero node row occurs earlier, and P=x∏_(earlier nonzero u)(x−u)² otherwise. Then P(0)=0, deg P'≤l, P' vanishes at every earlier node, and λP(Y)v is nonzero by evaluating at the latest row. The primitive condition gives λ N P'(Y)v=λP(Y)v instead of zero, contradiction. This proves the claimed gain. No condition on the first weight or first node is needed in the backward use. Backward polynomial independence modulo L_l follows by pairing any candidate relation among X^a bᵀ with the same polynomial in X on the forward side and using ∫ f²>0 when 2l≤p−1.

## Three setups for the exact extended chain

Throughout this section use hat to mean either the identity or restriction to the first s−1 stages, as specified. Products and C commute with restriction; A on retained prefix rows depends only on that prefix. In all cases \hat E_q has dimension 2q+1, and its lower \hat E_(q−1) has dimension 2q−1. Beyond q, the upper dimension cap is q+j+1 at grades j for which t=p−1−j≥1. The annihilator L_t on the retained coordinates has rank determined as follows.

1. Even p=2q: use all stages. L_(q−1)=E_q^⊥ has rank q−2, so ℓ1=0, L_t ranks t−1 for 1≤t≤q−1. Since b_s≠0, ℓ1=0 gives c_s=1, and all L_t have zero last coordinate. Therefore e_s∈E_q. Testing e_s times F_(q−1) proves F_(q−1) vanishes last; C-growth then shows F_q vanishes last. All extensions of the chain and W0 are consequently last-zero as well.
2. Odd p=2q+1 and ℓ1≠0: use all stages. L_q=E_q^⊥ has rank q; ranks L_t=t for 1≤t≤q. No last-coordinate assumption.
3. Odd p=2q+1 and ℓ1=0: use the prefix first s−1 stages; c_s=1 and L_t vanish last. We have e_s∉E_q: otherwise e_s=f(c)+d with d∈F_q, f=xg by first coordinate, and its moments through deg h≤q give ∫ f h=b_s h(1). Test h=(1−x)g, then h=1, contradiction. Therefore restriction injects on E_q, and L_q has rank q−1 by lower growth and annihilation of E_q+span(e_s). Ranks L_t=t−1 for 1≤t≤q. For any expression H(c) equal after restriction to a grade-a defect, in full coordinates their difference is a scalar times e_s. Its moment relation is ∫ H h=β h(1) for all deg h≤p−1−a, with unknown β but known endpoint 1.

In each setup, if \hat E_j achieves its upper cap and q≤j≤p−4, it is not C-invariant. If it were, its orthogonal complement (the restricted L_t) would be invariant, hence so would full L_t (in prefix case rows were zero at last). Strong backward amplification at grade t≥3 violates the known one-step rank increase, since floor((t+1)/2)≥2. At j=q use the next-rank upper cap from \hat E_(q−1) dimension 2q−1: L_(t+1) can grow by at most two relative to L_t, while t=q or q−1 is at least 7 and Hermite forces at least four. Thus C-noninvariance also holds at start j=q.

Write Z1=F_q+C F_q=W0+wP_(r+q−5)(c). It has dimension q+1 using the noninvariance and chain. In cases 1 and 2, P_q(c)∩Z1=0: a possible intersection f has f=x(1−x)g or f=xg respectively by endpoint evaluations. Test against g under defect moment conditions, of degree at most q−2 or q−1 respectively, spending a total grade at most p−1. Positivity of x(1−x) or x forces f=0. In case 3 the same nonintersection holds before restriction using the case 2 argument. After restriction, the only possible intersection is a line represented by a scalar polynomial of degree q proportional to xD_(q−1), where D_i is the degree-i orthogonal polynomial for x(1−x)dx (a multiple of the derivative of shifted Legendre of degree i+1). In fact f=xg; the relation ∫ f h=β h(1) for deg h≤q−1 implies g orthogonal to P_(q−2) under weight x(1−x) by taking h=(1−x)k. The restriction also preserves the whole dimension of Z1: an intersection of Z1 with span(e_s) would give b e_s=0 since Z1⊂F_(q+1), impossible.

In the **no-alias case** (including 1,2, and case 3 when there is no projected intersection), \hat E_(q+1)=\hat P_q⊕\hat Z1 by rank. Set d=q. In the **alias case** (case 3 only), let G=xD_(q−1) normalized monic. Then G(c) lies after restriction in Z1 but not in F_q, by prefix separation at grade q and deg G=q. In particular its coefficient on the newest chain vector in Z1 is nonzero. Thus \hat E_q=\hat P_(q−1)⊕\hat Z1: it contains Z1 (the lone extra vector is provided by G(c)∈E_q), and rank gives directness. Set d=q−1. In either case C P_d restricts into the starting E-space (contains c^(d+1)). From known noninvariance and the upper caps, each successive E-space through E_(p−3) is exactly the direct sum of \hat P_d, \hat W0, and the chain extended by one power. In particular
    \hat E_10=\hat P_d ⊕ \hat W0 ⊕ \hat w P_D(c),
where D=r+4 in no-alias and D=r+5 in alias. The same holds at E11 with D+1. (For q=9, no-alias starts at E10.) The monic grade-(d+1) polynomial G belongs after restriction to W0 plus chain: in no-alias, express c^(q+1) using E_(q+1), so G(c)∈Z1 after restriction; in alias use the preceding G. C-multiplication followed by polynomial division modulo G is therefore valid for comparisons modulo W0 and chain through E11.

Projected Euclidean orthogonality is not assumed. A retained node is an effective chain node if the component of the restricted w on its c-block does not belong to the corresponding node block of restricted W0. Because W0 splits by C, the dimension of the first k Krylov powers modulo W0 is min(k, number of effective nodes), by Vandermonde. Propagated directness through E_(p−3) therefore gives at least r+p−8 effective nodes in no-alias and r+p−7 in alias. In particular this number exceeds D+1 for all p=16,…,19.

## Polynomial contamination in grade 10 and its removal

For u∈F10 let H be its polynomial component in the E10 decomposition. In the alias case the entire extended chain at grade 10 has a full-tableau lift in F11, rather than F10, so use moment tests with one less degree in that case. In case 1 assume u vanishes last, as it does for every product of two F5 vectors under consideration. The remaining chain summand also vanishes last in case 1. In all cases H(0)=0. In full even case H(1)=0; write H=x(1−x)h0. In odd case 2 write H=x h0. These h0 are orthogonal to polynomials of degree ≤p−11 by testing the defect moment relation. In prefix case 3 write H=x h0, whose moments imply h0 orthogonal to polynomials of degree ≤p−12 under x(1−x)dx (test (1−x)k). In the alias case use the same prefix test but only degree ≤p−13 for h0, since that chain lift is in F11. The same degree logic applied to G (whose lift is specifically in F_(q+1) also in the alias case) gives these explicit lists (orthogonal polynomials below follow their indicated weight; all unspecified leading constants are nonzero):

- Even q=8: H/[x(1−x)] lies in the line of degree-6 orthogonal polynomials for x(1−x)dx, and G/[x(1−x)] is degree-7 orthogonal for the same weight. Even q=9: H=0 already.
- Odd q=8, ℓ1≠0: H/x lies in the degree-7 orthogonal line for x dx, and G/x is degree-8 orthogonal for that weight. Odd q=9, ℓ1≠0: H=0 already.
- Odd q=8, prefix/no alias: H/x is in span(D6,D7), and G/x is a nonzero multiple of D8+θD7. Odd q=9, prefix/no alias: H/x lies on line D8, and G/x is a nonzero multiple of D9+θD8.
- Odd q=8, prefix/alias: H/x lies in span(D5,D6), and G/x is a multiple of D7. Odd q=9, prefix/alias: H/x lies on line D7, and G/x is a multiple of D8.

For any u, Cu both in F10 (last-zero if even), their polynomial components satisfy H_1≡xH_0 modulo G. Indeed, shifting the E10 chain decomposition and subtracting uses only the E11 extension; after division by G the polynomial remainder of degree ≤d lies in the chain-plus-W0 part of E11 and hence is zero. Cancel the displayed common factor (x or x(1−x)) in each line case. By the three-term recurrence of positive orthogonal polynomials, multiplication of the indicated basis vector has nonzero coefficient on its previous degree; subtracting any leading multiple of the indicated G quotient cannot affect that coefficient. Thus in all line cases the two components must both vanish. In odd q=8 prefix/no alias use three consecutive products u, Cu, C²u in F10. The congruences imply that both H_0/x and H_1/x are multiples of D7, since xD6 has nonzero D5 term and reducing xD7 by D8+θD7 does not create any D5. But xD7 reduced modulo that polynomial has nonzero D6 coefficient; hence H_0=H_1=H_2=0. In odd q=8 prefix/alias the same argument is shifted down by one index: xD5 leaks to D4 and, modulo D7, xD6 leaks to D5, so three consecutive components vanish. These arguments apply to all needed overlapping triples. The three-term recurrence itself follows from symmetry of multiplication and degree orthogonality; its neighboring coefficients are nonzero by comparing positive leading coefficients.

## Head and final two causal contrasts

Take a node-local element f∈W0 and any g∈F5. Products fg,Cfg,C²fg are all in F10 (use C-invariance of W0), and last-zero in case 1. Thus the projected product is in W0 + chain degree≤D and supported on one c-block. Its chain part vanishes at all effective nodes except possibly that one, so is zero by the lower bound on the number of effective nodes. Consequently on the retained coordinates W0 is a C-invariant coordinatewise algebra, with disjoint indicator-mask basis, each class a single c-node. Proof of mask basis: interpolate a generic algebra element with distinct nonzero values on its nonzero classes by polynomials vanishing at zero, producing all support-class indicators. Call its union T. The same local product shows e_T times w belongs to restricted W0. Choose a lift in W0 and subtract it from w. This changes neither chain span nor any membership C^a w∈F5 for a<r, and makes retained head w disjoint from T, with nonzero support S using precisely the effective nodes. Set R=retained complement of S∪T.

For each 0≤a≤2r−2, C^a w² is a product of two members C^i w,C^j w of F5. There are at least three consecutive products since r≥2. Use the shift-one or shift-two argument on adjacent overlapping groups to get all their polynomial components zero. By disjoint supports they lie in w P_D on retained coordinates (any W0 part is zero). Divide by w on S. Successive polynomial uniqueness at the effective nodes (N>D+1) proves w=P(c) on S with deg P≤D−(2r−2), by comparing each multiplication by x to the next degree≤D representative. In no-alias this is deg P≤6−r≤4. In alias the bound is deg P≤7−r. The alias G relation on S is G=P Q at the N effective nodes, with deg Q exactly r+q−5 from its nonzero top coefficient in Z1. Their total degree is ≤q+2<N, so exact factorization gives deg P=q−(r+q−5)=5−r≤3. Thus in every case P is nonzero of degree≤4.

For masks M=x and later M=x(x−u), use the retained contrast v=(MP)(c)−(MP)(c)e_T−M(C)w. It vanishes on S and T. It is nonzero by polynomial separation in retained E_q (even its grade≤7), because its polynomial part MP is nonzero while the other terms are defects at grade≤5+deg M (using C-invariance of W0 for the mask term). Its first nonzero stage k lies in R with endpoint ξ=c_k≠0, M(ξ)P(ξ)≠0. For every h with deg(hM)≤3, the polynomial term hMP has degree≤7; the mask term hMP(C)e_T still lies in the restricted W0 (grade 5) by invariance, using its W0 lift. And (hM)(C)w has grade at most 7: the constant term uses w∈F5, while each positive C^a w through a=3 uses the starting vector Cw∈F5 and at most two more C multiplications. Hence A h(C)v uses grade≤8≤q, and is zero at the first nonzero row k by chronology. Its difference from the primitive ∫_0^x h MP belongs to a grade≤q defect, whose retained values vanish in R. Therefore ∫_0^ξ h MP=0. Use first M=x and tests h=1,x,x² at a nonzero endpoint u; then M=x(x−u) and tests h=1,x at a second endpoint u′ not 0 or u.

Rescale x by u; this is valid for negative u as well, using oriented integrals. First tests give P in the degree-3,4 Jacobi span J_k(x)=Jacobi(k,0,1,2x−1), orthogonal for weight x. Put a=u′/u; the second tests' entries M_ik=∫_0^a x^(i+1)(x−1)J_k(x)dx, i=0,1, k=3,4, are:
    a²(a−1)²(5a−2)(7a−6)/6,
    a²(a−1)²(108a³−190a²+100a−15)/6;
    a³(a−1)²(30a²−35a+8)/6,
    a³(a−1)²(7a−4)(27a²−30a+5)/12.
By multiplying out, determinant is a^6(a−1)^6(15a²−24a+10)/8, nonzero since a≠0,1 and the quadratic has negative discriminant. Entries are direct polynomial integration; J3,J4 can alternatively be defined by the Rodrigues formula J_k=(1/(k!x)) (d/dx)^k[x^(k+1)(x−1)^k]. Nonzero P is impossible. This concludes all four finite orders.
