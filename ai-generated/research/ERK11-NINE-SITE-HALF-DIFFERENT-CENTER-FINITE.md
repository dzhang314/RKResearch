# Nine live sites: finite clock atlas for half–half different-extra central dormancy

**Scope.** Continue the [half–half different-extra central chart](ERK11-NINE-SITE-CENTRAL-TWO-CLOCK-RANK-ONE-EXIT.md).
The active clocks and the genuine dormant clock \(\zeta=c_9\)
are distinct from one another and from \(0,1\). This reduces
the \(\Delta_0\ne0\) chart to a **finite clock set** over
characteristic zero, without relaxing the signed weight
premises. The rank-one \(\Delta_0=0\) chart was excluded
separately. This does not exclude the finite clock set, prove
existence of any actual tableau, or alter any numerical bound.

Use \(a,b=2-a,h=a/2,d=a-1\), \(t\) the common free extra,
and \(x=c_8,y=c_{10}\) the exclusive extras in one of the
two physical orientations \((u,v)=(x,y),(y,x)\).
We use the preceding definitions \(V_a,V_b,P,Q,W,H,C_1,C_2,J_0,I_j\)
and \(A_0,B_0,C_0,D_0,\Delta_0\) (in particular \(C_1\)
uses the physical stage-10 clock \(y\)). Define
\[
 E=A_0x+C_0,\quad N_y=-(B_0x+D_0),\qquad y=N_y/E.
\]
Indeed a zero \(E\) would force \(B_0x+D_0=0\) and
\(\Delta_0=0\). Put
\[
 \iota=E(I_2-xI_1)+N_y(xI_0-I_1)=E I_{xy},\quad
 \alpha=E C_1-N_y H,\quad \beta=C_2-xH .
\]
The paid central product is \(\alpha\beta=J_0\iota\).
The actual central product support \(I_{xy}=b_9
(\zeta-x)(\zeta-y)V_a(\zeta)V_b(\zeta)\) shows that
\(I_{xy}\) and \(\iota\) are live: \(b_9\ne0\) and
no clock factor here can vanish. This needs no sign.
In this half–half regular branch also \(J_0\ne0\)
by the earlier determinant factorization, though the
scaled equations below do not divide by \(J_0\),
\(\alpha\), or \(\beta\).

## 1. The two-live-site moment pivot is mandatory

Set
\[
 \Pi_5(X)=(X-a)(X-h)(X-d)(X-x)(EX-N_y),\qquad
 m_j=\int_0^1X(1-X)\Pi_5(X)X^j\,dX\quad(0\le j\le3).
\]
These are actual ordinary scalar tests of degree
\(7+j\le10\). Equal-clock carrier weights cancel, and
the nine ordinary clock sites have five tapped roots
of \(\Pi_5\), two endpoints killed by \(X(1-X)\),
and the two remaining sites \(\zeta,t\). Therefore
\[
 m_j=w_\zeta\zeta^j+w_t t^j,\quad
 w_\zeta=b_9\zeta(1-\zeta)\Pi_5(\zeta),\quad
 w_t=B_t t(1-t)\Pi_5(t),
\]
with both coefficients nonzero.

Here \(B_t\) is the **pooled** ordinary scalar
weight of the two active stages at \(t\), not an
individual physical weight. The [six-pair site proof](ERK11-SIX-PAIR-SITE-MASK.md)
gives \(\nu_t=0\) and \(\mu_t=\kappa/H_6'(t)\ne0\).
Indeed if \(\kappa=0\), every live active pair
with vanishing first and second response pools has
equal nonzero responses and cancelling weights.
Then only the three dormant sites could support
ordinary scalar quadrature, contradicting the
nonzero \(4\times4\) rational Hilbert moment
determinant (degree at most six). Since
\(B_t f_1 f_2=-\mu_t\) for the two active
responses \(f_1,f_2\) at \(t\), \(B_t\ne0\).
Thus the live pivot is not implied merely by
the two individual stage weights being nonzero.
Without this pooled premise, an invisible \(t\)
would yield only the raw shifted equations, not
the pivot or atlas claimed here.

Use a variable \(Z\) for a hypothesized dormant clock
and set \(q_j=m_{j+1}-Z m_j\), \(j=0,1,2\). At the actual
\(\zeta\) the two shifted nodal rows are \(q_1=tq_0\),
\(q_2=tq_1\), because the full scalar hull is paid
against \(X^j\) only for \(j=0,1\). In fact the
two-site moments already give
\[
 \begin{gathered}
 q_0(\zeta)=(t-\zeta)w_t\ne0,\qquad
 m_0m_2-m_1^2=w_\zeta w_t(\zeta-t)^2\ne0,\\
 D(Z):=q_0q_2-q_1^2=(m_0m_2-m_1^2)(Z-\zeta)(Z-t).
 \end{gathered}
\]
Thus \(D\) is genuinely quadratic at an actual solution,
its derivative at \(\zeta\) is the live
\((m_0m_2-m_1^2)(\zeta-t)\), and
\(t=q_1(\zeta)/q_0(\zeta)\). No unknown Hankel
degree, repeated nodal root, or singular shifted
row is silently dropped. This two-live-site argument
also proves that for any genuine nine-site four-known
active roots, the shifted \(2\times2\) minor is live
when its two remaining active pools are live:
apply the same calculation after multiplying the
known quartet by \(X-\zeta\).

## 2. The actual dormant sextic needs no shared-free clock

Let \(\ell_Z,r_Z\) be evaluations on the left and
right anchored grade-five model coordinates. Use
the inverse mixed Gram pairing with matrix \(210N\)
in bases \(X^{i+1}\), \((1-X)X^i\), \(0\le i\le4\).
The actual dormant responses for the two primitives
are determined (no stage of the other direction is
identified with an ordinary reverse order-eleven method):
\[
 P_9=\frac{\alpha(\zeta-x)V_a(\zeta)}{\iota},\qquad
 Q_9=\frac{\beta(E\zeta-N_y)V_b(\zeta)}{\iota}.
\]
These follow from the paid row-9 product equations
by dividing only by \(I_{xy}\) and the already live
scalar factors. On the dormant row, the only anchored
model corrections are the evaluations at \(a,d\);
\(P(a)=a^5/30,\ Q(d)=b^5/30\). Therefore the
two scaled anchoring rows are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 L^\#(Z)&=a^5\iota\,\ell_Z+
 30\{\alpha(Z-x)V_a(Z)-\iota P(Z)\}\ell_a,\\
 R^\#(Z)&=b^5\iota\,r_Z+
 30\{\beta(E Z-N_y)V_b(Z)-\iota Q(Z)\}r_d.
 \end{aligned}
\]
At \(Z=\zeta\) they are respectively \(a^5\iota\)
and \(b^5\iota\) times the literal dormant anchored
rows. The first polynomial functional vanishes at
\(Z=0,a\), the second at \(Z=1,d\).
Thus
\[
 L_3=L^\#/[Z(Z-a)],\qquad R_3=R^\#/[(1-Z)(Z-d)]
\]
are cubic functionals, with the divided factors all
live at the actual row. Their actual reciprocal
weight diagonal is exactly the following sextic:
\[
 S(Z)=210\langle L_3,R_3\rangle_N-
 a^5b^5\iota(Z-x)(EZ-N_y)Z(Z-a)(1-Z)(Z-d)=0 .
\]
Indeed \(\langle L_9,R_9\rangle=1/b_9\), and
the central paid formula gives
\(1/b_9=(\zeta-x)(E\zeta-N_y)W(\zeta)/\iota\).
No division by an off-diagonal coefficient or a
formal evaluation of an unknown stage is used.

## 3. First contact and degree bounds

For either physical orientation, write
\((u_N,u_D)=(x,1)\) or \((N_y,E)\), with the opposite
choice for \((v_N,v_D)\). After substituting the
actual \(t=q_1/q_0\) the cleared derivative characters
are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \widehat L &= (a q_0-q_1)(a u_D-u_N)D_L^{(0)}
                      -q_1 u_N D_L^{(a)},\\
 \widehat R &= (q_1-d q_0)(v_N-d v_D)D_R^{(0)}
               -(q_0-q_1)(v_D-v_N)D_R^{(b)} .
 \end{aligned}
\]
They scale the actual calibrated derivative rows
by live factors. Their \(N\)-pairing \(\Psi\) is a
quadratic in \(Z\) and vanishes at the actual
candidate by the physical row-7/row-11 off-diagonal.
The other two necessary current off-diagonals are
\(\langle \widehat L,R_3\rangle_N=0\) and
\(\langle L_3,\widehat R\rangle_N=0\). Both have
\(Z\)-degree at most four; they are not needed for
the finite atlas, but provide further tests.

For \(D,S,\Psi\), the respective upper degrees in
\((Z,x,a)\) are \((2,4,22)\), \((6,4,35)\),
and \((2,6,38)\). For the extra off-diagonals

and are checked for the rational \(a\)-degree

In particular all subsequent resultants use
the true or declared upper degrees, not a
degree assumption on a particular tableau.

## 4. Two transverse projections, with no vertical width fiber

Declare
\[
 R_S(a,x)=\operatorname{Res}_{Z}^{(2,6)}(D,S),\qquad
 R_\Psi(a,x)=\operatorname{Res}_{Z}^{(2,2)}(D,\Psi).
\]
Superscripts give the Sylvester degree declarations.
Their upper \(x\)-degrees are \(32,20\), and their
upper \(a\)-degrees \(202,120\). Every actual common
finite root \(\zeta\) makes the corresponding
Sylvester determinants vanish, even when some
other projected coefficient specializes to zero.

For each orientation consider the explicit width
polynomial
\[
 \mathcal W(a)=
 \operatorname{Res}_x^{(32,20)}(R_S,R_\Psi).
\]
It is **nonzero**. The replay builds all the
rational formulas at the arithmetic coefficient
specialization \(a=3\bmod 101\): the degrees in
\(Z\) remain \(2,6,2\); the degrees in \(x\) attain
\(32,20\), with respective leading coefficients
\(25\) and \(13\) or \(44\). The fixed-degree
width determinant is \(33\) or \(26\) modulo 101.
The rational coefficients are integral at 101
(all integral denominators come from small
monomial integrals and exact monic-factor
division). All degree declarations are respected,
so these nonzero residues prove nonzero rational
polynomials, not nonexistence of a tableau
modulo 101. Any actual width is a root of one
of these two polynomials, each of degree at most
\(20\cdot202+32\cdot120=7880\).

There is also no vertical fiber where \(R_S(a,x)\)
is identically zero in \(x\) at an actual width.
Specialize the **parameter** \(x\) to constants
\(3,4,5\) over \(\mathbb Q\). At all three choices
the \(Z\)-degrees are still exactly \(2,6\)
over the entire \(\mathbb Q[a]\) coefficient ring,
so the three exact univariate resultants really are
the evaluations \(R_S(a,3),R_S(a,4),R_S(a,5)\).
Each has degree \(202\) in \(a\), and their
exact common monic gcd is \(a^2(a-2)^6\).
Any width with \(R_S(a,x)\equiv0\) would annihilate
all three samples and hence that gcd. Both sample
gcd roots violate the inherited live width guards
\(a\ne0\), \(b=2-a\ne0\). No other specialization
or removable \(x\)-guard factor is assumed. Thus
for each actual width, \(R_S(a,x)\) is a nonzero
polynomial of degree at most \(32\), leaving
finitely many \(x\). For each of these the actual
quadratic \(D\) has at most two dormant candidates,
and \(E,q_0\) fix \(y,t\) uniquely as above.
This is a finite atlas of genuine clock tuples,
not merely widths, allowing arbitrary signed
weights over characteristic-zero fields.
