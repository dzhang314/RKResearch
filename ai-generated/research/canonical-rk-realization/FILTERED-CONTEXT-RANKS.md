# Dependency depths from the complete rooted-tree series

Fix a field and a common diagonal \(\gamma\). The
[edge-contraction shift](COMMON-DIAGONAL-HEIGHT.md) determines the complete
explicit series of \(B=A-\gamma I\) from the complete series of \(A\).
All weights below belong to this shifted series. Let
\[
f(t)\coloneqq\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(B).
\]
The tree height is the maximum number of edges from its root to a leaf;
in particular, \(\bullet\) has height zero. The depth of a context's hole
is the number of unary extensions used to build it. A root join does not
change that depth. See [context-matrix rank](CONTEXT-MATRIX-RANK.md) for
the insertion notation \(C[t]\).

Use the [canonical reduced tableau](PROOF.md) of \(f\). An edge goes
from donor stage \(j\) to receiving stage \(i\) if \(B_{ij}\ne0\).
For nonnegative integers \(h,k\), define
\[
\begin{aligned}
U_h&\coloneqq\{i:\text{a path of at least }h\text{ edges ends at }i\},\\
V_k&\coloneqq\{i:\text{a path of at least }k\text{ edges starts at }i
                   \text{ and ends at a stage of nonzero output weight}\},\\
H^{h,k}&\coloneqq\bigl(f(C[t])\bigr)_{\text{hole depth}(C)\ge k,\ \text{height}(t)\ge h}.
\end{aligned}
\]
A length-zero path is allowed. As with the unfiltered context matrix,
the rank is the supremum of the ranks of its finite submatrices. Then
\[
\operatorname{rank}H^{h,k}=|U_h\cap V_k|.
\]

To see this, first consider the span of the columns
\(\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(B)\) with height at least \(h\). The span for
\(h=0\) is the full stage space by canonical reduction. For each \(h\),
the span is closed under coordinatewise multiplication by any stage
vector: the stage vector is a linear combination of tree columns, and
root join preserves the height lower bound. The span for \(h+1\) is
generated by the products of arbitrary stage vectors with
\(B\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(B)\) for inputs of height at least \(h\). In one
direction these products expand into root joins with \([t]\). In the
other direction every tree of height at least \(h+1\) has a child of
height at least \(h\), and its column is the product of the column for
that unary extension and the columns for the remaining branches.
Multiplication by individual coordinate indicators isolates each
nonzero entry of \(B\). Induction therefore identifies the column span
with the vectors supported on \(U_h\).

For the rows, each unary extension applies \(B\) to the input, and each
root join multiplies the input coordinatewise by a fixed tree column.
Consequently a row with hole depth at least \(k\) vanishes outside
\(V_k\). For the converse, fix a path of length at least \(k\) from a
stage \(i\) to a stage with nonzero output weight. Between the matrix
actions, as well as before the first and after the last, multiplication
by the path's coordinate indicators restricts the readout to that one
path. The readout is a nonzero multiple of the \(i\)-th coordinate
functional: its coefficient is the product of the path entries and the
final output weight. Each indicator is a finite linear combination of
tree columns, so expanding those multiplications expresses the readout
as a linear combination of literal context rows with the same hole
depth. Over a field the coefficient is invertible. Thus the row span is
the coordinate dual supported on \(V_k\).

The matrix pairs the two spans. The rank of their pairing is the number
of shared coordinates. Finite bases selected from the literal rows and
columns attain that rank.

## Minimum depth of a realization

Define effective depth as the maximum length of a strict dependency
path ending at a stage with nonzero output weight. In any tableau,
a tree whose height exceeds that depth has zero shifted coefficient:
every nonzero contribution from its longest branch would give such a
path. In the canonical tableau of a nonzero series, take a path of
maximum effective depth \(D\). Its final stage belongs to \(U_D\) and
has nonzero output weight. Since the columns at height at least \(D\)
span the vectors supported on \(U_D\), at least one such literal column
has nonzero output. No taller tree can have nonzero output. Hence the
largest height of a nonzero shifted coefficient is the least effective
depth of any realization with the prescribed diagonal. The canonical
tableau attains it. Taking successive differences of the filtered ranks
also gives the number of canonical stages with each pair of maximum
incoming and outgoing path lengths.

Tall trees alone need not detect this depth. Over the rationals, take
five stages with \(B_{21}=1,\ B_{31}=2,\ B_{42}=1,\ B_{53}=1/2\), all
other entries zero, and \(\mathbf b=(0,0,0,1,-1)\). The height-two tall
tree has weight \(1-(1/2)\cdot2=0\), whereas
\(\bigl[[\bullet]^{\#2}\bigr]\) has weight \(1-(1/2)\cdot2^2=-1\).
The filtered ranks, in increasing \(h\) and \(k\) from zero to two, are
\[
\begin{pmatrix}5&3&1\\4&2&0\\2&0&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The clocks distinguish every pair except stages two and four, which the
height-two tall column distinguishes. Every stage reaches the output,
so this example is already reduced.

These are complete-series statements. A minor involving orders above
\(p\) is not determined by the order conditions through \(p\), and the
argument does not claim a new stage bound from those conditions alone.
