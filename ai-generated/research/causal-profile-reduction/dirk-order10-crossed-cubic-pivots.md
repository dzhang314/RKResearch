# An eleven-stage order-ten DIRK needs four zero diagonals

Let $A$ be an eleven-stage lower triangular Runge–Kutta tableau of
ordinary order at least ten. Work over any field in which the order
conditions hold. No individual nonzero weights, signs, distinct clocks,
or nonzero diagonal entries are assumed.

There are two distinct interior zero-diagonal stages $i<j$, in
addition to the physical first and last zero diagonals. At least four
distinct individually output-active clocks follow $i$; at least
four distinct physical clocks precede $j$. In 1-based numbering,
$3\le i\le7$ and $5\le j\le9$. If this pair is consecutive, its
direct tap $a_{ji}$ is nonzero and $c_i=c_j$.
This excludes the entire exactly-three-zero-diagonal face, not every
eleven-stage candidate. Over a nontrivial commutative ring the corresponding
necessary condition is four **nonunit** diagonal entries: pass to any
residue field; units survive, while at least four diagonal images vanish.
This does not assert four entries already equal zero in the ring.

## Paid ranks and endpoint rows

Write $c=A\mathbf1$, $C=\operatorname{diag}(c)$, $\beta=b^{\mathsf T}$,
$Y=I-C$, $R=CA-AC-A^2$, and $E_d,F_d$ for the literal stage and
pure defect flags of grade $d$ (tree size minus one).
Use the existing paid bounds and coordinate lemmas in
[DIRKOrderSix](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/DIRKOrderSix.lean),
[DIRKUniform](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/DIRKUniform.lean),
[DIRKTerminalRank](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/DIRKTerminalRank.lean),
[DIRKReverseGrowth](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/DIRKReverseGrowth.lean),
and [NonsingularDIRKStageFloor](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/NonsingularDIRKStageFloor.lean)
(the latter for $RE_d\subset F_{d+2}$, not its nonsingularity hypothesis).

* If C(2) fails, $\dim F_3\ge2$, $\dim F_4\ge3$, and coefficient
  separation gives $\dim E_4\ge8$. The unanchored reverse room
  at $(q,n)=(4,4)$ then gives at least twelve stages. Therefore C(2)
  holds and the first row is zero.
* With the physical origin, the same count at $(4,4)$ gives
  $\dim E_4\le7$; strict forward growth and coefficient separation
  give $\dim E_4=7$. At grade five, origin-anchored separation of all
  six clock powers gives $\dim E_5\ge6+\dim F_5\ge9$.
* The ordinary reverse rank budget at $(q,n)=(5,3)$ gives
  $\dim E_5+2+\dim(\mathrm{primitiveBack}_1)\le11$.
  Thus $\dim E_5=9$, $\dim F_5=3$, and both D(1) and D(2)
  vanish literally, including at zero-weight coordinates:
  $\beta A=\beta Y$ and $\beta Y A=\beta Y^2/2$.
  The same count at these cutoffs already proves the eleven-stage
  floor, so a terminal inactive row is impossible: dropping a trailing
  zero weight preserves every tree read by lower triangularity.
  At the last stage $t$, the two reverse seeds now force $c_t=1$,
  $a_{tt}=0$, and $b_t\ne0$. Lower triangularity gives $Ae_t=0$,
  hence $Re_t=0$. Here $e_t$ denotes the unit coordinate vector.

## The two genuine cubic kernels

On the forward side, $E_3$ is the five-dimensional direct sum of
the ordinary clock cubics and the line generated by
$g=Ac^2-c^3/3$. Indeed expanding all ordinary trees of grade at most
three under C(2) gives precisely these directions, and paid moments
separate the clock coefficients from the pure line. At the first
nonzero clock $u$, all earlier entries of $c,g$ are zero,
C(2) forces $a_{uu}=c_u/2$, and $g_u=c_u^3/6\ne0$.
Thus $g\notin\ker R$: the leading Volterra diagonal is $-a_{uu}^2$.
Since $RE_3\subset F_5$ and $\dim F_5=3$, the kernel in $E_3$
has dimension at least two. One basis direction is $\mathbf1$ by
C(2). Subtract an appropriate constant from another direction to get
a nonzero $v$ with zero initial coordinate and nonzero degree-at-most
three scalar profile $P(x)$ satisfying $P(0)=0$.
Its first nonzero coordinate $i$ has $a_{ii}=0$ and must be strictly
after $u$.

For the reverse side use **rows**, not an ordinary reflected tableau.
Put
$$
   G=\beta Y^2 A-\tfrac13\beta Y^3,\qquad
   W=\operatorname{span}\{\beta,\beta Y,\beta Y^2,\beta Y^3,G\}.
$$
Each element is a true linear combination of one-mark primitive and
clock words. The scalar density of $G$ is zero, by formal
integration by parts. Its latest nonzero coordinate is the
latest **active nonunit clock** $w$. Such a clock exists by
quadrature. D(2) gives $a_{ww}=(1-c_w)/2$, so
$G_w=b_w(1-c_w)^3/6\ne0$; every later coordinate of $G$
vanishes by triangularity. The four clock rows are independent and
separated from $G$ by the paid degree-six Hilbert tests. Hence
$\dim W=5$ and $GR\ne0$, by the reverse diagonal pivot at $w$.

Multiplying any row of $W$ by $R$ gives an annihilator of $E_4$:
the scalar commutator density is zero, and every read costs at most
$3+2+4=9$ grades. Explicitly, $R=AY-YA-A^2$; on rows, right
multiplication by $Y$ and $A$ induces multiplication by the
reflected clock and primitive integration from the reflected
origin. The Volterra identity on these two scalar operations gives
zero. These are affordable *single-mark* reads; products of two
marked rows are never used. Also $(\lambda R)_t=0$ for every
row $\lambda$, since $Re_t=0$. The one-coordinate subspace
$\operatorname{Ann}(E_4)\cap\{\lambda:\lambda_t=0\}$
has dimension three: $\dim E_4=7$ and the anchored terminal
coordinate lemma gives $e_t\notin E_4$.
Consequently $\ker(\lambda\mapsto\lambda R)$ inside $W$
has dimension at least two. Its constant direction is $\beta$
by D(1),D(2). Subtract a multiple of $\beta$ from another direction
to get $\lambda\ne0$ with $\lambda_t=0$. Since $GR\ne0$,
its scalar density $Q(x)$ is nonzero. It has degree at most three
and $Q(1)=0$, by the actual terminal evaluation of the four
clock rows and $G_t=0$. The latest nonzero coordinate $j$
of $\lambda$ has zero diagonal, and is strictly before $w$.

## Why they cannot cross

If $i\ge j$, every mixed read
$\lambda C^k A C^\ell v$ vanishes: on the support of $\lambda$,
the inner column is zero before row $i$, and it is still zero at
row $i$ because that diagonal vanishes. The same reads for
$k+\ell\le2$ are paid literal order tests of total grade at most
$3+1+2+3=9$. Thus
$$
 I\!\left(Q(x)x^k J(x^\ell P)(x)\right)=0,\qquad k+\ell\le2,
$$
where $I(x^n)=1/(n+1)$ is the formal unit-interval integral and
$J(x^n)=x^{n+1}/(n+1)$.
Write $P=a x+b x^2+d x^3$ and $M_r=I(Q(x)x^r)$.
For fixed $k+\ell=t$, the $\ell=0,1,2$ rows clear to
$(6,4,3),(20,15,12),(15,12,10)$ on the unknowns
$aM_{t+2},bM_{t+3},dM_{t+4}$. The complete $t=2$ matrix
has determinant $1$. The $t=1$ rows resolve the possible sparse
coefficient cases:

| Nonzero coefficients of $P$ | Three zero moments obtained |
|---|---|
| $a$ alone | $M_2,M_3,M_4$ |
| $b$ alone or $a,b$ or $a,d$ | $M_3,M_4,M_5$ |
| $d$ alone or $b,d$ or $a,b,d$ | $M_4,M_5,M_6$ |

For example, with $a,d\ne0$ and $b=0$, four times the
first $t=1$ row minus the second gives $4aM_3=0$; then the
first row gives $3dM_5=0$. $M_4=0$ was already separated at
$t=2$. Since $Q=(1-x)(h+\ell x+m x^2)$, three consecutive zero
moments starting at any $e=2,3,4$ annihilate the coefficient vector
in the shifted Beta matrix
$$
 \left(\frac1{(e+r+s+1)(e+r+s+2)}\right)_{0\le r,s\le2}.
$$
Its inverse is integral in each of these windows, and the largest
primitive denominator is ten. Hence $Q=0$, a contradiction.

The same scalar obstruction applies with $Q$ a degree-three
nodal mask for the individually active clocks after $i$.
If those clocks number at most three, such a mask can contain
$1-x$ because the actual last clock is $1$ (and if there are
no later clocks it can freely include this factor). The read
$\beta Q(C)C^k A C^\ell v$ then vanishes by the same causal
calculation. Reflecting only the scalar variables and interchanging
forward and backward integration proves the parallel prerequisite
of four distinct **physical** clock labels before $j$: apply the
same test to a cubic mask containing $x$ at the true origin,
using $Y$ as clock and the primitive row $\lambda A$. This
direction counts physical labels because a marked row can see a
stage whose original output weight is zero.

Finally, if $j=i+1$, the only possible contribution to
$\lambda C^k A C^\ell v$ is the tap
$a_{ji}\lambda_j v_i$ times the two clock powers. The tap cannot
vanish by the same scalar obstruction. The $\lambda A^2 v$ read
is zero by the two zero diagonals and the absence of an intermediate
stage. The identity $\lambda Rv=0$ therefore gives
$(c_j-c_i)a_{ji}\lambda_j v_i=0$, proving $c_i=c_j$.

## Cross-order scalar version

The key crossed moment obstruction is not cubic-specific. Over a field
with the denominators paid through order \(3d+1\), let \(P,Q
e0\) have
degrees at most \(d\), with \(P(0)=Q(1)=0\). Not all of
\[
 I\!\left(Q(x)x^kJ(x^\ell P)(x)\right),\qquad k+\ell\le d-1,
\]
can vanish. Let \(S\subseteq\{1,\dots,d\}\) be the set of nonzero
coefficient indices of \(P\), with \(r=|S|\). For each
\(t=r-1,\dots,d-1\), use the \(r\) tests with \(k+\ell=t\),
\(0\le\ell\le r-1\). The corresponding Cauchy matrix
\((1/(\ell+e+1))_{0\le\ell<r,\ e\in S}\)
is invertible, so each \(e\in S\) forces all moments
\(M_{r+e},\dots,M_{d+e}\) to vanish. Neighboring elements of \(S\) differ
by at most \(d-r+1\); the intervals therefore form a contiguous band of
length at least \(d\), beginning at \(s=r+\min S\le d+1\).
The size-\(d\) shifted Beta matrix against the coefficients of
\(Q/(1-x)\) has largest denominator at most \(s+2d\le3d+1\).
Its invertibility gives \(Q=0\), the contradiction. This would apply
at orders 13, 16, and 19 to separately earned pairs of quartic,
quintic and sextic kernel profiles, respectively. No higher-order rank
hypotheses are asserted or taken for granted here.
