# The terminal weight closes the regular one-dirty-clock non-Lobatto branch

For a genuine eleven-stage DIRK tableau of order ten over a characteristic-zero field, assume every physical row with nonzero cubic defect `g=Ac²−c³/3` has the same clock `ξ`, and some such row has nonzero weight. Let `h=(A−ξ/2)g`. The one-clock band gives terminal cleanliness through grade five and the exact identity

```
e_t = H(c) + δ g + L(c)h,                   (1)
H(X)=42XJ₄(X),  J₄(X)=X⁴−2X³+4X²/3−X/3+1/42,
c_t=1,  b_t=1/30,  g_t=h_t=0.
```

Here `t` is the last *physical* row; neither positive weights nor distinct physical clocks are assumed. The physical origin `o` has clock zero and `g_o=h_o=0`. Denote by `μ` the weighted aggregate over physical clocks and by `ν` the unique six-site Lobatto measure at `ℒ={0,1,J₄ roots}`, split over a field extension if needed. It is exact through degree nine: divide by the degree-six nodal polynomial `X(X−1)J₄`; its products with degrees at most three have zero unit integral by direct Jacobi moments, and the remainder has degree at most five. Its weight at 1 is the integral of the Lagrange cardinal `H`, i.e. `1/30`. Work with `Δ=μ−ν`.

**Result.** If `L` is a nonzero constant, or is affine with root `τ` and `H(τ)≠0`, then `μ=ν`. This excludes in particular the constant dirty pair with non-Lobatto aggregate and dirty physical rows 2 and 10 from the previous note, without a mixed-cut or any assumption on the zero diagonals. It also covers the pole collisions `τ=1` and `τ=ξ` when `H(τ)≠0`. This is a theorem about the ordinary quadrature of the active one-clock branch, not a full DIRK10/11 exclusion. Shared roots of `H,L`, all-ghost dirty supports, and the other research branches are outside the result.

## 1. A support debit that uses the physical terminal weight

Let `D` be any collection of `n` spent physical rows whose grouped signed ordinary contribution is zero at every clock, excluding the two distinct physical endpoints. Among the remaining rows, at most `11−n−2` are off `ℒ`. If fewer, then `Δ` is supported on at most `(11−n−3)+6=14−n` sites, counting all six fixed sites pessimistically. If exactly `11−n−2` are off `ℒ`, every remaining physical row except the two endpoints is off `ℒ`. The only remaining row at clock one is the terminal, hence `Δ(1)=b_t−ν(1)=0`; the same `14−n` bound follows. This argument uses an upper bound on *physical* off-Lobatto rows, so zero weights and repeated off-Lobatto clocks cannot invalidate it. The removed rows need not avoid clock one; only their aggregate must be zero. The same reasoning with any number of actual stages and any finite fixed support of size `m` gives support at most `stages+m−n−3`.

If `D` is all dirty rows, then `n≥2` since at least one is active and `βg=0`. For `n≤3` the dirty aggregate is zero without assuming constant `g`: choose the degree-at-most-three polynomial `1−∏_{i∈D}(1−X/g_i)`, with no constant term and value one at every nonzero defect. The three paid tests `βg=βg²=βg³=0` give zero dirty mass on the single clock; no distinction between ghost and active rows is needed. For `n≥4` without any zero aggregate assumption, at most `11−n−2≤5` clean off-Lobatto rows exist; the entire dirty cluster adds at most one further distinct clock. Thus always `|supp Δ|≤12` in the active branch. If dirty aggregate is known zero and `n≥3`, the terminal debit further gives `|supp Δ|≤11`.

For a field measure on at most `d+1` distinct sites, vanishing through degree `d` implies the measure is zero by the nodal witness at each site. The same applies after multiplying its masses by any factor that does not vanish on its actual nonzero support.

## 2. No pole, or a pole away from the dirty clock

The rooted-tree grades give `βh=βh²=β C h²=0`, and all `g`, `g²`, `g h` weighted totals vanish where used (the latter has grade seven). When `L` is constant nonzero, solve the identity for `h`, square it, and test with `β` and `β C`. Terms involving `g,g²` vanish since they are on the one clock, and the terminal terms give `−b_t` since `H(1)=1`. This yields `Δ(H²)=Δ(XH²)=0`. Their leading degrees are ten and eleven, so these two tests append to the ordinary degree-nine moment sequence. The support bound 12 now forces `Δ=0`.

Suppose `L(X)=ℓ(X−τ)`, `H(τ)≠0`, and `τ≠ξ`. If `τ≠1`, equation (1) prevents any row at τ (and there is no fixed Lobatto mass there). If `τ=1`, the same equation allows only the terminal at τ, so `Δ(1)=0`; explicitly omit it rather than evaluating a singular function there. Put `f=H/L` on the complement of τ. The identity and `βh=βh²=0` give `Δ(f)=Δ(f²)=0`. For `τ≠1` the terminal contribution is `b_t/L(1)` or its squared scalar counterpart; `ν(f)` and `ν(f²)` give exactly the same since `H` vanishes at the other fixed sites. For `τ=1` the terminal was omitted, so both reference tests are simply zero. In either case terms with `g,g²` vanish by the paid tests at their single numerical clock.

The polynomial parts of `f` and `f²` have degree at most four and eight. The coefficients of `(X−τ)⁻¹` in `f` and `(X−τ)⁻²` in `f²` are nonzero, since `H(τ)≠0`. Subtracting the ordinary polynomial moments gives the simple and double reciprocal tests for `Δ` (in that order). After scaling each residual mass by `(x−τ)⁻²`, its moments through degree eleven vanish. Equivalently apply the recurrence once per division: if `M_k` are the cleared moments after dividing by `X−τ`, then the original degree-`k` test is `M_(k+1)−τ M_k=0`; the reciprocal test starts with `M_0=0`. This recurrence is valid whenever the old mass at τ is zero, even when τ is in the enumerating set. Again size 12 is too small.

## 3. The pole coincides with the dirty clock

Now `τ=ξ` and `H(ξ)≠0`. At the first physical dirty row the terminal identity gives `δ≠0`. At any nonterminal row with clock ξ the same identity gives `g_i=−H(ξ)/δ`, a fixed nonzero scalar. So these rows are exactly `D`, with aggregate zero by `βg=0`, irrespective of their number. If `ξ≠1`, `ν(ξ)=0`, so `Δ(ξ)=0`; if `ξ=1`, the clean terminal alone supplies `b_t=ν(1)` after the dirty cancellation, giving the same equality.

Paid `βgh=0` shows `∑_{i∈D} b_i h_i=0`. Thus `βh` still gives the simple reciprocal read on the complement of the pole, by the calculation of §2 with the terminal either present or omitted. If `n≥3`, scaling once gives degree-ten tests and the 11-site endpoint debit suffices. If `n=2`, both rows are active and have opposite weights. The weighted `h` sum forces their `h` values to agree, so their `h²` sum also cancels. The double reciprocal read is recovered and the support at most 12 is too small for degree eleven. This argument does not require the stronger prior fact that an active dirty clock is not clock one.

## 4. Lean scope and open shared-root diagnostic

`DIRKOneClockEndpointDebit.lean` checks the general finite endpoint debit, the field Vandermonde witness, scaled annihilation, and the exact pole recurrences. `DIRKOrderTenOneClockEndpoint.lean` derives the actual terminal coordinate from native reverse saturation, checks its normalized mass from five exact Cauchy rows conditional on grade-five terminal cleanliness, and gives an actual `HasOrder`-based signed pair closure once the scaled degree-eleven tests are supplied. Its direct simple-pole (at least three dark rows) and double-pole (cancelling pair) corollaries use only the corresponding reciprocal reads and the ordinary degree-nine moments. The companion audit checks public signatures and axioms on Lean/Mathlib 4.36. The extraction of the reciprocal tests from the one-clock band, and the case ledger in §§2–3, are mathematical in this note; a native Lean theorem is not claimed for the entire case ledger.

When `H(τ)=0`, the rational argument does not apply. For example `f=H/L` is then a polynomial of degree four, with `f(τ)≠0` because `H` has simple roots. The correction `r=h+f(c)−e_t/L(1)+δg/L(ξ)` is supported on the pole fiber (if `ξ=τ` then `δ=0`, and omit that last term). Its first two signed moments are prescribed: `βr=ν(τ)f(τ)`, `βr²=ν(τ)f(τ)²`. These follow by expanding the two paid `h` tests and noting that `f` vanishes at all other fixed nodes except the terminal. Thus a single physical row at the common root has exact fixed Lobatto mass and `h=0`, but no reciprocal gain has yet been established. This is an open branch, not a loophole in the closed nonshared-pole result. The unrestricted DIRK10 stage interval remains 11–12.
