# Prime-interval extension of the negative-anchor screen

This extends the [negative-anchor proof](NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md) under a prime-interval condition on \(h=m-1\). It uses Schur’s irreducibility theorem for the ordinary Laguerre polynomials, the Newton polygon theorem over a local field, and Jordan’s prime-cycle theorem.

## 1. The coefficient at the \(\xi\) candidate never vanishes for \(h\ge5\)

Use the exact two-term expression (5) in NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md, in normalized coordinates. The odd-prime-divisor argument there covers all \(h\) with an odd prime factor. The remaining case is \(h=2^a\), \(a\ge2\). The coefficients of all nonleading terms of \(W_h\) are even by \(i!\binom hi^2\) (at \(i=1\), \(h^2\) is even). Thus all actual algebraic roots over an algebraic closure of \(\mathbb Q_2\) have positive valuation. Their average is \(v_2(h!)/h<1\); choose the embedding of the real root field sending \(\lambda\) to a root with \(0<v_2(\lambda)<1\), using Schur irreducibility. Here \(v_2(h)=a\ge2\), \(v_2(h-1)=0\), and \(v_2(h-2)=1\). Also \(\xi\equiv1\bmod2\), so \(W_h(\xi)\) is a unit. The first term of (5) has valuation at least \(-1\). Its second numerator has valuation \(2v_2(\lambda)\), because the other contribution \(h(h-1)\) has valuation \(a\ge2\). The second term therefore has valuation \(2v_2(\lambda)-a-2<-2\). They cannot cancel. Hence \(D_{h-3}(\xi)\ne0\) for every \(h\ge5\), with no statement about a field intersection yet. In particular any \(\xi\)-candidate forces the true penultimate coefficient \(a\) of NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md into \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\).

## 2. A prime interval makes the field tests automatic

Suppose a prime \(q\) satisfies
\[
                              h/2<q\le h-3 .                 \tag{1}
\]
Write \(E_h\) for the normal splitting field of \(W_h\) over \(\mathbb Q\). At the exponent \(j\) its normalized integer coefficient has absolute value \((h!)^2/[(j!)^2(h-j)!]\). Since \(q>h/2\), its \(q\)-valuation is one for \(0\le j\le h-q\), two for \(h-q<j<q\), and zero for \(q\le j\le h\). Hence the Newton polygon has the segment \((0,1)\) to \((q,0)\) of slope \(-1/q\), followed by the horizontal segment. A root on that segment has valuation \(1/q\), forcing its local ramification index to be a multiple of \(q\). Thus the decomposition group in the normal splitting field, and hence the global group, has order divisible by \(q\). An element of order \(q\) is one \(q\)-cycle on \(h<2q\) roots.

The global action is transitive by Schur irreducibility. It is primitive: in a proper nontrivial block system both block size and number of blocks are at most \(h/2<q\). The \(q\)-cycle must fix each block as a set, but its moved orbit would then fit inside one smaller block, impossible. Jordan's theorem applied to the primitive group and \(q\le h-3\) now gives \(A_h\) contained in the Galois action. In particular \([E_h:\mathbb Q]\ge h!/2>(h-1)!\).

Two consequences settle both field gates in NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md:
- The point stabilizer in a primitive action is maximal, so the irreducible root field \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\) has no proper intermediate fields. Its intersection with the normal \(E_{h-1}\) is therefore \(\mathbb Q\) or all of \(\mathbb Q(\lambda)\). In the latter case normality forces \(E_h\subseteq E_{h-1}\), impossible by the displayed degrees since \([E_{h-1}:\mathbb Q]\le(h-1)!\). Thus the \(\xi\)-condition would make \(a\), and hence \(u+v\), rational. The third central power sum calculation already given in NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md excludes that reflection at degree \(h-1\ge4\).
- All sums of distinct root pairs at degree \(h\) are themselves distinct. Pairs sharing a root cannot have the same sum. If two disjoint pairs had equal sum, choose a fifth root and use the 3-cycle through that root and the first pair, fixing the second pair. The sum equality would then identify two distinct roots. The 3-cycle belongs to \(A_h\). Hence the Galois action on all such sums is faithfully the pair action: fixing all pairs fixes each root by intersections. The normal closure of the field generated by one pair sum is therefore all \(E_h\) (the pair action is transitive, and the core of its stabilizer is trivial). If the mirror law (4) of NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md held at another root, its pair sum would belong to the normal field \(E_{h-1}\), again forcing \(E_h\subseteq E_{h-1}\). This is impossible.

Thus under (1), as also under the odd-prime hypothesis in NEGATIVE-ANCHOR.md, **no negative first late clock is possible** in the balanced canonical-forward minimum direct architecture with any real negative diagonal and any choice of the actual \(\lambda\). The [two-source sign argument](FIRST-CLOCK-SIGN.md) excludes the remaining nonnegative case for every real negative diagonal and every choice of the final early root.

The abstract condition holds at \(h=8,9\) with \(q=5\), hence at balanced orders \(18,20\). At \(h=6\) this prime interval is empty. No unrestricted stage bound follows.
