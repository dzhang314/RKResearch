# A proper split-trace intersection has all its weights on the zero component

This is an additional conditional diagnostic for the zero-residual branch, not an assumption about all tied-floor data. Use weights \(1,2\) for the coordinates and the critical degree pair \(m,n\) as in [The two equations and the weights](SIMPLE-LEGENDRE-COMPLETE-INTERSECTION-AND-WEIGHTS.md). Suppose all literal pairs are distinct and that there are vanishing equations in those two degrees, precisely one of which has zero scalar trace. Denote the pure equation by \(U(x,z)=zR(x,z)\), and let the other equation have scalar trace \(P(x)\ne0\). Suppose the two equations after substituting \(z=t^2\) are coprime. The labels of the two degrees may be interchanged.

Every nonzero-residual pair contributes two distinct lifted intersections. Every zero-residual pair yields one point \((c_i,0)\) of intersection multiplicity at least two: the pure equation has \(t^2\) as a factor, and the other equation has restriction \(P\) to \(t=0\). Intersecting the factor \(t^2\) with the other equation contributes \(2\,\operatorname{ord}_{c_i}P\); any additional vanishing of \(R\) would add positive intersection multiplicity. These contributions use the entire degree product \(mn=2s\). Hence every zero-site clock is a simple root of \(P\), \(R(c_i,0)\ne0\) there, there are no further roots of \(P\), and every pair is a simple affine intersection in \((x,z)\).

For a nonzero-residual site, the divided-difference separator \(D_i\) of the first note has zero trace, since one of the two defining scalar traces is identically zero. With its nonzero local Jacobian and ordinary moment integration this forces \(b_i=0\). At any zero-residual site the trace of the separator is \(-R(c_i,0)P(x)/(x-c_i)\), and its own value is \(-R(c_i,0)P'(c_i)\). Thus all output weights are the usual interpolatory quadrature weights of the zero-residual roots:
\[
 b_i=\int_0^1\frac{P(x)}{(x-c_i)P'(c_i)}\,dx\quad (g_i=0),
 \qquad b_i=0\quad(g_i\ne0).
\]
In the odd tied case \(P\) therefore has degree \(m\) (at least \(m\) ordinary quadrature sites are necessary), and its trace lies in \(\operatorname{span}(L_m,L_{m-1})\). In the even case when the non-pure equation has the smaller critical weight \(m\), \(P\) is a multiple of \(L_m\) and the weights are ordinary Gaussian quadrature. This conditional conclusion does not claim these interpolation weights yield a triangular tableau without its separate chronology equations. Conversely a genuine chart with a nonzero weight on a nonzero-residual site cannot at once have distinct pairs and this proper split-trace intersection.

If a different zero branch instead provides two lower pure factors \(R_1,R_2\), of weights at most \(m-2,n-2\), vanishing on all nonzero-residual sites, and their lifts after \(z=t^2\) are coprime, Bézout gives at most \((m-2)(n-2)/2\) distinct nonzero-residual pairs. Thus if those particular pairs do not coincide physically, at least \(m+n-2=p-1\) literal stages have zero residual. For the odd tied case two such low-factor directions are forced by rank two of the zero-site Legendre value matrix in (5); coprimeness is still a separate real condition, never inferred from linear independence.
