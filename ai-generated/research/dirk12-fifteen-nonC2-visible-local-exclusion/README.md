# DIRK12/15: the visible local-quadratic chart is impossible

For any ordinary real lower triangular fifteen-stage method of rooted order twelve, the [actual equality frame](../dirk12-fifteen-frame/README.md) forces both leading reverse rows to vanish and the physical terminal to be live, explicit, and at clock one. If \(C(2)\) fails, the same frame and its support reduction force the quadratic defect to be supported at at most two physical clock labels, all unweighted. The new conclusion in this packet is
\[
                     \mathbf b\odot F_3=0\qquad\text{if \(C(2)\) fails}.
\]
That is, every genuine grade-three pure direction is numerically zero at every nonzero-weight stage. The continuous pencil itself treats only the previously open case of **one physical quadratic-dirty clock, noninvariant \(F_3\), and a visible weighted grade-three direction**. The other non-\(C(2)\) cases follow from the low-rank and support reductions, as detailed below. This does **not** establish \(C(2)\) from equality, and the overall real stage interval remains **15–16**.

Throughout \(C=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf c)\), \(T_j\) is the genuine numerical rooted space and \(F_j\) is the genuine zero-profile space. Grades are tree order minus one. Use the [terminal-zero reverse band](../dirk12-fifteen-weighted-reverse-band/README.md). On every non-\(C(2)\) equality candidate, \(f_5=4\) and the terminal-zero reverse grade-six band has rank four by its paid \(q=5\) debit. The weighted four-debut projection through grades 3–6 applies and gives at least five physical clock labels for the nonzero reflected cubic \(\mathbf G\). There is **no** additional degree-six terminal debit. Every vector in \(\mathbf b\odot F_3\) lies on the \(\mathbf G\) line by the actual paid \(3+8=11\) pairing against \(T_8\).

Write \(\mathbf g=A\mathbf c-\mathbf c^{\,2}/2\). If there are two quadratic-dirty clock labels then \(F_3=\langle\mathbf g,C\mathbf g\rangle\) is wholly unweighted. If \(F_3\) is clock-invariant, its dimension two implies its support uses at most two numerical clocks by nodal localization. Its weighted image cannot be a nonzero multiple of the at-least-five-label \(\mathbf G\). It remains to exclude the chart described in the opening paragraph.

## One invisible seed and one visible genuine head

On the remaining chart \(U=\langle\mathbf g\rangle\) is invariant at the one quadratic-dirty clock, and \(F_3\) is not invariant. The ordinary genuine spaces have \(f_3=2,f_4=3,f_5=4,f_6=6\). \[
                    F_j=U+\mathbf w P_{j-3}(\mathbf c),\quad j=3,4,5,

and at least four effective head labels. With at most three effective clock labels the whole span of the three head powers in \(F_5\) would be invariant, which the incoming gain forbids.

Now \(\mathbf g^2\in F_4\) is supported on one clock. The head polynomial through degree one must vanish at the other effective labels, so \(\mathbf g^2\in U\). Thus \(\mathbf g\) is constant on its nonzero physical support. The same argument with \(\mathbf g\odot\mathbf w\in F_5\) uses head degree two and at least three other effective labels; this product also lies in \(U\). Subtract a multiple of \(\mathbf g\) from \(\mathbf w\) so that it vanishes on that physical ghost mask. All these operations use genuine forward products, not products of adjoint rows.

The degree-six kernel at the terminal has dimension five because of the genuine Legendre column \(L_6(\mathbf c)\in F_6\), with \(L_6(1)=1\). The square \(\mathbf w^2\) belongs to this kernel, since \(\mathbf w\in F_3\), \(\mathbf w(t)=0\), and squares through total grade six are genuine. Hence there is a polynomial \(P\), degree at most three, satisfying \(w_i=P(c_i)\) on the entire physical head support. The supports of \(\mathbf g,\mathbf w\) are disjoint. By the chart's visibility premise and the \(\mathbf G\) line, the number \(r\) of active head labels is at least five.

## The physical count and the exact shifted Jacobi pencil

The actual \(\mathbf w\)-moments through scalar degree eight vanish (grade \(3+8=11\)). If \(r\le9\), each weighted head label has zero aggregate by Vandermonde. Since the head's value is nonzero and constant on any such label, its ordinary weight also cancels and there are at least two active physical rows per label. The remaining clean scalar quadrature must have at least seven distinct active clocks, because it includes the live terminal at one and an at-most-six-node rule would annihilate \((1-x)S(x)^2\) for its other-node hull \(S\), of degree at most five. Thus \(2r+7>15\), impossible. So \(r\ge10\).

Let \(k\) count the distinct active head-clean nonterminal clocks, excluding the numerical value 1, and let \(V\) be their monic hull. If \(k\le2\), the paid \(\mathbf w\) test against \((1-x)V(x)^2P(x)\) gives the integral of \((1-x)V^2P^2\), which is strictly positive. The test has degree at most \(1+4+3=8\), and the scalar integral degree at most eleven. It is therefore within order twelve. Hence \(k\ge3\). In the non-\(C(2)\) equality frame no row is literally zero, so the first physical row has \(c_0=A_{00}\ne0\) and \(g_0=c_0^2/2\ne0\). This is at least one actual unweighted stage, disjoint from all active head and clean slots. Counting \(r+k+2\le15\) gives precisely:

- \(r=10\) active head labels, one physical row per label;
- \(k=3\) distinct active clean nonterminal clocks other than 1, one physical row each;
- the live physical terminal and exactly one unweighted ghost, the first physical row.

Thus \(\mathbf g\) is a nonzero multiple of the first-stage coordinate \(\mathbf e_0\), and there are no other unweighted rows or extra repeats within either color. Coincidences between the numerical clocks of different colors are not forbidden.

For every \(Q\in P_4\), the actual \(\mathbf w\)-moment of \((1-x)V(x)Q(x)\) gives
\[
                 \int_0^1 (1-x)V(x)P(x)Q(x)\,dx=0 .
\]
The degree of the probe is at most eight; the integrand degree is at most eleven. If \(\deg P\le1\), \(V P\) itself is an allowable test, with positive square integral, impossible. **Degree two must not be dropped**: at three clean clocks the squared mask needed to discard it would exceed the paid grade by one. Thus the two possibilities are:

- \(\deg P=2\), in which case \(V P\) is proportional to the degree-five Jacobi generator \(J_5\);
- \(\deg P=3\), in which case \(V P\in\langle J_5,J_6\rangle\) has degree six.

Here \(J_5,J_6\) are the explicitly verified shifted Jacobi polynomials for \((1-x)\,dx\) in the arithmetic replay. They have no common root, as verified by the separate exact finite-field gcd with both degrees preserved. The real polynomial \(V P\) has at least five distinct sign-changing roots in \((0,1)\) by its four-degree orthogonality and the positive weight. If degree five, all its roots are simple; if degree six, the one remaining degree gives another simple real root (it cannot increase the multiplicity of any sign-changing root by one). Consequently the factors \(V,P\) have disjoint simple roots.

## The first actual contrast, including the zero-clock exception

\[
                  J[P](u)=0,\qquad J[xP](u)=0

If \(u=0\), then \(P(0)\ne0\). On all earlier active rows \(w_j=P(c_j)\), and \(A\mathbf z=0\) there. ## Exact continuous compound argument, including singular companion

Put
\[
 Q_u(X)=\frac{J_6(X)J_5(u)-J_5(X)J_6(u)}{X-u}
       =\sum_{i=0}^5 q_i(u)X^i .
\]
The coefficients \(q_i\) have degree at most five in \(u\), by the divided antisymmetry. If \(V P\) has degree six and vanishes at \(u\), the distinct Jacobi generators imply \(J_5(u)\ne0\), and \(P\) divides the full-degree \(Q_u\). If \(V P\) has degree five, \(J_5(u)=0\) and \(J_6(u)\ne0\), giving the true degree-four \(Q_u\) proportional to \(J_5(X)/(X-u)\). Again \(P\) divides it. The possible vanishing of the degree-five companion coefficient is therefore real and is not ignored.

For each \(u\) let \(A_u\) be the \(5\times5\) matrix with subdiagonal ones and last column \((-q_0,\ldots,-q_4)^{\mathsf T}\), and \(B_u=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,1,q_5)\). For any selected root \(v\) of \(P\), its left Vandermonde row \(\lambda(v)=(1,v,\ldots,v^4)\) satisfies
\[
          \lambda(v) A_u=v\lambda(v) B_u .
\]
This identity uses the full \(Q_u(v)=0\); it holds even when \(q_5=0\). For \(k=\deg P\in\{2,3\}\), wedge the \(k\) distinct selected left rows to obtain a nonzero left vector \(W\). Then
\[
 W\,\bigwedge^k(ut B_u-A_u)
          =P_{\rm monic}(ut)\,W\,\bigwedge^k B_u .
\]
The two primitive equalities at \(u\ne0\) force \(W\) to annihilate the two integrated exterior matrices with weights \(t^a\), \(a=0,1\), on \([0,1]\). Their determinants must both vanish. **No inverse of \(B_u\) is used**, and the selected finite roots remain distinct in the singular degree-four case.
