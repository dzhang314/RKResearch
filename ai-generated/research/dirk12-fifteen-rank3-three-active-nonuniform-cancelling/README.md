# DIRK12/15: three all-cancelling dirty labels cannot have a nonuniform amplitude

In the ordinary real fifteen-stage order-twelve \(C(2),f_5=3\) equality face, suppose there are exactly three active cubic-dirty numerical labels and that the aggregate ordinary weight on the dirty rows of each of the three labels is zero. Then every such label has **only one active nonzero cubic-defect amplitude**. This note excludes the nonuniform-amplitude subface, not the uniform case, nonzero dirty aggregate masses, or all fifteen-stage tableaux. The real interval remains **15–16**.

Write \(\mathbf h=A\mathbf c^2-\mathbf c^3/3\). An active dirty row means \(b_i h_i\ne0\). At the three active labels \(z\) the first three weighted dirty amplitude moments vanish separately. Indeed the actual rooted probes \(\mathbf h^\ell\) against scalar powers through degree two have grade at most \(3\ell+2\le11\) for \(\ell=1,2,3\), and Vandermonde separates the three labels. Also \(m_0(z)=0\) by hypothesis. At any label with at most four active distinct amplitudes \(h_i\), a second Vandermonde on the amplitudes shows that each amplitude separately has ordinary weight sum zero there. Two or more amplitudes at one label therefore cost at least four actual active dirty rows. If a label had at least five distinct amplitudes, it would in any event cost at least five physical rows.

The scalar order-twelve quadrature is wholly on active clean labels (the dirty masses cancel). There are at least seven **candidate** clean labels when including the two actual endpoints \(0,1\), even if one endpoint weight is initially unknown: with at most six candidates, the degree-at-most-eleven scalar polynomial \((1-X)\) times the squared nodal hull of the other labels has positive integral and vanishes on the quadrature. Every candidate requires a physical row. There are at least two active dirt rows at every dirty label by the first amplitude moment. Hence a nonuniform label must cost exactly four rows, the other two labels exactly two rows apiece, and there are exactly seven equalities in the clean count. Every nonuniform amplitude accounts for a cancelling pair, so the nonuniform label \(z_*\) has exactly two amplitudes, each in a pair. All other dirty rows are the two uniform pairs. No remaining unweighted physical row is possible except potentially the literal origin: on the seven candidate clean labels including \(0,1\), exactness against \(X(1-X)P_4\) gives the standard unique seven-node Lobatto grid. Its weights, including both endpoints, are nonzero (the endpoint weight is \(1/42\)); the two cancelling amplitude pairs at \(z_*\) and the others have no effect on these weights. Thus all seven distinct clean rows and all eight dirty rows are live. This argument allows dirty numerical labels to coincide with clean ones.

As in the [four-label case](../dirk12-fifteen-rank3-four-active-cubic/README.md), the actual rank-three \(F_5\) is \(\mathbf hP_2(\mathbf c)\), by independence on the three distinct dirty clocks. A scalar profile \(S\) of degree at most five has an actual clean-supported state \(S(\mathbf c)-\mathbf hQ(\mathbf c)\), \(Q\in P_2\), if and only if \(S(z_*)=0\). At the nonuniform clock the two distinct amplitudes require both \(S(z_*)=0\) and \(Q(z_*)=0\). The other clocks impose the remaining two quadratic interpolation values \(Q(z)=S(z)/h_z\). The resulting numerical state is zero on every dirty row, regardless of clock overlap, and reads \(S\) at each clean row.

List the seven clean rows in physical order as \((\gamma_0,\ldots,\gamma_6)\) with \(\gamma_0=0,\gamma_6=1\). Use the same disjoint hulls \(F,G,F_4,G_4\), two lower-triangular cuts and grade-\((6+5)\) fixed integral matrices \(M,N\) as in the [four-singleton calculation](../dirk12-fifteen-rank3-four-singleton-end-exclusion/README.md). Only actual forward clean-supported states are needed, on both sides of the pairing. The scalar annihilating functional is now the single evaluation \(L(S)=S(z_*)\).

For either cut, the long (three-coefficient) and short (two-coefficient) scalar functionals cannot be zero. If the long one were zero, the short one would be nonzero by the disjoint hulls (they cover the clean grid at that cut). Then all long-side basis profiles and a nonzero short-side profile would contradict rank two of the corresponding \(2\times3\) or \(3\times2\) matrix. If the short one were zero, the long one would likewise be nonzero, giving a two-dimensional long kernel annihilated by its opposing rank-two matrix, also impossible. These arguments include \(z_*\) at any of the seven clean labels, with no rank statement about an unknown dirty clock.

Therefore the first cut places \(F(z_*)(1,z_*,z_*^2)\) in the row space of \(M\), and the second places \(G(z_*)(1,z_*,z_*^2)\) in the column space of \(N\). Dividing by the already established nonzero hull values gives two binary homogeneous quadratic equations on \([1:z_*]\): their three coefficients are the ordinary cross products of the two rows of \(M\) and of the two columns of \(N\). For each of the 120 ordered interiors, the exact matrices have rank two and the two quadratics multiplied by both linear monomials span the whole four-dimensional binary cubic space. The degree-three span excludes any simultaneous projective zero by the pure cube monomials.

The independent checker computes the fixed matrix rows from monomial antiderivatives and checks these ranks at the simple split prime \(1013\). The same fixed-grid coefficient-only transfer as in the preceding end-run packet applies: rank minors use no unknown dirty clock or tableau coefficient. The analytic implication is mathematical, not a native Lean stage-lower theorem.
