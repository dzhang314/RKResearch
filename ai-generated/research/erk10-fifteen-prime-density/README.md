# Density of prime-field specializations of two fixed 15-stage ERK10 tableaux

The fixed [signed-pair degree-18 tableau](../erk10-fifteen-two-pair-rational-closure/FIELD.md)
with literal donor parameter (d=8), and the fixed
[Stepanov tableau](../erk10-stepanov-all-primes/README.md), together give
15-stage explicit methods of exact order ten over a set of prime fields
of **natural density (99646543/212336640 simeq 46.928567%)**.
At good primes in this set the same coefficients work over
(mathbb Z_p) and every (mathbb Z/p^kmathbb Z).
Finitely many primes can be omitted without affecting the density.
The set of primes admitting an arbitrary 15-stage tableau consequently
has **lower natural density at least** this large; we do not assert that
the unrestricted set has a density or that methods cannot exist outside
this set. The real and rational stage intervals do not change.

## The signed-pair normal closure

Use (N,B,M_0,N_0) from the [coefficient-field construction](../erk10-fifteen-two-pair-rational-closure/FIELD.md).
Let (L) split (N), whose nine roots are (a_i), and put
(T=M_0^2-20BN_0). The tail quadratic has discriminant
(T(a_i)/(5B(a_i))^2). Thus the normal closure of
(mathbb Q(a,f)) is obtained from (L) by adjoining all nine
(sqrt{T(a_i)}). The fixed (d=8) signed-pair witness was
constructed in this degree-18 field using the verified 17-adic
branch. Its actual clocks recover (a,f), so these are coefficient
field generators, not auxiliary coordinates one could omit.

The exact replay verifies that (N) is irreducible modulo 29 and
has squarefree factorization of type (2+7) modulo 31. These
checks use (deggcd(N,X^{p^j}-X)) for each (j=1,ldots,9);
they determine the number of irreducible factors of each degree.
The Galois group on the (a_i) is transitive and contains a
permutation of type ((2,7)). Taking powers produces both a
transposition and a 7-cycle. A transitive group of degree nine
with a 7-cycle cannot preserve three blocks of size three:
the full stabilizer of such a partition has order (6^4), not
divisible by seven. Thus the group is primitive. The graph of
the conjugates of the transposition is invariant and has edges;
its components would form a nontrivial block system unless it
were connected. Hence these transpositions generate (S_9).

The replay computes

At 223 the leading coefficient of (N) is a unit and the monic
common-factor gcd of (N,T) is (X+130). Squarefree reduction
makes (L) unramified there (the roots split over an unramified
extension after lifting simple residue roots). At a prime of (L)
above 223 all roots (a_i) are integral and distinct in residue.
Exactly one (T(a_i)) is nonunit. By the product formula for the
resultant, its valuation is one, and the other eight valuations
are zero. Conjugating this prime in the transitive root action
gives every unit-coordinate valuation vector. Thus no nonempty
product of the nine (T(a_i)) is a square in (L).

The nine independent square classes give a multiquadratic
extension of degree (2^9) over (L). Its normal closure over
(mathbb Q) embeds in the signed permutation group on the nine
pairs ((a_i,pmsqrt{T(a_i)})), and has the full order
(2^9,9!). Its group is therefore (C_2^9times S_9),
with exactly that action on the 18 embeddings of the coefficient
field.

## Independence from the Stepanov field

Let (K) be Stepanov's Galois coefficient field of degree eight.
It contains the independent square roots of 3 and 7, and its Galois
group is dihedral of order eight. Thus its only quadratic subfields
have square classes (3,7,21).

Every homomorphism from the signed permutation group to a 2-group
kills the subgroup (A_9) of the permutation subgroup, because
(A_9) is generated by 3-cycles. Its nine sign generators are
conjugate under (A_9), so they all have the same image in such a
quotient. Therefore every 2-group quotient factors through the
two sign characters: permutation parity, and total sign parity.
The corresponding quadratic square classes are
[
 operatorname{disc}(N),qquad
 prod_i T(a_i)=operatorname{Res}(N,T)/operatorname{lc}(N)^8 ,
]
and their product. The first has odd valuation at 13; the second
and the product have odd valuation at 223. None is (3,7,21).
A nontrivial Galois intersection with (K) would have a nontrivial
2-group as its Galois group, hence would contain a quadratic
subfield. This is impossible, so the two normal fields are linearly
disjoint.

## Fixed points and the actual tableau

At unramified good primes, a degree-one place is equivalent to a
fixed embedding under Frobenius, and the fixed coefficient field
embeds into (mathbb Q_p) at that place. Outside a finite set,
the actual coefficients are integral and the nonzero order-eleven
defect is a unit. Reduction then retains order ten and the unit
failure of order eleven in every finite precision. Conversely,
away from finitely many bad primes, a reduction of that fixed
literal with all coefficients in (mathbb F_p) must contain its
recovered field generators in (mathbb F_p): the signed-pair
clocks recover (a,f) and the Stepanov clocks recover its three
roots. The recovery and integrality statements concern the two
fixed coefficient tuples, not all methods with their number of stages.

A permutation with (m) fixed indices has no fixed member among
the 18 signed positions with conditional probability (2^{-m}).
For a uniform permutation of nine indices,
(mathbb E(t^m)=sum_{j=0}^9(t-1)^j/j!):
expand (t^m=(1+(t-1))^m) and count the expected number of
(j)-element subsets fixed pointwise. Therefore the signed-pair
no-place probability is (112690097/185794560), giving density
(73104463/185794560simeq39.346934%). The dihedral field has
a degree-one place exactly at the identity Frobenius, so its
no-place probability is (7/8). In the compositum the Galois
group is the direct product. By the [Chebotarev density theorem
in natural prime-counting form](https://msp.org/ant/2019/13-5/ant-v13-n5-p02-s.pdf)
the union density is consequently

particular primes, and says nothing about primes outside these

## Replay and scope

Run `python3 -B verify.py` with SymPy. It reads the four coefficient
lists from the published univariate constructor without executing
its program, and recomputes the finite-field gcds, exact resultant and
discriminant, and rational group count. The normal-closure,
specialization and Chebotarev arguments are mathematical. The two
tableau constructions have separate exact and native certificates;
the density theorem is not an end-to-end Lean result.
