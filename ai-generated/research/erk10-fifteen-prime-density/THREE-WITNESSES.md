# Three fixed 15-stage ERK10 tableaux: exact finite-field coverage

The new [fixed-q tableau](../erk10-fifteen-fixedq-29adic/README.md), the
signed-pair tableau, and the Stepanov tableau have jointly linearly disjoint normal
closures. These three fixed tableaux give the following natural densities,
over rational primes \(p\) and for the specified extension at each \(p\):

| Field | Exact density | Percent |
|---|---:|---:|
| \(\mathbb F_p\) | 535038692663489723 / 789019852013568000 | 67.810548911 |
| \(\mathbb F_{p^2}\) | 2038195978321 / 2080899072000 | 97.947853682 |
| \(\mathbb F_{p^3}\) | 1070788017536573 / 1391569403904000 | 76.948229426 |
| \(\mathbb F_{p^4}\) | 1 | 100 |

These are exact counts for the three fixed witnesses and lower bounds for
unrestricted 15-stage coverage. The earlier two fixed witnesses cover
46.928567298% of prime fields. This result neither excludes 14 stages
nor supplies a rational 15-stage method.

## The new coefficient field

Write \(P\) for the degree-ten origin polynomial and \(c\) for its selected
root. The head polynomial is \(Y^2-sY+q\) with \(s,q\in\mathbb Q(c)\); let
\(H=s^2-4q\). All entries are rational functions of \(c,a\), where \(a\)
is the selected head root. Conversely the actual row sums of forward
stages 11 and 14 are \(1-c\) and \(1-a\), respectively
([raw labels](../erk10-fifteen-split-fixed-q-spectrum/spectrum.py) and
[literal reversal](../erk10-fifteen-split-fixed-q-spectrum/literal.py)).
Thus the literal coefficient field is \(\mathbb Q(c,a)\).
The nonzero order-eleven defect is witnessed at the chosen 29-adic place.

At 41, 53 and 269 the degree-ten polynomial is squarefree with factor
degrees \((10),(1,2,7),(1,1,1,7)\), respectively. At 41 irreducibility
gives transitivity. Powers of the element at 53 give a 7-cycle and a
transposition. Any nontrivial block system in degree ten has block size
2 or 5; its stabilizer has order prime to 7. The group is therefore
primitive. The graph of conjugates of its transposition is connected by
primitivity, and the graph edge transpositions generate \(S_{10}\).

The full Galois group embeds in \(C_2\wr S_{10}\). At 269 the head
discriminant has norm 88, a nonsquare unit. The prime is unramified in
the full tower: both the decic and the head quadratic have separable
reduction in their respective residue algebras. The product of the ten
head square roots is a square root of the norm, so Frobenius has odd
total sign in the signed action. The decic permutation has order seven;
its seventh power is an odd vector in the sign kernel. That kernel is
an \(S_{10}\)-stable subspace of \(\mathbb F_2^{10}\). An odd vector is
not constant (ten is even). Subtracting its image under a transposition
of unlike positions gives a two-coordinate vector. The orbit spans
the even subspace, and the original odd vector gives the full sign
kernel. The group is \(C_2\wr S_{10}\); in particular the literal field
has degree 20 with its natural signed-root action.

## Joint independence

The [earlier proof](README.md) gives disjoint normal fields with groups
\(C_2\wr S_9\) and \(D_8\) (dihedral of order eight). If their compositum
intersects the new normal field, the intersection group is a quotient
of both sides. It must kill the copy of \(A_{10}\) in the new group:
\(A_{10}\) is simple and its order is divisible by 25, while the old
product group has only one factor of 5. Killing \(A_{10}\) identifies
all ten sign generators by conjugation, leaving a quotient of
\(C_2^2\). So a nontrivial intersection would share a quadratic
subfield. The new quadratic characters are generated by the decic
discriminant and the norm of the head discriminant. The old four are
the nonic discriminant, the norm of its tail discriminant, 3 and 7.
In the order nonic discriminant, tail norm, decic discriminant, head
norm, 3, 7, the nonsquare bits at primes 19, 23, 31, 41, 43, 47 are
respectively 011010, 110001, 101010, 111111, 010011 and 001100.
They have rank six over \(\mathbb F_2\), excluding all nontrivial
square-class relations. Both norms are independently replayed as
resultants and as multiplication determinants.

## Counts and specialization

Outside a finite bad set all coefficients, construction denominators
and the inverse of the chosen next-order defect are integral. We may
also omit primes where distinct embeddings specialize to identical
tableaux. The reduction of a fixed tableau belongs to
\(\mathbb F_{p^k}\) for some place exactly when its Frobenius action on
the embeddings has a cycle whose length divides \(k\). The same place
gives the tableau over the corresponding unramified local ring, all
its prime-power quotients and its fraction field. The order
identities hold under these maps; the defect stays a unit.

For a base cycle of length \(j\), even sign splits it into two cycles
of length \(j\), and odd sign gives one cycle of length \(2j\).
The no-place weight for a base cycle is thus
\((\mathbf1_{j\nmid k}+\mathbf1_{2j\nmid k})/2\).
Average the product of these weights over the permutations of nine
and ten letters, and multiply by the dihedral complement (7/8 for odd
\(k\), 1/4 for \(k\equiv2\pmod4\), and zero for \(4\mid k\)).
Chebotarev for the product group converts that complement into the
density of primes not covered. In particular the prime-field coverage
is \(1-(7/8)C_9 C_{10}\) with
\(C_n=\sum_{j=0}^{n}(-1)^j/(2^j j!)\).
The least available residue degree has distribution approximately
67.810549%, 30.137305%, 0.581255%, 1.470891% at degrees one through
four, distinct from the specified-degree table above.

## Replay

Run `python3 -B verify-third-witness.py | diff -u third-witness-exact-replay.txt -`
with SymPy. The script regenerates the decic from the published origin
equations, replays the actual head solve in the modular polynomial
quotients with checked unit inverses, verifies all modular receipts,
and computes the densities by both a recurrence and cycle partitions.
