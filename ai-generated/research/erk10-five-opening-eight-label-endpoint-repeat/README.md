# Eight labels with two copies of the endpoint: arbitrary second placement

Under the actual five-opening order-ten reverse word budget,
ten high rows, all high forward clocks nonzero, and only the
opening origin possibly scored, suppose exactly two physical
high clocks equal \(1\), one being first in reverse, and
there are exactly eight distinct high clocks. Then there is
no rational continuation in this chart. The other duplicate
is one interior pair. All physical and pooled scores and
all second-endpoint positions are allowed. This is not an
unrestricted stage lower bound.

The leading block and the earliest separated \(0,a,0\)
case are already treated in the
[contiguous](../erk10-five-opening-contiguous-endpoint-repeat/README.md)
and [first-jump](../erk10-five-opening-first-endpoint-jump/README.md)
packets. In what follows \(D_j\), raw \(\boldsymbol v,
\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol k\),
their positive forward moment counts, the rank-three bound
and the actual early contrast use the same notation.
They are valid over every field with \(10!\ne0\).
Only the final rational nonic uses characteristic zero and
the specified rational coefficient field.

## Zero grade-two aggregate: the late endpoint cannot have the required jump

If \(\boldsymbol u\) has zero group sums, its two endpoint
values are zero since the first is literally zero. The remaining
support is the one opposite direction at the interior pair
\(a\), and \(\boldsymbol u\ne0\) by the first live score at
a nonzero reverse ordinate. If all grade-three aggregates
vanish, the same holds for \(\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h\).
Their proportionality to \(\boldsymbol u\) is impossible:
at the first live non-\(a\) interior label, the previous
scalar sources are only at \(a\), on which
\(g=a u+a^3w/6\). Thus a nonzero scalar tap to the earlier
\(a\)-scores contradicts its exact \(K\boldsymbol u=0\).

If grade three leaks, its aggregate dimension is one.
Indeed the six positive tests on all eight high labels give
a linear interpolation cofactor space (now including the
endpoints pooled at their label), and if both directions
occur their first clock marks would occupy three aggregate
dimensions in \(D_4\), besides the pure \(\boldsymbol u\).
Also \(\boldsymbol h\) itself leaks: otherwise it is a
multiple of \(\boldsymbol u\) and zero at the first live
row, hence identically zero. Before the first live new
label the strict \(a\)-block \(N\) would then satisfy
\((N-aI/2)N\boldsymbol w_*=0\), so \(N\boldsymbol w_*=0\).
Old \(a\) weights would be proportional to \(\boldsymbol u\),
incompatible with that first live different label's scalar
and composed reads. Thus \(D_4=\langle \boldsymbol u,
\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol y\odot\boldsymbol h\rangle\).
The first nonendpoint row is a live \(a\)-opener, else it
is blind or violates the pure \(u\)-support, and the same
literal identities as before follow:
\[
  \boldsymbol g=(2a/3)\boldsymbol u+\beta\boldsymbol h,\quad
  \boldsymbol k=(a^2/2)\boldsymbol u+\phi(\boldsymbol y)\odot
          \boldsymbol h,\quad
  K\boldsymbol h=\psi(\boldsymbol y)\odot\boldsymbol h .
\]
Here \(\phi,\psi\) are linear.

Apply the
[raw eight-label early-head calculations](../erk10-five-opening-eight-label-pairs-zero/README.md#grade-three-leakage-no-inverse-scores)
before the second endpoint. There is no dormant interior
repeat, so the nonzero-ordinate parts of those computations
force the second endpoint by the fifth physical reverse row.
More explicitly, with the active pair closed,
\(0,a,a,b\) and \(0,a,b,a\) allow no next new nonzero label;
with the pair open \(0,a,b,c\), a new nonzero label would
fail the two-read determinant and a closing \(a\) would fail
the delayed raw \(3\times3\) determinant.

The second endpoint must have nonzero \(h\) at such an
early placement: if \(h=0\) its \(u=0\), and both physical
endpoint rows are proportional on the actual observer
columns (only the constant score could be nonzero there).
There are at least two later distinct labels in reverse,
so the two forward witness rows remain independent away
from this early contrast.

Immediately after \(a_1\), a second endpoint cannot have
nonzero \(h\) because \(u=0\) kills its sole positive scalar
tap onto live \(a_1\). After \(a_1,a_2\), its \(u=0\)
kills the total scalar \(a\)-tap, and \(g=\beta h\) with
\(\beta=-2/3\) forces \(h=0\). After \(a_1,b\) with \(a\)
open, the literal formula
\(h_z=w_z a z^2(2z-3b)/(4b)\) evaluated at \(z=0\)
already gives zero.

If following three nonzero-ordinate rows, the two-read
equations used in the prefix proof are valid also at \(z=0\):
all nominal right sides contain \(z^2\), hence vanish.
The exact two left coefficients multiplying \(h\) therefore
cannot both vanish. The three prefix cases yield, up to
nonzero factors on each entry, respectively
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
 a,a,b & (5a-4b,\ a-2b)\\
 a,b,a & (4a-5b,\ 2a-b)\\
 a,b,c & (5b-4c,\ b-2c).
\end{array}
\]
In each case substituting the second zero would leave
\(6\) times a nonzero clock in the first, impossible in
a paid field. Thus \(h=0\) regardless of the endpoint's
individual score. This contradicts early contrast and
settles the entire aggregate-exact grade-two branch.

## Leaking grade two: the interior pair closes before or after the jump

If \(\boldsymbol u\) leaks, the eight-site grade-two cofactor
is supported at every high label, including the endpoint.
Thus the second endpoint has nonzero \(u\), and
\(D_4=\boldsymbol u\mathcal P_2\), \(D_3=\boldsymbol u
\mathcal P_1\), by marking and the rank bound. Scale the
first interior to \(1\), with \(Y=y/a\), \(H=1/a\) and
formal scalar integration \(I(F)=H^{-1}\int_0^H F\),
as in the
[nine-label head argument](../erk10-five-opening-nine-label-one-pair/README.md).
Retain its \(P,Q,R,\kappa\) conventions.
The first interior is live by early blindness.

If the second endpoint precedes the completion of the
interior pair, every interior label before it is distinct.
If at least two interiors precede it, the
[nine-label repeated-endpoint proof](../erk10-five-opening-nine-label-endpoint-repeat/README.md)
applies without requiring subsequent labels to remain distinct:
its determinants use only that prefix, and its final causal
identity uses \(Y\ne0\) at each nonendpoint, not distinctness.
It excludes both \(\kappa=0\) and \(\kappa=1/2\), and
forces one of these values by the same earlier compatibility
gates. If just one interior precedes it, the
[first separated jump](../erk10-five-opening-first-endpoint-jump/README.md)
settles that case; contiguity was already counted.

Otherwise the interior pair closes first. Its repeat
cannot be at the second or third physical interior
position, by the first-two-read contrast in the original
nine-label proof before any second endpoint. Thus there
are three distinct interior labels \(1,b,c\) first.
If \(\kappa=0\) these three pay the same identities
\(P(Y)=2Y/3,\ R(Y)+2Q(Y)=Y^2/2\) used in the causal
zero exclusion; if \(\kappa=1/2\), they pay the corresponding
half identity \(P+2Q=2Y^2/3\). Again these arguments ignore
any later nonzero-clock repetition, so neither value
is available. A fourth distinct label would force
\(\kappa=1/2\) by its exact augmented determinant.
Therefore the fourth physical interior must close the
pair at the sole noncontrast position \(c\), with
\[
                 c(2\kappa-1)=4b\kappa .
\]
The actual beginning is \(0,1,b,c,c\).

## The late endpoint cannot immediately follow the interior pair

At the next physical row, suppose the late endpoint
occurs. Divide its actual old taps by its nonzero
\(u\)-jump (not by its physical score). There are four
unknown inputs: the taps to the \(u\)-vectors at
\(1,b,c\), and a separate total scalar \(c\)-tap.
At \(Y=0\) its own score is absent from all five
positive scalar/marked primitive equations.
The augmented matrix is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 1&1&1&0&-\kappa\\
 1&b&c&0&Q(0)\\
 L_1&bL_b&0&c&1\\
 L_1&b^2L_b&0&c^2&P(0)\\
 L_1&b^3L_b&0&c^3&R(0)
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here the actual scalar ratios \(L_i=w_i/u_i\) and
polynomials are those of the first-three calculation.
Its determinant after \(c(2\kappa-1)=4b\kappa\) is
\[
 -32b^3\kappa^3(b-1)(2\kappa+1)(4b\kappa-2\kappa+1)/
                     (2\kappa-1)^3 .
\]
It is nonzero: \(2\kappa+1=0\) would force \(c=b\),
and \(4b\kappa-2\kappa+1=(2\kappa-1)(c-1)\ne0\).
Thus an interior singleton \(d\) follows the completed
pair instead.

## Terminal collapse with a repeated zero ordinate among the four last rows

The already verified post-closure \(5\times5\) scalar
ratio in the nine-label chart now applies at \(d\),
since no second endpoint was yet available as a donor:
\[
 L_i=\frac{N(i)}{i^2(i-c)}\quad (i=1,b,d),\qquad
 L_i\,i(i-c/2)=c-2\quad (i=1,b),
\]
where
\[
 N(Y)=\frac{2(4b\kappa^2Y-4b\kappa^2+2b\kappa Y+
                       2b\kappa-2\kappa Y+Y)}{2\kappa-1}.
\]
In particular \(N(0)=-4b\kappa\ne0\).
Put \(M=Y^2(Y-c)^2\), \(G=(Y-d)M'/4\) and keep the
same two actual donors from that chart:
\[
 \boldsymbol Z=\boldsymbol w M(\boldsymbol Y)
                  -(\boldsymbol Y-c)N(\boldsymbol Y)\boldsymbol u,
 \quad
 \boldsymbol G_*=\boldsymbol w G(\boldsymbol Y)
              +(2-c)(\boldsymbol Y-c)(\boldsymbol Y-d)\boldsymbol u .
\]
They are zero on the first six physical rows
\(0,1,b,c,c,d\), and their \(K\)-images minus their
nominal \(JM,JG\) weighted primitives are grade-five tests.

Let \(s,t,u,v\) be the four remaining **physical**
reverse labels in order (here \(u\) is a scalar label,
not the bold defect). One of them is zero (the late
endpoint), and the other three are distinct new singleton
labels. Set
\(\nu=(Y-H)(Y-t)(Y-u)(Y-v)\).
The two \(\nu\)-tests are blind, giving
\(I(\nu JM)=I(\nu JG)=0\); hence
\(I(\nu(Y-d)M)=0\). The other nodal moment and the
raw collapse need to account for the late zero:
\[
 P_9=\nu(Y-s)Y(Y-1)(Y-b)(Y-c)(Y-d)/Y .
\]
Here the division by \(Y\) cancels the **repeated label**
from the displayed polynomial product, not an evaluation
at zero. The scalar polynomial \(P_9\) has every distinct
high label and the opening origin \(H\) exactly once.
Set \(\mu_i=I(P_9(Y)/(Y-i))\), so \(W_i=\mu_i/P_9'(i)\).
The eight-high-site cofactor for \(\boldsymbol u\) is
\(U_i=-\lambda/P_9'(i)\) with \(\lambda\ne0\),
again with no extra factor of \(i\) because the late
endpoint is part of the cofactor's scalar support.
The ratios at \(1,b,d\) give
\[
 D(i):=\mu_i i^2(i-c)=-\lambda N(i).
\]

Test \(\nu(Y-s)M\). Only \(1,b,d\) contribute.
The extra \(Y\) factor in \(\nu(Y-s)\) ensures their
numerators in the three-site cofactor are exactly
\(D(i)\), not \(D(i)/i\). They agree with an affine
polynomial, hence \(I(\nu(Y-s)M)=0\). Since \(s\ne d\),
the two nodal equations give \(I(\nu M)=I(\nu YM)=0\).

If \(s=0\), then \(\nu\) does not contain a \(Y\) factor.
In \(I(\nu M)\) the three numerators at \(1,b,d\)
are \(D(i)/i=-\lambda N(i)/i\).
Its affine part after division becomes constant, whose
cofactor sum is zero; the remaining sum of \(1/i\)
against the three-site cofactor is \(1/(bd)\).
Thus \(I(\nu M)=-\lambda N(0)/(bd)\ne0\),
a contradiction already over paid fields.

Otherwise \(s\ne0\), so \(\nu\) itself contains
the late endpoint's \(Y\) factor. The possible four
contributors to \(I(\nu M)\) are \(1,b,d,s\), with
their numerators \(D(i)\). The four-site cofactor
annihilates the same affine polynomial, forcing
\(D(s)=-\lambda N(s)\). Therefore the literal
\(\boldsymbol Z_s=0\), without dividing by score.
Take \(U=(Y-H)(Y-u)(Y-v)\) in the actual
\(K\boldsymbol Z-\boldsymbol w JM\) test.
Any last two labels, including \(Y=0\) if there,
are killed, and the remaining rows can read only
zero donors through \(s\). Hence \(I(U JM)=0\),
and \(I(YU JM)=0\) by \(\nu=(Y-t)U\).
These two and the two nodal moments are the
[existing nonic equations](../erk10-fifteen-two-pair-rational-closure/README.md#the-unconditional-collapse-and-rational-exclusion)
with small parameter \(c/(2H)\), after rescaling.
The independent Cramer/p-adic rational obstruction
there now finishes the chart. Its polynomial elimination
places no nonzero-score or nonzero-root condition on
the two non-horizon roots of \(U\).
