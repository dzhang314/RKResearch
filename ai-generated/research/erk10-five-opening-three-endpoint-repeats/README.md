# Three or more endpoint copies are impossible in the five-opening cut

Use ten physical high rows, all high forward clocks nonzero,
and the actual five-opening order-ten reverse word budget with only the opening origin allowed an opening output weight.
Suppose three or more physical high clocks equal \(1\), with
at least one first in reverse. Then no such rational continuation
exists, arbitrary scores and repeated-endpoint positions allowed.
The cases with at least seven high labels are excluded over
**every field with \(10!\ne0\)**. Fewer labels fail the
[two-endpoint rational scalar floor](../rational-endpoint-moment-nine/README.md).
This is an architecture result, not a general stage bound.

Use raw reverse \(K=B^T\), \(y=1-x\), the usual score-weighted
\(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol k\),
their actual grades \(2,3,3,4\), and rank-three \(D_4\):
see the [contiguous endpoint proof](../erk10-five-opening-contiguous-endpoint-repeat/README.md).
At least five high labels have nonzero grouped scores, by
shifted Hilbert \(5\). At the first live interior physical
row, \(\boldsymbol u=-w y^2/2\ne0\).
The two independent true forward residual rows remain
independent away from an early reverse contrast whenever
at least two distinct high clocks come later in reverse.

## Count and the three-endpoint eight-label branch

With \(r\) physical endpoint copies and \(m\ge7\) high
labels, the excess interior multiplicity is \(11-r-m\ge0\).
For \(r\ge4\) the sole possibility is \(r=4,m=7\) and
no interior repeat. But the seven grade-two positive
moments force zero aggregate on all high labels, contradicting
the nonzero \(u\) of the first live interior singleton.
For \(r=3,m=8\) there are also no interior repeats, but
\(\boldsymbol u\) has nonzero aggregate by the same first
live singleton value. Its eight-label cofactor and its two
marks therefore span \(D_4\), and grade-three multipliers
of \(\boldsymbol u\) are affine. In particular the same
\(P,Q,R,\kappa\) identities as in the
[nine-label head](../erk10-five-opening-nine-label-one-pair/README.md)
apply after scaling the first interior to \(1\).

A second endpoint before any interior would have \(u=0\)
as the first does, giving early contrast since the two
observations on \(D_4\) and the nominal powers would see
only their score direction. Thus the first added endpoint
comes after at least one interior. If after two or more
interiors, the
[later-jump proof](../erk10-five-opening-nine-label-endpoint-repeat/README.md)
applies through that first jump. Indeed every preceding
interior label is distinct. For exactly two or three, a
zero \(u\)-jump would be an early contrast, and the
same exact \(3\times3\) or \(4\times4\) jump determinant
forces \(\kappa=1/2\) unless \(\kappa=0\). For at least
four interior labels their fourth distinct determinant
forces \(1/2\) if \(\kappa\ne0\).
The two causal contradictions for these values use a
vector with leading factor \(Y\), and so work with *any
number* of later zero-\(Y\) rows. The true dependencies
\(K\boldsymbol V=(Y/3)\boldsymbol V\) or
\(K\boldsymbol V=(Y/4)\boldsymbol V\) force \(\boldsymbol
V=0\) on both nonzero and zero ordinates by the same
induction. Their scalar moment contradictions persist.

If the first added endpoint immediately follows \(1\),
we have \(0,1,0\), and it has nonzero \(u\) by early
contrast. This forces \(\kappa=1/2\) by its actual
\(K\boldsymbol u\) read, as in the
[first jump packet](../erk10-five-opening-first-endpoint-jump/README.md).
A third zero immediately next gives an early contrast
supported on the three endpoint rows: at a common
ordinate the observer restriction has at most two
columns, \(\boldsymbol w\) and \(\boldsymbol u\).
If an interior \(b\) comes next, that packet's exact
\(4\times4\) determinant forbids a subsequent new
distinct nonzero ordinate before the third endpoint.
There are no interior repeats, so the third zero
would have to occur by the fifth physical reverse row.
Once more it completes an early three-coordinate contrast.
At least two different nonendpoint clocks follow
either early prefix, so the two forward residual
witnesses off the contrast are independent. This
finishes \(r=3,m=8\).

## Three endpoints and seven labels: leaking grade three

The remaining multiplicity is \(r=3,m=7\), with a
single interior pair \(a\). All grade-two aggregates
are zero (seven tests on the seven high labels).
The first physical endpoint has all positive defects
zero by strict causality. Thus the pure support of
grade two is the two-dimensional space \(S\) spanned
by the interior opposite-pair direction and an
endpoint direction opposed on the two later copies
(the first has zero coordinate).

If grade three leaks, its one-dimensional scalar
cofactor and first mark consume two directions.
A nonzero \(\boldsymbol u\) cannot then have both
endpoint and interior support, because its marked
copy would add a second independent pure direction.
It has interior support by the first live interior
row, hence is pure there. The same argument from
the [eight-label pure-\(u\) case](../erk10-five-opening-eight-label-endpoint-repeat/README.md)
forces \(\boldsymbol h\) to leak:
if it were pure it would be proportional to \(u\)
and then zero at the first live row; the strict
old single-label \(a\)-block would make its old
\(u\) proportional to its scores, incompatible
with the first live different label's scalar payment.
Thus \(D_4=\langle u,h,yh\rangle\) in vector notation
and the same exact grade identities follow.

Before the first added endpoint, the old single
active-pair prefix proof applies. It requires that
first added endpoint by the fifth physical reverse
row, since there is no dormant interior pair.
If it is before the first interior, it has \(h=0\)
by causality and pure interior \(u\), and is already
a two-coordinate early endpoint contrast.
Otherwise the same actual two-read coefficients at
zero from the eight-label pure-\(u\) case force
\(h=0\) there. Their computation concerns that
individual earlier endpoint, even though the later
third endpoint might have nonzero \(h\) and carry
the cofactor. In all cases the first two endpoint
copies are already proportional on the observer
columns and early enough for the two true forward
witnesses, contradiction. No assumption was made
that the pooled cofactor equals the first later
copy's value.

## Three endpoints and seven labels: exact grade three

Suppose all grade-three aggregates vanish. Then
\(D_3\subseteq S\) with the two pure directions
described above: the first endpoint remains zero
and its two later coordinates are opposite.
If \(\dim D_3=1\), its nonzero \(\boldsymbol u\)
and its first mark confine support to the interior
pair \(a\), and \(\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h\)
are proportional to \(\boldsymbol u\).
At the first live singleton \(t\), only scalar
label \(a\) is available in positive degrees;
on its earlier \(a\)-copies
\(g=a u+a^3w/6\), so the required scalar tap to
their scores cannot simultaneously kill their
proportional \(\boldsymbol u\).
Thus \(D_3=S\) and has rank two.

Call a nonzero-score singleton or the closure
of a nonzero-total \(a\)-pair ordinary, not
counting endpoint copies as positive-ordinate
targets. The first ordinary cannot be a pair
closure at \(a\): the only scalar positive
donor is at \(a\), and the exact first/second
group moments would require \(a=3a/2\).
Thus it is a live singleton \(t\), and the same
two scalar payments with just label \(a\)
give \(t=3a/2\) and a nonzero scalar \(a\)-tap.

The old scalar \(a\)-scores are tracked by the
pure interior component \(\boldsymbol U_a\) of
\(\boldsymbol u=\boldsymbol U_a+\boldsymbol U_E\),
where \(\boldsymbol U_E\) is its endpoint component.
Indeed, at least one \(a\)-score must have been
live before \(t\). The first physical \(a_1\)
must then be live: if its second copy alone
were live and available, its closure would
already be ordinary. At \(a_1\) the raw \(\boldsymbol
u\) equals \(-a^2 w_{a_1}/2\).
If the second \(a\)-copy is already available
its pool is zero, so both the score and \(\boldsymbol
u\) are opposed there and the same ratio holds.
Therefore on the available \(a\) donors
\(\boldsymbol U_a=-(a^2/2)\boldsymbol w\).

At \(t\), \(K\boldsymbol u=0\) forces
\((K\boldsymbol U_E)_t\ne0\), since the weighted
\(a\)-tap contributes nonzero \(K\boldsymbol U_a\).
Thus \(\boldsymbol U_E\ne0\) on both later endpoint
copies. Its first nonzero endpoint copy \(e\)
must precede \(t\), and it must follow live
\(a_1\), since \(\boldsymbol u_e\) needs a nonzero
scalar positive tap at zero ordinate. At \(e\)
only scalar label \(a\) has yet occurred, so
\(g_e=a u_e,\ k_e=a^2 u_e\).
There is no older nonzero endpoint \(u\) before
\(e\), hence \(h_e=(K\boldsymbol U_a)_e=-a u_e/2\).
At \(a_1\) the first actual low values fix
\(g_{a_1}=(2a/3)(U_a)_{a_1}\), \(h_{a_1}=0\)
and \(k_{a_1}=(a^2/2)(U_a)_{a_1}\).
Since \(\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h\in D_3=S\),
the two independent coordinates \(a_1,e\) give
the global identities
\[
 \boldsymbol g=(2a/3)\boldsymbol U_a+a\boldsymbol U_E,
 \qquad \boldsymbol h=-a\boldsymbol U_E/2 .
\]

Set \(\boldsymbol Q=K\boldsymbol U_E\), of grade
four. It is zero at \(a_1,e\) and nonzero at \(t\).
It and the two pure modes therefore span all
\(D_4\). With \(S=(K\boldsymbol w_a)_t\) the
scalar \(a\)-tap, \(Q_t=a^2S/2\) as above, while
\(k_t=a^3S-w_t t^4/4\).
Exact \(S=w_t t^2/(2a)\) and \(t=3a/2\) give
\(k_t/Q_t=-a/4\).
The evaluations of \(k\) at \(a_1,e,t\)
therefore establish its global identity
\[
 \boldsymbol k=(a^2/2)\boldsymbol U_a+a^2\boldsymbol U_E
                                      -(a/4)\boldsymbol Q .
\]

If the second \(a\)-copy occurs after \(t\) but before
the next live singleton, evaluate this identity there.
Let \(S,T\) be its weighted scalar taps from the first
\(a\) and from \(t\), respectively. The endpoint modes
have zero scalar positive-ordinate powers; every
intervening zero-score singleton is zero in all \(D_3\).
The target \(a\)-copy has \(U_E=0\), and the tracked
first \(a\)-copy gives \(Q=a^2 S/2\) from the global
\(h=0\) at \(a\).
The two actual \(g,k\) identities give
\[
 \tfrac{a^2}3 S+t(t-2a/3)T=0,\qquad
 \tfrac{5a^3}8 S+t(t^2-a^2/2)T=0 .
\]
At \(t=3a/2\) these reduce to
\(S/3+5T/4=0,\ 5S+21T=0\).
Their determinant is nonzero in a paid field,
so \(S=T=0\). In particular this new \(a\)-copy
also satisfies \(U_a=-a^2 w/2\).
If the second \(a\)-copy was already before \(t\),
it was tracked by the zero-total rule; if after the
next singleton, only the first is needed.

Now at the next live singleton \(x\), all available
\(a\)-copies track. Such a singleton exists from the
scalar support bound. Let \(S,T\) be its total scalar
\(a,t\) taps. Since \(\boldsymbol U_a,\boldsymbol U_E\)
target values vanish, \(K\boldsymbol u=0\) gives
\(Q_x=a^2 S/2\), and \(k_x=-a^3 S/8\).
The exact two scalar primitives and the corrected
third one therefore are
\[
 aS+tT=w_x x^2/2,\qquad a^2S+t^2T=w_x x^3/3,\qquad
 (9/8)a^3S+t^3T=w_x x^4/4.
\]
They give the same two-label corrected cubic square
as the fourfold packet. For instance multiply the
first by \(4t^2\), the second by \(-12t\), and the
third by \(8\). The left side is
\(a(2t-3a)^2 S=0\); the right is
\(2w_x x^2(x-t)^2\ne0\). Contradiction.
This completes the last triple-endpoint case.

## Result across endpoint multiplicities

For the actual five-opening reverse budget, all nonzero
high forward clocks, exactly ten high physical rows and
a reverse-first endpoint \(1\), **every** rational
multiplicity/position of endpoint \(1\) is now excluded:
the [unique endpoint census](../erk10-five-opening-nine-label-one-pair/TEN-DISTINCT.md#complete-rational-exclusion-of-this-decoder-architecture),
the [exactly-two endpoint census](../erk10-five-opening-seven-label-endpoint-repeat/README.md#combined-exactly-two-conclusion),
and the theorem above. Still open are zero high forward
clocks, endpoint not first in reverse, an absent endpoint,
and other decoder architectures. The unrestricted
rational ERK10 interval is unchanged.
