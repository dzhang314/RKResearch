# The two surviving one-active leakage heads in the five-opening cut

Keep the exact word budget, eight distinct nonzero high clocks,
unique reverse-first endpoint \(1\), two physical pairs, and
nonzero individual and grouped scores of the
[previous two-pair obstruction](../erk10-five-opening-two-pair-obstruction/README.md).
Only one pair carries the grade-two first-primitive defect
\(\boldsymbol u\), and some grade-three defect leaks in aggregate.
This packet classifies the first four interior reverse **physical
rows** in that remaining case. It does not exclude a 15-stage
upper bound; one surviving shape [gives the 17-adic witness](../erk10-fifteen-17-power/README.md).
All head arguments use a field with \(10!\ne0\). The final
symmetric radii exclusion is specifically rational.

Reverse ordinates are \(y=1-x\), so the first reverse row is
\(y=0\). Write \(a\) for the active pair, and identify each
singleton or pair by its scalar \(y\)-label. The surviving heads
of length five **including** 0 are:
\[
 (0,a,a,b,b),\quad b=2a,\qquad\hbox{or}\qquad
 (0,a,b,c,c),\quad c=2b,\quad a\hbox{ still open}.
\]
The repeated pair other than \(a\) is dormant for the
\(\boldsymbol u\) defect but must have genuinely different
normalized \(\boldsymbol h=L\boldsymbol u\) entries in its
two rows. The heads are necessary only, not sufficient.

## Actual grade-four observer space and the dormant contrast

The earlier rank bound is three for reverse defects through
grade four. A grade-three aggregate leakage and its clock
mark have independent aggregates by the seven-site cofactor
line. Therefore the kernel of aggregation inside this defect
space is exactly the one-dimensional opposed
\(\boldsymbol w\odot\boldsymbol u\) mode. In fact
\(\boldsymbol h=L\boldsymbol u\) has genuine aggregate leakage:
otherwise it would be a scalar multiple of \(\boldsymbol u\),
and its first active-row value zero forces it to vanish
identically. But at the next reverse row, whether it is the
second \(a\) or the first different label, the paid raw first
primitive forces a nonzero read from the first \(a\),
giving \(h\ne0\).

It follows that the entire grade-four defect space is spanned
by the weighted versions of
\(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol y\odot\boldsymbol h\).
The raw second primitive defect \(\boldsymbol g\) has
grade three, so its aggregate is a multiple of the grade-three
cofactor. The first \(a\)-row gives \(g=-a^3/3,\ u=-a^2/2,\ h=0\),
hence
\[
 g=(2a/3)u+\beta h,\qquad
 k=(a^2/2)u+\phi(y)h,\qquad Lh=\psi(y)h .
\]
Here \(k=Ly^3-y^4/4\), and \(\phi,\psi\) are linear
polynomials. The \(k\) coefficient and absence of \(u\) in
\(Lh\) follow from the same first \(a\)-row.
These are actual rowwise identities, even when the raw row sums
are not individually exact.

If a completed dormant pair in this initial reverse head
has equal \(h\)-entries, the actual grade-four observer
rows (weighted clock powers through degree four and these
three defects) see its two coordinates only by their weighted
sum. Thus their two-dimensional annihilator contains a
nonzero contrast supported on that pair.
The projection of that annihilator away from the pair
has dimension at most one. At least two distinct nonzero
single-copy labels precede both pair members in **forward**
causal order: here the dormant pair completes by the
fifth reverse physical row, and at most the second active
copy remains besides the later singletons.
The forward residuals \(BEF-EJF\) for \(F=X,\ldots,X^4\)
have rank at least two on the first two such forward rows,
by the same finite difference proof as before.
Since those rows are the actual first two forward high
rows, the one-dimensional projected annihilator is
impossible. This is a contrast obstruction, not an
assumption that the two actual incoming rows were equal.

## Case 1: the active pair closes immediately

Write its first share relative to the total as \(\lambda\).
The second \(a\) reads the first by \(a/(2(1-\lambda))\),
as follows from the true opposed \(\boldsymbol u\) sum.
The first two physical \(h\)-values are
\(0,-a^3/(4(1-\lambda))\). Direct use of the two moments
gives \(\beta=-2/3,\ \phi(a)=-a,\ \psi(a)=0\).
At the first different \(b\), the raw \(u=0\).
With only \(a\) available as nonzero clock,
\[
 h_b=b^2(2b-3a)/4 .
\]
It cannot be zero: at \(2b=3a\) the actual cubic defect
\(a^2b^2/2-b^4/4\) would be nonzero, incompatible with
\(k=\phi(b)h_b\). Reading the two earlier \(a\)-copies
by their true first/composed moments then gives
\[
 \phi(y)=-a-\frac{(b-a)(y-a)}{2b-3a},\qquad
 \psi(y)=-\frac{a(y-a)}{2b-3a}.
\]
On the four preceding physical donors the actual \(h\) is
\[
                    -abY/2+(2b-a)Y^2/4-au/2 .
\]
For the next row \(z\), the earlier cubic powers obey
\(Y^3=(a+b)Y^2-abY\).
Substitute both identities into the paid cubic and composed
reads. Removing their harmless row factor \(z^2\) on the
scalar right sides, the two scalar equations for \(h_z\)
have determinant
\[
                     \frac{a(a-z)(b-z)^2}{4(3a-2b)} .
\]
Thus \(z=b\), since \(a\) is already used twice.
The two coefficients at \(b\) are nonzero multiples of
\(2a-b\), with respective proportionality factors
\(b/(3(3a-2b))\) and twice that.
If \(b\ne2a\), the two \(h\)-values at \(b\) are forced
equal (the earlier \(b\)-row also satisfies these same
identities on its smaller predecessor support).
The contrast rules this out, giving precisely the first
head.

## Case 2: a different nonzero clock comes before closure

The first \(b\) has \(u=0\) and reads the first \(a\)
by \(b^2/(2a)\). Thus
\[
 h_b=-ab^2/4,\qquad
 \beta=(4b-6a)/(3a),\qquad
 \phi(b)=b^2/a-2a,\quad \psi(b)=0.
\]
If the next row closes \(a\), its total \(h\)-mean over the
active pair is \(-a^3(3b-2a)/(4b)\) by the first/second
paid reads. It is nonzero because the aggregate is
the nonzero cofactor line. Directly, the taps of the second
\(a\) on the first \(a\) and on \(b\) are
\[
 \frac{a(3b-2a)}{2b(1-\lambda)},\qquad
 \frac{a^2(a-b)}{b^2(1-\lambda)} .
\]
These, with the first two histories, give
\(\phi(a)=(a+b)\beta-b(a-2b)/(3(3b-2a))\) and
\(\psi(a)=b(a-b)/(3b-2a)\).
On the first four donors the exact \(h\) input equals
\(-a^2Y/2+a(2a-b)Y^2/(4b)-a(3b-2a)u/(2b)\).
The next row's two-read determinant is now
\(-b(a-z)^2(b-z)/(4(2a-3b))\), forcing \(z=b\).
At \(b\) its two coefficients are
\(b(2a-b)/(3a)\), \((a+b)/3\); they cannot both be zero
in the paid field. The \(h\)-entries coincide and the
contrast contradicts order.

If instead the next row immediately repeats \(b\), while
\(a\) remains open, all earlier nonzero raw \(u\) is in
the single first \(a\). Solving the actual first, second
and composed primitive reads on \(0,a,b\) gives
\[
                         h_z=\frac{a z^2(2z-3b)}{4b}.
\]
At a repeat \(z=b\) this is again \(h_b\), so the
contrast rules out this repeat. The only possibility now
is a third distinct internal ordinate \(c\) while
\(a\) stays open. The same formula gives \(h_c\).
It is nonzero because the actual \(Lh\) there is
\(-a c^2(c-b)/4\ne0\), using the \(b\)-tap
\(c^2(c-b)/b^2\). Thus \(3b-2c\ne0\).
Its actual cubic value fixes
\[
 \phi(c)=\frac{-c^4/4+(a+b)c^3/3-ab c^2/2}{h_c}
                                      +(a+b)\beta ,
 \qquad \psi(y)=-\frac{b(y-b)}{2c-3b}.
\]

At the next row \(z\), if this closes \(a\), use the
weighted mean on its two actual copies. For any other
possible row \(u=0\) itself. In either case the mean
first, second and cubic reads have zero \(u\)-term, with
predecessors at \(0,a,b,c\) only (the first \(a\) of a
just-completed group just uses zero). On those
predecessors the sole \(u\) input at \(a\) is
\(-aY(Y-b)(Y-c)/(2(a-b)(a-c))\); the actual \(h\)
input is
\[
 -acY/2+a(2c-b)Y^2/(4b)
                     +[-c+a(2c-b)/(2b)]u .
\]
These are exact at the actual four input coordinates.
The composed and cubic compatibility determinant is
\[
       -\frac{ab(z-b)(z-c)^2}{8(a-b)(a-c)(3b-2c)} .
\]
It forces \(z=b\) or \(z=c\), not a closure of \(a\)
or a new singleton. At \(z=b\) the two coefficients are
\(b(2c-b)/(6(a-b)(a-c))\) and \((b+c)/3\), not both
zero; equal \(h\) is again impossible. At \(z=c\) both
coefficients contain \(2b-c\) and are nonzero if
\(c\ne2b\). Thus only the second listed head remains.

## One exact rational symmetric subcase

For the first head in a reflection-symmetric scalar mesh,
take \(a=1/3,\ b=2/3\); suppose the next new singleton
is \(c=1/2\). The other four scalar ordinates are two
reflected pairs. Let \(U,V\) be their squared rational
radii in the centered coordinate \(2Y-1\).
The next true cubic read in the
[exact lift construction](../erk10-fifteen-17-power/README.md)
requires \(c(c-b)\mu_c+a^2\mu_a=0\).
Here it becomes \(3\mu_c-4\mu_a=0\), whose exact
numerator in the centered coordinate is
\(7UV-2U-2V+1\).
For rational radii \(u/v,w/z\), clear their primitive
denominators. The numerator
\[
            7u^2w^2-2u^2z^2-2v^2w^2+v^2z^2
\]
cannot be zero: each numerator/denominator pair is not
both even. The portable Lean modulo-eight receipt proves
this for arbitrary primitive pairs of integers.
This still leaves noncenter singleton placements and
nonsymmetric rational meshes.
