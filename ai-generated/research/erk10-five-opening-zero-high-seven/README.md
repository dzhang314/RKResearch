# The seven-visible-label zero-clock exits

**Theorem.** In the
[literal five-opening decoder](../erk10-five-opening-nonsplit-kernel/README.md),
with ten physical high rows, the actual order-ten short reverse
word budget, and only the opening origin optionally scored,
suppose \(x=1\) is the first physical reverse row. Then no
rational continuation exists, **including arbitrarily many
physical high forward clocks \(x=0\)**. This concerns the stated
decoder/purity and first-reverse-endpoint architecture only;
it is not an unrestricted rational ERK10 bound.

The new cases below treat seven distinct nonzero high forward
labels with one or two high zeros. All new cases are excluded
over every field with \(10!\ne0\), except the final terminal
nonic for one case with a dormant interior pair; in that case
the scalar exit is rational. Cases with no high zero are in
the [nonzero-high endpoint census](../erk10-five-opening-three-endpoint-repeats/README.md#result-across-endpoint-multiplicities);
[eight and nine visible labels and three high zeros](../erk10-five-opening-zero-high-repeats/README.md)
are already covered when there is a high zero. With at most
six distinct nonzero high clocks the
[two-endpoint rational scalar floor](../rational-endpoint-moment-nine/README.md)
applies to their union with the scalar origin. The origin is
one scalar site even when it has many high physical copies.

## Count and notation

Keep the raw observers \(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol g,
\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol k\) of grades \(2,3,3,4\), respectively,
from the [largest zero-clock packet](../erk10-five-opening-zero-high-largest/README.md).
Thus \(K=B^T\) is strictly causal in reverse, \(y=1-x\),
and \(\boldsymbol h=K\boldsymbol u\). Let \(m\) be the number
of distinct **nonzero forward** labels; \(r\) the number of
high zeros; and \(e\) the number of additional endpoint
copies after the first. For \(m=7\), \(r\ge1\), the extra
ordinary visible multiplicity is \(3-r-e\ge0\).
The cases with \(r=3\) have already been proved.
Throughout this note \(m=7\).

Aggregate only on the visible high labels, and impose zero
coordinate at the first reverse endpoint for positive defect
grades. Let \(S\) be the pure kernel under these restrictions.
Then \(D_2\subseteq S\), by the seven positive forward moments.
If \(e=0\) the endpoint can be removed and \(D_3\subseteq S\)
by the six remaining positive moments. If \(e>0\), every
grade-three visible aggregate is on at most one cofactor
line on all seven visible labels. A nonzero such cofactor
is nonzero at every visible label, and its first mark is
an independent aggregate at grade four. The true rank
bound \(\dim D_4\le3\) is unaffected by high zeros. At
least five visible pools are live by shifted Hilbert \(5\);
at least four of those are visible interiors. A pure vector
is allowed arbitrary separate entries at the high zeros.
These entries are not an opposed pair unless a true extra
equation says so.

## Unique endpoint: permanent one-coordinate donors and a full two-coordinate invisible group

In this part the endpoint is unique, and \(D_3\subseteq S\)
has the same three-coordinate support space used in the
old complete seven-visible-label proofs. **No virtual rows
are inserted in the tableau.** These are adaptations of
their low donor-column comparisons only. A live visible
singleton or a live-mass visible repeated group's last
copy is an ordinary target. The invisible ordinate is
never an ordinary target; its scores are arbitrary.
There are at least four distinct ordinary target labels,
so the first two exist and the early suffix witnesses
use actual nonzero forward clocks.

### One high zero and two visible pairs

Here \(S\) is the direct sum of the two ordinary opposite
pair modes and the unrestricted single ghost coordinate.
Take the
[three-pair argument for arbitrary scores](../erk10-five-opening-three-pair-complete/ALL-PAID-FIELDS.md).
Its effective opener coordinates are the first copy of
each ordinary pair and the ghost's single physical
coordinate. This is a linear isomorphism on \(S\).
At each ordinary pair before live completion only its
first copy is available, or its two available scores
and defect entries are opposite if its total is zero.
The ghost remains a single unrestricted coordinate
at every time. Check the three uses of the opener
count and completion in that proof:

* With no grade-four leakage, the first two targets
  compare the same old opener columns, allowing up to
  one new scalar tap at the first target's own label.
  If the first target is a pair, its \(S\)-mode
  contribution is killed by taking a vector with that
  mode zero; the other two columns have the same
  nonzero cubic interpolation determinant. An absent
  ghost closure is not used. If the first target is a
  singleton, the first three effective coordinates
  still identify \(S\), and the rank-two/proportionality
  square is unchanged.
* In leakage before every opener, use the next
  ordinary target or still-unopened first live
  coordinate, whichever is earlier. If that coordinate
  is the ghost it is tested as an actual individual
  clean row (not an aggregate). There can be no
  untracked later input at its label because it has
  only that one copy. Old modes continue their
  required exact tracking, including any visible
  zero-mass fold.
* In leakage after every opener, a live clean reference
  requires the first two subsequent ordinary targets
  to be its own label. If the reference is the ghost,
  even the first completion cannot have that label.
  If an opener is blind instead, two distinct visible
  ordinary completions are still later in reverse,
  preserving both actual forward residual witnesses
  off the blind coordinate. The raw source/read
  proof of blindness requires only the old modes on
  available columns and the \(y=0\) first endpoint,
  not a ghost completion.

Thus the old paid proof closes this chart with no ghost
score restriction or fictitious opposite-sign partner.

### One high zero and a visible triple

Here \(S\) is the two-dimensional sum-zero space of the
visible triple \(a\), plus the unrestricted ghost coordinate
\(b\). Use the
[triple-and-pair comparisons](../erk10-five-opening-triple-pair/README.md),
including the
[zero openings](../erk10-five-opening-triple-pair/ZERO-OPENINGS.md)
and [zero triple total](../erk10-five-opening-triple-pair/ZERO-TRIPLE-POOL.md).
Interpret the old pair label \(b\) as a one-coordinate
group that never closes. Its mode has a nonzero first
coordinate whenever the mode is nonzero, and its scalar
tap always tracks that mode after one normalization
(or its physical score is zero and the scalar tap is
zero). The only third copy whose availability and
tracking matter is at the genuine triple \(a\).
In the no-leakage proof a first ordinary closure can
be only \(a\); other donor label \(b\) is already
available if there are two scalar labels (one is
insufficient by cubic precision). If the first
ordinary row is a singleton, all no-leakage
coordinate/projection arguments for the triple use
its actual three physical entries; no second \(b\)
coordinate is needed. In leakage the local
\(a_1,a_2,b_1\) computations, including zero
\(b_1\) score, use only the single \(b\)-tap and its
mode; the virtual clean comparison if \(a_2\) is pending
remains an ordinary linear combination of two actual
row evaluations. When \(a\)'s total is zero, the
third tracking equality uses the true sum-zero of
\(a\)'s three entries. When nonzero, its closure
counts as ordinary and only opens a scalar input
at the previous target's label. The two exceptional
cubic payments force the same \(a\) as both distinct
ordinary targets (or force it at the first target
if its total is zero). This is the same paid exit
with no use of a second \(b\)-copy or its score sum.

### Two high zeros and one visible pair

Here let \(a=1\) be the doubled invisible ordinate, \(b\)
the visible pair. \(S_a\) consists of **both** unrestricted
physical \(a\) coordinates, dimension two; \(S_b\) is the
opposed pair. The
[zero-triple-total branch](../erk10-five-opening-triple-pair/ZERO-TRIPLE-POOL.md)
applies with a missing \(a_3\), but not by assuming
\(w_{a_1}+w_{a_2}=0\). Here is the exact replacement.

If \(D_4\subseteq S\), the first ordinary target cannot
pay the first three primitives with fewer than two
nonzero scalar donor labels. If it is \(b\), the cubic
primitive of \(Y(Y-a)(Y-b)\) forces \(b=2a\) and the
same polynomial has a double nonzero root at \(b\).
There is no new scalar donor label before the second
ordinary target, so its distinct ordinate is impossible.
If the first ordinary target \(z\) is a singleton, there
are live scalar donors at \(a,b\), and \(\dim D_3\ge2\):
rank one would confine \(\boldsymbol u\) and its mark
to the first live ordinate, make \(\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h\)
proportional to it, and so fail the first live different
ordinate's scalar and \(K\boldsymbol u\) payment.
Before that different ordinate \(p\) is the only old
live scalar label and \(g=p u+p^3w/6\) there, giving
the required incompatible proportionality.

Project \(D_3\) onto the two actual \(a\) coordinates.
If its rank is below two, a nonzero kernel vector
is supported on the \(b\)-mode. Its \(K\)-mean kills
the necessary scalar \(b\)-tap at \(z\) (the first
score there is nonzero), contradiction. If the
projection has rank two, **the actual arbitrary
two-coordinate \(a\) score vector** has a preimage
in \(D_3\). No zero-total relation is needed for this.
The \(K\)-means of that preimage on the first two
ordinary targets yield \(A+\rho B=0\) with the
same constant at both, where \(A,B\) are the raw
scalar taps into \(a,b\). At \(b\), first copy
availability or the true zero-total opposite fold
justifies the fixed ratio. Thus the old scalar taps
at the two ordinary rows are proportional, even
if a new \(a_2\) becomes available, since the
preimage matched its score as well. Only the first
singleton label \(z\) can add a scalar input.
The three exact primitive conditions give the usual
\(x^2(x-z)^2=0\), impossible.

If \(D_4\) leaks, the single-label support exit uses
the same strict cyclic argument and the second live
clean label, ignoring any \((D_3,w)\)-zero sources.
Otherwise \(D_3=D_4\cap S\) is two-dimensional and
mark invariant, hence consists of one mode at
each \(a,b\). The old \(a_1,a_2,b_1\) tracking and
exceptional computations still apply: they use at
most these two actual \(a\)-coordinates, and at \(b\)
the first or canonically folded mode. If an ordinary
target occurs before the other label opens, the
exact \(Y,Y^2,K\boldsymbol u\) one-donor read forces
two independent restrictions at the older label;
any support at the unopened label would give a
third pure direction by marking, so this was the
single-label case. In the two-mode tracking case
there is no \(a_3\) to track. If \(a_2\) is pending
after the calibration row, the same virtual-row
comparison of \(a_2\) and \(a_1\) forces it to match;
if the first \(a\) is unscored its nonzero mode
pivot follows from the necessary cancellation of
the sole live \(b\)-tap in \(K\boldsymbol u\), just
as in the zero-opening case. Once all available
\(a\)-scores track, only the label of the preceding
ordinary target may change the scaled taps; the
same two-target square closes the case.

An exceptional nontracking \(a_2\) before the first
ordinary target gives the old exact relation
\(\operatorname{mean}_z(\boldsymbol k)=b(a-b)(a-2b)B(z)/2\).
It is obtained solely on the first two \(a\) copies
and the first/folded \(b\) source; its residual
against the first two primitive reads is
\(-z^2(z-a)^2/4\). Thus the first ordinary point
would have to be \(a\), impossible because the
invisible group is never a target. The proof is
uniform in all four physical scores.

## Repeated endpoint: common early leakage calculation

With \(e>0\), the grade-three visible aggregate space is a
cofactor line; if it leaks, \(D_4\cap S\) is the line spanned
by the nonzero \(\boldsymbol u\). It is nonzero at the first
live nonendpoint (the ordinary Hilbert support guarantees
such a row). Its first mark confines it to one reverse
label \(a\ne0\); endpoint support cannot coexist with this
nonzero first-live entry. The active label is thus an
invisible ordinate or an ordinary repeated label.
As in the [previous repeats case](../erk10-five-opening-zero-high-repeats/README.md#if-grade-three-leaks),
\(\boldsymbol h=K\boldsymbol u\) must leak: if pure
it is proportional to \(\boldsymbol u\), zero at
the earliest live nonendpoint, and the resulting strict
single-label nilpotent calculation fails at the next
live different label. Previously occurring endpoints
with \(Y=0\) carry no scalar \(Y\)-input and no
\(\boldsymbol u\) input in this branch.
Therefore \(D_4=\langle\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol h,
\boldsymbol y\odot\boldsymbol h\rangle\), with
\(\boldsymbol g=(2a/3)\boldsymbol u+\beta\boldsymbol h\)
and affine \(\boldsymbol k-(a^2/2)\boldsymbol u\)
and \(K\boldsymbol h\) multipliers of \(\boldsymbol h\).
The first nonendpoint must be the live \(a\)-opener;
an earlier ordinary row without \(\boldsymbol u\) support
would be blind. An additional early endpoint with
\(h=0\) is a nonzero contrast against the first endpoint,
since both have zero \(\boldsymbol u\) and only their
scores on nominal columns. The untouched reverse suffix
still contains two distinct visible labels in each use
here. No physical score must be inverted for a contrast.

If an added endpoint comes before the first distinct
nonendpoint \(b\), then \(h=0\). With at most one
prior \(a\), the first primitive kills its tap into that
live donor. With two \(a\) copies, absence of an earlier
contrast forces their first scalar tap nonzero and
hence \(\beta=-2/3\) as in the raw active-pair head.
Only \(a\)-donors contribute positively to scalar
primitives, so \(u=g=0\) at that endpoint; this gives
\(h=0\). If an added endpoint comes after the prefix
\(0,a,b\), the first two raw reads give
\(a A+b B=0,\ 2abA+3b^2B=0\) at ordinate zero,
forcing its \(h=0\) as well.
For the respective later prefixes \(0,a,a,b\),
\(0,a,b,a\), and \(0,a,b,c\) the exact new-row
determinants from the
[active-label comparisons](../erk10-five-opening-eight-label-triple/README.md#scalar-two-read-comparisons)
are nonzero at target \(z=0\) (their factors are guarded
\(a,b,c,3a-2b,2a-3b,3b-2c\) in the applicable case).
They also force \(h=0\). This checks all early endpoint
positions that occur before the standard rank-three
active/dormant head has been established.

### Two high zeros and a duplicated endpoint

All visible interiors are singletons and \(S\) consists
only of the two copies at the invisible ordinate.
If \(D_3\) is pure the single-label nilpotent and
feedback argument applies; both endpoints have zero
\(D_3\) coordinates and all positive scalar inputs
at them vanish. Otherwise the active label is the
two-copy ghost. The above early endpoint calculation
excludes the added endpoint through the candidate head.
The old active-pair calculation otherwise demands a
repeat at \(b\) or \(c\); neither physical singleton
can repeat. This is a paid exclusion.

### One high zero and a tripled endpoint

All six visible interiors are singletons. If grade three
leaks the active label is the sole ghost; the early
endpoint calculation forbids the first additional endpoint
until the impossible fourth distinct nonendpoint.
This leaves \(D_3\subseteq S\). Here \(S\) consists of
the sole ghost coordinate and the direction on the two
added endpoints with their entries opposed (first endpoint
zero). The first live visible interior row \(t\) has exact
\(\boldsymbol u,\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h\) coordinates
zero. To pay its first two primitives it needs the earlier
live ghost at \(a=1\) as its sole nonzero-ordinate scalar
donor, and \(t=3a/2\). The ghost's
\(u_a=-a^2w_a/2\); before it any endpoint \(u\) entry
is zero because no nonzero-ordinate score has appeared.
Since \(h_t=0\), there must also be an earlier nonzero
endpoint \(u\)-coordinate to cancel the read of \(u_a\).
Decompose \(\boldsymbol u=\boldsymbol U_a+\boldsymbol U_E\)
into its ghost and endpoint directions, both present
in \(D_3\) by the clock mark. Use the earliest nonzero
endpoint entry \(E\). Its only earlier positive scalar
donor is the ghost, so \(g_E=a u_E,\ h_E=-a u_E/2,\
k_E=a^2 u_E\). At the ghost
\(g=(2a/3)u,\ h=0,\ k=(a^2/2)u\). In particular
\[
 \boldsymbol h=-a\boldsymbol U_E/2,\qquad
 \boldsymbol Q=K\boldsymbol U_E,\qquad
 \boldsymbol k=(a^2/2)\boldsymbol U_a+a^2\boldsymbol U_E-a\boldsymbol Q/4 .
\]
The last identity
follows by comparing the three coordinates at \(a,E,t\):
\(\boldsymbol Q\) is zero at \(a,E\), and at \(t\) is
\(a^2 S_t/2\ne0\) with \(S_t\) the weighted ghost tap.
The actual \(k_t=a^3 S_t-w_t t^4/4=-a Q_t/4\),
using \(a S_t=w_t t^2/2\), \(t=3a/2\).
These three coordinates determine all \(D_4\), since
\(\boldsymbol U_a,\boldsymbol U_E,\boldsymbol Q\)
are independent and rank is at most three.

At the next live visible interior \(x\), there is no
additional ghost copy. The only positive scalar donor
labels are \(a,t\); call their taps \(S,T\).
From \(h_x=0\), \(Q_x=a^2 S/2\), giving
\(k_x=-a^3 S/8\). Thus its exact first two reads and
corrected cubic give
\[
 2(aS+tT)=w_x x^2,\quad
 3(a^2S+t^2T)=w_x x^3,\quad
 9a^3S+8t^3T=2w_x x^4 .
\]
The paid fraction-free square \(2w_x x^2(x-t)^2=0\)
is impossible. This works with any position/score of
the added endpoint rows.

### One high zero, a duplicated endpoint, and an ordinary pair

If \(D_3\) is pure, \(S\) consists of the ghost coordinate
and the visible opposite pair. Both endpoint \(D_3\)
coordinates are zero. The two-mode rank and tracked
two-target square from the
[eight-visible repeats proof](../erk10-five-opening-zero-high-repeats/README.md#if-grade-three-is-entirely-pure)
therefore apply, even with the added endpoint (zero
positive scalar and \(D_3\) inputs).
If \(D_3\) leaks, consider the active label. If the active
label is the ordinary pair, the early endpoint calculations
and the single-active-pair local heads are incompatible
with the rest of the nonendpoint rows being singletons.
If the active label is the sole ghost \(a=1\), an early
added endpoint is again forbidden. As in the previous
eight-visible ghost-active/dormant-pair case, the only
remaining head is
\(0,a,p,q,q\), \(q=2p\), with visible dormant \(q\).
The second endpoint cannot be row six: the next
cubic coefficient is \(-p(Y-q)/a\), nonzero at
\(Y=0\), and its scalar right side has \(Y^2\).
This would give \(h=0\) and an early endpoint contrast.

So let \(c\ne0\) be row six, and \(s,t,u,v\) be the
last four physical labels, exactly one equal to zero.
The same raw \(\boldsymbol H=(p/a)\boldsymbol h\)
has the \(p,c\) anchors, and the same corrected
\(\boldsymbol G_*,\boldsymbol Z_*\) vanish through row
six as in the
[ghost-active terminal calculation](../erk10-five-opening-zero-high-repeats/README.md#if-grade-three-leaks).
The extra endpoint does not affect their construction.
Let \(\nu=(Y-1)(Y-t)(Y-u)(Y-v)\).
The two grade-five blind primitive integrals
\(I(\nu JG)=I(\nu JM)=0\) give \(I(\nu(Y-c)M)=0\).
The correct nodal polynomial is
\[
                 P_8=\nu(Y-s)G/Y .
\]
It contains every scalar site exactly once: \(Y=1\)
is the ghost and opening origin together; the extra
tail endpoint at \(Y=0\) cancels one apparent factor
\(Y\), leaving the true endpoint root. For its
cardinal numerators \(\mu_i=I(P_8/(Y-i))\), the
grade-three seven-site visible cofactor now includes
the endpoint and is \(H_i=-L/P_8'(i)\), with
**no** extra factor of \(i\). The early anchors
give \(D(p)=D(c)=-4L\ne0\) for
\(D(i)=\mu_i i^2(i-q)\).
Exactly as in the
[seven-visible terminal collapse](../erk10-five-opening-seven-label-endpoint-repeat/README.md#the-same-terminal-collapse-with-the-endpoint-in-the-cofactor),
\(I(\nu(Y-s)M)=0\) by the two anchor contributions,
so \(I(\nu M)=I(\nu YM)=0\). If \(s=0\), the missing
zero factor instead gives \(I(\nu M)=-D(p)/(pc)\ne0\),
a paid contradiction. If \(s\ne0\), the three-site
nodal cofactor gives \(D(s)=-4L\), hence \(Z_*(s)=0\)
at its actual singleton (no score division). Then
\(U=(Y-1)(Y-u)(Y-v)\) is the further blind test
\(I(U JM)=0\), and \(I(YU JM)=0\) follows from \(\nu\).
These four scalar moments give the same verified
[projectively rootless rational nonic](../erk10-fifteen-two-pair-rational-closure/README.md#the-unconditional-collapse-and-rational-exclusion).
It uses distinct \(p,q,c,s\) and nonzero \(p,c\) as
proved, not a positive or nonzero tail weight.
This closes the last rational case.

## Reproduce and scope

Run `python audit.py` with SymPy and compare to `exact-audit.txt`.
The [portable scalar module](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/ERK10ZeroHighSevenScalars.lean)
checks the homogeneous early endpoint solve, the two
cleared cubic coefficients and the corrected final square.
The whole field/rational theorem is the mathematical
word-budget/chronology proof, not a kernel theorem on
the literal full order-ten tableau. It excludes every
reverse-first endpoint placement in this decoder; an
endpoint later in reverse, an absent endpoint, or other
opening/cross profiles remain separate.
