# Sixteen-stage ERK10 over clean rings and their algebras

The [five literal rational tableaux](README.md) give a single exact
sixteen-stage tableau over **every nonzero commutative clean ring**
inverting \(10!\), and over every nonzero algebra over such a ring.
A ring is clean if every element is the sum of a unit and an
idempotent. This includes all commutative rings of Krull dimension
zero, without a finiteness or characteristic assumption. This is a
broader domain for the **upper bound 16**, not a change to the real
14–15 stage interval or the factorial-localization question.

## Select locally, then use idempotents to obtain one literal tableau

Write \(D_i>0\) for the common coefficient denominators of the five
literal tableaux. Their integer gcd is \(816480000\), and
\((10!)^2=16128\cdot816480000\). Thus they generate the unit ideal in
any coefficient ring \(R\) inverting \(10!\).

In any commutative clean ring, a finite list \(D_1,\ldots,D_m\) that
generates the unit ideal admits a finite idempotent partition on which
one list entry at a time is a unit. Indeed write \(1=\sum r_iD_i\)
and use cleanness to write \(r_1D_1=u+e\) with \(u\) a unit and
\(e^2=e\). On the factor \((1-e)R\), \(r_1D_1\) is a unit, hence
\(D_1\) is a unit. On the complementary factor \(eR\), the sum of the
remaining terms is \(1-r_1D_1=-u\) in that factor, and is a unit.
Divide by that sum to reapply the argument to the shorter list.
Each factor is clean because it is a unital quotient of \(R\).
The case of one entry is immediate. Zero factors may be discarded.

For each nonzero factor choose its indicated literal tableau and
interpret its coefficients by inverting the associated \(D_i\). The
orthogonal idempotents summing to one identify \(R\) with the product
of these factors. The sixteen physical row slots are the same in
each tableau; take the product entry by entry. Strict triangularity
and every rooted condition through order ten are polynomial
identities, so they hold in the product and hence in \(R\).

Every chart has the same two order-eleven residual tests, the
ten-leaf bush and the tree with four leaves and one five-leaf-bush
child. After dividing by powers of that chart's unit denominator,
the residuals are rational numbers with coprime integer numerators.
The two residuals generate the unit ideal in each factor: multiply
each by its unit reduced denominator and use the integer Bézout
identity. Taking the product of those Bézout coefficients shows
the same unit-ideal relation for the assembled tableau in \(R\).
In particular the two residuals cannot both vanish after any unital
map to a **nonzero** ring. Thus the pushed-forward tableau still
has exact order ten, not just order at least ten, even if the target
itself is not clean.

## Dimension zero and an explicit mixed-characteristic example

If \(R\) has Krull dimension zero, the quotient by its nilradical is
reduced and every prime remains maximal. It is absolutely flat,
so each principal ideal is generated by an idempotent.
[Stacks Project, Tag 092F](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/092F)
gives this characterization and the principal-ideal property.
Lift the idempotent generating the principal ideal of \(\bar a\)
to an idempotent \(e\) of \(R\), using
[Stacks Project, Tag 00J9](https://stacks.math.columbia.edu/tag/00J9).
On \(eR\), \(a\) is a unit modulo a nil ideal and hence is a unit.
On \((1-e)R\), \(a\) is nilpotent, so \(a-(1-e)\) is a unit there.
Together \(a=(a-(1-e))+(1-e)\) is a clean decomposition in \(R\).

For example, \(R=\mathbb Q\times\mathbb F_{11}\times\mathbb F_{17}\)
has characteristic zero and is not local. None of the original
five denominators is a unit in it, since each is divisible by
11 or 17. The glued tableau nevertheless works: use the first
witness in the rational factor, the 11-unit witness in the second,
and the horizon-four witness in the third. Its coefficients also
work over \(R[T]\), a nonclean algebra, by the residual Bézout
argument. One way to see noncleanness is to project to
\(\mathbb Q[T]\): its only idempotents are 0 and 1 and its only
units are nonzero constants, so \(T\) is not a sum of the two.

## Smaller scalar tests

There is a criterion weaker than global cleanness. In addition to
\(10!\) being a unit, suppose for each \(p=11,13,17,19,229\) there
is an idempotent decomposition with one factor where \(p\) is a
unit and the other where it is in the Jacobson radical. The latter
factors for different primes are disjoint: on any nonzero overlap
two coprime integers would both be in the Jacobson radical.
On the common complementary factor the compact 5/4-horizon
denominator is a unit, because its only unpaid prime factors
are \(11,13,17,19,229\). On the factor for a fixed \(p\), every
integer coprime to \(p\) is a unit by integer Bézout and the
defining property of the Jacobson radical. Use the 11-unit,
13-unit, horizon-four, first and first witnesses in those five
factors, respectively.

A single unglued chart can also suffice on connected rings.
For example, suppose the set of positive maximal-residue
characteristics is contained in \(\{p,q\}\). A chart denominator
is a unit exactly when neither actual residue characteristic
divides it; residue fields of characteristic zero add no
obstruction. To see if the two listed primes block every chart,
retain the denominators coprime to \(p\) and take their gcd.
The prime \(q\) blocks the remaining selection exactly when it
divides this gcd. In particular when \(p=229\) this gcd has only
paid prime factors, so such a pair never blocks selection.

Run `python3 -B clean-ring-replay.py` in this directory to verify
all integer data used here. The exact ring argument above uses
the literal charts' existing rooted checks; it is not yet a
complete native Lean theorem for clean rings. It does not give a
16-stage tableau over \(\mathbb Z[1/10!]\), where the finite
idempotent partition cannot split the domain.
