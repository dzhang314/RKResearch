## 4. The exact final-three scalar lemma and proof

This is the reusable algebraic interface. On a finite totally ordered support of at least three sites, let T₀ be a strictly upper triangular linear operator on class functions. Let y and U be two columns and suppose
(k+1) T₀(U y^(k−1))−U y^k = g_k(y)   (k=1,2,3),
where g₁ is a nonzero constant, g₂ is constant and g₃ is affine. Suppose T₀(1) is affine in y and T₀(y) is polynomial of degree≤2 in y. Assume that no two distinct sites with the same y have the same nonzero U. Then these conditions are impossible over any characteristic-zero field. In the application U=β y/λ just on λ support; all required y powers are allowed, including y=0.

Proof (common scalar calculation, independently audited): at the last support site the k=1 equation forces nonzero clock Y₀ and nonzero U₀. Normalize x=y/Y₀, f=U/U₀ and T=T₀/Y₀. The first two normalized residual polynomials are −1 and the third is affine with value −1 at 1. Also T1=α(x−1) because the last row of T is zero. At the second support site x=s, write f_s for its value. Looking only at the later last site gives
2α(s−1)−s f_s=−1,   3α(s−1)−s² f_s=−1.
At s=1 these force f_s=1, forbidden. At s=0 they are inconsistent. Otherwise they give α=(3−2s)⁻¹, f_s=α/s≠0, and the third equation determines its affine residual exactly as −1−α(s−1)(x−1). The polynomial T(x−1) has degree≤2 and vanishes at both 1,s by strictness and the actual last input value x−1=0.

At the third support site x=v, let p,q be the coefficients of the last and second sites in the corresponding row of T, and ξ its f value. Then
p+q=α(v−1),
2(p+qα/s)−vξ=−1,
3(p+qα)−v²ξ=−1,
4(p+qαs)−v³ξ=−1−α(s−1)(v−1).
Put Δ=3−2s, P=Δ p, Q=q/s, Z=Δ ξ. Eliminating P and vZ gives
(s−1)(2sv−3s−v) Q=(s−v)(v−1),
2(s−1)(2sv²−6s−v²) Q=(s−v)(v−1)(2v+3).
Cross-multiplying and using
2(2sv²−6s−v²)−(2v+3)(2sv−3s−v)=3(v−s)
forces (v−1)(v−s)²=0. There was no division by v. At either repeated clock v∈{1,s}, the polynomial T(x−1) vanishes, while its actual third-row value is q(s−1), so q=0. If v=1, then p=0 and ξ=1; if v=s, then p=α(s−1) and ξ=f_s≠0. Both violate distinct sites with equal clock and equal nonzero normalized mass. This proves the obstruction.

Apply it to section 2 with U=β y/λ. Its steady mass condition from section 3 rules out the repeated values, concluding the remaining 18-class residual in both square cases and both genuine terminal-entry grades. The deduction also applies with terminal absent to grade ten. In particular no assumption that the order-13 terminal pair survives an order-12 merge is involved.
