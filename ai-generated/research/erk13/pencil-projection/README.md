# Two direct projections for the early order-13 pencil

**Scope.** This replaces the arithmetic step of the existing marked width-19, first-escape degree-7 paper proof over an arbitrary characteristic-zero field. No physical row or reviewed `HasOrder 13 A b` premise is changed. The method does not lower the stage count further. The arithmetic below is independently replayable mathematics, not a kernel certificate.

Let `F,j,D,N,A,C,P_z,H` be the integer cubic-gate polynomials of the published early-pencil proof. Here `F(X)=−1+20X−120X²+300X³−330X⁴+132X⁵` and `j(X)=7−168X+1260X²−4200X³+6930X⁴−5544X⁵+1716X⁶`. Write `f_i,j_i` for their coefficients (zero outside range), `D=(5z−2)(7z−6)`, `N=100z⁴−460z³+819z²−672z+210`, and `c_i=(D z^i+z³N)/(z−1)`. All `c_i` are integer polynomials since `D(1)+N(1)=0`. Use

    A(t,z) = Σ_{i=0}^5 f_i c_i t^i,
    C(t,z) = Σ_{i=1}^6 (j_i+7 f_i)c_i t^(i−1),
    P_z(X) = 12z−15z² + (60z²−24z−18)X − 10(5z²+4z−6)X² + 15(4z−3)X³,
    H(t,X) = F(t)j(tX) − j(t)F(tX).

Define `res_v(f,g;m,n)` to be the Sylvester determinant in `v` at the **fixed** degree bounds `m,n`, also at parameter values where leading terms disappear. Its sign convention has `res_X(X−a,g;1,n)=g(a)`. If `m+n>0` this resultant belongs to the ideal `(f,g)`, as follows, for example, by applying the adjugate of the Sylvester matrix to the constant monomial. Thus common zeros always annihilate the specialized fixed determinant, without assumptions on either fiber degree. A nonzero fixed determinant over a field gives a Bezout identity, not just absence of rational roots.

**Cubic port.** Set `R̃=res_t(A,C;5,5)`, `Q=res_X(H,P_z;6,3)`, `T=res_t(A,Q;5,33)`. The integer polynomial `R̃` is a nonzero content times `z^15 R`, where primitive `R` has degree 50, nonzero constant and a leading coefficient nonzero modulo 1009. The modular projected polynomial `T` has degree 258, and `gcd(R,T)=1` modulo 1009. Therefore `R(z)` and `T(z)` cannot vanish together at any point of a characteristic-zero field. At an admitted physical `t,z`, `z≠0` and `A=C=0` force `R=0`. So `T≠0`, hence `Q(t,z)≠0` since `A(t,z)=0`; consequently the actual two polynomials `H(t,X)` and `P_z(X)` are coprime over the field. This is uniform in the whole pencil. No linear subresultant, choice of a recovery denominator, division inside a non-domain quotient, or passage from one enumerated root to an arbitrary field is used.

**Quartic port.** Use the earlier proof's `W` (the quotient of `H` by `(X−1)(X−z)`, divided by `t²`), `M=H(t,z)/((z−1)t)`, and the two primitive constraints. For convenience do not primitive-normalize the inputs: take `A=420∫₀¹ XW(X)dX`, `B=420[F(tz)∫₀¹ X²W(X)dX−F(t)/z·∫₀ᶻ(X²−X)W(X)dX]`. These are integer polynomials even at `z=0`; their construction uses no evaluation-time division. The ascending coefficients of `W` are obtained by descending recurrence `w_i=t^i(j_(i+2)F(t)−f_(i+2)j(t))+(1+z)w_(i+1)−z w_(i+2)` for `i=4,…,0`, with `w_5=w_6=0`. Their bidegree bounds `(t,z)` are `A:(9,4)`, `M:(10,5)`, `B:(14,9)`. Put `R̃=res_t(A,M;9,10)`, `T=res_t(A,B;9,14)`. Here `R̃` is a nonzero scalar times primitive `R` of degree 40. `R` keeps its degree modulo 1009, `T` has degree 84 modulo 1009, and their modular gcd is 1. The same specialization argument forbids `A=M=B=0`.

**Why one prime suffices.** More generally, let `R,T` be integer polynomials, with the leading coefficient of `R` nonzero modulo a prime p and the reductions coprime. Their fixed determinant at the two *integer* degrees stays nonzero modulo p: if the second degree falls by `k`, its adjusted determinant is `lc(R)^k` times the nonzero ordinary resultant. Thus the integer determinant is nonzero and stays so in any characteristic-zero field. This is the only modular transfer here. It is not the generally false claim that lack of Fp-rational points implies lack of characteristic-zero solutions.

**Reproduction.** `python3 check_pencil_projection.py --json projection_data.json` uses the standard library and performs no network or floating-point polynomial arithmetic. It reconstructs the first two determinants over the integers using exact Bareiss elimination at a sufficient integer grid, with integer Newton interpolation. Cubic: `deg_z R̃≤65`, `deg_(t,z) Q≤(33,12)`, `deg_z T≤258`. Quartic: `deg_z R̃≤85`, `deg_z T≤137`. Thus interpolation in characteristic 1009 with enough distinct nodes gives the actual polynomial over the entire algebraic closure, not point testing. Fixed-size determinants are used at every node, including 0 and 1. Standard ascending coefficient arrays for `R`, `T mod 1009`, and the reduced `T mod R` are archived in `projection_data.json`. Final residues `res(R,T mod R)` are 235 and 682 (with unreduced `R` leading coefficient as archived). A separate symbolic SymPy implementation, computing actual polynomial-ring resultants without grid evaluation, agrees on both modular degrees and both gcds. It also gives gcd 1 at the publication's original small primes 43 and 101, respectively.
