# The remaining zero-aggregate cases of the six-opening, two-pair ERK cut

This finishes the fixed six-opening cubic-decoder, two-pair high architecture: it has no fourteen-stage order-ten tableau in characteristic zero, even with arbitrary signed, zero, or opposing output shares. The previous [raw transpose result](../erk149-two-pair-zero-shares/README.md) treated all nonzero high aggregates. The argument here covers the remaining zero aggregates, not a different architecture. The general ERK10 stage interval over the real algebraic numbers is still **14–15**.

The model is the existing literal strict six-row prefix with clocks \(0,e,2p/3,p,q,r\), with weight allowed only at its origin, the cubic decoder \(\Pi\) at the four distinct old donor positions \(0,p,q,r\), and the literal later cross block \((EJ-BE)\Pi\). Exactly eight later rows have the scalar sites \(1\), two distinct interior pairs, and three distinct singleton interiors. All interior sites are distinct from 0 and 1. The last physical stage has nonzero weight and site 1 by the equality ledger, so zero aggregate cannot occur there. No ordered-field assumption enters the argument.

## Exactly one dormant scalar site; the four live interiors are fixed

There must be at least six distinct contributing scalar sites. Indeed if at most five sites contribute, and one is 1, let \(Q\) be the monic product of all contributing factors except the one at 1. The true degree-nine quadrature makes \((1-X)Q(X)\) orthogonal to every polynomial of degree at most four. But \(Q\) has degree at most four and the rational moment matrix for \((1-X)\) on that space has determinant \(1/67212633600000\ne0\). This is a contradiction over every characteristic-zero field. Hence if a high site has zero aggregate, exactly one such site is dormant, and the old origin must contribute along with 1 and the other four high interiors.

Let \(P\) be the monic product over those four interiors. Exactness on \(X(1-X)P(X)\) times every polynomial of degree at most three gives the usual rational four-by-four moment system, whose determinant is \(1/53343360000\). Its unique solution is
\[
                42P(X)=42X^4-84X^3+56X^2-14X+1.
\]
It is invariant under \(X\mapsto1-X\). Therefore in reverse \(y=1-x\) the four contributing interiors also have precisely these four roots.

Two elementary incompatibilities among such roots will be useful:
1. \(2t=3a\) cannot hold when \(a,t\) are contributing interiors.
2. \(6ab-4(a+b)t+3t^2=0\) cannot hold for two distinct contributing donor interiors \(a,b\) and any contributing target interior \(t\), including when \(t=a\) or \(t=b\).

An exact proof uses the prime 227. The quartic has four distinct roots modulo 227, namely \(24,51,177,204\), and the two relations fail on those residues. To lift that finite check, work in the splitting field over \(\mathbb Q\) of \(P\) and in the residue field at a prime above 227. The four roots are integral at 227 because \(42\) is a unit and \(P\) is monic over \(\mathbb Z_{(227)}\). Their reductions lie among the four displayed residues and are distinct, since reducing the monic linear factorization would otherwise give a repeated root. Any characteristic-zero relation would give the forbidden residue relation. This argument applies to the splitting subfield generated by the four live nodes in the original field, even when that field is not ordered.

## Raw reverse equations and which groups are counted

Use the raw physical transpose \(T\), diagonal \(Y\), weight column \(\mathbf w\), and covectors \(\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H,\mathbf K\) from the [zero-individual-share note](../erk149-two-pair-zero-shares/README.md). No inverse of a zero individual weight or zero aggregate is taken. At a contributing group, only divide by its nonzero aggregate. Its normalized row \(P_t\) must have exact \(Y\mathbf w,Y^2\mathbf w\) and kill \(\mathbf U\) whenever the grouped lower defects vanish. If \(\mathbf K\) is paid at that site it must also have exact \(Y^3\mathbf w\). Exact first two scalar moments with only one earlier weighted nonzero clock force \(2t=3a\); exact first three with two earlier distinct weighted nonzero clocks force the forbidden quadratic in item 2 whenever these clocks and the target all lie on the four Lobatto interiors. Exact three scalar moments with just one earlier weighted nonzero site are always impossible (not only for Lobatto clocks).

In the generic branch \(\ell\ne E_r\), the direct source space through size six is \(X\mathcal P_4\), so it separates all five physical interior scalar sites. The true four-letter tests force \(\mathbf K\) paid at all of them. In the point off-mesh branch (\(\ell=E_r\) but \(r\) outside the five interiors) one has the weaker rank-one cap on the grouped \(\mathbf K,T\mathbf v\) for all opposed pair directions, and exact grouped \(\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H\). In the point-collided branch the [anchored contrast](../erk149-two-pair-point-collision/README.md) yields the seven constraints of the [chronology census](../erk149-point-collision-exhaustive/README.md) at every *nonanchor* group. In particular it gives exact three scalar moments and four zero defect reads at each contributing nonanchor group. At the dormant group we may use any paid grouped defects, but never normalize its zero aggregate or count its completion as a contributing completion.

## When the dormant site has no individually weighted row

**Dormant singleton.** Off the point anchor its \(\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H\) coordinates are zero by the grouped equations, and all its \(Y^j\mathbf w\) coordinates are zero. It contributes nothing to the scalar and defect reads in the chronology proofs and may be suppressed *from those reads*. This is not a claim of literal row deletion. If it were the first nonendpoint physical row, its only possible endpoint tap would vanish by \(T\mathbf w=Y\mathbf w\), so literal pruning would already contradict the fourteen-stage floor.

For generic or off-mesh point sources, if the first contributing group has fewer than two weighted earlier interior donors, item 1 of the residue census rules out its exact two scalar moments. Otherwise both first active pair occurrences have already appeared and are weighted. Suppressing the dormant singleton leaves exactly the same first- and second-completion minors as the generic two-pair proof; both contributing groups exist with two live singleton sites remaining. The suppressed singleton's \(\mathbf K\) could be unconstrained in the off-mesh rank-one argument, but those minors compare only the two actual contributing completions and do not use it.

For point collision at a contributing site, suppressing the dormant singleton gives the exact same chronological rules as in the [point and zero-share analyses](../erk149-two-pair-zero-shares/README.md). They use at most two completed nonanchor groups. There are still two actual contributing pairs and two contributing singleton interiors, so the needed groups exist even after exempting the anchor. The bare \(\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H\) row computations in those analyses see no contribution from the suppressed coordinate. For point collision at the dormant singleton itself, it may have nonzero defects, but it never supplies a weighted scalar donor. Before the first contributing group completes there can be at most two distinct weighted earlier interior clocks (the first occurrences of the two pairs); its three exact scalar moments are impossible by item 2 or the one-site test.

**Dormant pair with both physical weights zero.** Until the first contributing group completes in the generic or off-mesh branch, there is only one possible weighted earlier interior: the first occurrence of the other, contributing pair. Its two exact scalar moments are ruled out by item 1. In the point-collided branch, take the first completed contributing *nonanchor* group instead. There can be at most two weighted earlier interior clocks: the first active pair occurrence and possibly the contributing singleton anchor. If the anchor is the active pair or is dormant, there is at most one anchored/active scalar donor. None can pay its three exact scalar moments, by item 2 and the one-site test. This does not assume the two dormant defects vanish individually.

## Opposing nonzero weights at the dormant pair

If the dormant pair \(D,D'\) is not the point anchor, then grouped \(\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H\) all vanish there. Its two physical weights are \(w,-w\), \(w\ne0\). Therefore for each of the three defect coordinates their values divided by their *individual* weights are equal. The shared reverse clock is \(d\). Any receiver's taps \(p,q\), with a tap set to zero if that occurrence is still unavailable, give exactly one effective read
\[
                 p f_D+q f_{D'}=(pw+q(-w))\,\frac{f_D}{w}
                   \qquad(f=\mathbf U,\mathbf G,\mathbf H).
\]
The same factoring holds for \(Y^j\mathbf w\) and \(Y\mathbf U\). Thus, for these equations only, the receiver uses one effective weighted donor at \(d\), available starting with the actual first occurrence \(D\). Its normalized profile is that first physical row's profile. A later \(D'\) can change the total tap, but creates neither a new scalar support nor a new defect axis. This replacement is valid even in the computation of \(\mathbf H=T\mathbf U\) at a later actual row, since the input covector factors before applying its taps. It does not claim that actual forward stages are identical.

### No point collision at a high interior

Here all lower defects have zero aggregate and the generic rank-one cap still holds. The first physical nonendpoint is either the first dormant or first active pair row: it must be live by pruning, and a singleton would have nonzero \(U=-w_i y_i^2/2\). Before the first contributing completion, both the active and dormant pair's first occurrences must be available. Otherwise there is just one effective earlier nonzero donor, whose \(U/w=-y^2/2\) since it was first; exact \(P_t\mathbf U=0\) suppresses even that donor, contradicting the first moment.

If both first pair occurrences are live, the early normalized \(U\) at the first is nonzero and the second's normalized \(G/2+H\) equals \(-y^3/6\) if needed to get the second opposed direction; the earlier scalar action in that sum cancels against \(\mathbf H\) because the first normalized \(U\) was \(-y^2/2\). This computation still holds if \(D'\) has already appeared, by the exact effective-read identity. Thus the raw opposed-space proof of the rank-one cap is unchanged. In the first-two-contributing-completion minors use the actual dormant opposed test \(\mathbf v_D=\mathbf w|_{\{D,D'\}}\), whose response **is** the total scalar tap at \(d\) even after \(D'\). For the active pair its opposed test has first value equal to the first physical weight and its second opposite value. It is read at most on its first occurrence when that test is used. If the first contributing completion is the active pair, the first-completed-pair determinant applies with the dormant site as the other donor. If the first contributing completion is a singleton, the next group's lower reads give the same proportional effective taps to the two pair sites, since the singleton has \(U=0\), and the first-singleton determinant applies. An intervening \(D'\) changes only the total at the same donor clock; it does not invalidate either determinant.

If the first active pair row is a ghost, the first actual nonendpoint is the live dormant row at \(d\). If \(U\) at the active ghost is zero, then \(\mathbf U\) is just \(-d^2/2\) times \(\mathbf v_D\), so any group's \(P_t\mathbf U=0\) forbids its only weighted prior donor at the first completion. If \(U\) at the active ghost is nonzero, the two opposite directions are spanned by \(\mathbf U,Y\mathbf U\). The cap and the earlier early-ghost argument apply with first weighted donor \(d\) and direction \(\mathbf v_D\): the first contributing completion has \(t=3d/2\), and the second's required residual is \(-s^2(2s-3d)^2/16\ne0\). This does not assume the dormant donor lies on the Lobatto mesh.

### Point collision at the dormant pair itself

Do not compress the dormant pair. Both of its physical shares are nonzero, opposite. The previous paired-anchor chronology requires no equation at the anchor and **no division by its aggregate**. With a live first active pair occurrence it applies exactly to the two retained physical anchor rows; at every relevant nonanchor group the raw normalized seven reads are the same. A ghost first active occurrence instead leaves only one weighted donor site before the first contributing group, which cannot pay three moments. The two paired-anchor resonances exhaust the remaining chronology; the next contributing singleton exists if the active pair has completed first.

### Point collision at the contributing pair

Compress the opposing dormant pair as above; its first occurrence is now the other effective donor. The first completed contributing nonanchor group is a singleton, since the active pair is the anchor. Before that singleton can pay its three moments there must be effective earlier sites \(a,d\), and the separated reads on \(\mathbf U,Y\mathbf U\) exclude either having only the very first effective row as its profile when that row is nonanchor. More explicitly, if the dormant \(d\) is the first effective donor, its \(U/w=-d^2/2\) and its normalized profile cannot be changed by a second occurrence, so its necessary nonzero scalar tap is forbidden. If the anchor \(a\) is first, its \(U\ne0\) and both actual anchor rows must occur before the first contributing nonanchor completion. The two possible effective patterns are thus anchor–anchor–dormant and anchor–dormant–anchor. The paired-anchor equations give respectively \(t=d\) with \(d=2a\), and \(t=a\) with \(a=2d\). Both contradict that the target is a distinct contributing singleton. A possible zero second anchor weight is handled by the same raw honest tap products as before.

### Point collision at a contributing singleton

Compress the dormant pair to its one live effective profile. Before the first complete contributing nonanchor group, the only possible effective donors are the singleton anchor and the first occurrences of the active and dormant pairs. There must be three actual effective slots: with one weighted site three moments are impossible, and with two weighted single profiles the separated defect reads forbid the very first \(U\ne0\) donor. This is exactly the singleton-anchor census with the three donors named in their chronological order \(c,a,b\), including the possibility that the active pair's first occurrence is a ghost.

If all three effective donors are live, the three-donor resonance forces first target \(t=b\). This third earlier donor cannot be the dormant pair or the anchor singleton: neither can be that contributing nonanchor completion. It must be the active pair's first occurrence, and its mate makes the first completed group. A second contributing nonanchor target exists among the other singletons. The rank-two cardinal minor forbids it; an intervening second dormant physical row preserves the same effective profile and scalar support. If the active pair's first row is a ghost in the final chronological slot, its first completion still has \(t=b\), its live mate extends the scalar donor support exactly as in the raw singleton-anchor proof, and the same minor applies. If that ghost is in the middle slot, the raw middle-ghost equations give \(t=b=2c\); now \(b\) is either the dormant site or the singleton anchor, which cannot be the first completed contributing nonanchor group. A first effective ghost is impossible by endpoint pruning. These are all possible weighted statuses.
