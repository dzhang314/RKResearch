## 1. Cancellation of the cost of erasing displayed off-stop marks

Use the hypotheses of FiniteStoppedBracket.strict_bracket_of_pole_grid and FiniteStoppedBracketCuts on a true stopped side, reflecting first for a right suffix. Write H for the allowed positive pole product, phi=1/H on the displayed stopped-side knots, A for the stopped polynomial weight, P for the signed density, and n for the number of distinct atoms including the live stop t. Use a sample list containing the two meshes and t, optionally extended; suppose its P sign variations are ≤k. The root sign-cut fit gives orientation of the sign of P(t).

Let E=∏_{c∈J}(X−c), where J consists of r distinct *off-stop* atoms. Assume the real local moment of A P E vanishes. The global law for P E keeps exactly the undeleted nodal terms, with signed masses μ_i E(i); a zero off-stop mass can also simply be retained before erasure without affecting the argument. A degree-b law for P pays a degree-(b−r) law here. On the same sorted mesh the sign changes of P E are ≤k+r (a linear factor contributes at most one, and deleting zero samples cannot increase changes). With n−r labels its tester degree is ≤k+r+2(n−r−1)=k+2(n−1)−r. Thus k+2(n−1)≤b pays every such erasure uniformly. The polynomial degree evaluated on the finite integration rules is ≤deg A+deg H+deg P+k+2(n−1), also invariant. The separate strict-survivor test should not be omitted: require deg(P E)+#undeleted stopped-side labels<19. It is unchanged as a bound when erasures are all on the stopped side; deg P+n−1<19 is a uniform sufficient bound allowing erasures on either side. Local degree must also fit; it does in the applications below. No mass at t was erased in this argument.

If the true stopped moments vanish for every E of degree ≤d, there are ≥d+1 distinct atoms strictly on the stopped side: otherwise erase all of them and the strict bracket would have empty stopped sum. If exactly d+1 are there, keep just x among them and erase the other d. For B consisting of the stop and those d+1 labels, let D_B=∏_{j∈B}(X−j). The single surviving stopped term has sign opposite the stop term and strictly smaller absolute value; multiplication by |x−t| gives
|μ_x| phi(x) |D_B'(x)| < |μ_t| phi(t) |D_B'(t)|.
The signs of μ alternate through B when traversed away from the stop. Indeed the erasing factors at t and x have opposite relative sign for each intervening erased label; one more change comes from the strict two-term bracket. This forces the stop and each surviving mass nonzero; off-block labels never enter the sum. At equality of the count this proves both order and a strict comparison of *absolute* masses, not an inequality of raw signed masses.

Compose two such tests, including any already-absorbed polynomial G_j nonzero on the compared pair and the corresponding block polynomials D_j. Write K_j(x)=phi_j(x)|G_j(x)| |D_j'(x)|. If one test requires |μ_x| K_1(x)>|μ_y| K_1(y) and the other requires |μ_x| K_2(x)<|μ_y| K_2(y), necessity is K_2(x)/K_1(x)<K_2(y)/K_1(y). An equal or reversed ratio is a contradiction. In particular opposite saturated endpoints with the same block and kernel cannot coexist. These conclusions use only paid true zeroes, not arbitrary tests on a joint sheet.

## 5. Sharpness of this list of threshold comparisons only

The following is an abstract signed atomic model, **not** an actual polynomial density, a quadrature rule for the actual q, a solution of the six global or six local words, or a counterexample to an RK claim. In particular it does not impose the original exterior primitive, arbitrary further tests exploiting the actual sign-change locations of q, any higher-degree channel, or the actual magnitude tying θ_a to q. The claim is strictly that the listed threshold consequences, including the paid opposing channel, are mutually consistent.

Fix arbitrary a<0<z<h<u<1. Let ell_i be the cardinal quadratics of the three nodes z,h,u, choose e>0 sufficiently small, and set d_i=ell_i(e)+ell_i(1−e), η_i=i(1−i)(i−a)d_i, and θ_i=i(1−i)d_i for the three interior i. The d signs are +,−,+ because both exterior-to-block samples e and 1−e lie outside [z,u] and each cardinal numerator is positive there. Set b=2e(1−e)<min(z,1−u). Interpolation of degree ≤2 gives for *every affine* G:
Σ_i η_i phi_R(i)G(i)=G(b),
Σ_i θ_i G(i)/(1−i)=G(1−b),
where these two sums are over the three interior knots only. These are just polynomial interpolation identities in an auxiliary model, not claims that every such sign bracket is paid. Set θ_a first to zero to see the margins, and then choose it strictly negative with sufficiently small absolute value. This perturbs the full F_L(G) by θ_a G(a)/(1−a); all finitely many strict required comparisons persist, including s>u and F_L(1)>0.

For clarity on *strict excluded-stop* signs: at z after erasing u (or h) the singleton suffix has sign opposite to the stop, and its inclusive signed total is G(b), of the stop sign. At h after erasing z, the u term is positive and F_R(X−z)=b−z<0. The left label-erasure comparisons at θ_a=0 are the reflected singleton checks from 1−b>u (erasing a gives an affine positive on the unit), then persist for small negative θ_a. Indeed every affine sign-preserving G on [0,1] meets strict full and excluded-stop inequalities on both outer ends in the model (whether or not a particular comparison is earned). By conicity it suffices to check X and 1−X. At e=0 the strict right comparison for 1−X follows from (1−z)² ell_z(0)>1, since (1−z)h>h−z and (1−z)u>u−z; its comparison for X has positive nodal value against zero. At u the strict left comparison for X follows from u² ell_u(1)>1 via u(1−z)>u−z and u(1−h)>u−h, and the 1−X test has a positive nodal value against zero. The inclusive values G(b),G(1−b) are strictly positive at positive small e even when the e=0 limit is zero. These finite strict polynomial inequalities admit positive sufficiently small parameters over any ordered field (e.g. bound coefficient sums on [0,1], then use half the smallest positive margin). For perturbing θ_a apply the same finite-margin argument after fixing e. This proves feasibility of these scalar consequences, not of the original signed measure.
