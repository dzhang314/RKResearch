# An ordered-field root bridge

The crossed interior exclusion previously used the intermediate value theorem over the reals. [`WeakFiveCrossedPositiveTail.no_actual_crossed_positive_tail_ordered`](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/ERKOrder16WeakFiveCrossedPositiveTailOrdered.lean) proves the same statement over every ordered field, with the same moment and phase hypotheses and no extra root-existence premise.

The main ingredient is independent of Runge–Kutta methods. Given a polynomial with opposite signs at two points of an ordered field, it constructs an ordered field extension containing a root in that gap. For a monic irreducible polynomial, adjoining a formal root gives a field. To extend the order with the formal root in the desired interval, the proof forms the preorder generated by the two interval inequalities and the old order. If this preorder contains minus one, lifting its square certificate produces a multiple of the polynomial that is strictly positive throughout the gap, even in ordered extensions. Its quotient has smaller degree and must have opposite endpoint signs. A root of the quotient, supplied by induction, contradicts strict positivity. The preorder thus extends to an ordering, and the formal root lies strictly between the endpoints.

For a fixed polynomial, choose an ordered extension having the largest possible number of distinct roots. If any original sign gap were still missing a root, the single-gap theorem would enlarge that count. This supplies the root hypothesis used by the existing finite Gaussian proof without importing real completeness or a real-closure construction.

The result excludes one sector of the order-16 weak-five case. It does not change the unconditional stage-count bound.
