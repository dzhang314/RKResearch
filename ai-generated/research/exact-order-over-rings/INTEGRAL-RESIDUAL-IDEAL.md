# The denominator-cleared residual equations generate the rooted-tree ideal

Fix a finite tableau size and a positive order \(p\). In the polynomial
ring over \(\mathbb Z\) in the entries of \(A\) and \(\mathbf b\), write
\[
\mathbf c\coloneqq A\mathbf 1,\qquad
\mathbf Q(t)\coloneqq t!\,A\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(A)-\mathbf c^{\odot |t|},
\qquad
\Delta_t\coloneqq t!\left(\mathbf b\cdot\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(A)\right)-1 .
\]
Thus \(\mathbf Q(\bullet)=0\). The ideal generated by the
\(\Delta_t\) for \(|t|\le p\) is generated equally well by the bush
equations
\[
\beta_n\coloneqq n\left(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c^{\odot(n-1)}\right)-1,
\qquad 1\le n\le p ,
\]
and the residual-product equations
\[
r_t\coloneqq
\mathbf b\cdot\left(
  \mathbf c^{\odot a}\odot
  \mathbf Q(u_1)\odot\cdots\odot\mathbf Q(u_k)
\right).
\]
Here \(t\) ranges over the nonbushy trees of order at most \(p\):
its root has \(a\) leaf children and the nonleaf children
\(u_1,\ldots,u_k\), with \(k\ge1\). The exponent \(0\) on \(\mathbf c\)
denotes the all-ones vector. The result concerns equality of ideals,
not algebraic independence of the evaluated equations.

## Proof

For a tree \(t\) on \(n\) vertices, set
\[
\mathbf E_t\coloneqq \bigodot_{u\text{ a child of the root of }t}
     \bigl(u!\,A\boldsymbol{\Phi}_u(A)\bigr).
\]
The recursion for the tree factorial gives
\(n\mathbf E_t=t!\,\boldsymbol{\Phi}_t(A)\).
For \(S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}\), let \(t_S\) retain the nonleaf
children indexed by \(S\) and replace each omitted child \(u_i\)
by \(|u_i|\) leaves at the root. The order remains \(n\).
The positions of repeated children are counted separately.

At each kept root branch, the identity
\(u_i!\,A\boldsymbol{\Phi}_{u_i}(A)=
\mathbf Q(u_i)+\mathbf c^{\odot|u_i|}\)
gives two choices. Expanding the product, the term with no
\(\mathbf Q\) factor is \(\mathbf c^{\odot(n-1)}\). A nonempty
subset \(S\) gives the residual product of \(t_S\). Thus
\[
\Delta_t=\beta_n + n\sum_{\emptyset\ne S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}}
r_{t_S}.
\]
For a bush this reads \(\Delta_t=\beta_n\). This proves one inclusion
of ideals.

For the other inclusion, expand the residual product by
inclusion–exclusion. Taking its inner product with \(\mathbf b\)
gives, for \(k\ge1\),
\[
nr_t=\sum_{S\subseteq\{1,\ldots,k\}}(-1)^{k-|S|}\Delta_{t_S}.
\]
The constant terms cancel because the signs sum to zero. Now put
\(v_n\coloneqq\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c^{\odot(n-1)}\). The bush
equation satisfies \(n v_n-\beta_n=1\), so
\[
 r_t=v_n(nr_t)-\beta_n r_t.
\]
The right-hand side belongs to the ideal generated by the
\(\Delta_t\), proving equality without division. In each fixed order
the two root expansions are inverse integral triangular changes on
the formal root-tree expressions: every proper leaf replacement
strictly lowers total vertex depth.

For example, \(r_t=\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf Q([\bullet])\) for the
three-vertex tall tree \(t=[[\bullet]]\), and
\[
3r_t=\Delta_{[[\bullet]]}-\beta_3,\qquad
r_t=(\mathbf b\cdot\mathbf c^{\odot2})
       (\Delta_{[[\bullet]]}-\beta_3)-\beta_3 r_t .
\]
The equality of ideals survives specialization to any commutative
ring with identity. In a quotient satisfying the bush equations,
each paid integer \(n\) becomes a unit, even if it was not a unit
before imposing the equations. The displayed certificate explains
how that fact is used without inverting any ambient scalar.
