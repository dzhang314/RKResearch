# Finite rooted-output reconstruction: filtered witnesses and sharp triangular cutoffs

**Setup.** Over a field of characteristic zero, write $c=A\mathbf1$, $D=\operatorname{diag}(c)$, and $f(t)=b^T\Phi_A(t)$. “Data through order $P$” means actual values of every scalar tree through $P$; it need not be exact classical order $P$. “Paid through $p$” means these values are the exact integration values through $p$. A stage grade is tree order minus 1. The full-output minimal observable quotient is the split stage algebra $K^r$ from the preceding source note, obtained by merging equal full tree-stage profiles and pruning classes with no aggregate path to a nonzero output. A scalar reconstruction cannot label the discarded original physical stages, even given *all* orders. Here “reconstruction” means the effective tableau (or the actual physical tableau when the given hypotheses make it observable and reachable, or directly when physical $b,c$ and a support class are given).

## 1. Finite filtered reconstruction, without all products of basis trees

Let $V_g$ be the span in the minimal stage algebra of tree expressions of grade at most $g$, and $U_h$ the span of linear rows $b^T\mathcal C$ read by one-hole contexts of cost at most $h$. A path $A$ costs 1; a fixed stage multiplier of grade at most $j$ costs at most $j$. These subspaces can be computed with the exact recursions, writing span on products implicitly,

$$
V_0=K\mathbf1,\qquad V_g=V_{g-1}+AV_{g-1}+\sum_{j=1}^{g-1} V_j V_{g-j};
$$
$$
U_0=K b^T,\qquad U_h=U_{h-1}+U_{h-1}A+\sum_{j=1}^h U_{h-j}M_{V_j}.
$$

The first recursion splits a tree at its root, with the unary case handled by $A$ and the non-unary case by root merger. The second removes an operation at the hole, with $M_x$ denoting the multiplier by $x$. If the respective saturation grades are $\rho,\sigma$, choose $r$ basis inputs $t_j$ of grade at most $\rho$ and $r$ basis contexts $C_i$ of cost at most $\sigma$. Let $\delta$ be any grade for a **separating family** $G$ of formal stage expressions (the corresponding tuples distinguish the $r$ primitive coordinates). This grading concerns the actual observable quotient, not a formal claim that order forces any chosen filters to be independent. In particular $\delta\le\rho$ is always possible; distinct clocks give $\delta=1$, and separation by $(c,Ac)$ gives $\delta\le2$.

**Finite criterion.** With an a priori bound $r$ on effective stage count, an invertible $r$-block
$$H_{ij}=f(C_i t_j)$$
and a separating family $G$ as above suffice for complete effective reconstruction from order

$$P_{\rm rec}=\rho+\sigma+\max(1,\delta)+1. \tag{1}$$

In particular this is a paid criterion when $P_{\rm rec}\le p$. Given exact values, the matrices $H^{-1}[f(C_i A t_j)]$ and $H^{-1}[f(C_i (g t_j))]$ are respectively the unary operator and multiplication by each $g\in G$ in the input basis. The leaf coordinates follow by the same solve and the output row is $[f(t_j)]$. The multiplication operators for $G$ determine the primitive-coordinate basis: their joint eigenspaces are the individual axes, as separation makes each joint eigenspace one-dimensional; in each use the corresponding component of the unit as the canonical idempotent. Equivalently take products of separating coordinate differences to construct the joint selectors. This uses no algebraic closure or factoring beyond the known split representation. Rank $r$ and the a priori bound make the basis full in every realizable completion, and the reconstruction matrices force the same separation there. Therefore all completions within that stage bound are permutation equivalent. The bound is essential: one finite invertible block does not preclude further dimensions above the horizon.

Only **generator** multiplications are needed; reconstruction through double the largest input grade is unnecessary if lower-degree expressions already distinguish stages. The actual smallest $\rho$ and $\sigma$ can be read from the dimension-growth recursions. Independent elementary bounds follow from $\delta$ as well. Put $m=\max(1,\delta)$. Words of multiplicative length at most $r-1$ in a separating family span the full split algebra (grow dimension at each step until the space is stable, or interpolate each coordinate). Hence $\rho\le(r-1)m$. Grow observable rows by $A$ and the generator multipliers only. When stable their annihilator is an $A$-stable ideal, because the generators generate the algebra, so observability forces this annihilator to vanish. Starting from the nonzero output there are at most $r-1$ strict growth steps, each of cost at most $m$. Thus $\sigma\le(r-1)m$ as well. In particular a distinct-clock minimal tableau admits order $2r$ reconstruction by (1), even from observed paths when some direct output weights vanish. In the classical near-maximal-order cases below, the bound $2r$ really is sharp. No general sharpness claim is made for every possible triple $(\rho,\sigma,\delta)$.

If one instead has **stage columns** (not just scalar data) through grade $g$, let $\mathcal P_g$ be their joint physical partition. It is the full tree-stage partition exactly when $A$ preserves the whole coordinate algebra constant on $\mathcal P_g$. The algebra is generated by these columns, by the same separator argument. The test is the row sums into each block, equal across each fixed block. It can also be checked without seeing $A$ entries by building class indicators from grade-$g$ selectors: if there are $m_g$ blocks these indicators have representatives of grade at most $(m_g-1)g$, so seeing $A$ applied to them, through tree order $(m_g-1)g+2$, suffices. The stage-level proviso cannot be deleted.

## 2. Fixed physical clocks and weights: reconstruct the triangular edges at order s or s+1

The scalar **double-bush** data are

$$M_{a,k}=b^T D^a A c^k=\sum_{i,j} b_i A_{ij} c_i^a c_j^k,\qquad\text{tree order }a+k+2. \tag{2}$$

Here the root has $a$ leaf children and one bush child of order $k+1$. Its paid value is $1/((k+1)(a+k+2))$. The following sharper reconstruction does not use a full Hankel rank and does not assume (2) is paid; it only needs the stated actual scalar values.

Suppose ordered physical clocks $c_i$ and individual weights $b_i$ are known. Let $E_i$ describe the unprescribed entries of row $i$; all other entries are fixed by the class. Row sums are already fixed to $c_i$. Rows with zero or one unprescribed entry are already determined; let $R$ be the others and assume $b_i\ne0$ on $R$. Take any nominal matrix with the prescribed row sums. Choose a reference column $j_i\in E_i$ and set $x_{ij}=b_i( A_{ij}-A^0_{ij})$ for $j\in E_i\setminus\{j_i\}$. The remaining difference at $j_i$ is minus their sum. Thus the exact matrix for reconstruction through order $d+2$ has entries

$$\mathcal M_d[(a,k),(i,j)]=c_i^a(c_j^k-c_{j_i}^k),\qquad k\ge1,\ a\ge0,\ a+k\le d. \tag{3}$$

It is injective if and only if these **double-bush** data determine the unprescribed entries in the affine support class. In a characteristic-zero field a nontrivial kernel gives alternative matrices with the same data; if nonzero diagonals or other generic nonvanishing are required one can choose nonzero perturbations avoiding finitely many bad scalar values. Equation (3) asserts sharpness for the linear probe family on general support, not that arbitrary nonlinear trees never do more on that support.

Here is a simple structural degree guarantee. Assume the clocks are distinct among the varying rows, and the column clocks in each $E_i$ are distinct. If $q_1\le\cdots\le q_e$ are the varying-row support sizes in the chosen order, then

$$d\ \ge\ \max_{k=1}^e(e-k+q_k-1)\quad\text{suffices}. \tag{4}$$

Proceed row by row. Multiply a polynomial in the first clock variable vanishing at the $e-k$ not-yet-peeled *other* rows by the Lagrange selector in the second clock variable of degree $q_k-1$ for the desired entry of this row. Previously peeled contributions are known and subtracted. Normalize the retained row factor to one. The constant-in-second-coordinate moments, if used in the expansion, are already known from row sums. This recovers each unknown. Sorting sizes increasingly minimizes this simple bound; (3) supplies the exact test even if the bound can be improved for a given support.

**Full triangular cases.** Let $s$ be the physical stage count, the chronological clock list pairwise distinct, and the weights nonzero on varying rows.

- For ERK, and for lower triangular RK with a given diagonal, reconstruction uses rooted data through order **$s$** (strict lower free entries, sizes $i-1$). A common diagonal is already given by the first clock in a chronological lower triangular table; the same cutoff holds. This includes any exact-order-$p$ instance satisfying $p\ge s$ and these hypotheses.
- With unknown unrestricted lower triangular diagonal, reconstruction uses order **$s+1$** (inclusive sizes $i$), also in the nonsingular subclass. This includes any instance satisfying $p\ge s+1$ with the stated $b,c$ hypotheses.
- In either case nonzero weights are only needed on rows with at least two genuinely unprescribed entries. Given a diagonal, the first two rows are fixed by their row sums; without one, the first row is fixed. No issue of observing their outputs arises. If all rows are already determined, no rooted data are needed.

These cutoffs are sharp even against **all** rooted scalar data in the respective fixed-clock matrix classes for $s\ge3$ (given diagonal common or zero) and $s\ge2$ (free diagonal). Choose nonzero weights throughout and pairwise distinct real/rational clocks. For a fixed common diagonal $\alpha$ take $c_1=\alpha$ and $A_0=\alpha I+(c-\alpha\mathbf1)e_1^T$, so it is lower triangular. On vectors $F(c)$ the operator gives $\alpha F(c)+(c-\alpha\mathbf1)F(\alpha)$, and every tree column is a polynomial in $c$ of degree at most its grade. Change only the final row, by a nonzero multiple of a vector on stages $1,\ldots,s-1$ annihilating powers through $s-3$ but not $s-2$ (Vandermonde). This fixes $c$, the diagonal, and every stage tree through order $s-1$ by induction, since each child there has grade at most $s-3$. At order $s$ the unary root above the bush of order $s-1$ detects the variation since the final output weight is nonzero. Set $\alpha=0$ for ERK. For the free-diagonal case take $A_0=D$ with all $c_i\ne0$ and similarly vary the last row on all $s$ entries with a vector killing degrees through $s-2$ but not $s-1$. The matrices agree on every stage tree through order $s$ and differ in the analogous order-$s+1$ scalar. Nonzero diagonals survive a sufficiently chosen perturbation. This is a sharpness statement for these finite-reconstruction classes; the general sharp witnesses are not claimed to be of large classical order. This does not prove existence of arbitrary-order tight tableaux. As small exact-paid witnesses, the checker also exhibits four-stage ERKs of exact order 3 and two-stage nonsingular DIRKs of exact order 2 with the same fixed $b,c$ whose first distinctions occur at orders 4 and 3 respectively.

## 3. The classical paid borderline: maximal Gauss uniqueness and the odd freedom

The following is a compact reconstruction proof and application of classical simplifying conditions, not a claim of a new maximal-order RK theorem. Suppose the effective tableau has $r$ stages and $p\ge2r-1$. The ordinary $r\times r$ moment block $[1/(i+j+1)]_{0\le i,j<r}$ has full rank (a positive Gram over the reals, with nonzero rational determinant, hence over any characteristic-zero field). Factoring it into Vandermonde, output weights, and transposed Vandermonde shows all effective clocks distinct and all effective weights nonzero. If $p\ge2r+1$ the next moment block would already require $r+1$ stages, so the only even tight case is $p=2r$.

**At $2r$.** The monic nodal polynomial of degree $r$ is uniquely determined by its orthogonality with $1,x,\ldots,x^{r-1}$, using the paid moments through degree $2r-1$. The coefficients follow from the invertible moment block, the clocks are its roots, and the weights follow from the first $r$ moments. Then all $r\times r$ entries of (2) for $0\le a,k<r$ are paid. In fact (2) and the ordinary moments give $b^TD^a(Ac^k-c^{k+1}/(k+1))=0$ for all such $a,k$. Hence $A c^k=c^{k+1}/(k+1)$ on this entire Vandermonde basis. This is the Gaussian collocation tableau. Its classical order $2r$ also follows by the same simplifying-condition induction used below, with $C(r)$ and $D(r)$ instead of $C(r-1)$ and $D(r-1)$. An upper bound of $r$ canonical stages suffices for this conclusion, since the moment minor already forces $r$. If the ground field does not contain the required split Gaussian nodes, no such tableau exists over it.

**At $2r-1$ ($r\ge2$).** Given fixed $b,c$ with the above moment identities, pairwise distinct clocks and nonzero weights, (2) fixes every pairing of $A$ except one mark $(a,k)=(r-1,r-1)$. Define $v^T$ by $v^Tc^k=0$ for $k<r-1$ and $v^Tc^{r-1}=1$, and $u$ by $b^TD^a u=0$ for $a<r-1$ and $b^TD^{r-1}u=1$. Both exist uniquely by Vandermonde and nonzero weights. The allowed matrices are exactly

$$A=A_{\rm coll}+\lambda u v^T, \tag{5}$$

where $A_{\rm coll}$ integrates the cardinal polynomials of the given clocks from 0 to each clock. A proof that this is the exact **rooted** family, not merely the single-link family, is useful for the information claim. For all $k\le r-2$, (5) has $A c^k=c^{k+1}/(k+1)$ (classical $C(r-1)$). For all $a\le r-2$ and arbitrary stage vectors $w$,

$$ b^TD^a A w = \frac{b^Tw-b^T D^{a+1}w}{a+1}. \tag{6} $$

For $A_{\rm coll}$, interpolate $w$ by a polynomial of degree $\le r-1$, apply exact quadrature to both sides of elementary integration by parts, and note the largest integrand degree is $a+r\le2r-2$. The perturbation is killed by every such row, so (6) holds for (5) (classical $D(r-1)$ in equivalent normalized form). It also proves that the collocation reference matches all stated single-link data: use $C$ when $k\le r-2$, and (6) plus paid moments for $k=r-1,a\le r-2$. The free-kernel description then proves necessity of (5).

For sufficiency, show by induction that $b^TD^a\Phi(t)$ has its exact value whenever $a+|t|\le2r-1$. Order $\le r$ stage columns are the correct clock monomials, using $C(r-1)$. In a root with paid $a+n\le2r-1$ there is at most one child of order $m\ge r$; if none, all factors are exactly integrated stage monomials. Otherwise the other children contribute an exact constant times $c^{n-1-m}$. Apply (6) at exponent $a+n-1-m\le r-2$ to the large child. Both resulting ordinary probes on that child are paid by the induction, giving the formal exact integral: for child stage coefficient $α=m/\gamma(t_{\rm child})$ and exponent $h=a+n-1-m$, the child contribution after (6) is $\alpha/[m(m+h+1)]$. This is the single-heavy-child proof of the classical simplifying-assumption condition, without invoking an outside order theorem. Every $\lambda$ therefore satisfies every rooted condition through order $2r-1$.

Without fixed clocks, the nodal polynomial at this odd budget ranges over the affine line of monic degree-$r$ polynomials orthogonal to degrees $<r-1$; whenever it splits into $r$ distinct clocks, use the unique interpolated weights. This gives the quadrature data through degree $2r-2$ by polynomial division. The same $r$-minor ensures those weights are nonzero. Equation (5) then describes the remaining matrix freedom. In particular, choose the Gaussian $b,c$ themselves. Such nodes exist over the reals: the moment-defined monic orthogonal polynomial has $r$ distinct roots in $(0,1)$, since otherwise multiplying by the product of its sign-changing roots there would give a nonzero constant-sign integrand orthogonal to a polynomial of lower degree. Division by that polynomial gives exact quadrature through degree $2r-1$, and the weights are positive by integrating squares of cardinal polynomials. Take two distinct $\lambda\ne0$. Each tableau has exact order $2r-1$, yet their order-$2r$ double-bush witnesses are different: the collocation scalar is $1/(2r^2)$, and the change is exactly $\lambda$. This proves that order $2r$ is necessary in general even for this small minimal, positive-weight, fixed-clock comparison. In these examples $\rho=\sigma=r-1$ and $\delta=1$: the early stage grades are single clock-monomial directions, and (6) collapses every lower-cost observer into the corresponding ordinary-clock row span. Thus (1) is attained on a classical paid boundary.

## 4. Consequences and the earlier 500-stage pair

The known 500-stage ERKs have exact classical order 20 and merely **agree** through 54. Their full-series minima each have 500 stages. They do not contradict either finite reconstruction criterion. Even their actual filtrations give $\rho,\sigma\ge53$: there are 54 terminal stages parameterized by distinct $z$, and a tree column of grade $g$ has on those stages values of a polynomial in $z$ of degree at most $g$ (each rooted branch in their affine coefficient row supplies a single affine factor). A one-hole context with an $A$ on its marked path annihilates any input supported on those terminal stages, which feed no row. Contexts without such a path step restrict to each terminal output weight times a polynomial in $z$ of degree at most the context cost. Neither stage nor row filtration can have full rank on this set before its 54 polynomial dimensions are available. Thus (1), even with $\delta=1$, needs at least 108 orders for these *particular* full Hankel witnesses; this is a lower bound on that criterion, not on the first distinguishing tree (which is order 55). The generic fixed-clock triangular cutoff is $s=500$; specific structural support or parameter restrictions can of course distinguish earlier.

For actual paid-order low-stage work, the fully paid rectangle restriction is decisive: $\operatorname{rank}H_{\rm paid}\le\min(\rho+1,\sigma+1)$ by the single-order normalized columns and the ordinary-clock partial-fraction row span. Thus if (1) is entirely paid and the block has full rank $r$, it forces $p\ge 2r$, the maximal-order situation; at the odd boundary an absent upper-right operator mark is genuinely necessary. Extra support can evade this limitation without using a full Hankel block, as in (3)–(4). In general (1) is usable if a full effective rank block plus its unary and separator marks fits within the budget, not merely if the block itself is paid. The maximal-order Gauss branch and the distinct-clock weighted triangular cuts above are precise sufficient cases. One further quick check: in a nonsingular DIRK with $s$ distinct clock stages and nonzero weights, paid order $p\ge s+2$ would give $A c=c^2/2$ by the full $s$ ordinary observer rows applied to this grade-two defect, contradicting the first diagonal clock $c_1=A_{11}\ne0$. Thus the fixed-$b,c$ reconstruction at $s+1$ matches the possible upper order edge of that restricted class. For a general order-20 branch with repeated clocks, vanishing weights or unknown physical $b,c$, none of these shortcuts can silently replace the actual observable filtration or structural constraints. No new unconditional order-20 floor is claimed.
