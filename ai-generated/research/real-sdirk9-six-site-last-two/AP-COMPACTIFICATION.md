# Regular arithmetic-progression arm: exact compactification at prime 37

This is the final regular branch of the [six-site two-port exclusion](README.md). The first three actual old reverse clocks, in unit-diagonal coordinates, are $1,a,2a-1$, the second quadratic defect is nonzero, and the first old terminal is completed at physical row four. Its clock $t$ is a scalar node disjoint from the two protected ports but may repeat an earlier unprotected occurrence. The proof explicitly handles all such repeats and port collisions.

## The row-four terminal has a literal shifted primitive gate

The first-three quadratic defect interpolant is forced. For the third strict tap $u_3$ the actual length-three identity on $A\mathbf g$ gives
$$
   u_3(a^3-7a^2+10a-2)=-2(a-1)(3a-5).
$$
The left denominator cannot vanish: $a\ne1$, and at $a=5/3$ the displayed cubic denominator is $-4/27$. The actual first-three quadratic defect values are $1/2$, $2a-1-a^2/2$, and $2(2a-1)-1-(2a-1)^2/2+u_3(a-1)$. Let $\widehat g$ be their quadratic interpolant on the three distinct clocks. The row-four terminal may include a signed earlier occurrence at its site, but its **literal strict mean** is supported on the first three old coordinates. The required primitive values on $1,X,X^2$ therefore fix that row as $E_t(J-I)$ on the interpolant, and exactness on the actual quadratic defect gives $(J-I)\widehat g(t)=0$.

The exact numerator for this last gate, with the nonzero denominator just noted, is
$$
\begin{aligned}
 B(a,t)={}&(a^3-7a^2+16a-12)t^3\\
 &+(-9a^3+54a^2-102a+63)t^2\\
 &+(18a^3-90a^2+138a-66)t\\
 &-6a^3+24a^2-30a+12.
\end{aligned}
$$
The replay derives this numerator by rational interpolation and exact primitive computation, not by sampled site conditions.

In the normalized physical reflected variable $Y$, set $g=\gamma$, $h=\gamma(a-1)$, and $c=\gamma t$. The first-three old clocks are $g,g+h,g+2h$ and the two protected clocks are $p=1-gv,q=1-gw$. Use the integral polynomial
$$
 L(g,h,c)=g^6 B(1+h/g,c/g)=0;
$$
all terms have nonnegative powers of $g$. The coefficient of $c^3$ is
$$
 (h-g)^2(h-2g).
$$
This leading coefficient matters at infinity. The old terminal $c$ can coincide with at most one of $g,g+h,g+2h$, and never with $p,q$. The six scalar nodes are distinct and their full hull is orthogonal to $1,Y,Y^2$ on $[0,1]$ by precision eight.

## Why a finite residue computation is enough here

An affine modular obstruction would not suffice by itself: an unknown clock could have negative valuation. Instead embed the verified forward port field into $\mathbb Q_{37}$ using the three simple Hensel lifts $(s_f,v,w)=(8,3,19)$; the field proof is in the neighboring three-terminal packet. The field generated by a proposed real-algebraic tableau is an algebraic extension of this port field. The chosen embedding extends to it in $\overline{\mathbb Q}_{37}$. We use the extended valuation there. Its integral elements reduce into $\overline{\mathbb F}_{37}$. This is also enough for impossibility over any characteristic-zero field extension of the port field in this polynomial chart: a solution with the required nonzero clock differences can be encoded by adjoining their inverses, and a proper ideal over the port field then has an algebraic point over its algebraic closure.

For each divergent node $r$ (negative valuation), replace $Y-r$ by $1-Y/r$ in the hull, multiplying the whole hull by a nonzero scalar. Every finite node factor remains monic. Reducing the scaled hull modulo the maximal ideal leaves exactly the product of the **finite** node factors. They may collide after reduction; repeated factors are retained with their multiplicities. The argument never divides by a site difference and does not assume that any two divergent nodes have different valuations. Scalar moment integration commutes with reduction, since the only denominators are the integers through nine, hence units at $37$. Thus the product of the finite node factors remains orthogonal to $1,Y,Y^2$. Four or more divergent nodes are impossible: the resulting nonzero monic polynomial would have degree at most two and be killed by the nonsingular Hilbert moment matrix (its size-three determinant is $1/2160$).

The two ports have unit residues $v=3,w=19$. In addition $1+v$ and $1+w$ are units. The exact replay uses the following exhaustive compactification.

### No early clock is a protected site

If $g$ diverges, $p,q,g$ diverge. At least one of $g+h,g+2h$ diverges too, because twice the former minus the latter is $g$. These are distinct actual node factors, even if their scaled coordinates cancel, so this is forbidden. Thus $g$ is integral.

If $h$ diverges, both $g+h,g+2h$ diverge. The three fixed finite factors are those of $p,q,g$; at most one other node can remain finite. In the no-repeat case it is $c$; in a repeat case it is the single unassigned site. The three moments for the fixed three-node hull have no common vanishing at a residual $g$. Likewise the coefficient rows on $1,d$ for that hull multiplied by any extra finite factor $Y-d$ cannot all vanish: their $2\times2$ minors have gcd one in the residue field. These two independent univariate computations eliminate every case with $h$ divergent.

If $g,h$ are integral and the one remaining node diverges, the other five factors are those of $p,q,g,g+h,g+2h$. With no repeat the divergent node is $c$. Divide $L$ by $c^3$ before reducing: its leading coefficient forces $\bar h=\bar g$ or $\bar h=2\bar g$. This argument also covers $\bar g=0$, when both choices require $\bar h=0$. Neither reduced five-factor hull satisfies the three scalar moments, by the two univariate gcd checks. With a terminal repeat, $c$ is one of the three already integral old nodes; the finite five-factor hull and the specialized gate $L=0$ have unit ideal modulo $37$ in all three cases.

With all nodes integral, the three scalar moment equations and $L=0$ have unit ideal modulo $37$, both for six specified factors and for each of the three choices of a repeated terminal and its extra node. These computations allow collisions in the reduction and do not add noncollision inequalities.

### Exactly one early clock is a protected site

There are two choices of port and three positions $i=0,1,2$ of the pin in the early progression. Its equation is
$$
    (1+v_0)g+i h=1,\qquad v_0\in\{v,w\}.
$$
If $i=0$, $g$ is a fixed integral unit. If $h$ diverges, the two other old early clocks diverge. The finite hull therefore consists of the two fixed protected factors times a factor of degree at most two. The $3\times3$ Hankel matrix for these two factors is nonsingular modulo $37$, so there is no such nonzero factor.

For $i=1,2$, express $h$ affinely in $g$. The other positive-index early clock has coefficient of $g$ equal to $1-j(1+v_0)/i$ with $j\in\{1,2\}\setminus\{i\}$. This coefficient is a unit at the selected residues (the replay checks it separately). Thus if $g$ diverged, both ports, $g$, and this other old clock would diverge, at least four distinct actual nodes. Consequently both $g,h$ are integral.

In either case there are four prescribed finite factors (the two ports and the two unpinned old clocks). If one or both of the other two sites were divergent, the reduced hull would have these four factors and at most one extra linear factor. The associated three-by-two affine moment rank test excludes this for every remaining parameter: its minors have gcd one over the residue field. This check is made in $h$ when $i=0$, and in $g$ otherwise. It covers the possibility that the terminal itself diverges, without using its leading gate again.

With all sites integral, use the three full hull moments and $L=0$. For a repeat the terminal is one of the two unpinned old clocks, so $c$ is substituted exactly and there are two unassigned nodes. For no repeat $c$ is one of the two additional sites. All eighteen systems (six no-repeat, twelve repeat) have unit ideal modulo $37$.

### Exactly two early clocks are protected sites

Assign distinct early indices $i,j\in\{0,1,2\}$ to the $v,w$ ports. Then
$$
  (1+v)g+i h=1,\qquad (1+w)g+j h=1.
$$
The $2\times2$ determinant is a unit for all six ordered choices at $37$. Thus $g,h$ and the three specified node factors (the two ports and the remaining old clock) are integral constants. The precise three moment equations determine the monic cubic for the other three nodes. Its Hankel determinant is also a unit, so all its roots are integral, even if they collide after reduction. If there is no terminal repeat, the terminal $c$ is one of these cubic roots. The cubic and $L(g,h,c)$ have gcd one modulo $37$ in all six cases. If there is a repeat, $c$ is the sole unpinned early clock and $L(g,h,c)$ is already nonzero modulo $37$ in all six cases.

There cannot be three early pins because the two protected sites are distinct and the three actual early clocks are distinct.

## Exact receipt

The independent exact replay in `check_ap.py` computes these moment matrices, univariate gcds, and finite Gröbner bases from the original polynomials, treating all coordinates algebraically. `check.py` verifies exactly 59 true checks, including both finite-old/infinite-new subcases when the first early clock is pinned. The affine tests are performed over the residue field only **after** the valuation arguments above, so no potentially divergent clock is silently discarded.

The [family result](README.md) uses this final branch exclusion with its other exact sibling, degenerate and earlier-terminal gates. This is not a statement about arbitrary thirteen-stage strict SDIRK9 tableaux.
