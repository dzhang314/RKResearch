# Supported affine traces and null quadratic graphs at three distinct SDIRK8 zeros

Consider a strict ten-stage SDIRK tableau of order at least eight over a field of characteristic zero or greater than seven, with common nonzero diagonal $\gamma$, $c=A1$, and $g=Ac-c^{\odot2}/2$. There are exactly three physical zero entries of $g$ at three pairwise distinct clocks. Let $U=\ker G$ for $G=\operatorname{diag}(g)$. Use the [saturated forward and reverse spaces](../../lower-bounds/characteristic-zero/sdirk_saturated_weighted_mirror_duality_20261123.md). Every nonzero degree-two vector supported on $U$ has the form $v=r(c)+\alpha g$, with nonzero trace $r$ of degree at most two. These receipts rule out $\alpha=0$ and separately rule out degree-one $r$. Degree-zero $r$ is excluded by the [constant-trace packet](../sdirk-eight-constant-zero-trace/README.md). In the remaining positive-rank case the support must therefore impose a genuine quadratic graph on all seven off-zero rows. This does not exclude that graph or give a general ten-stage lower bound.

## 1. A null graph would put all off-zero rows on two clocks

Write $d_j=\dim(E_j\cap U)$. The two-sided support rank formula gives $\dim(W_2\cap U_{\rm row})=3-d_3$. Weighting maps $E_2\cap U$ injectively there: if the weighted column vanishes, the ordinary scalar probes detect its trace, and the remaining pure line $\langle g\rangle$ has no nonzero supported vector. Thus $d_2+d_3\le3$. There cannot be only one physical off-zero clock $d$: then $c-d$ and $C(c-d)$ are distinct independent members of $E_2\cap U$, since they are supported on at least two distinct zero clocks different from $d$. That would give $d_2+d_3\ge4$.

If $\alpha=0$, then $r(c)$ vanishes at every off-zero clock. Hence $r$ has degree two and there are exactly two physical off-zero clocks, call them $d,e$. The moments $b^{\mathsf T} C^j G^\ell 1=0$, $0\le j\le1$, $1\le\ell\le3$, separate the clocks and vanish in each of the first three residual powers at each clock. Any one group with nonzero ordinary aggregate needs at least four distinct nonzero residual values, by using their annihilating polynomial in the residual variable. Only seven off-zero rows are available, so at most one group has nonzero aggregate. Since ordinary scalar precision requires four distinct sites of nonzero aggregate, exactly one off-zero group has nonzero aggregate, say $d$, and it and the three literal zero clocks are precisely the distinct roots of $L_4$. This follows by ordinary orthogonality of their monic four-site nodal polynomial.

With two distinct physical off-zero clocks, $E_1\cap U=0$ and $F_3=GE_1$ is clean on $U$. The earliest zero $a$ is at stage three or later, by the grade-one prefix read determinant $-a^2/2\ne0$ at that Gauss clock and the nonzero prefix commutator annihilator. The actual first three rows of a strict floor tableau have distinct clocks whether their third-phase is star or arithmetic, so the third must be zero. Only the arithmetic branch can have a zero third with nonzero second residual. Put $u=(c_2-\gamma)/\gamma$; the [literal phase formulas](../clock-localized-tail/tied-stage-floor/FIRST-THREE-ROWS-AND-PHYSICAL-LEVEL-OBSTRUCTIONS.md) give
$$a=2c_2-\gamma,\qquad Q(u)=4u^3-12u^2+3u+2=0.$$
The terminal weight is nonzero and the terminal residual is nonzero, since the pure $b^{\mathsf T}G$ line is proportional to $b^{\mathsf T}M$ and otherwise ordinary quadrature would be supported on only the three zero clocks. Thus $\gamma,c_2,1-\gamma$ are all off-zero clocks, with $\gamma\ne c_2$.

If $\gamma=d$ then $e=1-d$ (a fourth Gauss site cannot be $1/2$), and $a=2-3d$. If $\gamma=e$ and $1-\gamma=d$, then $e=1-d$ and $a=3d-1$. Finally if both endpoints equal $e$ then $\gamma=e=1/2$ and $a=2d-1/2$. Each branch requires $L_4(d)=L_4(a)=0$ and the homogeneous $Q(c_2-\gamma,\gamma)=0$, where the homogeneous notation is $4n^3-12n^2h+3nh^2+2h^3$. The exact integer resultant numerators for $70L_4(a)$ and $Q(c_2-\gamma,\gamma)$ against $70L_4(d)$ are respectively $(1016064000000,849674)$ for the first two arrangements, and $(848931890625,917773)$ for the last. Their gcds are $14,14,1$. The only rational coefficient denominators in these input polynomials are powers of two, and the Gauss leading coefficient is paid. Thus none of these simultaneous roots exists in any paid field, even over a nonsplit extension.

## 2. A nonconstant affine trace makes both ends literal

Now suppose that $r(X)=X-t$ (normalize its nonzero leading coefficient) and $\alpha\ne0$. The seven off-zero rows satisfy the affine graph $g=-r(c)/\alpha$. The six weighted $C^j g$ moments force seven different active off-zero clocks, otherwise all active off-zero aggregate weights would vanish, leaving at most three ordinary sites. The row $v^{\mathsf T}B$ is supported on the zero sites with trace $r$. It cannot be supported on at most two live sites: multiply its trace by their nodal polynomial and test all degrees through three, contradicting the ordinary grade-three Hilbert form. Thus all three literal zero weights and all three values $r(c_i)$ there are nonzero. In particular the tableau is all-active; the [weighted saturated mirror](../../lower-bounds/characteristic-zero/sdirk_saturated_weighted_mirror_duality_20261123.md) gives a genuine reflected strict tableau with clock $y=1-c$, same $\gamma$, and quadratic residual proportional to the original $g$. Both $F_3$ and $Z_3$ are clean on $U$, since there are at least two distinct active off-zero clocks.

At either end the second row is off-zero: if it were zero, the actual first-edge equation $(C-\gamma I)g=\kappa(A-\gamma I)g$ with $\kappa=g_2/g_1=0$ would put every off-zero row at $\gamma$. Write $u=(c_2-\gamma)/\gamma$ for the normalized nonzero second gap in that orientation, and $t_e$ for the root of the affine graph ($t$ or $1-t$). Set $R=\gamma-t_e\ne0$. The graph requires
$$R(2-u)=\gamma,\qquad t_e=\gamma(1-u)/(2-u).$$
A star third is impossible: it has the same nonzero residual as the second; the affine graph then repeats the second clock in a literal twin. If the arithmetic third is off-zero, the graph comparison at its clock $\gamma(1+2u)$ forces its adjacent tap ratio to be $u$. With the actual arithmetic tap equation, this implies $(u-1)^2(u-2)=0$, so $u=1$ and $t_e=0$. Call this **A**. Otherwise the arithmetic third is zero; call this **Z**, with $Q(u)=0$ and at the same clock $\gamma(1+2u)$. In an A end with $t_e=0$, the clean supported diagonal entry at the earliest zero instead gives $a=2\gamma$ from $J(X)(a)=\gamma a$. Both ends cannot be A, since their affine roots sum to 1.

## 3. Exact linear moment reconstruction of the zero polynomial

The three zero sites have signed weights $b_i(c_i-t)\ne0$ for the functional $I((X-t)\,\cdot)$. They match its moments through degree five by the actual supported weighted vector. The $3\times3$ Hankel factorizes into an invertible Vandermonde, the diagonal of these signed nonzero weights, and the transposed Vandermonde. Consequently its determinant is nonzero without any sign assumption. Let $P_t$ be the monic cubic of the three zero sites. Its coefficients are the unique solution of
$$I((X-t)P_t(X)X^j)=0,\qquad j=0,1,2.$$
If $t=T/D$ with $D\ne0$, the replay takes scaled moments $m_i=D/(i+2)-T/(i+1)$, $0\le i\le5$, and uses Cramer's rule. For a point $A/D$, the homogeneous evaluation is
$$D^3\det(\mathrm{Hankel})\,P_t(A/D)
= \det(\mathrm{Hankel}) A^3 + p_2 A^2 D+p_1 A D^2+p_0D^3,$$
where $p_j$ is the corresponding column-replacement determinant. No normalization by a possibly vanished fiber determinant is used in the resultant.

* **A/Z**, up to reflection. Then $t=0$ and the reflected gap $u$ is a root of $Q$. The anchor $1=\gamma(1-u)/(2-u)$ gives $D=1-u$, $\gamma=(2-u)/D$; $D\ne0$ since $Q(1)=-3$. The two extreme zero clocks are $2\gamma$ and $1-\gamma(1+2u)$. Their two homogeneous evaluation norms against $Q$ have numerator gcd 1.
* **Z/Z**, equal gaps. The affine anchors force $t=1/2$. In centered coordinates the signed measure has odd density, so the moment Hankel of size three has entries zero when the row and column index have the same parity. Its rank is at most two. This contradicts the invertible three-site Hankel.
* **Z/Z**, distinct gaps $u,v$. Here put $D=4-3(u+v)+2uv$ and $G=(2-u)(2-v)$. The two affine anchors give $\gamma=G/D$, $t=(1-u)(2-v)/D$, with $D\ne0$ because $\gamma D=G\ne0$. The extreme zero clocks are $\gamma(1+2u)$ and $1-\gamma(1+2v)$. Both homogeneous evaluations, say $P_a,P_b$, must vanish. The oriented pair equations are $Q(u)=0$ and $(Q(v)-Q(u))/(v-u)=0$; the latter is literally a polynomial with leading coefficient 4 in $v$. Take the successive resultants in $v,u$ of $P_a$, $P_a+P_b$, and $P_a-P_b$. In that order their numerator gcds end at $81$. The Hankel coefficients use only paid denominators, and the replay checks this also for every Gröbner reduction multiplier (monic leading monomials $v^2,u^3$ are coprime). Thus no candidate is lost on specialization. The nested resultants encode ordered pairs in the algebraic closure, not only split prime-field roots.

All paid affine cases are excluded. The remaining three-distinct-zero supported graph, if any, has a quadratic rather than affine trace. The tests do not apply to tableaux with a larger physical zero set.
