# The five-live-zero graph cannot repeat an off-zero clock

Let $A,\mathbf b$ be a strict ten-stage SDIRK tableau of ordinary order at least eight over a field of characteristic zero or greater than seven. Write $\mathbf c=A\mathbf1$, $\mathbf g=A\mathbf c-\mathbf c^{\odot2}/2$, $C=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf c)$, $G=\operatorname{diag}(\mathbf g)$, $U=\ker G$, and $\gamma\ne0$ for the common diagonal. Assume exactly five physical quadratic zeros, genuine quadratic graph support, and no active off-zero stage.

**Result.** The five physical off-zero clocks must be pairwise distinct, as are the five live zero clocks. Moreover their pure reverse weights times residuals are a nonzero multiple of the barycentric weights at these off-zero clocks, and the literal normalized second reverse residual ratio is nonzero. The surviving five-off-clock chart is not excluded here.

## 1. There is a unique off-supported grade-four reverse pure line

In this chart the [support classification](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-support-rank/README.md) gives $d_2=\dim(E_2\cap U)=1$, $d_3=2$, and four or five distinct physical off-zero clocks. Every physical zero is live and their five clocks are distinct by the [four-ordinary-site exclusion](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-graph-four-ordinary-duplicate/README.md). Here $E_j,W_j$ are the saturated grade spaces and $F_j,Z_j$ their pure spaces.

The supported row space $W_3\cap U_{\rm row}$ has dimension four. It is spanned by the **actual** rows $\mathbf b^{\mathsf T}C^j$, $0\le j\le3$ (all off-zero weights are zero). These rows are independent by the size-four paid ordinary Hilbert matrix: pair with $\mathbf c^{\odot k}$, $0\le k\le3$, to obtain $1/(j+k+1)$. Their span contains no nonzero pure row in $Z_3$, since such a row would have all four pairings zero and the same nonsingular matrix would kill all its coefficients. Thus
$$Z_3\cap U_{\rm row}=0,$$
so the two-dimensional pure space has off-zero restriction of rank two.

Let $M=CA-AC-A^2$, $N=A-\gamma I$, and $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}=-\mathbf b^{\mathsf T}M/2$. It spans $Z_2$, and $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T},\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}N$ span $Z_3$ by the [literal reverse rank](../sdirk-eight-four-zero-gaussian-rear/README.md). The terminal $z_{10}=b_{10}\gamma^2/2$ is nonzero. The pure row $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}G$ belongs to $Z_4=\operatorname{Ann}(E_3)$ because $GE_3\subset F_5\subset E_5$, and $Z_2=\operatorname{Ann}(E_5)$. It is supported only on off-zero columns and nonzero, since $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$ has a nonzero off-zero restriction and $G$ is invertible there.

On the off-zero coordinates $E_3$ restricts to all cubic clock polynomials: its scalar cubics are arbitrary, and its pure part is $\mathbf g$ times affine and hence cubic on the quadratic graph. Its rank is four independently of a single repeated off-zero clock. Therefore the space of **off-supported** rows in $Z_4$ is one-dimensional, generated by $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}G$.

## 2. A repeated graph off-zero pair is impossible

If there were four distinct off-zero clocks, the unique annihilator of cubic evaluation on the five off-zero positions would be the difference of the two equal-clock coordinate rows. Their residuals are equal and nonzero because they lie on the graph. It follows that the **off-zero restriction of $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$** is supported precisely on the two repeated positions, with opposite coefficients.

The [literal second reverse gap relation](../sdirk-eight-four-zero-gaussian-rear/README.md) is
$$
\kappa\,\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}N
=\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}((1-\gamma)I-C),\qquad
\kappa=1+2v-v^2,\quad v=(c_{10}-c_9)/\gamma.
$$
If $\kappa\ne0$, the off-zero restrictions of $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$ and $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}N$ are proportional, since the off-zone support of the first lies at one clock. This contradicts the rank-two restriction of $Z_3$.

If $\kappa=0$, every nonzero entry of $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$ lies at the single clock $c_{10}=1-\gamma$. Among the distinct **zero** clocks it can occur only at physical stage 10; the last weight is live, which is why terminal is one of these zero rows. In the off-zero block the two nonzero entries still cancel. But $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}\mathbf1=0$ because $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$ is pure. Hence $z_{10}=0$, another contradiction. Neither case divides by an off-zero weight, all of which vanish.

## 3. Barycentric pure row in the remaining chart

Let $d_1,\ldots,d_5$ denote the five **distinct** physical off-zero clocks and $P_{\rm off}(T)=\prod_{i=1}^5(T-d_i)$. The vector
$$\left(\frac{1}{P_{\rm off}'(d_i)}\right)_{i=1}^5$$
annihilates every cubic evaluation, by taking the leading coefficient of degree-four Lagrange interpolation for a polynomial of degree at most three. It consequently spans the just-identified pure off-supported line. There is a nonzero common scalar $\eta$ such that
$$
 z_i g_i=\frac{\eta}{P_{\rm off}'(c_i)}
 \quad\text{for each physical off-zero stage }i .
$$
In particular **no physical off-zero stage has $z_i=0$**. Therefore the same literal reverse relation cannot have $\kappa=0$: it would localize the nonzero part of $\mathbf z^{\mathsf T}$ on one clock even on these five distinct positions. This proves the promised necessary nonzero ratio. It does not imply any reflection symmetry or an inverse for a silent weight.
