# One active literal front: Gaussian penultimate or eighth

At the ten-stage strict SDIRK8 floor over a characteristic-zero field, suppose the sole nonzero quadratic defect is at the weight-zero first stage. Exactly eight individually active physical stages have distinct clocks, the extra silent has actual reverse read \(w_\zeta=0\), and only one actual literal front is active. The [eight-active reduction](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-eight-reverse-vanish/README.md) gives the active cubic profile \(L_4/Q\). This packet excludes the subchart in which \(Q\) is nonconstant coprime to \(L_4\) and either of the **two nonterminal rear clocks** is Gauss. The [terminal](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-eight-coprime-one-front-terminal/README.md) is separate and previously excluded. Thus the remaining one-front coprime branch here has no Gaussian rear clock. The unrestricted **10–11** interval stays unchanged.

## Actual affine-root contacts

An active Gauss clock has zero cubic defect, but \(T\) and its actual reverse weight are nonzero by coprimality. It is therefore the unique root \(r\) of the actual affine multiplier \(P_1\). We do **not** assign a Gaussian ordinary weight to that row or remove an atom from the ordinary moments. The other two active rear clocks have nonzero cubic defect, and no Gaussian value at them can be declared zero from residue data.

Write \(x=1-\gamma,\ c=x-\gamma u,\ d=x-\gamma(2-u)\) in reverse star or \(d=x-2\gamma u\) in arithmetic. For a Gauss penultimate \(c=r\), use \(u=(x-r)/\gamma\), and the actual affine multiplier is proportional to \(X-r\). With the actual reverse ratios the necessary star relation is \(u h_8=k(2u-2)h_{10}\), and the arithmetic relation is \(H h_8=kJ h_{10}\), where \(H,J,k\) are as in the [arithmetic tail](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-eight-coprime-arithmetic/README.md). The latter uses \(d=2r-x\); neither relation divides the zero penultimate amplitude.

For a Gauss eighth \(d=r\) in arithmetic, use \(u=(x-r)/(2\gamma)\), \(c=(x+r)/2\), and \(2h_9=k h_{10}\) directly from the affine midpoint. In star, put \(\widehat u=2-u\) and exchange the two nonterminal pegs in the scalar equation: \(k\) is unchanged, and the cleared star equation changes sign. The \(\widehat u\) clock formulas are then exactly those of the Gaussian penultimate model after that exchange. The subsequent scalar ordinary masks use the unordered set of six occupied pegs, and the actual sixth-stage read depends only on its real earlier chronology. Thus the one checked star model covers both physical contact positions, without reflecting or merging an actual tableau stage.
