# Finite strict diagonals for the regular constant reverse-live quintic, with ordinary eighth

This is the regular companion to the [excluded fourth-anchor exceptional case](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-eight-reverse-live-exceptional/README.md). At ten strict SDIRK stages over a characteristic-zero field, physical one is the sole nonzero quadratic defect and has zero ordinary weight. Eight ordinary stages have distinct clocks, the second ordinarily silent stage \(\zeta\) has actual nonzero reverse read at its distinct ninth clock, and the exact nine-site reverse numerator is constant nonzero. Its actual cubic-defect profile \(H\) has degree five in the span of \(L_3,L_4,L_5\). The two literal quartic values and the regular fourth-anchor coefficient \(V\) are nonzero; physical eight is ordinarily weighted. **Every true strict diagonal \(\gamma\) is a root of a specified nonzero univariate polynomial** in each chart below. This is a finite-diagonal restriction, not an exclusion of every algebraic root; no change to the unrestricted **10–11** interval. Early Gauss contacts and ordinarily silent eighth are separate.

## Common true coefficient model

Use the [actual two-parameter profile](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-eight-reverse-live-constant/README.md#2-two-literal-anchors-and-the-guarded-mixed-quintic-chart). Write \(a=\gamma A_0,b=\gamma B_0,q=\gamma s\) for the actual early physical clocks, \(x=1-\gamma\), \(c=x-\gamma u\), \(k=1+2u-u^2\). For exact arithmetic clear the true factor \(D_0=\gamma(B_0-A_0)L_4(a)L_4(b)\) from both profiles \(H_0,H_1\), then use
\[
 H_* = V(D_0 H_0)+P(D_0H_1)=D_0 V H .
\]
Here the two padded factors \(s-A_0,s-B_0\) were divided from \(P,V\) in the preceding exact projection, not at a residue search. The replay checks each actual early read of \(H_*\) as a polynomial identity.

Take the set of known ordinary pegs from \(a,b,q,x,c,d=c_8\), omitting the actual ordinarily silent literal if \(\zeta\in\{2,3,4\}\). If \(\zeta\in\{5,6,7\}\) all six are ordinary and its clock does not occur among these pegs. Let \(E\) be their monic product and \(R\) the degree-two or degree-three monic missing factor. The same deflated Bézout equation at precision seven gives the nodal minors of the four coefficient rows \([X^1],\ldots,[X^4]\) of
\[
 H_* W_{EX^j}-(EX^j) W_{H_*},\qquad 0\le j\le\deg R .
\]
Every true minor must vanish, since the actual monic missing coefficients form a nonzero column relation. For five known pegs use its one \(4\times4\) determinant \(N\); for six use its four \(3\times3\) minors. These are exact consequences of ordinary precision seven, not an assumed scalar error cancellation at degrees eight or nine.

In all polynomial divisions the guard is actual: \(\gamma\ne0\); the literal quartic values and \(V\ne0\); \(s\) differs from both literal normalized clocks; and \(x\) is distinct from all nine reverse sites other than itself. For formulas using the leading profile \(H_5=[X^5]H_*\), \(H_5\ne0\) holds by the genuine degree-five chart. The terminal \(H_*(x)\ne0\) follows from the actual deflated pointwise identity. Denominators normalized further by these known guards have the same nonzero property at a candidate.

## Rear star: exact centered-square projections

Put \(v=u-1,\ w=v^2\). The true physical clocks are \(c=x-\gamma-\gamma v,\ d=x-\gamma+\gamma v\), and \(k=2-w\). The true star tail after its exact forbidden factor \(v(v^2-1)\) is \(A+B w=0\); these symbols denote scalar coefficient polynomials, not the tableau matrix. For a literal silent clock \(z\in\{a,b,q\}\), both actual affine reverse comparisons \((x-z)H_*(c)-k(c-z)H_*(x)=0\) and the same equation at \(d\) hold. Their average is a polynomial \(G(w)\) of degree at most two. The nodal columns do not distinguish an exchange of the two pegs, so \(N\) is a polynomial of degree at most four in \(w\) (degree at most three for the six-peg minors).

For any such polynomial \(C(w)=\sum c_i w^i\), take the homogeneous projection \(\sum_{i=0}^m c_i(-A)^i B^{m-i}\) with its true degree bound \(m\). Its value is zero at an actual solution, including simultaneous \(A=B=0\). It does not invert \(B\) or select one star orientation. For physical silent 2 or 3 the projected \(G,N\), after exact guard division, have bidegrees \((\deg_s,\deg_\gamma)=(9,29),(26,79)\); for physical silent 4 they are \((10,29),(24,79)\). The removed factors are only powers of \(\gamma,L_4(a),L_4(b)\) recorded in the source.

In the nonliteral six-peg case also remove the normalized \(V\)-factor from each projected minor. It is a true guard because the fourth-anchor condition here is regular, not an accidental common factor in a reduction. The four resulting degrees are \((18,55),(17,54),(16,54),(16,54)\). At the good specialization \((1129,t=203,\gamma=2)\) the full displayed \(s\)-degrees are preserved and the pair in the literal cases has gcd one. For the six-peg case a recorded integer linear combination of the three later projections has gcd one with the first. Their exact \(s\)-resultant is therefore a nonzero polynomial in \(\gamma\); all characteristic-zero tableau candidates in the corresponding chart must make it vanish. The specialization is only a witness that this polynomial is nonzero, not a claim that testing \(\gamma=2\) disproves any other diagonal.

## Rear arithmetic: exact nested-resultant projections

Now \(d=x-2\gamma u=2c-x\). Let \(H_{\rm r}=-2+u+4u^2-u^3\), \(J_{\rm r}=2+3u-12u^2+4u^3\). The actual arithmetic tail gives the necessary equation
\[
 H_{\rm r} H_*(d)+2J_{\rm r}H_*(c)-kJ_{\rm r}H_*(x)=0 .
\]
If the ordinarily silent row is literal, use also its actual known-clock comparison \(G_{\rm raw}=(x-z)H_*(c)-k(c-z)H_*(x)=0\). No division by \(H_{\rm r}\), \(J_{\rm r}\), or equality of actual and virtual silent amplitudes is assumed. The tail and comparison have exact \(u^2,u\) padding, respectively. After removing those and already nonzero homogeneous coefficient factors, the actual equations \(F,G\) have \(u\)-degrees six and four. Form the exact \(D(\gamma,s)=\mathrm{Res}_u(F,G)\), then divide only listed true factors (including a normalized \(H_5\) factor). The retained bidegrees are \((27,96)\) for literal 2/3, and \((33,96)\) for literal 4. Their nodal determinant \(N\) is necessary as above.

With six known ordinary pegs and no known silent root, instead use \(D=\mathrm{Res}_u(F,N_0)\) with the first of the four nodal minors, normalized by the exact listed true factors including relative \(V\). All four minors still vanish individually at a candidate. The exact symbolic resultant has \(s\)-degree at most \(6\cdot3+6\cdot9=72\), attained at the good reduction. It is not necessary to expand its characteristic-zero coefficients to prove the diagonal finiteness.

At \((1129,t=203,\gamma=2)\) the full \(s\)-degree is preserved, and in **every** irreducible reduced \(D\) component the whole degree-six tail polynomial retains its leading unit. In the literal case \(G+N\) is coprime to \(F\) in every such component. In the six-peg case the sum of the four divided exact minors is coprime to \(F\) there. Write \(J_0\) for the indicated sum. The explicit necessary univariate polynomial is
\[
 \mathrm{Res}_s\!\bigl(D(\gamma,s),\ \mathrm{Res}_u(F(\gamma,s,u),J_0(\gamma,s,u))\bigr).
\]
It must vanish at any true candidate. The good specialization certifies it is nonzero: the inner resultant is a unit at every reduced \(D\) root, since \(F\) retains degree and \(J_0\) has no common root with it. The outer resultant uses the full specialized \(D\) degree. The other polynomial may lose leading terms under specialization: a padded resultant is still nonzero if one polynomial retains full degree and there is no finite common root (the only new factors are powers of the retained leading coefficient). Thus this witness neither deletes a residue component nor assumes generic leading values at an actual unknown diagonal.
