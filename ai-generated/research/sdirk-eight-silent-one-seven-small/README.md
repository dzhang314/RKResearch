# Detected seven-active single-invisible tableaux need six distinct active clocks

At the ten-stage strict SDIRK8 floor over a characteristic-zero field, suppose only stage one has nonzero quadratic residual and its weight is zero. With seven active physical stages, suppose \(B\boldsymbol h\ne0\) for \(\boldsymbol h=(A-\gamma I)\boldsymbol g\). This packet excludes **every case with at most five distinct active clocks**. The six- and seven-clock detected branches remain open here. Four/five undetected and higher detected cases have their own packets. No unrestricted change to **10–11**.

Use \(x=c_{10}=1-\gamma\), \(c=c_9=x-\gamma u\), \(k=1+2u-u^2\), \(N=A-\gamma I\), and \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol b^{\mathsf T}M\). The reverse \(Z_3\) basis is the actual pair \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\). At \(k\ne0\), \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B=\boldsymbol wP(C)\) for a nonzero affine \(P\), using \(\boldsymbol w(C-xI)=-k\boldsymbol wN\). At \(k=0\), \(\boldsymbol w\) is physically supported at \(x\). The terminal pair is active and distinct, \(w_{10}=-\gamma^2b_{10}\ne0\), and \(w_9=-\gamma^2b_9k\). If physical stage eight is silent, \(w_8\ne0\) and its actual two successor taps give \(2(c_8-x)=\gamma k(u-2)\).

## Causal and pure multiplicity census

There are at least four ordinary sites (nonzero aggregate weight) by the cubic Hilbert Gram. If all actual active clocks are Gauss, then \(\gamma\) is Gauss. Neither literal front clock \(a,b\) is Gauss at a Gauss diagonal, by the exact split check of the normalized front ratios \(2\pm\sqrt2,3\pm2\sqrt2\). Thus both front rows are silent. The actual reverse triple cannot all be Gauss, so physical stage eight is also silent, giving more than the two available silent quadratic-zero rows. This argument does not assume \(L_4\) nonzero on active rows.

On seven active axes the cubic scalar Gram complement is three-dimensional. The nonzero isotropic \(\boldsymbol h\) imposes one further orthogonality constraint on active pure-four columns. If its active values and \(C\boldsymbol h\) are independent, they span that pure-four space, hence \(L_4(c_i)=Q(c_i)h_i\) with \(Q\) affine. The degree-four paid scalar probes of \(h\) make the signed \(h\)-mass vanish at each of the at most five distinct active clocks. At a non-Gauss clock the affine equation forces a single-valued nonzero \(h\) and thus ordinary mass zero. Therefore the ordinary support is the four Gauss sites. There cannot be two different non-Gauss clocks (each would need a canceled double), and not all active clocks are Gauss as above. This leaves one non-Gauss double, and the second detected clock must be the sole Gauss root of the nonzero affine \(Q\), also doubled. The other three Gauss clocks are singleton. At the non-Gauss double the second defect mass is zero since the first amplitudes are equal and the ordinary weights cancel. The paid second defect moment now makes it zero also at the Gauss double. There, two nonzero detected amplitudes and both zero defect moments require cancellation of the ordinary weights too, contradiction. This covers the shared-root case without assuming that h is initially single-valued at the Gauss double.

It remains that \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h\) are dependent on active axes; the active nonzero defects live at one clock \(r\). At least two active physical occupants are needed by the first paid \(h\) moment. If four distinct sites, all are Gauss, already excluded. At five distinct active sites, the \(r\) group has size two or three. A double has equal nonzero \(h\)'s and opposite weights, hence leaves four ordinary Gauss clocks. If a triple cancels, the other four distinct singleton sites are again the four Gauss clocks. In either situation \(r\) is non-Gauss and all other active clocks are Gauss, with no assumption about a possible extra repetition of a Gauss site. The reverse support analysis and exact **tail** obstructions in the [detected six-active packet](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-six-detected/README.md) use only this clock profile and the support of \(\boldsymbol h\), not the number of actives: the active eighth alternatives are impossible, and with a silent eighth all the exact Gauss/gap fields have both literal front clocks different from \(r\). So the two front rows and eighth would again be silent together. We do not use that earlier packet's subsequent first-active read, which depends on a six-active count.

The only remaining census is **one physically triple active clock \(r\) with nonzero ordinary mass**, and four singleton active clocks, all five ordinary. The nonzero amplitudes in the triple are not assumed equal, and one of its occupants may have \(h=0\). The active vectors \(\boldsymbol h,L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) are independent: if \(L_4\) vanished outside \(r\), the other four distinct sites would all be Gauss; the ordinary \(L_4\) moment and nonzero mass at \(r\) would force \(r\) Gauss too. Thus these two vectors span active \(F_4\). On all nine quadratic-zero physical axes the three vectors \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h,L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) span \(F_4|_U\), by the two literal distinct nonzero-\(h\) front values and the active independence just proved. Every actual pure-four read is therefore a constant times \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) plus an affine clock multiple of \(\boldsymbol h\), even on silent rows.

## Ordinary triple with \(k\ne0\)

The two terminal \(w\)'s are nonzero. Since \(h\) is active only at \(r\), exactly one of \(x,c_9\) is \(r\), and the other is the unique root \(R\) of \(P\). Indeed two distinct zeros of \(P\) would be impossible with these nonzero \(w\)'s. The physical support of \(\boldsymbol w\) is contained in \(\{r,R\}\). Its first two scalar moments make the aggregate at either clock zero, so there is a silent occupant at \(R\) (the active clock \(R\) is singleton). If both literal front clocks are silent, \(R\) is one of them; otherwise the active front clock is \(r\).

With an active eighth stage, its clock differs from \(r,R\) by the actual reflected three-row chronology, so \(w_8=0\). The normalized reverse third quadratic residual is therefore zero. Star would give \(k\ne0\); arithmetic gives the cleared numerator
\[
 (1+4u-4u^2)(-2+u+4u^2-u^3)+4u^2(3u-2)
   =-k(4u^3-12u^2+3u+2).
\]
Divide by the exact forbidden factor \(k\). One of \(x,c_9\) equals a literal front clock \(\gamma A_0\), where \(A_0\) is one of the two normalized front values, so \(\gamma=1/(1+A_0)\) or \(1/(1+A_0+u)\). In this fixed monic cubic quotient \(c_8=x-2\gamma u\). The three distinct ordinary pegs \(x,c_9,c_8\) leave two sites; the signed \(3\times3\) Hankel must be zero. It is a unit in all eight orientations. [Replay](triple_active8.py).

With a silent eighth stage, exactly one of the literal front rows is active (two silent front rows plus stage eight would exceed the silent count). This front clock is \(r=\gamma A_0\). The other silent clock is the eighth at \(R\). If \(r=c_9\), then \(c_8=x\) and the literal silent equation gives \(u=2\), hence \(\gamma=1/(3+A_0)\). If \(r=x\), \(\gamma=1/(1+A_0)\), and no further gap information is needed for the front cut below.

## Ordinary triple with \(k=0\)

The actual basis \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\) shows \(r=x\) or \(c_9\): if the coefficient of \(\boldsymbol wN\) in the active defect row is nonzero, its penultimate value is nonzero; otherwise the defect is supported at \(x\). If physical stage eight is silent, there is one active front clock \(r=\gamma A_0\) as in the last part above. The fixed-gap choices \(u=1\pm\sqrt2\) give \(\gamma=1/(1+A_0)\) or \(1/(1+A_0+u)\). The same prescribed-diagonal front cut applies in all sixteen orientations.

If physical stage eight is active, \(x,c_9,c_8\) are the three distinct ordinary pegs with the fixed \(k=0\) tail phases. The [five-site tail projection](../sdirk-eight-silent-one-six/README.md) gives the exact degree-nine or degree-\((1,8)\) diagonal factors and reconstructs the two missing ordinary sites. In these fields a nondefect \(h=0\) physical row four at its ordinary clock is impossible by \(H_4\). Its possible clocks exclude the occupied singleton \(c_8\), but include both terminal pegs \(x,c_9\), since an occupant of the triple may have \(h=0\). All are checked. A physical row five with \(h=0\) at its ordinary clock is also impossible by the same two literal pure-four cubics from the earlier early-silent projection: these use the actual first row-four atlas and a row-five read with \(h=0\) and \(L_4\) proportionality, which the global profile proves. The extra virtual test at \(c_9/\gamma\) is checked here in addition to the earlier \(x/\gamma\) and two missing ordinary roots.

If both front stages are silent, rows four and five are active. Therefore they must both be at \(r=x\) or \(c_9\) with nonzero \(h\). At this same fixed clock, row-five actual pure-four reads are affine in its \(h\), with the padded row-four read at \(h=h_4\). The derivative rank minor with rows \(2,3,4\) is nonzero in every field after removing only exact factors where the row-four clock itself would repeat a front stage. The rank-three restriction forces \(h_5=h_4\), a literal duplicate.

If one front is active, it is at \(r=a\) or \(b\). Row four must then be the other silent zero: if active it would either have \(h=0\) (excluded) or repeat the literal front clock (also excluded). Row five is active at \(r\) with \(h\ne0\). If its \(h\) equals that front's, it is a literal duplicate. Hence they agree identically as affine functions. In particular the read at \(h=0\) has the \(L_4\) line and is tested by the same earlier pure-four cubics at \(x/\gamma\) or \(c_9/\gamma\).
