# Equal tags at two invisible one-clock stages of strict SDIRK8

Consider a ten-stage strict SDIRK tableau of order at least eight over a characteristic-zero field. Exactly two **physical** stages have nonzero quadratic residual, both have zero weight, both have the same clock, and their residuals are equal. No canceled aggregate of individually nonzero weights is counted as silence.

**Result.** The entire branch with vanishing reverse quadratic restriction on those two stages is impossible. In the nonvanishing branch, four or six active stages are impossible, as are five active stages with only four ordinary sites. The surviving equal-tag branches have either five active stages at five ordinary sites, with undetected cubic defect and the third silent zero at physical row four or five, or seven/eight active stages, with detected cubic defect. This does not change the unrestricted **10–11** interval. The [unequal tags](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-unequal/README.md) are already excluded. The single invisible-stage case is separate.

## 1. Flags, literal prefix, and trailing chronology

Use \(L,U,F_j,Z_j,\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h,\boldsymbol w\) from the [invisible one-clock packet](../sdirk-eight-silent-oneclock/README.md): \(L\) contains the two invisible nonzero residual stages, \(U\) the eight quadratic-zero stages, \(\boldsymbol h=(A-\gamma I)\boldsymbol g\), and \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol b^{\mathsf T}M\). The two \(L\) coordinates have clock \(\gamma\) and residual \(g_1=\gamma^2/2\). Let \(r_2=\operatorname{rank}(Z_2|_L)\). The restriction \(E_2|_L\) has rank one. The saturated support identities give \(\operatorname{rank}(Z_3|_L)=1\), \(\operatorname{rank}(E_3|_L)=2-r_2\), and
\[
 \dim(F_4|_U)=8-\dim(W_3\cap U)=8-(6-1)=3.
\]
The one-dimensional pure row space supported on \(U\) in \(Z_3\) contains both \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B\) and \(\boldsymbol w(C-\gamma I)\). The first two nonfirst physical stages have \(g=0\), at distinct clocks \(a,b\) of the two first-three phases; both have \(h\ne0\). Thus \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h\) have independent restrictions to \(U\).

The second invisible stage cannot be row four. Its same \(c,g\) as stage one makes the three modified strict moment equations at \(\gamma,a,b\) homogeneous: the cubic pure residual at a row is \(4\gamma g/3+2h/3\), and \(h_4=g_1A_{41}\) if row four is the second invisible. The modified coefficient for the \(\gamma\) donor in the third equation is \(2\gamma^2/3\). The invertible matrix gives a literal duplicate of stage one. Hence row four is quadratic-zero. For its normalized clock \(s=c_4/\gamma\), let \(H_4(s)=h_4/\gamma^3\), recovered by that same three-tap linear system; it is a cubic. It cannot repeat \(a\) or \(b\), since at such a clock the same system gives the padded earlier row, violating stage saturation. At \(H_4(s)=0\), its sole invisible feed vanishes and the other two feeds give \((A\boldsymbol h)_4\ne0\). The [exact cubic field check](equal_six.py) tests the latter assertion at every root of both front cubics.

Whenever an active stage has \(h=0\), the two physical invisible strict feeds have zero sum, because their common nonzero tag multiplies that sum to give \(h\). Thus they contribute zero to **every scalar clock moment** of that row, even if their individual strict feeds are nonzero. A pure read of \(\boldsymbol h\) at the invisibles does not necessarily cancel. In particular, when used below, the column \((C-\gamma I)\boldsymbol h\) kills both invisible inputs without any further assumption.

Write \(x=c_{10}=1-\gamma\), and \(\boldsymbol w_{10}=-\gamma^2 b_{10}\ne0\). Both terminal weights are nonzero, \(c_9\ne x\), and \(A_{10,9}\ne0\). If \(b_8=0\) then \(w_8\ne0\) by the two strict weighted successor equations \(D(1)\) and the pure reverse quadratic row. Set \(u=(x-c_9)/\gamma\ne0\), \(k=1+2u-u^2\), and \(N=A-\gamma I\). The basis \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\) of \(Z_3\) is detected by its last two coordinates. Literal evaluation there gives
\[
 \boldsymbol w(C-xI)=-k\boldsymbol wN .
\]
For \(b_8=0\) the same unnormalized two weighted taps give \(2(c_8-x)=\gamma k(u-2)\), as checked in the [earlier Lean receipt](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/SDIRK8UnequalInvisibleReceipts.lean).

If \(b_8\ne0\) the actual last three rows can be locally reflected using their nonzero weights. Their clocks are distinct, and their reflected clocks are either arithmetic or satisfy the star sum \(y_2+y_3=4\gamma\). The [exact Gauss triple check](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-unequal/gauss_triple.py) excludes having all three clocks Gauss. If \(k=0\), those rows instead give fixed clocks \(c_9=1-\gamma A'\), \(c_8=1-\gamma B'\), with \(A',B'\) the same two normalized front phases with either orientation. The exact gcd in the [fixed tail check](../sdirk-eight-silent-oneclock/check_tail.py) shows \(c_8,c_9\) cannot both be Gauss. This stronger statement is used even when \(x\) is not Gauss.

An ordinary site here is a clock whose aggregate active weight is nonzero. There are at least four such sites by the nonsingular cubic Hilbert Gram. Precisely four are the four roots of \(L_4=70X^4-140X^3+90X^2-20X+1\) by precision seven; we say they are Gauss. With four active physical rows, their weights are nonzero and the scalar powers through degree three detect all four coordinates, so all active pure degree-four columns vanish. This last inference is **not** applied to repeated physical clocks.

## 2. The entire \(r_2=0\) branch

Now \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wC\) both lie in the supported \(Z_3\) line. Terminal evaluation gives \(\boldsymbol w(C-xI)=0\), so \(\boldsymbol w\) lives solely at the terminal clock and \(k=0\).

### Detected defect: \(B\boldsymbol h\ne0\)

The active \(h\) lives at \(x\), and its terminal value is nonzero. There are at least six active stages: the nonzero active \(\boldsymbol h\) is isotropic in the nondegenerate orthogonal complement of the cubic scalar span, so that complement has dimension at least two. It is impossible that both different front clocks are active. At six active stages the sole active \(h\) clock either has two physical occupants whose nonzero defects agree and weights cancel (from paid \(h\) and \(h^2\)), or has at least three occupants, leaving at most four ordinary sites altogether. In either case all other active clocks are Gauss. A silent eighth stage would have \(w_8=0\), since \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B\) is a nonzero multiple of \(\boldsymbol w\), impossible. So \(c_8,c_9\) would be Gauss, contradicting the fixed tail check.

At seven active stages precisely one of \(a,b\) is active, and it equals \(x\). Thus \(\gamma\) belongs to the finite set \(1/(1+a/\gamma)\), \(1/(1+b/\gamma)\), all non-Gauss. Row four is active with \(h_4=0\), and its clock differs from the three tail pegs, checked by \(H_4\) at their values. If two occupants at \(x\) cancel, five remaining physical stages can carry only four or five ordinary sites. Four ordinary sites would make both tail anchors Gauss: a canceled pair outside \(x\) would leave fewer than four, so both anchors would be ordinary. With five ordinary sites the three pegs \(c_4,c_8,c_9\) leave two unknowns and force the signed \(3\times3\) Hankel determinant to vanish. Its polynomial in \(c_4\) is coprime to \(H_4(c_4/\gamma)\). If three stages occupy \(x\), it cannot cancel without making the outside four Gauss. It is itself non-Gauss, so there are five ordinary sites, now pegging \(x,c_8,c_9\). The Hankel is nonzero except one arithmetic orientation; there the reconstructed monic residual quadratic is coprime to \(H_4\). Four or more at \(x\) cannot satisfy the ordinary quadrature. [Replay](equal_detected.py).

### Undetected defect: \(B\boldsymbol h=0\)

The front \(a,b\) stages are silent, and there are four to six active zeros. On the active set \(F_4\) has rank at most one: \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h\) vanish there but are independent on \(U\). If \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) is nonzero on the active set, it spans the pure line. If the active set consists of distinct Gauss sites and has size four, every active grade-four pure read is zero.

At six active stages, \(b_8\ne0\): the only silent zero stages are the front pair, and a silent invisible stage eight would contradict \(w_8\ne0\). The two fixed trailing anchors cannot both be Gauss, so \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) spans the active pure line. Row four is active with \(H_4(s)=0\), and its nonzero \(A\boldsymbol h\) read makes \(c_4\) non-Gauss. Since \(\boldsymbol w\) annihilates \(\boldsymbol1\), there is another physical zero stage at \(x\) besides the terminal. If \(x\ne a,b\) it is active. Four ordinary sites are impossible: \(c_4\) must cancel at its non-Gauss clock, and either this is \(x\) (leaving the two tail anchors Gauss) or the additional repetition at \(x\) leaves too few ordinary sites. If \(x=a\) or \(b\), it is itself non-Gauss, so four ordinary sites would require two distinct canceled pairs as well as four Gauss sites.

If there are five ordinary sites and \(x\ne a,b\), then \(c_4=x\) gives the finite diagonal \(\gamma=1/(1+s)\) and the fixed three-peg Hankel excludes it. Otherwise the four distinct pegs \(c_4,x,c_8,c_9\) leave one missing site. Their first two rank-one moment minors have exact common diagonal projection supported solely at zero, inadmissible. This projection treats \(s\) in its exact monic cubic. For \(x=a\) or \(b\) again the diagonal is fixed. With five ordinary sites, the same rank-one minors exclude it at every root of \(H_4\). With six, the four pegs reconstruct the two missing nodes by a signed \(2\times2\) pivot. The next active row after row four (physical five or six) has only \(a,b,c_4\) in its strict scalar feed after the off-group cancellation. Its actual \(\tau_4=A\boldsymbol c^{\odot3}-\boldsymbol c^{\odot4}/4\) must be on the same \(L_4\) line as row four. The corresponding quartic is coprime to the reconstructed nodal quadratic. [Replay](equal_six.py).

At five active stages, a canceled pair cannot leave four ordinary sites. If \(b_8\ne0\), four ordinary sites would make both fixed tail anchors Gauss. With five ordinary sites one physical silent zero has \(w_i\ne0\) at the terminal clock, because there are no repeated active clocks. When row four is active, the four-peg rank-one check applies. If row four is silent and \(x=a\) or \(b\), the only other stage at \(x\) is that front stage (row four cannot repeat a front clock). Then \(\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol h=0\) contradicts its nonzero \(h\) and the zero terminal \(h\). Hence \(x=c_4\), with \(h_4=0\) by the same pure relation; the fixed three-peg Hankel excludes this.

If the eighth stage is silent, it is a zero stage at \(x\) (not an invisible), so row four is active. Four ordinary sites would make \(\gamma,c_4\) Gauss, contradicting the literal \(H_4(c_4/\gamma)=0\) at every Gauss pair. With five distinct ordinary sites, the three pegs \(c_4,x,c_9\) leave two missing. The \(3\times3\) Hankel gives a degree-nine diagonal polynomial after inserting \(H_4\); the \(2\times2\) Hankel is nonzero by signed Vandermonde, because the missing two ordinary masses and the nodal factors there are nonzero. The guarded exact projections reconstruct the missing quadratic, and the same actual \(\tau_4\) quartic of the next active row excludes it. The four retained cubic/nonic fibers lift their unit tests at \(1009,t=285\). [Replay](equal_five.py).

At four active stages, \(b_8=0\) by the fixed trailing pair. It is a silent quadratic-zero stage at \(x\). There is precisely one extra silent zero besides the front pair and stage eight; it must be literal row four, since if that row were active its nonzero \(A\boldsymbol h\) would contradict clean pure degree four at four distinct Gauss sites. The first two active rows precede stage eight. Write \(r,r'\) for their actual Gauss clocks, and \(z=c_4\). The first active row has its strict scalar feeds solely at \(a,b,z\), giving
\[
 {\cal J}_r((X-a)(X-b)(X-z))-\gamma(r-a)(r-b)(r-z)=0,
 \qquad {\cal J}_r f=\int_0^r f(X)\,dX.
\]
This is linear in \(z\) with a guarded nonzero slope. The first two actual strict aggregates are reconstructed at \(a,b,z\) and \(a,b,z,r\) respectively. On four active Gauss sites every pure degree-five column is in the shifted \(L_3\) line by orthogonality to scalar degree at most two. Comparing the two literal rows on \(\tau_5=A\boldsymbol c^{\odot4}-\boldsymbol c^{\odot5}/5\) and \(A(C-\gamma I)\boldsymbol h\) gives two independent equations. They generate the unit ideal in every Gauss orientation. The second equation does not assume either invisible stage has \(h=0\). [Replay](equal_four.py).

## 3. More exclusions when \(r_2=1\)

The rank \(E_3|_L=1\) gives \(h=0\) at both invisible stages. The second invisible index \(j\) is at least six: at row five its \(h=0\) forces the tap from stage one to vanish. Its first three strict scalar reads at the remaining three distinct prior zero clocks would all be homogeneous, giving a literal duplicate of stage one. Thus \(j\in\{6,7,8\}\) since the terminal pair is active.

### Four active, or five active and four ordinary

The active defect is undetected by the cubic Gram argument. If \(b_8\ne0\), the last three active clocks cannot all be Gauss. A silent invisible stage eight would have \(c_8=\gamma\), contradicted by the literal silent-eighth equation at every Gauss \((\gamma,c_9)\). At five active stages with four ordinary sites there is no canceled pair, so all active are Gauss. The literal row-four \(H_4\) gate then forces row four silent, making it the only silent zero besides \(a,b\). Hence a silent eighth would be invisible, already excluded. At four active stages, if stage eight is a silent *zero*, physical row four must again be the other extra silent zero (pure degree four is clean on the four active sites). The first active sees only \(a,b,z=c_4\) in its strict scalar feed. The same linear cubic nodal equation recovers \(z\), and the actual \(A\boldsymbol h\) read is nonzero in every Gauss pair. This time using \(h=0\) at both invisible inputs is legitimate because \(r_2=1\). It contradicts pure degree four on four active nodes. All claims here use the [Gauss replay](equal_four.py) and do not identify an ordinary site with a physical stage when weights repeat.

### Six active with detected defect

In the two-dimensional nondegenerate orthogonal complement of the cubic scalar powers on six active rows, the nonzero isotropic \(\boldsymbol h\) spans its own orthogonal complement. Both \(C\boldsymbol h\) and \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) are in that complement and perpendicular to \(\boldsymbol h\) by paid degree-seven pairings; thus both are proportional to \(\boldsymbol h\). The active defect lives at one clock and every active clock outside it is Gauss.

If \(\gamma\ne1/2\), the supported pure row \(\boldsymbol w(C-\gamma I)\) is nonzero at the terminal, so the one active defect clock is \(x\). If \(c_9\ne\gamma\), then \(w_9=0\) from \(h_9=0\), so \(\boldsymbol w(C-xI)\) vanishes at both terminal coordinates; by the two-coordinate \(Z_3\) basis it is zero. This contradicts the nonzero invisible restriction of \(\boldsymbol w\), since that restriction is at \(\gamma\ne x\). Hence \(c_9=\gamma\) is Gauss, and \(x=1-\gamma\) is also Gauss. This makes the entire active set Gauss; an active eighth fails the Gauss triple, and a silent eighth has \(c_8=\gamma\) by \(b_8h_8=0\), \(w_8\ne0\), and the supported pure row proportionality. The latter is excluded by the silent-eighth Gauss gate.

If \(\gamma=1/2\), then \(x=\gamma\) is not Gauss; \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)\) proportional to \(\boldsymbol h\) forces nonzero \(h_{10}\). If \(k\ne0\), \(\boldsymbol w(C-\gamma I)=-k\boldsymbol wN\) spans the supported pure line but vanishes at the terminal, contradiction. If \(k=0\), then \(c_9=1-\gamma A'\) and it is non-Gauss in both orientations, though \(h_9=0\). [Replay](equal_four.py).

### Six active with undetected defect

Now \(a,b\) are the only silent zeros and physical rows four and five are active, before the second invisible. Use scale-free clocks \(s=c_4/\gamma,\ y=c_5/\gamma\). All strict scalar feeds from the invisible pair cancel, and its cubic-defect feed is also zero because both invisible \(h\) are zero. Row four has \(H_4(s)=0\) and a nonzero \(A\boldsymbol h\) read. Row five's three strict scalar reads fix its taps at \(a,b,c_4\). The active grade-four pure rank is one, so its \(\tau_4\) and \(A\boldsymbol h\) must have the same cross ratio as row four. The resulting quartic in \(y\) has a known factor \(y-s\), a literal duplicate at row five because it precedes the second invisible. Both residual cubics are exactly coprime to the \(A\boldsymbol h\) read; they also have no root at \(a/\gamma,b/\gamma,s\). Thus both active reads are nonzero.

The active \(L_4\) vector cannot vanish identically: all active clocks Gauss would make \(\gamma,c_4\) Gauss, violating row four. Hence \(c_4,c_5\) are both non-Gauss, because the nonzero reads are on its line. Four ordinary sites would require two separate canceled pairs and four active Gauss sites, too many. The ratio to \(L_4\) at these first two active rows gives a diagonal quartic
\[
 (A\boldsymbol h)_4 L_4(\gamma y)-(A\boldsymbol h)_5 L_4(\gamma s)=0.
\]
Use the scale-free versions of the two reads in this polynomial. The fixed monic \((3,3,4)\) projection verifies the leading coefficient is a unit. The terminal \(x\) is different from both pegged front clocks (substitute respectively \(\gamma=1/(1+s),1/(1+y)\) in the diagonal quartic). There are at least these three distinct known pegs for the ordinary rule. If there are five ordinary sites their signed \(3\times3\) Hankel must vanish; it is a unit in the quartic quotient.

If all six clocks are distinct, the third active row has preceding strict scalar clocks \(a,b,c_4,c_5\). Its first four scalar moments, with \(\tau_4\) prescribed by the active \(L_4\) line, recover all strict aggregates. The prescribed \(A\boldsymbol h\) read gives a quartic for its clock \(z\) in scaled time. Its two known padded roots \(s,y\) divide it exactly and are inapplicable by distinctness. This yields a guarded quadratic for \(z\). The four pegs \(\gamma s,\gamma y,\gamma z,x\) fix the remaining two nodal roots by the signed \(2\times2\) reconstruction.

At the fourth active clock there is just one further strict scalar predecessor, at \(\gamma z\), and the four scalar moments leave one free aggregate tap. The actual \(A\boldsymbol h\) read fixes it since the remaining pivot is a unit. On six distinct clocks the three polynomials \(L_3,L_4,XL_4\) are independent and pure at grade five; they span the active pure-five subspace by ordinary quadratic scalar rank. Their evaluation determinant at the first three active sites is verified a unit. The actual \(\tau_5\) on row four (in active chronology) must therefore be the value of their precise linear combination read on those first three sites. The residual is a unit in the final nodal quadratic. The [fixed-degree replay](equal_undetected_front.py) checks all quotients in degrees \((3,3,4,2,2)\) at \(1129,t=203\), including the divisions, rather than testing candidate numerical roots.

### Five distinct active sites with both physical rows four and five active

This is already excluded by the same front projection and its three-peg Hankel unit. That argument uses only (h_4=h_5=0), the actual two literal rows before the second invisible, and the one-dimensional active pure-four line. These still hold with five active sites; their distinctness makes the (L_4) vector nonzero. Thus the extra silent zero beyond (a,b), if there are five active sites, must be physical row four or five.

## Replay and proof boundary

Run `python3 check_equal.py` with `sympy==1.14.0` and `python-flint==0.9.0`. Once these libraries are installed, the check is offline and reads only prior sibling research code.

All good-reduction tests use fixed finite monic bases over the local coefficients at the indicated prime. The Gauss algebra can be built successively from the two quadratic front orientations, the monic quartic for \(\gamma\), and the monic complementary Gauss-site factors (dividing out the simple, labeled roots). The chosen prime splits all four roots, so the tests exhaust its reduced components; the linear recovery of \(z\) and all tap denominators are units there. For the other fibers a nonzero multiplication determinant (or unit ideal from Euclidean elimination) modulo the prime lifts to characteristic zero in these fixed bases. This does **not** require the hypothetical tableau to admit reduction. In particular no ordering or positivity of the coefficients is assumed.

The [Lean receipt](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/SDIRK8EqualInvisibleReceipts.lean) verifies the common-tag moment identity and the exact signed two-site Hankel and reconstruction formulas over commutative rings. These compiled in Lean/Mathlib 4.36.0 with standard axioms. The flag argument, chart census, and finite-algebra arithmetic are the source-level proof above, not a single end-to-end Lean theorem.

The [low-active follow-up](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-low/README.md) excludes the last five-active placement cases left here. With this, at most six active zero-residual stages are impossible in the two-invisible-stage single-clock chart.

The [high-active follow-up](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-high/README.md) excludes all seven-active configurations, all midpoint diagonals, and all cases with a vanishing clock aggregate of the reverse quadratic row, now including the two separate arguments when row eight is invisible. The remaining cases in this chart have eight active stages at seven or eight distinct clocks, a non-midpoint diagonal, and a nonzero clock aggregate. This does not exclude a ten-stage method in general.
