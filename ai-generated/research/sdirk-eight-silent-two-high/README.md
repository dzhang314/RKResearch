# High-active equal tags at two invisible one-clock stages of strict SDIRK8

Use the [equal-tag chart](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-equal/README.md) at the ten-stage strict SDIRK8 floor over a characteristic-zero field. The two physically invisible stages have weight zero **individually**, equal nonzero quadratic residual, and the same clock \(\gamma\). Let \(r_2=1\), the only surviving reverse quadratic restriction, and retain all physical stages. The [low-active packet](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-low/README.md) excludes all cases with at most six active stages.

**Verified exclusions and scope correction.** Seven active stages are impossible. With eight active stages, the midpoint and zero-aggregate arguments have two remaining placements to check: the second invisible stage can be physical row eight, which is not covered by a test of the third-to-last **active** clock using the row-eight formula. In particular, do not use this packet to eliminate (i) the non-midpoint, zero-aggregate two-front-group configurations with invisible row eight, or (ii) the midpoint (k=0) configuration with seven distinct active clocks and invisible row eight. The non-midpoint, nonzero-aggregate cases at seven or eight distinct clocks also remain; they have the constraints in §5. No change to the unrestricted **10–11** interval or to the single invisible-stage branch.

## 1. Reverse moment transport

Let \(\boldsymbol h=(A-\gamma I)\boldsymbol g\), \(w_i=(\boldsymbol b^{\mathsf T}M)_i\), \(N=A-\gamma I\), and \(x=c_{10}=1-\gamma\). Both invisible stages have \(h=0\) by the rank \(r_2=1\), and their two \(w\)-coordinates sum to zero by the paid equal tag. Literal rows two and three have distinct clocks \(a,b\ne\gamma\) and nonzero \(h\), while the second invisible stage is at \(j\ge6\). In all higher-active cases the active defect is detected because at least one of these front rows is active. The supported line of \(Z_3\) contains both \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B\) and \(\boldsymbol w(C-\gamma I)\). With \(u=(x-c_9)/\gamma\) and \(k=1+2u-u^2\), the actual two terminal coordinates give
\[
 \boldsymbol w(C-xI)=-k\boldsymbol wN,\qquad
 w_9=-\gamma^2 b_9 k,\qquad w_{10}=-\gamma^2b_{10}\ne0 .
\]
If \(x\ne\gamma\), then \(k\ne0\) since \(r_2=1\). In this case and also when \(x=\gamma,k\ne0\), the line \(\boldsymbol w(C-\gamma I)\) is nonzero, so
\[
 b_i h_i=\lambda(c_i-\gamma)w_i\qquad(\lambda\ne0).       \tag{1}
\]
For a silent eighth stage, the actual chronology says \(w_8\ne0\), hence \(c_8=\gamma\) under (1). The exact silent tail test at Gauss \((\gamma,c_9)\) uses \(2(c_8-x)=\gamma k(u-2)\). If instead the eighth is active, the two reflected star/arithmetic incidences prevent all three terminal clocks being Gauss. These checks include distinct terminal clocks and do not divide by a silent weight.

For a set of distinct sites with monic nodal polynomial \(D\), a row of site weights \(e_i\) has numerator \(P\) defined by \(P(c_i)=D'(c_i)e_i\) and \(\deg P<\deg D\). The partial fraction \(P(X)/D(X)=\sum_i e_i/(X-c_i)\) shows that \(q\) vanishing moments at infinity bound \(\deg P\le\deg D-q-1\). In (1), \(w\) may be nonzero at one additional silent zero with clock \(\gamma\) in the seven-active chart; there is no silent zero in the eight-active chart. The invisible contributions cancel. On the union of active clocks and any such extra site let \(T,S\) be the numerators of the clock aggregates of \(\boldsymbol w\) and \(w_i h_i\), respectively. The first has six zero moments by \(Z_2=\operatorname{Ann}(E_5)\); the second has three by testing on \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h,C^2\boldsymbol h\). The invisible contributions to the second are zero since both invisible \(h\)'s are zero. For \(m=\deg D\),
\[
 \deg T\le m-7,\qquad \deg S\le m-4 .
\]
If \(m=7\) and \(T\ne0\), \(P=S/T\) is a cubic agreeing with \(h\) at every singleton site, except for an explicitly identified coincidence with a silent weight when present. If it differs at only one active clock \(r\), then paid \(\sum_i b_i h_i f(c_i)=0\) for \(\deg f\le4\), together with ordinary precision seven, gives \(I(P f)=d f(r)\), using \(I(f)=\int_0^1 f\). If \(P\ne0\), the cubic Hilbert Gram forces \(d\ne0\); multiplying the test by \(X-r\) gives \((X-r)P\propto L_4\). Hence \(r\) is a Gauss clock and \(P\) is a nonzero Gauss cardinal cubic there. No real embedding or positivity is used. When \(m=8\) and the active sites are distinct, \(S(c_i)=T(c_i)h_i\) on active sites. The ordinary tests through degree three imply \(I(S f)=0\) for \(\deg f\le3\), because \(\deg(T f)\le4\) and the scalar \(h\)-pairings are paid. Thus \(S\propto L_4\).

## 2. Closing the \(T=0\) census under (1)

When every aggregate of \(\boldsymbol w\) is zero, (1) forces the first signed defect mass to vanish at *each* clock. Nonzero active \(h\) can occur only at a clock with at least two active physical occupants, and it cannot occur at \(\gamma\). If a double clock has both its signed first and second \(h\)-masses zero and one nonzero \(h\), the two amplitudes agree and the ordinary mass cancels. This follows from the two equations by subtracting one multiple of the first from the second. There must be at least four ordinary sites; exactly four are the Gauss nodes. Four distinct active clocks therefore already fail the Gauss tail tests, since every site is ordinary (or the actual midpoint would be a Gauss site).

With seven active rows and five distinct clocks, if there are two nonzero-defect sites (necessarily both doubles), the two paid \(\boldsymbol b,\boldsymbol h^{\odot2}\) scalar moments through degree one isolate both second masses, so both ordinary masses cancel. That leaves fewer than four ordinary sites. For six distinct sites there is room for only one double. If there is exactly one active nonzero-defect site, at least one of the front \(a,b\) rows is silent, so the sole silent zero is not at \(\gamma\). For non-midpoint diagonal the nonzero terminal \(h\) puts the only active defect site at \(x\); \(w_9\ne0\) with \(h_9=0\) then forces \(c_9=\gamma\). At midpoint \(k\ne0\) the nonzero \(w_{10}\) is already at \(\gamma\). In either case zero clockwise \(w\) aggregate requires an extra active occupant at \(\gamma\). At six sites two double groups are impossible; at five sites these are exactly the two doubles. The nonzero-defect double cancels ordinary mass by its second moment. The other remaining four sites include \(\gamma\) and must all be Gauss. This is impossible at midpoint. Away from midpoint it forces \(x=1-\gamma\) Gauss as well, though \(x\) is the distinct canceled site.

With eight active \(T=0\), at least two nonzero-defect sites are needed because both front stages are active at distinct clocks. Seven distinct clocks then cannot suffice. At six sites there are two doubles, and at five sites the only possible repeat patterns are triple plus double or three doubles (a quadruple cannot contain two nonzero-defect clocks). For the at most three nonzero-defect sites, the three paid \(\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol h\) scalar probes localize each second defect mass: at \(c\ne\gamma\), (1) identifies that mass with \(\lambda(c-\gamma)\) times the corresponding \(\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol h\) aggregate. There are no silent-\(h\) contributions in this case. Two canceled doubles at five sites already leave fewer than four ordinary. If the diagonal is midpoint, the terminal \(w_{10}\) at \(\gamma\) requires an extra occupant there, separate from all defect doubles, forcing two canceled doubles at five sites or an impossible repeat budget at six sites. At non-midpoint the only remaining patterns have two defect doubles at six sites, or one defect triple and one defect double at five sites. Their defect clocks are \(a,b\). In both cases the terminal \(x\) is one of these two clocks, so \(\gamma\) is one of the two literal front ratios \(1/(1+a/\gamma)\), \(1/(1+b/\gamma)\). Both are non-Gauss in each phase. The other actual active sites are Gauss by the ordinary count. Hence no active site is at \(\gamma\), and \(w_9\ne0\) forces \(c_9\) to be the other defect clock. If physical row eight is active, its clock must be Gauss, since it differs from both terminal sites. At the fixed first-two reversed clocks, each possible star/arithmetic third tail is instead non-Gauss in the exact quadratic field. If row eight is the second invisible stage, this particular argument does not apply: the third-to-last active stage is earlier and does not satisfy the row-eight formula. This placement is open here.

## 3. Seven active with nonzero status under (1)

At non-midpoint with seven distinct active clocks, suppose there is no separate silent \(w\)-site at \(\gamma\). Then \(m=7\), \(T\) is nonzero constant and \(P=S/T\) has degree at most three. It equals the actual \(h\) on each active site except for a possible contribution from a silent \(h\) sharing an active \(\gamma\). With no discrepancy, the ordinary cubic Hilbert Gram makes \(P=0\), contradiction. With a discrepancy only at \(\gamma\) it is the Gauss cardinal cubic at \(\gamma\). This also fails: \(x=1-\gamma\ne\gamma\) is then Gauss, so \(P(x)=0\) contradicts the nonzero terminal \(h\). At midpoint with \(k\ne0\), the terminal \(\gamma\) is already an active clock and \(w_9\ne0\), so there cannot be a separate site. The same cubic discrepancy alternative fails because the midpoint is not Gauss.

Thus seven distinct active clocks require non-midpoint and a separate silent zero at \(\gamma\) with \(w\ne0\). For six distinct active clocks, nonzero transport also forces such a separate site (otherwise \(m\le6\)). Both front zero rows are active. On seven active coordinates the ordinary cubic Gram complement has dimension three, and all pure degree-four columns lie in the orthogonal complement of the nonzero isotropic \(h\) there. Since \(h,Ch\) are independent at the two front rows, they span the active pure-four space. In particular \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)=Q(\boldsymbol c)\odot\boldsymbol h\) there for some affine \(Q\). The literal first-four rank check from the [unequal chart](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-unequal/README.md) now forces the sole silent zero to be **physical row four** (otherwise both literal degree-four reads on the first three zero rows would give an excluded duplicate). Its clock is \(\gamma\), with \(h_4/\gamma^3=H_4(1)\ne0\).

If there are six active clocks, exactly one active clock \(r\) is doubled. The cubic transport \(P=S/T\) matches \(h\) at the singleton sites and the separate silent site at \(\gamma\). The alternative \(P=0\) contradicts (1) at a singleton active clock, so \(P\) is the Gauss cardinal cubic at \(r\). The active affine \(Q\) must be proportional to \(X-r\) since it agrees with \(L_4/P\) at at least two singleton active clocks with nonzero \(h\). Consequently \(L_4(\gamma)=Q(\gamma)h_4\) holds on the silent stage too.

If there are seven active clocks, use \(S\propto L_4\) since the separate silent site makes \(m=8\). It is nonzero by (1), evaluation at the two distinct active front stages, and \(T\) having degree at most one. Evaluation at these front stages and the silent stage shows that the same affine \(Q\), proportional to \(T\), satisfies \(L_4(\gamma)=Q(\gamma)h_4\).

In either branch \(\gamma\) satisfies an exact quartic. Normalize \(A_0=a/\gamma,B_0=b/\gamma,H_a=h_a/\gamma^3,H_b=h_b/\gamma^3,H_s=H_4(1)\). The quartic is obtained by linear interpolation:
\[
 (B_0-A_0)H_aH_b L_4(\gamma)
 -H_s\{(B_0-1)H_b L_4(\gamma A_0)
          +(1-A_0)H_a L_4(\gamma B_0)\}=0.                 \tag{2}
\]
The [exact replay](seven_distinct.py) gives one monic degree-four field in each phase, non-Gauss diagonal, nonconstant \(Q\), and \(\gcd(Q,L_4)=1\). In the six-clock case the last guard contradicts the forced Gauss root \(r\). In the seven-clock case there is no pole at any actual clock and \(h=L_4/Q\). The comparison of (1) at the last two literal rows gives \((x-\gamma)h_9=k(c_9-\gamma)h_{10}\). Its guarded projection in \(u=(x-c_9)/\gamma\) is cubic. A silent eighth requires \(c_8=\gamma\); its actual unnormalized equation is coprime to this cubic. An active eighth has literal \(c_8=x-\gamma(2-u)\) or \(x-2\gamma u\). The five actual pegs \(a,b,c_8,c_9,x\) leave two unpegged ordinary sites. The paid \(h,h^2\) determine the two inverse-pole moments, so the signed Hankel of the quadrature weighted by \(Q^{-2}\) is defined through the necessary degree nine. It is a unit in every retained cubic tail fiber at \(1009,t=285\), rather than the required zero. This excludes all nonzero-transport seven-active configurations under (1).

## 4. Closing the remaining midpoint branches

When \(x=\gamma,k\ne0\), (1) makes every active \(h\) at \(\gamma\), in particular \(h_{10}\), zero. For eight active and seven distinct clocks with \(T\ne0\), \(P=S/T\) is a cubic agreeing with actual \(h\) except possibly on the double. If the doubled site is \(\gamma\), then \(S(\gamma)=0\) as both \(h\)'s there are zero, so there is no discrepancy there. Otherwise the Gauss cardinal alternative at the double has \(P(\gamma)\ne0\), impossible because \(\gamma\) is not Gauss and is then a singleton with \(h=0\). Eight distinct clocks give \(S\propto L_4\), but \(S(\gamma)=0\) at the terminal, so \(S=0\). Then every nonzero \(h\) must be at a root of the affine \(T\), contradicting the two distinct nonzero front defects.

Now assume \(x=\gamma,k=0\). Here \(\boldsymbol w\) is physically supported at \(\gamma\), and its invisible pair has zero aggregate. Thus another zero row at \(\gamma\) has \(w\ne0\) to balance \(w_{10}\). The row \(\boldsymbol wN\) gives the supported \(Z_3\) line after subtraction of a multiple of \(\boldsymbol w\), by rank \(r_2=1\). At the penultimate coordinate \(w_9=0\) but \((\boldsymbol wN)_9=w_{10}A_{10,9}\ne0\), hence \(h_9\ne0\). The actual \(c_9=1-\gamma A'\) is distinct from the front clocks \(a,b\) and from \(\gamma\). All four of those possible physical clocks \(a,b,c_9,\gamma\) are non-Gauss in the exact phase fields.

In this midpoint chart let \(R,S_B\) be the numerators of the clock aggregates of \(b_i h_i\) and \(b_i h_i^2\), on the \(n\) distinct active clocks. Then \(\deg R\le n-6\) and \(\deg S_B\le n-3\) from the five and two paid moments. At a clock with a single-valued \(h\), \(S_B=Rh\) there, regardless of ordinary mass.

* **Seven active.** Two distinct active \(h\)-clocks exist (both front clocks, or one front and the penultimate), so \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)=Q(\boldsymbol c)\odot\boldsymbol h\) is again affine on active rows by the dimension-three Gram complement. There are at least three distinct non-Gauss active clocks: a front clock, \(c_9\), and \(\gamma\). If \(R=0\), all those ordinary masses vanish since their \(h\) are nonzero and single-valued from the affine relation. So \(n\le6\) would leave fewer than four ordinary sites. If \(n=6\) and \(R\ne0\), \(R\) is constant. When both front rows are active the midpoint affine \(Q\) they fix is nonconstant and coprime to \(L_4\), hence \(h\) is single-valued everywhere. If a front row is silent, it is not at \(\gamma\), so the only doubled active clock must be \(\gamma\); \(h\) is single-valued there because \(L_4(\gamma)\ne0\). In either case \(S_B/R\) is cubic and equals actual \(h\), contradiction by ordinary moments and the Hilbert Gram. For \(n=7\), the additional zero balancing \(w_{10}\) is silent at \(\gamma\); both front rows are active and the literal rank check makes row four silent. The paid \(\sum w_i h_i=0\) gives \(h_{10}=h_4\). The affine \(L_4=Qh\) at the terminal would then give (2) at \(\gamma=1/2\), which the quartics exclude.

* **Eight active.** An active \(\gamma\) repeat is mandatory. If \(R=0\), each of the three distinct nonzero-defect clocks \(a,b,c_9\) needs at least two physical occupants in addition to the \(\gamma\) repeat, so \(n\le4\). Four ordinary sites would include the midpoint, impossible. For \(n=6\), \(R\ne0\) is constant and \(S_B/R\) is cubic. If \(\gamma\) is triple, the only possible discrepancy is there, impossible because \(\gamma\) is not Gauss. If it is double, its \(w\)-entries cancel, so the paid \(\boldsymbol w\boldsymbol h=0\) makes the two actual \(h\)'s equal at \(\gamma\). The only possible discrepancy is the second double at \(r\). The cubic must be a Gauss cardinal there, and therefore matches the two known non-Gauss front defects. This is impossible: the affine \(Q\) fixed by those two front values would have a Gauss root, contrary to the midpoint gcd. If \(n=7\), the only repeat is \(\gamma\), and its two \(h\) again agree. Thus \(S_B=Rh\) at every site. Ordinary tests against cubics and paid \(h\) give \(S_B\propto L_4\) (degree \(\le4\) and \(\deg R\le1\)). It is nonzero by the two nonzero front sites and \(R\ne0\). This recovers \(h=L_4/Q\) with the same exact nonconstant coprime \(Q\). If physical row eight is active there are five distinct pegs \(a,b,\gamma,c_8,c_9\) in every literal phase/orientation. The five-peg signed Hankel with inverse-pole moments is nonzero in all eight exact quadratic-field tests. If row eight is invisible, its clock is not an additional active peg and this argument does not apply. Finally \(n=8\) leaves no repeat at \(\gamma\), impossible by support.

## 5. Precisely remaining eight-active charts

At non-midpoint with seven distinct clocks and \(T\ne0\), the constant \(T\) gives the cubic \(P=S/T\) on the six singleton sites. The possible discrepancy at the one double \(r\) is nonzero (otherwise ordinary paid moments kill \(P\)), so \(r\) is Gauss and \(P\) is its Gauss cardinal cubic. Here \(L_4=Qh\) holds at all physical active rows with \(Q\) proportional to \(X-r\), even if the two amplitudes at \(r\) differ. Interpolation at the known literal \(a,b\) recovers \(Q\). The [guarded shared-root projection](shared_proxy.py) has exact squarefree factor degrees \(4,4,12\) in each phase, after allowing only nonzero diagonal, non-midpoint and nonzero leading coefficient of \(Q\). An actual supported vector of \(E_3\) is
\[
 \boldsymbol h-P(\boldsymbol c)+\frac{P(\gamma)}{g_1}\boldsymbol g.
\]
Its support is precisely contained in the double clock, without assuming those two amplitudes are equal. A general grade-four \(\boldsymbol w\)-transport can gain a nonzero contribution at the invisible \(\gamma\) from different pure-four values at the two invisible nodes. Thus that transport does not yet rule out this discrepancy.

With eight distinct active clocks and non-midpoint, \(T\) is affine and \(S\propto L_4\), giving \(T(c_i)h_i=S(c_i)\) at every actual active row. If \(T\) has a root at an active clock it is Gauss, and (1) forces \(h=0\) there. The profile reduction alone does not handle a constant \(T\), a proper affine pole, or that shared-root discrepancy to completion.
