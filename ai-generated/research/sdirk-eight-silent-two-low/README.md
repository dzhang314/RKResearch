# Closing the last low-active equal invisibility branch at strict SDIRK8

In the [equal-tag one-clock packet](../sdirk-eight-silent-two-equal/README.md), let \(L\) consist of the two separately weight-zero, equal nonzero quadratic residual stages at common physical clock \(\gamma\). Take \(r_2=1\), so the reverse pure quadratic row \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol b^{\mathsf T}M\) is nonzero on \(L\). The remaining five-active branch has five distinct ordinary sites and three silent quadratic-zero rows, at the literal front clocks \(a,b\) and at one other clock \(z\). The prior packet forces this third silent zero to be physical row four or five. This note excludes both, completing every case of two wholly invisible one-clock stages with at most six active stages. The seven/eight-active and single invisible-stage branches are separate; the unrestricted **10–11** interval is unchanged.

## A physical support cut

The cubic defect \(\boldsymbol h=(A-\gamma I)\boldsymbol g\) vanishes at both invisible inputs by \(\operatorname{rank}(E_3|_L)=1\), and at all active rows by the cubic Gram bound. Hence its entire physical support is among the three silent zero rows. Its first two nonzero entries at \(a,b\) are known independently of the third. Since \(\boldsymbol w\) annihilates \(E_5\), the three moments of \(\boldsymbol w\) tested against \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h,C^2\boldsymbol h\) vanish. Multiplying these equations by the coefficients of \((X-b)(X-z)\) forces \(w_a h_a=0\) if \(a\ne z\); likewise for \(b\ne z\). This argument also covers a repeated silent clock and does not infer that either contribution at a repeated clock vanishes separately.

The two invisible coordinates have zero aggregate \(\boldsymbol w\) by the paid quadratic tag. After they are aggregated away, the scalar support of \(\boldsymbol w\) is among the five distinct active clocks and, possibly, the one extra clock \(z\). It has six zero scalar moments through degree five. The Vandermonde argument kills each clock aggregate. In particular, the nonzero terminal component \(w_{10}\) forces \(z=x=c_{10}=1-\gamma\), while \(c_9\ne x\) implies \(w_9=0\). The latter implies \(k=0\), where \(w_9=-\gamma^2 b_9 k\). By the actual reverse identity \(\boldsymbol w(C-xI)=-k\boldsymbol w(A-\gamma I)\), and the nonzero invisible restriction of \(\boldsymbol w\), we have \(x=\gamma=1/2\). Consequently \(z\) differs from \(a,b\); the nonzero \(\boldsymbol w\) at the third silent zero cancels \(w_{10}\), and its weighted \(\boldsymbol h\)-probe forces \(h_z=0\).

## Two literal scalar exclusions

If the third silent zero is row four, the exact normalized cubic requires \(H_4(1)=0\). Its values are \(-1/2\) in the star phase and \((-29+21\sqrt2)/4\) in the arithmetic phase, both nonzero.

Otherwise it is row five; row four is active, and its normalized clock \(s\) satisfies \(H_4(s)=0\). Both literal rows precede the second invisible. At row five the strict stage-one tap is zero since \(h_z=0\), so the three scalar taps at \(a,b,c_4\) are those of the row-five atlas in the prior six-active proof, now evaluated at normalized clock \(1\).

To justify applying that atlas to this *silent* row, \(F_4|_U\) has rank three, while its active restriction has rank one: \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h\) vanish on active rows, remain independent on \(U\), and \(L_4\) is nonzero on five distinct active sites. Thus the latter pair span the exact kernel of the active restriction. The active proportionality between the pure \(\tau_4\) and \(A\boldsymbol h\) columns, fixed by the nonzero \(A\boldsymbol h\) read at row four, extends modulo that kernel to all of \(U\). At row five the kernel is zero coordinatewise since \(h_z=0\). Therefore the same normalized quartic relation holds there. Its literal duplicate factor \(y-s\) is nonzero at \(y=1\), since \(H_4(1)\ne0\). The residual cubic is a unit at \(1\) in both exact front fields, so this row is also impossible.
