# Two unequal invisible residuals at one physical clock are impossible at strict SDIRK8

At the ten-stage strict SDIRK8 floor over a characteristic-zero field, suppose precisely two physical stages have nonzero quadratic residual, both at one physical clock and both with zero weight. If their residuals differ, this packet excludes the tableau. The equal-residual and single-invisible-stage branches are separate. No per-clock aggregate cancellation of nonzero weights is treated as physical silence. The unrestricted interval remains **10–11**.

Use \(L,U,\boldsymbol g,\boldsymbol h=N\boldsymbol g,B,C,M\) as in the [invisible one-clock packet](../sdirk-eight-silent-oneclock/README.md). Put \(\boldsymbol w=\boldsymbol b^{\mathsf T}M\), \(x=c_{10}=1-\gamma\). The off-zero clock is \(\gamma\), as stage one is in \(L\). The two first subsequent rows are zero with distinct clocks \(a,b\) and nonzero \(h_a,h_b\). On \(L\) the two different residuals give \(\operatorname{rank}(E_2|_L)=2\), so the saturated support identity gives \(Z_3|_L=0\), and \(F_4|_U\) has dimension two. In particular \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\) vanish on both physical invisible coordinates. The physical restrictions \(\boldsymbol h,C\boldsymbol h\) span \(F_4|_U\). Every grade-four pure column on \(U\) is therefore affine in the clock times \(h\); in particular use this for \(A\boldsymbol h\), \(A\boldsymbol c^{\odot3}-\boldsymbol c^{\odot4}/4\), and \(L_4(\boldsymbol c)=Q(\boldsymbol c)\odot\boldsymbol h\).

## 1. Exact first-five atlas, independent of the output support

If row four were zero, its only previous off-zero donor would be stage one. The actual three strict taps at \(\gamma,a,b\) are recovered from the usual sum, quadratic read and pure cubic read, using \(h_4=g_1 A_{41}\). On the first three zero rows the columns \(A\boldsymbol h\) and the pure quartic-moment defect must both lie in the span of \(h,Ch\). The two rank-three determinants have exact common factor consisting only of the two earlier clock choices, in both front phases; see [first_four.py](first_four.py). This check is scale-free (\(c/\gamma,g/\gamma^2,h/\gamma^3\)). At either earlier choice the unique strict tap solution gives a literal twin. Thus the second off-zero stage is **physical row four**.

Its clock is \(\gamma\). Its three literal strict taps solve the same invertible moment matrix, with right-hand side \((0,g_4-g_1,(4\gamma/3)(g_4-g_1))\) after moving \(2h_4/3\) into the first column. Consequently
\[
 h_4=\sigma(g_4-g_1),\qquad
 \sigma/\gamma=1\quad\hbox{or}\quad (29-21\sqrt2)/2
\]
in the two front phases (the chosen front sign names the square root). This is a relation between the two physical donors, not an output aggregate. For any later row with defect \(h\), its aggregate strict taps at the common off-zero clock, say \(T_\gamma\), have total cubic-defect feed \(\sigma(h-g_1T_\gamma)\), because \(h\) is the \(g\)-weighted strict feed from the two off-zero donors. This identity is checked in the [Lean receipt](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/SDIRK8UnequalInvisibleReceipts.lean).

At row five put \(s=c_5/\gamma\), \(H=h_5/\gamma^3\). Its strict scalar aggregates at \(1,a/\gamma,b/\gamma\) are determined affinely in \(H\) by the first three scaled reads. The exact pure quartic-moment read and \(A h\) read are each affine in \(H\) (apply the off-group feed identity above). The padded earlier rows solve them at \(s=a/\gamma,b/\gamma\), and at least one affine slope is nonzero at those values, so they would be literal twins (the \(g\)-weighted feed separates the two invisible taps). Removing those factors, the clock resultant of the two affine reads is
\[
                    s-1 \quad\hbox{or}\quad
        s^3-(9+2\sqrt2)s^2+\frac{312+152\sqrt2}{17}s
                         -\frac{108+84\sqrt2}{17}.
\]
The first pure read recovers \(H\) uniquely in every retained fiber; the star value is \(-5/16\). The affine line \(L_4=Qh\), evaluated at stage five, gives a quartic scalar condition in \(\gamma\) over this clock algebra. The exact projection gives irreducible degree four (star) or twelve (arithmetic) over \(\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)\), with the surviving \(s\) recovered by a linear gcd in each diagonal quotient. These two scalar polynomials are coprime to the constant-\(Q\) locus, the shared-root locus \(\gcd(Q,L_4)\ne1\), and \(L_4(\gamma)=0\). These are checked by [variable_gamma.py](variable_gamma.py). In particular the three fixed nonzero defects \(h_a,h_b,h_5\) occur at distinct clocks; the terminal clock \(x=1-\gamma\) is non-Gauss. This calculation did not assume any of those first three zero weights are active.

## 2. Undetected defect, or six active zeros

If \(B\boldsymbol h=0\), pure degree four is clean on all active zero rows, and \(L_4(c_i)=0\) there. If \(b_8\ne0\), the actual normalized last three rows all have Gauss clocks in reflected time. But the literal reverse triple satisfies either \(y_3=2y_2-\gamma\) (arithmetic) or \(y_3+y_2=4\gamma\) (star). Neither can have \(\gamma,y_2,y_3\) Gauss with \(y_2\ne\gamma\), by [the unit test](gauss_triple.py) in the fixed monic quartic/cubic Gauss-pair quotient. Its good reduction at \(1129\) checks all ordered pairs.

If \(b_8=0\), \(w_8\ne0\) by the two strict successor tap equations, so stage eight is a silent **zero** row. Apart from the two front silent zeros, there are then at most two extra silent zeros. With exactly five active rows, stage eight is the sole extra silent zero; the earliest active one sees only \(\gamma,a,b\), violating the previously [verified Gauss cubic gate](../sdirk-eight-silent-oneclock/README.md). Six active rows would leave no such silent eighth. With four active rows, two active rows precede stage eight (the last two weights are nonzero). The remaining extra silent zero \(z\) must precede the earliest active row by that same cubic gate. The [four-active quotient test](../sdirk-eight-silent-three-closure/README.md) applies unchanged to these first two active rows: the actual predecessor clock sets remain \(\gamma,a,b,z\) and possibly one previous active, the same pure degree-four span gives the silent-clock cubic, and the actual active grade-five line gives the same two unit tests. Its proof did not depend on where any further silent zero occurs after both of those rows.

Now suppose the defect is detected. The ordinary cubic Gram is nondegenerate and \(h\) is isotropic in its orthogonal complement, so at least six zero rows are active. Normalize \(h=p/q\), \(q\) monic linear and \(p\propto L_4\). The exact diagonal projections certified the requisite coprimality. At \(n\) distinct *active* zero clocks the aggregate defect interpolants satisfy \(\deg R_h\le n-6,\ \deg S_h\le n-3,\ q S_h=p R_h\) (or \(R_h=0\) already by Vandermonde if \(n\le5\)). Therefore when there are six active rows \(R_h=0\), and all ordinary sites are Gauss; any nonzero defect lives only at an aggregate-canceled non-Gauss clock. With six physical active rows and four ordinary sites, the non-Gauss terminal belongs to the unique canceled double clock. All other active clocks, including \(c_9\), are Gauss.

Write \(u=(x-c_9)/\gamma\ne0\), \(k=1+2u-u^2\) for the literal reverse second residual ratio. The basis \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\) gives \(\boldsymbol w(C-xI)=-k\boldsymbol wN\). If \(k=0\), \(c_9\) is an excluded root by the exact gcd of \(k\) and \(L_4(\gamma(1+u))\). If \(k\ne0\), \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B=\boldsymbol wP(C)\) for some affine scalar \(P\), since \(\boldsymbol h^{\mathsf T}B\in Z_3\) is a linear combination of \(\boldsymbol w,\boldsymbol wN\). Explicitly \(b_i h_i=P(c_i)w_i\). The penultimate component \(w_9\ne0\) forces \(P(c_9)=0\), as \(h_9=0\). An active eighth stage is at another Gauss clock, so its reverse quadratic residual must vanish. That is impossible in the reverse star phase, and in arithmetic its denominator-cleared residual is coprime to \(L_4(\gamma(1+u))\). If the eighth stage is silent, \(w_8\ne0\) and \(P(c_8)=0\), so \(c_8=c_9\). The literal two weighted taps to the later active rows give \(c_8=x+\gamma k(u-2)/2\); this is [checked in Lean](../../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/SDIRK8UnequalInvisibleReceipts.lean). It would require \(2u+k(u-2)=0\), also coprime to the penultimate Gauss equation. The [six-active replay](six.py) checks all three exact gcds in both diagonal fields.

## 3. Seven or eight active zero rows

At least two of \(a,b,c_5\) are active, at different clocks with nonzero \(h\). If \(R_h=0\), ordinary support would consist only of Gauss clocks and these two non-Gauss stages would need their own canceled groups of at least two active rows apiece. This contradicts seven *active* rows, and with eight active would leave exactly four Gauss and two canceled pairs, hence only six distinct clocks. In that eight-active case \(a,b,c_5\) are all active, requiring **three** canceled groups, impossible. Thus \(R_h\ne0\) here and \(q\mid R_h\) forces at least seven distinct active clocks.

When the last three rows are active, use the actual normalized reverse triple (only its local weights are divided). If its third gap is \(v\) and normalized quadratic residual is \(q_3\), the star case has \(v=2-u,q_3=k\) and the arithmetic case \(v=2u\) with its known rational \(q_3\). For \(k\ne0\) the affine relation \(b_i h_i=P(c_i)w_i\) gives the exact scalar equation
\[
                   k u h_8=q_3\{k(u-v)h_{10}+v h_9\} .
\]
The native receipt checks this cross multiplication without requiring a nonzero third reverse residual. With a silent eighth stage, \(w_8\ne0\), \(P(c_8)=0\), and one uses \((x-c_8)h_9=k(c_9-c_8)h_{10}\) with the literal clock just derived. Include \(k=0\) separately in the finite pole polynomial. After substitution of the affine rational \(h\) profile and exact removal of only known nonzero factors, the projected \(u\)-polynomials have degrees six (silent eighth), four (active/star) and seven (active/arithmetic), in either front case.

As in the [three-invisible closure](../sdirk-eight-silent-three-closure/README.md), \(\ell(h)=\ell(h^2)=0\) fixes the two inverse moments of \(q\), hence computes \(\ell(f/q^2)\) through degree nine. Use its signed nodal moments in the [finite replay](check_unequal.py):

* With seven active rows, all seven clocks are distinct. If stage eight is silent the known sites \(a,b,c_5,c_9,x\) are all active. Otherwise pick two known active front sites among \(a,b,c_5\) and the three trailing sites. Their product \(P_5\) leaves at most two ordinary sites, so the three-by-three Hankel determinant of \(\ell(P_5 X^{i+j}/q^2)\) must vanish. Every such determinant is a unit in the finite tail fibers.
* With eight active rows, the trailing three are active (a silent eighth would be off-zero, where \(w\) vanishes, giving an absent column). If only seven distinct sites exist, at least one five-element subset of the six known stage clocks \(a,b,c_5,c_8,c_9,x\) has five distinct sites, and the same Hankel test applies. The replay verifies units for **all** six choices.
* If all eight clocks are distinct, the six pegs \(P_6\) leave just \(c_6,c_7\). The two-by-two signed Hankel reconstruction using moments of \(P_6/q^2\) through its third shift gives their monic quadratic. The literal row-six clock must be one of its roots. In front star, \(c_5=\gamma\), so the third scalar strict-clock cubic again applies to row six and its pure quartic read. In front arithmetic, \(\gamma,a,b,c_5\) are four distinct predecessor clocks. The four actual moments recover every strict aggregate, substituting \(h=L_4/Q\) at row six. The pure \(A h\) read, with the known off-group feed \(\sigma(h-g_1T_\gamma)\), gives its scalar gate. The quadratic quotient verifies that gate a unit in each case.

These are fixed monic good-reduction checks at \(1009,t=285\). The scalar diagonal polynomial retains its degree and squarefree reduction; its finite field factors and the monic tail, nodal and moment pivots are all checked. Units lift as multiplication ranks in the fixed finite bases, so no hypothesis is made that a proposed tableau itself reduces at the chosen prime.
