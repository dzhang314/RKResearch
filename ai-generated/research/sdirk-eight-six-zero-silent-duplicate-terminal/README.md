# Six quadratic-zero rows with all off weights silent with \(d_3=2\): the duplicate terminal clock

Consider the six-zero strict ten-stage SDIRK order-eight census over a characteristic-zero field. In this note **all four off-zero rows are silent**. We treat the two possibilities with \(d_2=1,d_3=2\): four distinct off clocks with a genuine graph, or two distinct off clocks each with two different nonzero residuals and pure-polynomial support. Both charts are impossible over every characteristic-zero field.

## 1. The terminal zero label is repeated

Use the [support rank identity](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-line/README.md). The \(W_2\) off restriction is zero because \(d_3=2\), so its pure row \(\mathbf z^\mathsf T\) vanishes off. But the \(W_3\) off restriction has rank one because \(d_2=1\). Its second pure row \(\mathbf z^\mathsf T N\) therefore does not vanish off, where \(N=A-\gamma I\). The [literal reflected relation](../sdirk-eight-four-zero-gaussian-rear/README.md) implies \(\kappa_R=0\); otherwise \(\mathbf z^\mathsf T N\) would be an affine clock multiple of \(\mathbf z^\mathsf T\) and also vanish off. Thus \(\mathbf z^\mathsf T\) is supported only at the terminal clock. It has a nonzero coordinate at row 10 and annihilates the scalar constant \(1\), so another physical zero row has the same clock as row 10. Stage 8 cannot be silent: the literal ghost would require nonzero \(z_8\) at a clock distinct from the terminal clock.

Here all ordinary mass is on the zero rows, so there are at least four distinct nonzero aggregates and at most five literal labels. If four ordinary labels are live, both stage 8 and stage 9 are Gaussian. Indeed a canceled nonterminal group would need its own duplicate distinct from the terminal duplicate, leaving at most four literal labels and fewer than four live aggregates. The [two-Gaussian zero-ratio reflected projection](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-graph-one-active/README.md#2-four-ordinary-sites-are-impossible) excludes those two Gauss clocks in characteristic zero. Thus there are five live distinct ordinary labels and exactly one duplicate, at the terminal; every singleton zero row is live, but either weight split at the duplicate is allowed.

Normalize by \(\mathbf x=\mathbf c/\gamma\), \(A'=A/\gamma\), \(D=1/\gamma\). The last three distinct live ordinary labels are \(t=D-1\), \(s=D-1-v\), \(r=D-1-w\), where \(v^2-2v-1=0\), \(w=2v\) (arithmetic) or \(2-v\) (star). The first three physical zeros are before stage 8; they consist of the earlier occurrence of \(t\) and the other two live ordinary labels, in arbitrary order.

## 2. A fixed first zero: pure trace, arithmetic third, or four-off front

If support is pure with two off clocks, the first two physical rows are off and the third is zero by the literal forward distinctness. Also a graph first zero at row 3 is exactly this literal third-zero phase. In both cases the first zero is \(a=1+2u\) with \(Q(u)=4u^3-12u^2+3u+2=0\). Take the exact gap field of degree six with checked primitive element \(u+2v\).

If \(a=t\), \(D=1+a\) is fixed. Mask the three rear ordinary labels: the \(3\)-by-\(3\) ordinary Hankel determinant must vanish because only two other ordinary labels remain. The exact determinant is nonzero for both rear phases. If \(a\ne t\) it is also different from \(s,r\), since the only duplicate is at \(t\). Thus four live labels are known; their mask leaves a single ordinary label, with nonzero weighted mask pivot. The two corresponding scalar moment minors have guarded common gcd of degree zero in \(D\) in both phases. This excludes the pure two-varying case and the graph first-zero-three case by ordinary moments alone.

In the graph case the first two rows are off, and there are four off rows in total, so the first zero lies at row 3, 4 or 5. If it is at row 5, all four distinct off clocks precede it. The [literal four-off graph argument](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-graph-four-off/README.md) forces \(P(T)=T^j/2\), \(j=0,1\), by the primitive identity \(JP=-P H_1\) at those four clocks, and then \(a=j+1\) by its diagonal supported read. The additional apparent root \(a=0\) for \(j=1\) is excluded by the nonzero Legendre pure line on the first ordinary zero. The same two masked ordinary tests just given now apply with \(a=1\) or \(2\) over the quadratic rear gap field. The fixed-diagonal determinants and remaining guarded gcds exclude all four monomial/rear combinations.

## 3. Graph first zero at physical row 4

Let \(P_3(T)=(T-t)(T-s)(T-r)\) be the mask of the three rear labels. Ordinary order through degree seven gives a \(3\)-by-\(3\) Hankel determinant for the two unknown ordinary labels. Its guarded polynomial in \(D\) has degree nine in both rear phases. The principal \(2\)-by-\(2\) ordinary pivot is structurally nonzero (two remaining distinct live labels), and its inverse is checked after exact guard removal. The two new clocks are recovered as \(b_0\pm S\) in a free quadratic algebra, using the exact monic quadratic on the remaining labels. The first three chronological zeros have six possible orders: the earlier copy of \(t\) can be first, second, or third, and either sign of \(S\) exchanges the other two labels. All are covered.

On the zero rows \(F_3=GE_1\) is clean. The grade-four and grade-five pure restrictions are the line of \(L_4\) and the span of \(L_3,L_4\), respectively: \(GE_2,GE_3\) have ranks three and four, and the corresponding scalar Legendre evaluations retain independence on five distinct labels, even with the terminal duplicate. All ordinary labels are non-Gaussian by the [five-live-site guard](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-graph-arithmetic-silent/README.md#1-literal-clock-data-and-the-ordinary-cubic). Thus the same first, second and third chronological supported gates \(F,G,H\) apply as in the [five-zero graph pencil](../sdirk-eight-five-zero-graph-arithmetic-silent/README.md#2-three-supported-equations-in-the-same-arithmetic-pencil). The graph is fixed \(P_0\) if the three off prefix clocks are star, or the arithmetic pencil with its actual cubic relation otherwise.

For each placement of \(t\), take the quadratic norm over both orders of the two unknown clocks. The norms of the fixed star gates, regular arithmetic gates, and singular arithmetic gates all have constant joint gcd with the guarded ordinary degree-nine polynomial, in both rear phases. No split assumption for the recovered clock pair and no assumption on the duplicate weight split is required.
