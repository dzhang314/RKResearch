# Exactly two distinct quadratic-zero clocks are impossible in every paid field

Let a strict ten-stage SDIRK tableau of ordinary order at least eight have a nonzero common diagonal $\gamma$. Write $c=A1$, $g=Ac-c^{\odot2}/2$, $C=\operatorname{diag}(c)$, $G=\operatorname{diag}(g)$, and $M=CA-AC-A^2$. A *physical zero* means a literal $g_i=0$, whether or not $b_i=0$. Over every field of characteristic zero or greater than seven, $g$ cannot have exactly two distinct clocks among its physical zeros. This does not rule out a tableau without any zeros, or one with at least three distinct zero clocks.

The [previous two-clock theorem](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-clocks/README.md) already handles characteristic zero. In the new paid positive-characteristic proof, the [exactly-two-physical-zero theorem](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-paid/README.md) handles multiplicity two. Sections 1–4 below prove that any multiplicity of at least three on exactly two distinct clocks is exactly three, then exclude that remaining shape. The two clocks are not assumed to lie in the prime field. Individual weights are not assumed positive or all nonzero.

In combination with the [paid singleton theorem](../sdirk-eight-singleton-paid/README.md) and the single-clock argument in Section 5 of the [two-zero-line theorem](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-line/README.md), this says that any positive literal zero count needs at least three distinct clocks in every paid coefficient field. For the latter argument only invertibility of the ordinary Hilbert matrices through size four and the triangular commutator diagonal are used; both hold in these fields. A commutative-ring corollary requires nonzero ring, $8!$ and $\gamma$ units, all nonzero residuals units, and the difference of the two claimed zero clocks a unit: passing to a maximal-ideal residue field preserves exactly this pattern. No lower-bound gain for arbitrary ten-stage coefficients is asserted.

## 1. Rank and the graph, without ordering

Use genuine forward and reverse spaces $E_j,F_j,W_j,Z_j$ with the standard grades and scalar traces from the [saturated flags](../../lower-bounds/characteristic-zero/sdirk_saturated_weighted_mirror_duality_20261123.md). In the paid fields, the same proofs work: all scalar integration orders are at most eight, the ordinary Hilbert matrices through size four have invertible determinants, and $M$ is triangular with diagonal $-\gamma^2$. Let $U=\ker G$ be the zero-coordinate column subspace; write $U_{\rm row}$ for its row counterpart. The two-sided rank proof in the [two-zero-line theorem](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-line/README.md) applies to any number $m$ of coordinates:
$$m=\dim(E_3\cap U)+\dim(W_2\cap U_{\rm row}).$$
The terminal weight is nonzero by truncation at the stage floor; the terminal residual is nonzero because $b^{\mathsf T}G$ is proportional to $b^{\mathsf T}M$, whose last entry is nonzero. If the residual there were zero, proportionality would force all active clocks onto the two zero clocks, contradicting the ordinary quadrature rank. Thus $Z_2=\langle b^{\mathsf T}G\rangle$, and $F_2=\langle g\rangle$ by $g_1=\gamma^2/2\ne0$.

Let $a,b$ denote the two distinct zero clocks. For a supported column in $E_1$, or a supported row in $W_1$, pair its trace with $(X-a)(X-b)X^i$ for $0\le i\le3$. The product of its linear trace with $(X-a)(X-b)$ is killed by the degree-three Hilbert form. The pure spaces of grade one are zero. Thus $E_1\cap U=W_1\cap U_{\rm row}=0$, and $F_3=GE_1$, $Z_3=W_1G$ are both clean at every zero coordinate. Consequently a supported grade-three column is constant on each zero-clock group and its dimension is at most two. For the same four probes at row grade two, the quadratic trace times the two factors is proportional to the fourth monic shifted Legendre polynomial $L_4$. A zero trace would be pure and both supported and clean, hence zero. This bounds the supported reverse dimension by one. The rank sum forces $m=3$, a two-dimensional supported column space of grade three, and a nonzero supported reverse line of grade two. In particular $a,b$ are distinct fourth Gauss clocks.

The earlier [algebraic supported-column argument](../sdirk-eight-two-zero-clocks/README.md#only-one-shape-could-have-more-than-two-zeros-at-two-clocks) also applies here. Briefly, since the two supported grade-three columns separate the two clock groups, their nonzero scalar traces are $\Pi_a(X)=(X-b)r(X)$ and $\Pi_b(X)=(X-a)r(X)$ up to normalization, with $r=L_4/[(X-a)(X-b)]$. If at least three different off-zero clocks occur, write their pure corrections as $gH_a(c),gH_b(c)$, with affine $H_a,H_b$, and eliminate $r$ on the off-zero rows. The quadratic $(X-a)H_a-(X-b)H_b$ has three roots and is zero; therefore $H_a=\alpha(X-b)$ and $H_b=\alpha(X-a)$ for one $\alpha$. This gives a column $v=r(c)+\alpha g\in E_2\cap U$. If there are at most two different off-zero clocks, Hilbert rank forces exactly four distinct active clocks. The four-node quadrature has nodal polynomial $L_4$; the two complementary clocks are then roots of $r$, so $v=r(c)$ works instead.

There is no null graph $\alpha=0$. In that case all seven off-zero rows lie on the two complementary Gaussian sites, each of nonzero total weight (use the degree-three Gaussian cardinals). The paid mixed moments $b^{\mathsf T}C^jG^k1=0$, $0\le j\le1$, $1\le k\le3$, separate the sites and kill the first three residual powers at each. The annihilator in the residual variable of at most three nonzero residual values has degree at most three and nonzero constant term; its weighted mean would be this constant times the nonzero Gaussian mass. This contradiction would require at least four rows at each of the two sites, not seven. Thus all off-zero rows follow the nonzero quadratic graph $g_q=-r(c_q)/\alpha$. They are also all live and at pairwise distinct clocks: if at most six different live off-zero clocks occurred, the six moments $b^{\mathsf T}C^jG1=0$, $0\le j\le5$, and the graph would make their total output weight zero at every such clock. The scalar moments would then be carried by only two zero clocks, contradicting the ordinary rank.

## 2. Independent anchors and paid chronology

Call $a$ the earliest physical zero clock and $b$ the other. At that earliest row the prefix columns $1,c$ are independent: the literal row probes $e_i^{\mathsf T}$ and $e_i^{\mathsf T}A$ on these two columns have determinant $-a^2/2\ne0$. They are in the kernel of the nonzero prefix row $e_i^{\mathsf T}M$, because $F_3=GE_1$ is clean there. Hence that earliest zero is at physical stage three or later. Writing $J$ for the primitive from zero, the read of $Av$ at the earliest zero sees only the diagonal; its pure grade-three part is zero. Thus
$$\gamma=(Jr)(a)/r(a),\qquad g_q/g_1=r(c_q)/r(\gamma)\quad(g_q\ne0).$$
The primitive $J^*$ on reverse rows integrates from $X$ to one. The supported reverse line at grade two, normalized to trace $r$, is the row with entries $b_i r(c_i)$ on $U$. It is nonzero on each clock group in aggregate: otherwise $(X-z)r(X)$ for the only supported clock $z$ would be a nonzero cubic killed by the Hilbert form. Its latest live zero $z$ is either $a$ or $b$, and multiplication by $A$ there sees only its diagonal. Since $Z_3=W_1G$ is clean at that coordinate, $\gamma=(J^*r)(z)/r(z)$.

The first two forward rows are nonzero-residual. So are the last two: they are live with distinct clocks by the [local reverse rank identity](../../lower-bounds/characteristic-zero/sdirk_saturated_weighted_mirror_duality_20261123.md#both-ends-of-a-saturated-strict-sdirk), and $b^{\mathsf T}GA$ at column nine would contradict $Z_3$ cleanliness if $g_9=0$ and $g_{10}\ne0$. The actual adjacent tap is nonzero by the literal degree-one row read. At the rear, on any live suffix, use the weight-normalized reflected block with clock $y=1-c$ and the opposite primitive as in that same rank identity; it does not assume a globally normalized adjoint. Its normalized quadratic residual is proportional to the physical $g$, via $b^{\mathsf T}G\propto b^{\mathsf T}M$.

In either direction take $r_e=r(X)$ or $r(1-X)$, respectively. Put $R_e=r_e(\gamma)\ne0$ and let $k_e$ be its linear coefficient. The nonzero second gap $u$ divided by $\gamma$ is paid by
$$(R_e+\gamma^2)u=2R_e-\gamma(2\gamma+k_e).$$
For a live third row, use the [actual first-three-stage identities](../clock-localized-tail/tied-stage-floor/FIRST-THREE-ROWS-AND-PHYSICAL-LEVEL-OBSTRUCTIONS.md). Set $\kappa(u)=1+2u-u^2\ne0$ and $H(u)=-2+u+4u^2-u^3$. Its star option has clock $\gamma(3-u)$ and relative residual $\kappa(u)$; its arithmetic option has clock $\gamma(1+2u)$ and relative residual $\kappa(2u)+2u\,t$ with $tH(u)=2u(3u-2)$ and $t\ne0$. The arithmetic option really has $H(u)\ne0$ in paid characteristic: if its right side were zero at nonzero $u$, then $u=2/3$ and $H(u)=4/27\ne0$. A zero third stage can only be arithmetic; then also
$$4u^3-12u^2+3u+2=0.$$
At the front it must be at $a$; at the rear it must be at the latest live zero $z$ from the anchor. A *silent* reverse third row must be a physical zero by the seven-live-off-zero result. The two zero reads of $b^{\mathsf T}A$ and $b^{\mathsf T}GA$ at its column give the linear equations in its two remaining incoming taps whose determinant is $b_9b_{10}(g_{10}-g_9)$. If $g_{10}\ne g_9$, both taps vanish, making this column absent in every scalar reverse word, contrary to full reverse rank. Thus $g_9=g_{10}$ and $\kappa(u_{\mathrm{rear}})=1$ forces $u_{\mathrm{rear}}=2$. This necessary condition is included without imposing any further assumed clock or tap on the silent row.

## 3. Exact characteristic support and all splitting fields

For the nonmirror pairs $a+b\ne1$, the equations are $L_4(a)=0$ and $b^2-b+a^2-a+2/7=0$, by factorizing $L_4(b)-L_4(a)$. In the degree-eight ordered-pair algebra use $\theta=a+2b$. The script checks the polynomials expressing $a,b$ in $\theta$ and its monic equation by reduction in the original two-variable quotient. The only rational coefficient denominators used in those reductions, the inverse $1/r(a)$, and the cleared gates are supported on $2,3,5,7,13$. Thus **13** is tested directly, not discarded. For a mirror pair use $b=1-a$ in the same coordinate algebra. Any given mirror $a$ can first be paired with one of its nonmirror Gauss partners in the algebraic closure. This route covers all conjugates, including when no Gauss site belongs to the coefficient prime field.

The script does not divide by either end's gap denominator. If it vanishes, its numerator must vanish too and both norm numerators are used. The live-third nonzero gates are the homogeneous star quadratic and the homogeneous arithmetic quintic obtained by clearing the actual gap fraction. For a zero third, both the homogeneous cubic and the corresponding clock pin must vanish; the silent reverse case contributes the linear gap equation at $u=2$. Each alternative gives its own product factor, with a gcd for joint zero conditions. We also multiply the two possible latest live anchors:
$$2(Jr)(a)-I(r)=0\quad\text{or}\quad
  (Jr)(a)r(b)+((Jr)(b)-I(r))r(a)=0,\qquad I(r)=(Jr)(1).$$
The gcd of the forward, reverse and anchor norm products has only prime **107** in the nonmirror case outside the known inverted primes. In the mirror case the second anchor is identically zero. The remaining finite mirror support is
$$19,\ 139,\ 227,\ 233,\ 241,\ 509,\ 739,\ 10067,\ 234847,\
1207117,\ 1352963,\ 127835437,\ 3716728837183.$$
The product receipt includes the multiplicities, not a bounded scan. The norms are resultants in the monic primitive equation. A root in the algebraic closure with a vanishing cleared polynomial necessarily gives a zero resultant in that characteristic. Thus the guard does not just exclude split prime-field points.

The direct exceptional census uses the least common multiple of the factor degrees of the reduced Gauss polynomial, verifies four distinct roots in that finite splitting field, and tests all twelve directed pairs. This covers any ambient field via its algebraic closure. It verifies that no simultaneous zero of a second-gap numerator and denominator survives the anchor and nonzero-graph conditions. ## 4. Fourth-stage and single-group clean reads exclude all five

When the first three physical rows have distinct clocks, let $P$ be the quadratic interpolating their literal normalized residuals. This applies to each of the five branches at either end. At stage four in that end's coordinates, the actual read of $A(g-P(c))$ sees only its diagonal. Its scalar trace is $-JP$, and its pure part is $g(\xi+\eta c)$ from $F_3=GE_1$, or the identical statement in the live weight-normalized rear block. The two coefficients $\xi,\eta$ are determined by the first two clocks and nonzero residuals. The resulting necessary equation is
$$ (JP)(x)-\gamma P(x)+g_x(\gamma-\xi-\eta x)=0.$$
On an off-zero row put $g_x=P_{\rm graph}(x)$, the known graph scaled to $g_1=\gamma^2/2$; on a zero row use $g_x=0$. The off-zero cubic factors completely over the prime field in each case; its first two roots are the earlier graph clocks. Repeated off-zero clocks are already excluded by the six ordinary mixed moments. A fourth zero at the third zero's clock cannot repeat that earlier row: its reads of $1,c,c^2$ agree with the third row (the $c^2$ read is fixed at that clean coordinate), and the three preceding clocks give an invertible Vandermonde on the strict taps. Subtracting its common diagonal term then copies the earlier row's strict history with only that earlier diagonal replaced by its own, so all tree feature values coincide, contradicting full forward rank (or reverse scalar-word rank in a reflected live suffix).

The last four weights in this replay really are live, even if one of the two repeated zero rows is silent elsewhere. If physical row seven had zero weight it would be a physical quadratic zero. The rows $b^{\mathsf T}C^j A$ for $j=0,1,2$ have zero entries at its column: their scalar parts vanish by weight, and their pure parts by $Z_2,Z_3$ being clean. The last three rows are all live and distinct in the census. The three equations then force the three incoming taps to vanish by a Vandermonde, so that column is absent from every reverse word, impossible.

The literal fourth receipt shows that in the two nonzero-third cases the fourth rows in each direction must be zeros, respectively at $a$ and $b$. In the three zero-third cases the fourth rows in either direction cannot be zero (each has exactly one new off-zero root). Thus in every case the earliest zero is $a$ and the latest physical zero is $b$. This is stronger than the earlier latest-live-zero statement and is needed here.

Of the three zeros, one clock group is a singleton, and that singleton is one of the two outer zeros. The $E_3$ columns with traces $\Pi_a=(X-b)r$ and $\Pi_b=(X-a)r$ are literal supported group indicators, up to their nonzero values on the respective groups. Weighting them gives corresponding supported rows of $W_3$. Hence $F_4=\operatorname{Ann}(W_3)$ is clean on a singleton zero column whose weight is nonzero, and $Z_4=\operatorname{Ann}(E_3)$ is clean at that same coordinate. The singleton weight is nonzero by the two nonzero aggregate weights in Section 2. If the singleton is the first zero $a$, apply $A$ to the supported column for the *other* group, at stage $a$. The actual read is zero, and it must equal its scalar primitive $J\Pi_b(a)$. So
$$N=(J(Xr))(a)-a(Jr)(a)=0.$$
If the singleton is the last zero $b$, use the supported reverse row for the other group and the clean reverse read. Its primitive there is $J^*\Pi_a(b)=-N$ by reflection, so again $N=0$.

But for a mirror pair $r(X)=X^2-X+q$ with $q=a^2-a+2/7$. Direct multiplication gives
$$84N=-a^2(7a-2)(7a-6).$$
The Gauss root $a$ is never zero. At the two other possible zeros of this right side, $343\cdot70L_4(2/7)=-57$ and $343\cdot70L_4(6/7)=-137$. In paid characteristic these exceptions are only $19,137$, neither present among the five actual survivors. This excludes them all.
