# Fixed-common-diagonal extrapolation by repetitions is expensive

This treats repeated copies of **one** ordinary method. It does not exclude combining genuinely different aligned seeds or using the ordinal stage contrasts of the companion note.

Let a strict SDIRK seed of exact order \(d\ge2\) have nonzero common diagonal \(\gamma_0\). Literal copies at durations \(\tau_i h\) keep the same physical diagonal only when all nonzero \(\tau_i\) are the same \(\tau\), since their diagonals are \(\tau_i\gamma_0\). Consider any affine combination of the repetition endpoints \(P_h^{\,n}(\mathbf y)\), where \(P_h\) is the duration-\(\tau h\) map and \(n\) is a nonnegative integer. The same sequential prefix can be shared across all branches. Write \(M_j\coloneqq\sum_n w_n n^j\) for the mixture moments, and \(\mu\coloneqq1/\tau\).

The lower orders require \(M_j=\mu^j\) for \(0\le j\le d\). For necessity even the linear ODE suffices, since the seed is exact through \(d\). If at most \(d\) distinct endpoints had nonzero weight, their hull would have degree at most \(d\) and would force \(\mu\) itself to be one of them. If \(\mu\) is one of at most \(d+1\) nodes, the low moment equations force the single weight 1 there, by Lagrange interpolation. A single nonzero repetition does not improve the seed order: its first local error at degree \(d+1\) is the repetition count times the original nonzero error. Thus every genuine improvement needs at least \(d+1\) distinct weighted endpoints, or at least \(d+2\) when it includes \(\mu\). In particular its largest active repetition \(N\) is at least \(d\).

There is a stronger obstruction than merely counting the unmerged copies. The seed stability function \(R\) has one pole, at the inverse of its physical diagonal, of multiplicity \(r\ge1\). Its numerator has degree at most \(r\) after cancellation: the original stability function is bounded at infinity since \(A\) is invertible. An affine combination \(\sum_n w_n R(z)^n\) has pole multiplicity exactly \(Nr\), since the term with \(n=N\) cannot be canceled by lower powers. Any Runge–Kutta realization requires at least \(Nr\) physical rows, regardless of legitimate sharing.

Indeed \(r\ge d-1\) over the real algebraic numbers. Scale to duration one and write the denominator of \(R\) as \(Q(z)\), of degree \(r\) and with only real roots. If it had order at least \(r+2\), numerator degree at most \(r\) would make the coefficients of \(e^zQ(z)\) at both degrees \(r+1,r+2\) zero. Thus \(T=(1+\partial)^{r+1}Q\) has \(T(0)=(T+T')(0)=0\), a multiple root. For each real-rooted polynomial \(P\), the map \(P\mapsto P+P'\) reduces each old root multiplicity by one and has only simple new roots. At an old root this follows by factoring the multiplicity. Away from old roots, \(P'/P\) decreases strictly from \(+\infty\) to \(-\infty\) between consecutive roots and decreases from \(0\) to \(-\infty\) on the leftmost interval, supplying the remaining roots of \(P+P'\) with no more roots on the right. After \(r+1\) iterations the roots are therefore simple, contradiction. The mixture needs at least \(d(d-1)\) rows. For \(d\ge3\) through the current benchmark range this already exceeds the published rational and real-algebraic upper bounds at \(d+1\) and \(d+2\). The exact rational low-order minima separately handle \(d=2\); no improvement is inferred from the weak bound there.

## Which two errors can such mixtures even correct?

There is also an exact off-model obstruction, including every rooted tree rather than just stability. For a formal vector field, let \(D_f\) be its derivation on polynomials and let \(P_h^*\) denote pullback. Write
\[
 P_h^*=\exp(\tau hD_f)+h^{d+1}E+h^{d+2}F+O(h^{d+3}).
\]
At grades \(d+1,d+2\) only one defect insertion is possible in a product of \(n\) factors. The contribution at \(d+1\) is \(nE\), and at \(d+2\) it is \(nF+\tau\binom n2(D_f E+E D_f)\). Apply to the coordinate function \(\mathrm{id}\). Once the lower orders are fixed, the coefficients multiplying these defect terms are fixed by \(M_1,M_2\). The only degrees of freedom that remain are the exact-flow vectors \(D_f^{d+1}\mathrm{id}\) and \(D_f^{d+2}\mathrm{id}\), via the higher scalar moments. Thus the first defect must be proportional to the degree-\((d+1)\) exact-flow vector as a universal rooted coefficient family. The second remaining defect, \(\mu F+\tau(\mu^2-\mu)(D_f E+E D_f)/2\) as applied to \(\mathrm{id}\), must likewise lie on the next exact-flow line. When these two collinearities hold, interpolation on sufficiently many distinct repetition counts can prescribe the needed higher moments. This characterizes the transfer at those two orders, while the physical pole bound makes the eligible mixtures expensive.
