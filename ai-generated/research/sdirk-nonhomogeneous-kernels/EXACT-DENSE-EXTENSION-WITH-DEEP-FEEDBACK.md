# Exact dense extension cost and added strict depth at the optimal width

Use the conventions of `DENSE-POLYNOMIAL-DONOR-MINIMUM.md` and the actual relation polynomial space \(U_n=G_n(\ker E_n)\subseteq P_n\) from `CAUSAL-RECEIVER-CUT-AND-MINIMAL-CACHE.md`. Here \(P_n=K[X]_{<n}\), \(E_n\) maps formal combinations of trees of grades at most \(n\) to the old stage columns, and \(G_n\) maps the same input to its exact derivative polynomial. The Laguerre-depth construction below works in characteristic zero over the stated split field. It uses common nonzero diagonal \(\gamma\) and \(T=\mathcal I-\gamma\).

## Exact cost of keeping the old rows and advancing the dense grade by one

Let \(n\ge2\), and take any coefficient field in which the casts of \(1,\ldots,n-1\) are nonzero, so all tree models of grade up to \(n\) and \(T\) on \(P_{n-1}\) are defined. Suppose the old rows admit a polynomial-dense decoder through \(n-1\), and set \(d=\dim U_n\). At least \(d\) extra physical rows are required to obtain a dense decoder through \(n\) **keeping the old rows**. This lower bound holds over every such field, with no nonzero-diagonal or clock assumption and even if the extra rows are not individually exact and may have arbitrary internal edges: each new actual coordinate imposes at most one additional linear equation on \(\ker E_n\), and the dimension of its image under \(G_n\) drops by at most one. An exact dense decoder is equivalent to that image being zero.

Conversely, if there exist \(d\) allowed distinct anchors \(x_i\) whose evaluations \(S:P_n\to K^d\) restrict injectively to \(U_n\), the bound is attained with any prescribed nonzero diagonal on the new rows. In particular such anchors exist over any infinite field if all field clocks are allowed. They may be rational in any prescribed interval of positive length for a real field: choose the next evaluation that does not kill the current nonzero polynomial kernel, avoiding finitely many roots and already chosen anchors. No attainment is asserted when available field elements or allowed anchors do not furnish such a set. Append individually exact grade-\(n\) rows at these anchors, using \(T\) applied to the old grade-\((n-1)\) decoder. This is an actual strict row on the old stages and is sufficient by the unary-child factorization and tree induction of the receiver criterion. The new obstruction is \(U_n\cap\ker S=0\), so all tree profiles through \(n\) now factor through a fixed linear polynomial decoder. If the chosen diagonal and anchors are in the old coefficient field, no field extension is needed. Thus under the stated anchor hypothesis the exact extra cost is \(d\). This uses the polynomial image of all actual relations and is stronger than just measuring the error of a preselected decoder.

## A deeper minimum-width dense chart, with actual all-tree proof

Start from the minimum-width cap-\(q\) Laguerre cache, \(q\ge1\). Its actual decoder \(D\) of degree below \(q\) is exact through \(q\), there are \(L(q)=\lfloor(q+1)^2/4\rfloor\) rows, and its strict depth is at most \(q\) (depth counts vertices). The exact first-excess error span equals \(U=U_{q+1}\) and has dimension \(d=\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1\) by the cap lower bound and the recurrence (3) in the receiver note. For \(q=1\) this is just \(\operatorname{span}(W_1)\). Choose rational sites \(x_i\) with \(S|_U\) invertible and \(W_q(x_1)\ne0\), and let \(J=(S|_U)^{-1}:K^d\to U\). Choose any strict lower matrix \(L_*\) of size \(d\). In row \(i\) use
\[
 Z_i=(T D)(x_i)+\sum_{j<i}(L_*)_{ij}\,[H_j-D(x_j)],
       \qquad H_i=h f(y+Z_i+\gamma H_i). \tag{5}
\]
Here \(D\) is applied to the old stage-derivative data, so the displayed \(Z_i\) is a fixed linear combination of earlier actual stages, not function evaluation at a new virtual stage. At leaf grade the brackets vanish, so the physical clocks are exactly \(x_i\). The new stages are individually exact through grade \(q+1\): induct first by chronology of rows, and within each row by tree grade. Each child of a tree through \(q+1\) is paid through \(q\), where both earlier brackets and the old decoder error are zero. The unary state input at the new row is therefore \((Tg)(x_i)+\gamma g(x_i)=(\mathcal I g)(x_i)\); taking the product pays the tree. This proves the assertion on all rooted profiles, not only tall or star profiles.

The output decoder is \(D+J(H-SD)\), with the argument split into old and new coordinates. Through \(q\) the correction is zero. At grade \(q+1\) the residual \(H-SD\) is \(S(g-D\Phi)\) and \(g-D\Phi\in U\), so the correction recovers the exact polynomial. The actual stage count is \(L(q)+d=L(q+1)\), the sharp polynomial-dense minimum. The ordinary output obtained by integrating this decoder is consequently of order at least \(q+1\). Given a real algebraic \(\gamma\), the field can stay \(\mathbb Q(\gamma,\text{old selected Laguerre roots})\), taking rational new anchors and rational \(L_*\). This does not assert rationality of the old selected roots.

The new feedback gives **effective** extra depth, not just visual edges. On the scalar linear equation set \(z=h\lambda\), \(u=z/(1-\gamma z)\), and divide the derivatives by the initial scalar \(y\). Because the old strict depth is at most \(q\), its scalar chart is a polynomial in \(u\) of degree at most \(q\). By exact dense order it is \(\sum_{a=0}^{q-1}u^{a+1}T^a1\): the exact scalar chart \(z e^{Xz}\) solves \(F=u(1+TF)\), and has these coefficients through the paid degree. The normalized residual \(\Delta=(H-SD)/y\) in (5) then has the exact scalar identity
\[
 \Delta=u^{q+1}(I-uL_*)^{-1}\,\big(W_q(x_i)/q!\big)_{i=1}^d. \tag{6}
\]
Choose \(L_*\) to have nonzero subdiagonal, with all other entries zero. Its top residual coefficient \(u^{q+d}\) in stage \(d\) is the product of the subdiagonal entries times \(W_q(x_1)/q!\), which is nonzero. Thus the actual shifted matrix satisfies \((A-\gamma I)^{q+d-1}\mathbf1\ne0\); the strict depth is exactly \(q+d\), since no longer path is possible from the old \(q\) layers followed by \(d\) rows. For example \(q=10\) gives 36 actual rows, dense through grade 11, and effective depth 16. The scalar computation certifies depth only; it does not assert ordinary order beyond the rooted proof above.
