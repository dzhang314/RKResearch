# Two contributing clock groups for a four-order excess

For every \(p=q+4\ge6\), there is a strict SDIRK method of exact ordinary order \(p\) with the same sufficiently small prescribed positive real algebraic diagonal \(\gamma\) at every physical stage, whose size is
\[
 \begin{cases}
   L(q)+\lceil p/2\rceil+2,&p\text{ odd},\\
   L(q)+\lceil p/2\rceil+3,&p\text{ even}.
 \end{cases}
\]
This saves one physical stage in each parity relative to the earlier three-co-site endpoint construction at the same cutoff. It saves an additional quadrature pin: the auxiliary pair uses two *distinct existing contributing clocks*, and only one of them is prescribed. No unrestricted stage-table improvement is asserted.

Use the old preparation \(D_0\), first-excess \(U\), and the actual block \(\Psi,B,O\) of SHARED-PHYSICAL-PIVOTS.md. Put \(m=\lceil p/2\rceil\), and use \(k=m\) regular quadrature sites in the odd case or \(k=m+1\) in the even case. One of them is prescribed at \(y=1-\gamma\). Choose a different site \(x\) for the other auxiliary row. Take their chronological order \(x,y\), and set
\[
 C_Y\coloneqq\operatorname{diag}(x,y),\qquad
 \nu\coloneqq(1,1),\qquad
 D\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}\gamma&0\\1-x-\gamma&\gamma\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then \(\nu D=\nu(I-C_Y)\). The exact decoder of length at most one is especially simple: \(\mathcal D(\nu f(C_Y))\) is the unique affine polynomial with those two coordinate values. Indeed \(\nu\), \(\nu C_Y\), and \(\nu D\) correctly give \(1,X,1-X\).

The first-excess error at length two is generated by just two polynomials. Define
\[
  W(X)\coloneqq(X-x)(X-y),\qquad
  R(X)\coloneqq \mathcal D\bigl(\nu C_YD\bigr)(X)-J^*X
                           \pmod{W},
\]
where \(R\) in this display means the unique affine remainder of the displayed difference. In particular
\[
     R(y)= y\gamma-(1-y^2)/2=-\gamma^2/2\ne0 .
\]
The formula for the whole difference at \(CB\) is \(R+W/2\), since \(J^*X=(1-X^2)/2\). The error at \(CC\) is \(-W\) in the convention actual decoder minus nominal. At \(BC\) it is \(W\), because \(\nu D C_Y=\nu(1-C_Y)C_Y\). The actual \(BB\) row is \(\nu D-\nu C_YD\), and its model is \(J^*(1-X)=J^*1-J^*X\), so its error is the negative of the \(CB\) error. Thus \(V\) can be exactly the span of \(W,R\) in the companion solve.

Choose the last two regular rows at \(z,y\) for some \(z\ne x,y\). The evaluation determinant for \(W,R\) there is \(W(z)R(y)\ne0\). The same two physical rows solve the two discrepancies at every earlier column, even though the two auxiliary signals live at different labels. The other \(k-2\) regular nodes have sufficiently many distinct positive limits for \(U\), since
\[
       \dim U\le\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1\le k-2 .
\]
The general proof now certifies every rooted order condition, not just bushy and tall trees.

## One prescribed quadrature site suffices

For odd \(p=2m-1\), take the \(m-1\) free roots of the monic polynomial orthogonal for \((y-X)\,dX\). When \(\gamma=0\) these are the ordinary right Radau free nodes with simple positive roots below 1. The Gram determinants, simple root brackets, and positive weights persist for sufficiently small positive \(\gamma\); \((y-X)\) need not be positive beyond \(y\) for these continuity assertions. Division by the node hull gives precision \(2m-2\), and the degree-\((2m-1)\) witness from the Radau limit persists.

For even \(p=2m\), subtract a fixed small positive rational mass \(\epsilon\) at \(y\) from the uniform moment functional, take its \(m\)-node Gaussian rule, and restore the \(y\)-atom. At \((\epsilon,\gamma)=(0,0)\) there is the ordinary Gaussian Gram system, distinct interior roots and positive weights. For positive sufficiently small \(\epsilon\) and then sufficiently small \(\gamma\), all these gates persist and \(y\) is separate. Degree-\((2m-1)\) exactness follows by polynomial division against the monic orthogonal hull; a nonzero next-moment mismatch persists from the unperturbed Gaussian rule. All coefficients and roots are real algebraic.

## Exact remaining test for an even minimum-clock variant

This last observation concerns only the stated two-ghost architecture, not arbitrary strict SDIRK. Suppose that for even \(p=2m\) one instead chooses a prescribed \(y\) among the \(m\) Gaussian nodes, and \(\gamma=1-y\). Assume \(S_XU\) has rank \(m-1\), with one-dimensional annihilator spanned by a row \(\zeta\) and \(\zeta_y\ne0\). Put \(\Delta_a=L_aG-L_{J^*a}\) for any polynomial \(a\). At the last two regular columns \(z,y\), the two entries of \(\Delta_W\) are already fixed:
\[
 \Delta_W(y)=-\beta_y J^*W(y),\qquad
 \Delta_W(z)=\beta_z(\gamma W(z)-J^*W(z)).
\]
The remaining tap \(G_{y,z}\) cannot change them, since \(W(y)=0\). Thus the two specified discrepancy rows \(\Delta_W,\Delta_R\) can both annihilate the actual \(S_XU\) by this tap rule if and only if
\[
                   \Delta_W(z)\zeta_y=\Delta_W(y)\zeta_z.
\]
Necessity follows by proportionality to the annihilator. For sufficiency choose \(\Delta_W\) and \(\Delta_R\) proportional to \(\zeta\), with their forced values at \(y\). Use \(G_{y,z}\) to achieve the requested \(\Delta_R(z)\) because \(R(y)\ne0\), and use the free two-pivot solves at all earlier columns. The same rooted proof then applies. This scalar test is not assumed automatic; no additional even absolute bound is claimed from it.
