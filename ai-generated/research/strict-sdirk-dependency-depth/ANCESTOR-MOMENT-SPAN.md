# Exact higher-budget ancestor moments for layered RK compression

This is a source-only refinement of [one-degree paid cancellation](PAID-WIDTH-REDUCTION.md).
It preserves paid scalar output, not all hidden reference columns.
The main statements distinguish the exact uniform cancellation
criterion, realizability by split hulls, and actual physical upper
bounds. No sharp shallow positive-diagonal case is inferred.

## 1. The exact cut problem

Work over a characteristic-zero field K. As before, I is the
zero-origin polynomial primitive, ℓ(f)=(If)(1), J=I−γ id. Let
p be the paid order, D the terminal layer, and h≥1 a layer with
d=D−h unary ancestors en route to the terminal layer. Put
s=p−d. Suppose we use r distinct nodes there, 1≤r<s, with
monic hull H. The leftover degree budget after the hull is

    b = s−1−r ≥ 0.

Thus removing b+1 nodes relative to the full local polynomial
cutoff s corresponds to budget b; a reduction relative to the
actual reference width also needs r<min(q,s), where q is the
terminal rule size. Leave layer zero aside: its only usable
subtree is a leaf, and any singleton is sufficient there.

At a vertex with local tree order n≤s, the error F−ρ_h F made
by removing interpolation is H E with deg E≤n−1−r (or zero).
Removing interpolations ancestor-first as in the one-degree
proof, all ancestors of this vertex already have identity
projection. A sibling branch of order a contributes a factor
of degree at most a. Starting with weight 1, moving toward
the vertex multiplies the current weight by the other child
factors and then applies the adjoint

    T V = ℓ(V) − IV − γ V,    ℓ(V J U)=ℓ((T V) U).

The sum of the degrees of the sibling factors and of E is at
most p−d−1−r=b. In y=1−x, T=V−γ id with
(V P)(y)=∫_0^y P(u) du. Let M denote multiplication by y.
The exact space of tests, including the error quotient, is

    C(d,b) = span { M^{a_d} T M^{a_{d−1}} ··· T M^{a_0} 1 :
                    a_i≥0, Σ_i a_i≤b }.

The leftmost multiplier is the error quotient. All other
multipliers are generated by actual leaf siblings: a same-ancestor
set of a leaf siblings supplies (x−γ)^a, since J1=x−γ.
These powers and y^a span the same filtered polynomial spaces.
If the quotient is not included, the pure ancestor test space
for d≥1 is simply T C(d−1,b) (the leftmost operation is T).

It follows that a sufficient **local** cancellation condition
for simultaneous arbitrary compatible layer replacement is

    ℓ(H W)=0 for every W∈C(d,b).                               (1)

It is also necessary for an **isolated** changed layer in this
specific interpolation scheme: take a path with the indicated
leaf siblings at its d ancestors, and make the input at its
bottom a bush with r+a_d leaves. Its order is
d+1+r+Σ_i a_i≤p and its height d+1≤D. The raw bush is
(x−γ)^{r+a_d}. On division by H, the quotients for
a_d=0,...,b−Σ_{i<d}a_i are monic of successive degrees, so
form a triangular basis for precisely the allowed quotient space.
The leaf sibling factors are the exact filtered spanning powers
above. This proves necessity without assuming arbitrary matrix
columns can be made into trees. In this single-layer test all
other projections can be removed by their unchanged terminal
hull or degree cutoff, irrespective of the error at this layer.
The same necessity applies if any other changed layers separately
satisfy (1): on the test tree all nonleaf vertices other than the
bottom bush are its strict ancestors, and all lower or off-path
vertices are leaves with no projection error. The theorem does
**not** rule out cancellation
between several layers that separately violate (1), or other
physical row constructions.

## 2. Exact word span in all degrees

For γ≠0 define

    m = max(d−b,0),     ν = b + min(d,b).

Then

    C(d,b) = T^m K[y]_{≤ν},          dim C(d,b)=ν+1.            (2)

For γ=0 one instead has

    C(d,b) = y^d K[y]_{≤b}.                                    (3)

Here is a short algebraic proof. Apply the factorial-basis
bijection B sending y^k/k! to z^k. Under B, V is multiplication
by z, T is multiplication by f=z−γ, and M is
D_+=z²∂_z+z. B(C(d,b))=S(d,b) is the word span with exactly
d f's and at most b D_+'s. A word has degree at most d+b and
is divisible by f^{(d−b)_+}; each D_+ can lose at most one
f factor. Conversely prove equality with that full filtered
multiple space by induction, using
S(0,b)=K[z]_{≤b} and S(d,0)=K f^d. For d,b≥1,

    S(d,b)=f S(d−1,b) + D_+ S(d,b−1).

For d>b the first summand already equals the upper bound.
For d≤b it supplies all polynomials of degree≤d+b divisible
by f, a codimension-one space. If d=b the second summand
contains D_+f, which has value γ² at z=γ. If d<b it contains
D_+1, of value γ there. Both are nonzero. Undo B to get (2).
For γ=0 each word with j multipliers is a nonzero scalar
multiple of y^{d+j}, which gives (3). This also gives the
pure-ancestor dimensions: for d≥1 and γ≠0 they are
b+min(d−1,b)+1. The pure space already equals the full
space when b<d; including the quotient adds a dimension
when b≥d.

In particular for a two-node loss (b=1) and d≥1, the three
independent conditions for γ≠0 are orthogonality to
T^d 1, y T^d 1 and V² T^{d−1}1. To get this basis directly,
use M T−T M=V² and [V²,T]=0 to move the one M through
the path. In terms of (2) these three tests are another basis
of T^{d−1} K[y]_{≤2}. A one-node loss (b=0) recovers exactly
the previously used test T^d 1.

Therefore the general paid gate in (1) is explicitly

    ℓ(H · T^m Q)=0 for all deg Q≤ν
       ⇔ ℓ(J^m H · Q)=0 for all deg Q≤ν,                       (4)

with the same y=1−x change understood in the first expression.
For b≥d and γ≠0 this is the full unweighted polynomial test
range deg Q≤p−1−r. In that case it is ordinary degree-(p−1)
quadrature exactness of the r-node hull. A real hull of r nodes
is impossible if r<ceil(p/2), since then Q=H is allowed
and the integral of H² is positive. This bound also holds over
any characteristic-zero field by the nonsingular rational
Hilbert matrix, rather than a sign argument. For r≥ceil(p/2)
the degree-r shifted Legendre polynomial supplies a real split
hull, whenever this full-range regime is relevant.

## 3. General rank obstruction, without assuming a real root shape

In the nonzero-γ case, write F_j=T^m(y^j/j!). For r≤ν, a necessary
condition for any nonzero degree-≤r solution is singularity of

    A_r(γ) = [ ℓ(y^i F_j) ]_{0≤i,j≤r}.

Its determinant is a nonzero polynomial in γ of degree exactly
m(r+1); the top coefficient, up to sign and the factorial
column scalings, is the invertible rational Hilbert determinant.
Indeed the leading test term is (−γ)^m y^j/j!. This excludes
an r-node solution even over an algebraic closure of the coefficient
field for all γ outside finitely many algebraic exceptions. In
particular there is no such solution if [Q(γ):Q]>m(r+1), or
if γ is transcendental. For m=0 this is an obstruction for
every γ. Conversely, if r≥ν+1, nonsingularity of the
(ν+1)-square moment submatrix generically allows one to solve
for a monic degree-r polynomial by adjusting its first ν+1
coefficients. Solvability alone does not establish real splitting.

## 4. Clean split existence, interior and algebraic

### All fixed negative diagonals

Let γ<0 be real, α=−γ>0. For every m≥0 the initial sequences
F_0,...,F_{k−1} are strict Chebyshev systems on (0,1). Here is
an elementary proof of the relevant positivity. In the normalized
monomial basis y^n/n!, the coefficient matrix of T^m is the
m-th power of the lower bidiagonal matrix α Id+S, where S
is the unit subdiagonal shift. Any finite truncation of that
bidiagonal matrix is totally nonnegative: in an ordered
minor the only possibly nonzero permutation is increasing,
since each row can use only its equal or immediately preceding
column. Matrix powers preserve this property by Cauchy-Binet.

For increasing positive y_i and increasing exponents n_j,
the generalized Vandermonde determinant det(y_i^{n_j}) is
positive. One direct proof is nonvanishing by induction and
Rolle: a nonzero combination of k monomials has at most k−1
distinct positive zeros, by dividing by the smallest power
and differentiating. Its sign is constant on the ordered
domain, and is positive at y_i=ε^{k−i} for sufficiently small
positive ε: the identity permutation has the unique lowest
exponent of ε. Factorial column scalings preserve positivity.
For det[F_j(y_i)] on the first k tests, Cauchy-Binet of this
evaluation matrix and the nonnegative coefficient matrix has
all terms nonnegative and one strictly positive term, using
exponents 0,...,k−1 and the α^m diagonal. This proves the
asserted strict Chebyshev determinants.

Now a nonzero real polynomial P of degree l cannot annihilate
F_0,...,F_l. If it changes sign at y_1<...<y_k in (0,1), k≤l,
build W in span(F_0,...,F_k) by bordering their evaluation
matrix on these k rows with the evaluation row at y. Its
sign alternates exactly at the y_i, by the strict determinants
and row ordering. P W has constant nonzero sign off its zeros,
so its integral cannot vanish. In particular the moment pairing
of polynomials of degree<r with F_0,...,F_{r−1} is invertible.
There is a unique monic degree-r polynomial orthogonal to
those r functions, and the same sign argument makes all its r
roots simple and interior.

Consequently for γ<0, a monic split hull satisfying (4) exists
over the reals **if and only if** r≥ν+1. For sufficiency simply impose the
stronger r tests; for necessity test the sign changes as above.
If γ is real algebraic, the solved polynomial has coefficients
in Q(γ) and its interior roots are real algebraic. Its splitting
field can thus be taken finite and contained in the real algebraic
numbers. When needed,
convert the monic polynomial in y to a monic polynomial in x
by multiplying P(1−x) by (−1)^r. There is no claim that its
roots already lie in Q(γ), or in the previous terminal field.

### Positive diagonals near zero or infinity

For every fixed triple (d,b,r) with r≥ν+1, the same split
existence holds for all sufficiently small positive γ, and
for all sufficiently large positive γ. Use the same r chosen
tests as in the negative case. At γ=0, T^m=V^m and these
tests are positive scalings of y^{m+j}, a weighted monomial
system. Their monic orthogonal polynomial has simple interior
roots. The moment matrix is invertible at zero and its entries
are polynomials in γ. Hence the monic solution is continuous
near zero; its r simple interior roots persist, as witnessed
by r disjoint interior intervals of opposite endpoint signs.
At infinity normalize the tests by (−γ)^m and use 1/γ as
parameter. Their limit is the ordinary monomial system,
and the identical argument applies. A finite set of layers
can use a common positive interval, depending on the order.
For real algebraic γ the resulting nodes are again real
algebraic; no fixed positive interval working for all orders
or arbitrary positive algebraic γ is asserted.

### Why all positive diagonals do not follow from the dimension

For every b≥1 take d=b+1 and r=2b+1, so m=1 and ν=r−1.
There exists a positive real algebraic γ for which (4) has
**no monic degree-r solution**, even without requiring splitting.
Let U_r=P_{<r} V|_{<r} be the compression of Volterra integration
onto degree<r using the ordinary uniform inner product. The
matrix in a rational polynomial basis has rational entries.
Its dimension r is odd, hence there is a real eigenvalue γ
with a real nonzero eigenvector Q; over Q(γ) the same singular
linear system gives one with coefficients in Q(γ). Since

    ⟨VQ,Q⟩ = (∫_0^1 Q)²/2,

γ≥0. In fact U_r is invertible: if P_{<r} VQ=0, then VQ,
of degree≤r, is a multiple of the degree-r monic orthogonal
polynomial. But VQ(0)=0, whereas that polynomial is nonzero
at zero by interior simplicity, so Q=0. Thus γ>0 and is
algebraic. At it, (V−γ)Q is a nonzero multiple of the
degree-r orthogonal polynomial, since it is orthogonal to
degree<r and cannot be identically zero (V raises the degree).
Its pairing with every monic degree-r polynomial is a nonzero
multiple of the orthogonal polynomial's norm squared.
This contradicts (4), in any coefficient extension. As an
actual cut the parameters are s=3b+2 and p=4b+3 (and a
terminal rule with q≥s lets this attempt a b+1-node loss).
This family is independent of any particular order and refutes
a uniform positive claim at the exact dimension threshold.
It is **only** an obstruction to this interpolation hull and
its exact local cancellation condition, not to positive-diagonal
SDIRK tableaux or to other scalar compression designs.

### The zero diagonal

Equation (3) instead gives the positive weighted tests
y^d K[y]_{≤b}, with exact split threshold r≥b+1. Take the
monic orthogonal polynomial for weight y^d on (0,1), with
simple interior roots by the elementary sign test. If
r≤b, testing H itself rules out a nonzero H. This is a
separate case, not the limit of the exact nonzero-γ span.

## 5. Consequences for simultaneous physical layers

Assume p≥2, 1≤D≤p−1, a terminal rule with 1≤q≤p
distinct nodes, and exact terminal moments through degree p−1.
No extra terminal moment or positivity condition is required
for the cancellation proof. The same actual row formula as
for one-degree compression works with the distinct roots
of the new chosen hulls: apply
J to each preceding cardinal and evaluate at the current
nodes. Weights remain on the terminal rule, and the physical
clocks at h≥1 are the assigned nodes because interpolation
fixes 1. Layer zero has one stage of clock γ. For a tree in
the band, remove projections ancestor-first. Case (1) kills
every reduced-layer difference; at an unchanged layer either
the polynomial already fits its local cutoff or the paid
terminal hull annihilates the degree-bounded error times the
ancestor test. No requirement that other intermediate
columns be equal enters. For a taller tree the strict
adjacent output is zero. Thus the same omitted-paid-target
hypothesis as before proves full order after restoring γ I;
the exact order and depth witnesses are unchanged whenever
available. This is a mathematical construction, not a claim
about the previously checked full-column rank module.

In particular take sharp generic D=p−1 and a minimal
q=ceil(p/2) Gaussian terminal rule. At every real algebraic
negative diagonal, or at sufficiently small or large real
algebraic positive diagonal for this p,
the following widths are achievable over the finite real field
generated by γ, terminal nodes, and the chosen earlier nodes:

    r_h = min(q, ceil((2(h+1)−1)/3)),       0≤h≤p−1.           (5)

To see the only nontrivial case, set s=h+1 and
r_0'=ceil((2s−1)/3)<q, with s≥2. Then b=s−1−r_0'≥0
except when r_0'=s, which is an unchanged exact layer.
In the former case b≤d=p−s: otherwise
p+r_0'<2s−1≤3 r_0', which implies p<2r_0', impossible
since r_0'<ceil(p/2). The condition r_0'≥2b+1 is then
exactly 3r_0'≥2s−1. For r_0'≥q use the terminal hull;
s≥q and the unchanged-width argument applies. These
widths include the 1-stage initial layer and q-stage final
layer. For γ<0 all λ_n(1)=ℓ(J^n 1) are positive; in the
positive perturbative ranges retain λ_{p−1}(1),λ_p(1)≠0
by shrinking/enlarging the interval. Thus depth p−1 and
exact order p hold, using the paid and first-failing
shifted tall trees. The terminal weights may be the positive
Gaussian ones; earlier weights are zero.

Writing N=floor((3q−2)/2)=3u+a with a∈{0,1,2}, the
stage count in (5) is

    Σ_{s=1}^p min(q, ceil((2s−1)/3))
       = (p−N)q + 3u²+u+a(2u+1)
       = 5p²/16 + O(p).

Indeed N is the last s with the rounded cutoff <q, and
each three-s block of the unsaturated widths has values
2k+1, 2k+1, 2k+2. This improves the prior full-column
3p²/8+O(p) construction on the stated regimes (a leading
saving of p²/16), whereas the earlier one-degree construction
saves only O(p) from that baseline. The maximum
fibre is unchanged (q). For negative γ the tests in (4)
also exclude smaller degree than (5) for individual robust
layer cancellation: smaller r<q either has d≥b and
3r<2s−1, or d<b and 2r<p. This is not a lower bound for
unrestricted physical tableaux.

For γ=0 the positive weighted rule instead gives widths
ceil((h+1)/2), totaling floor((p+1)²/4) stages at depth
p−1, with exact order p by the above-height tall failure.
There is also a matching lower bound restricted to
the adjacent-layer, terminal-only readout format in this
explicit (γ=0) case. The paid trees made of d unary
ancestors over a bush factor a moment Gram matrix through
the h-th physical clocks, with entries
∫_0^1 y^d x^{a+b}dx/d!, for
0≤a,b<ceil((h+1)/2) and y=1−x. That matrix is positive
definite, so each noninitial fibre needs this many
stages; the initial fibre must be nonempty to realize
the height-(p−1) tall output. This does not assert an
ordinary explicit RK stage minimum.

The q-node terminal support is unchanged, but these multiple
constraints do not give the one-degree proof's rational
free-root parameterization. Earlier hull coefficients lie in
Q(γ), yet their roots may require a coefficient-field enlargement
beyond the terminal split field with γ. There is no additional
extension if the ambient field is already the field of real
algebraic numbers. In particular the small/large positive-sign
regimes are not a license to apply (5) at a prescribed
exceptional Laguerre diagonal. The independent order-20
145-stage result uses that fixed shallow diagonal and its original
coefficient field; this result improves a different, general regime.
