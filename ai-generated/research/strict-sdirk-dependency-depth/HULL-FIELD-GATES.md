# Coefficient-field gates for simultaneous split-hull cuts

**Separate mathematical analysis.** Fix the Gaussian terminal output
rule and the common diagonal. The already proved local cancellation
conditions are unchanged. This note checks field containments for
the actual cardinal-row construction, not for every tableau of the
same order or width. No actual new hull irreducibility or field
enlargement at a named diagonal is assumed or claimed.

## 1. The exact field and simultaneous degree bounds

Put K=Q(γ), let G/Q be the splitting field of the fixed Gaussian
terminal hull, and put F=KG. Suppose the changed positive-layer
monic hulls H_i belong to K[y] and have distinct real roots.
Let L_i/K be their splitting fields, and L their compositum.
Assume the other layers use nodes already in F. The field generated
by the actual coefficients of the cardinal-row tableau is

    E = FL.                                                   (A)

For the forward inclusion, cardinals, rational polynomial
integration, γ-shift and evaluation take place in the field of
all these nodes; terminal weights are cardinal integrals in G.
Conversely the physical diagonal recovers γ and each assigned
positive-layer clock is a coordinate of A1, since the cardinals
sum to 1. This recovers every new hull root (up to the rational
change y=1−x) and every original terminal Gaussian clock.
This argument is for the constructed tableau with its represented
sites, not one from which stages have been deleted.

In particular preservation is equivalent to every H_i splitting
over F. Since L/K is Galois,

    [E:F] = [L:L∩F],
    [L:K]/gcd([L:K],[F:K])  divides  [E:F].

The latter follows because [L∩F:K] divides both degrees. If
the distinct irreducible factors of the chosen hulls over K
have degrees e_j, then [E:F] divides ∏ e_j!, by the faithful
root-permutation action and Galois restriction. One may instead
factor over F for a potentially sharper upper bound. In
particular ∏ deg(H_i)! is always a valid, usually loose, upper
bound, not a claim that extensions are pairwise disjoint. For
each irreducible factor over K of degree e, a necessary divisor
of [E:F] is e/gcd(e,[F:K]), since E contains a root and
e divides [E:K]. These divisors may also be combined by LCM.

There is a reusable improved upper bound when the paid hull
gate is orthogonality to an n-dimensional strict Chebyshev
space W⊂K[y], and r≥n nodes are desired. Choose r−n distinct
rational roots outside [0,1], and let J be their monic product.
Multiplication by J preserves the Haar property on (0,1).
Solve for the unique monic degree-n polynomial P orthogonal
to JW; it has n distinct interior roots, and has coefficients
in K. Then H=JP is a permitted monic degree-r hull, with only
the degree-n factor requiring a split field. For multiple
eligible cuts this gives the improved relative upper bound
∏ n_i! (and no extension for a one-moment cut). If all r roots
are wanted interior, start the argument with J=y^(r−n), then
move the imposed roots to distinct small positive rational
points; the paid matrix remains invertible and the n roots
persist in disjoint interior brackets away from them. This
refines a choice of hull, not a bound for any previously fixed
H_i. The all-tree cut proof is otherwise unchanged.

## 2. Quadratic classes and a quantitative lower divisor

For a monic separable factor f over K put δ_f=disc(f) in
K*/K*². If all roots of f lie in F, their Vandermonde square
root of δ_f lies in F. Let U be the F₂-span of the δ_f for the
individual irreducible factors (or use whole-hull discriminants
for a weaker test), and set

    Q_F = ker(K*/K*² → F*/F*²).

The multiquadratic extension over F generated by the
Vandermonde roots of the factors has exactly degree

    2^(dim U − dim(U∩Q_F)),                                   (B)

by independence of the images of the square classes in
F*/F*². It lies in E, so (B) divides [E:F]. Thus preservation
forces U⊂Q_F, not just a square test on the product of the
hulls. Products can cancel quadratic characters, and the
product polynomial can even have a zero discriminant if two
layers reuse a root. For a split imposed factor J as in
Section 1, disc(JP)=disc(J)disc(P)res(J,P)², so the square
class of the full hull and the remaining factor is the same.
Positive real discriminant does not make it a square in F.

An exact arithmetic input for a saturated moment hull is
available without root approximation. For n independent tests
W_j and n nodes, set M_(j,a)=∫₀¹ W_j(y)y^a dy, 0≤a<n, and
v_j=∫₀¹ W_j(y)y^n dy. Form

    D(y) = det [ M | v ;  1, y, ..., y^(n−1) | y^n ].

Then D/det(M) is the unique monic hull, by the duplicate-row
moment identities. The discriminant square class can be
computed from D itself: scaling a degree-n polynomial
multiplies its discriminant by the (2n−2)-th power of that
scalar. This gives a normalized resultant calculation in K
without first isolating the roots; the same applies to
the residual moment problem for JW. In a saturated cut an
obstruction for this unique hull cannot be removed by
retuning its coefficients while keeping that local gate.

## 3. The two explicit Gaussian quadratic characters

Let q=2a+ε (ε=0 or 1) be the Gaussian size, and use centered
coordinate t=2x−1. Write the centered monic Legendre hull
as t^ε R_a(t²). The a nonzero pairs {±sqrt(z_j)} are the roots
over the distinct positive zeros z_j of R_a. The ambient
Gaussian permutation group embeds in the signed pair group

    W_a = (C₂)^a ⋊ S_a.

In particular Gal(F/K) embeds in W_a by restriction, and
[F:K] divides 2^a a!. This does not assert a full Gaussian
Galois group in general.

Assume now the full group is known for F/K; for example it
follows from a full group for G/Q and coprime degrees
[K:Q], [G:Q]. Put

    π = ∏ z_j = (−1)^a R_a(0),   d_pair = disc(R_a).

For a≥2 the independent quadratic characters of W_a are
exactly overall sign-flip parity and sign of the permutation
of pairs. Indeed a homomorphism to C₂ must send every single
coordinate flip to the same bit by conjugation, and on S_a
it is trivial or the sign. These two characters are
represented by ∏ sqrt(z_j) and the Vandermonde in the z_j,
respectively. Hence

    Q_F = span_F₂ { [π], [d_pair] }.

For a=1 only [π] remains, and for a=0 there is no class.
Thus in the full case with a≥2 every hull-factor discriminant
must be congruent in K to 1, π, d_pair or π d_pair for field
preservation. In particular t independent classes from
chosen cuts force a relative degree divisible by at least
2^max(t−2,0), and (B) gives the exact quadratic divisor.
The discriminant of the *unfolded* Gaussian hull only
detects [π]: pair swaps are even on the original root set.
Equivalently its discriminant is 4^a π disc(R_a)^2 in the
even centered case, with an additional square π² in the
odd centered case.

For a proper subgroup of W_a the two displayed classes still
give available characters, but need not exhaust Q_F: a
subgroup can have more independent characters than W_a.
Use Q_F itself, or the safe bound
dim Q_F ≤ v₂([F:K]), not the full-group two-class conclusion.
If [K:Q] is odd in the full case, a practical necessary
test already uses rational norms: N_(K/Q)(δ_f) modulo rational
squares must lie in the span of [π] and [d_pair], because
the norm of a rational representative raises it to the odd
degree. A passing norm is not a proof of preservation.

The fixed-terminal hypothesis matters: a later construction
using a different odd-order terminal quadrature must use
its actual terminal split field in (A), not the old Gaussian
field unless that field is separately present.

## 4. Other exact certificates of nonpreservation

* Group quotient: preservation implies Gal(L/K) is a quotient
  of Gal(F/K). The same applies to every L_i. If an actual
  irreducible factor of degree r>a has group S_r or A_r with
  r≥5, preservation is impossible even without assuming the
  full Gaussian group. The abelian kernel of pair projection
  must map to an abelian normal subgroup of S_r or A_r, hence
  to the identity; a quotient of the remaining subgroup of
  S_a cannot have order ≥r!/2>a!. Under full W_a with a≥5,
  an S_a splitting field which is preserved must be exactly
  the pair splitting field K·Spl(R_a), since the base flips
  are killed and the quotient on S_a is an isomorphism.
  These are conditional on an actual group computation or
  proof, not on a random-polynomial heuristic.
* Ramification: at a prime ideal of K where F/K is unramified,
  an odd valuation of any factor discriminant cannot come
  from F. Adjoining its square root forces ramification
  since it has half-integral valuation. This covers all
  residue characteristics; no lifting heuristic is involved.
* Good reduction: take a monic hull integral at an unramified
  prime of K and with squarefree reduction over its residue
  field κ. If the old Gaussian-plus-diagonal field has
  residue degree f there, all factor degrees of the reduction
  must divide f for preservation. Indeed all roots, if in F,
  reduce into the degree-f residue extension of κ. In
  particular a residue factor of degree >1 at an old
  completely split prime suffices to disprove preservation.
  This is a finite-residue-field test of an algebraic number
  field inclusion; it does not assert that any given finite
  field factorization proves solvability over a p-adic field.

The quadratic, group, degree and prime tests are necessary
conditions; none is a converse to (A) unless full splitting
itself has been established.

## 5. Generic extension in the actual underdetermined hull family

One can prove generic enlargement here without asserting a
generic symmetric Galois group. Keep γ and a paid Haar space
W fixed, let n=dim W≥1, and suppose r>n. The monic degree-r
solutions orthogonal to W form an affine space over K of
dimension r−n, by invertibility of the first n columns of
the moment matrix. The universal monic polynomial on this
affine space is irreducible over F with the free parameters
adjoined as independent indeterminates. It remains so after
extending the *constant* field to an algebraic closure.

For the proof let P be the monic degree-n solution and Q the
monic degree-(n+1) solution with degree-n coefficient set to
zero. The Haar sign lemma says every nonzero orthogonal
polynomial has at least n interior sign changes: border n−1
evaluation rows by a variable row, treating any missing
sign-change sites as fillers tending to 0, then take a
normalized sign-test limit. In particular P has n simple
interior roots. If Q shared one with P, a real combination
Q+tP could make that root multiple, since P has nonzero
derivative there. A degree-(n+1) polynomial with a multiple
root at that site has at most n−1 distinct sign changes
(even multiplicity costs at least two, odd multiple costs
at least three). This contradicts orthogonality. Thus P and Q
are coprime over even the algebraic closure of K.

For r=n+1 take Q_r=Q. For r>n+1 choose any monic solution of
degree r; adding a rational multiple of Q avoids the finitely
many bad choices that vanish at a root of P. Then Q_r and P
are again coprime. The pencil Q_r+tP is irreducible in
the two-variable polynomial ring over any constant algebraic
closure, since its degree in t is one and a factor having
degree zero in t would divide both Q_r and P. It is monic
in the root variable, so remains irreducible over the
constant field with t adjoined. Finally, a factorization
of the universal monic polynomial over the full parameter
function field would have monic factors with coefficients
in the polynomial parameter ring (integrally closed, and
the monic roots are integral). Specializing to this affine
line would retain their positive degrees, a contradiction.

Thus generically even one root has relative degree r over
the old field with parameters adjoined. In the r=n+1 pencil,
every real specialization actually has r simple real roots:
n sign changes take n distinct simple roots and the single
remaining degree must be a distinct real root. For larger r,
the real-split locus is nonempty and open by Section 1.
Neither observation says a fixed specialization must enlarge
the field. For example n=1 simultaneously has generic
irreducibility when r>1 and has field-preserving split choices
by the imposed-root construction. Prescribing rational
factors puts the construction in a special subfamily; no
generic claim about its residual P follows without a new
proof. For r=n the hull is unique and has no parameter:
(A)–(B) and the exact arithmetic gates are the relevant
questions. This is the distinction between genuine generic
extension of the affine solution family and compulsory
extension for a particular collection of cuts.
