# Elementary weight span of a prescribed matrix support pattern

Fix finite physical stages with permitted proper edges i→j for
j<i, and *fixed* diagonal values δ_i in a nonzero commutative unital ring
R. The permitted offdiagonal entries vary **independently**; the
output vector may vary. Let T be the finite set of rooted unordered
tree shapes of size at most p (p≥1). For a fixed matrix A write

    F_i(A) = (Φ_t(A)_i)_{t∈T} ∈ R^T.

The **family span** is the R-module generated by all F_i(A) as A
varies. This agrees with the span of all actual b-outputs, since
outputs are linear and the coordinate output b=e_i is allowed.
It is not the row span of one fixed A and does not assert that an
arbitrary target is realized at the prescribed single width.
Rooted unordered shapes suffice because the native commutative
recursion is invariant under the ordered branch presentation. If
weights mean the denominator-cleared coordinates, multiply column
t by factorial(t): this preserves the span criterion when p! is
a unit, and can be included literally in C otherwise.

## Exact coefficient / dual criterion

Give each permitted edge e its own formal variable X_e. Expand at
a root stage i by assigning each test-tree vertex a physical stage
label, with its root fixed to i. Each edge of the test tree either
uses a permitted proper edge or stays at stage v with factor δ_v.
For an exponent vector ν of proper-edge uses, put

    C[(i,ν),t] = Σ_{admissible assignments with proper counts ν}
                     ∏_{stage v} δ_v^(number of stays at v).

In particular `F_i(X) = Σ_ν X^ν C[(i,ν),•]`. The support and the
fixed numerical diagonal values determine this coefficient matrix.
Every per-edge exponent is ≤p−1; there are only finitely many
coefficients. This is a multiplicity count, not just a test of
whether a rooted embedding exists. Repeated labels and repeated
uses of one proper edge are allowed.

**Exact test.** Assume every independently variable edge admits
p values whose pairwise differences are units. Then the family
span is **exactly the row module of C**. One direction follows
by evaluation. For the other, use the tensor product of the
univariate size-p Vandermonde inverses on the allowed edge grid
to extract each coefficient as an R-linear combination of actual
stage evaluations. If p! is a unit, the grid 1,2,…,p works even
when every permitted entry is required to remain a nonzero unit.
No evaluation with a zero edge is necessary. If there are no
proper edges, use the single empty grid. For a union of patterns
or prescribed diagonals, concatenate their coefficient rows.

Consequences when this grid hypothesis holds:

- Over a field, the family spans iff C has full column rank. The
  exact dual obstruction is a nonzero array a=(a_t) annihilating
  every row of C, equivalently the formal identity
  `Σ_t a_t Φ_t(A(X))_i=0` holds for every output stage i. A particular target w (such as
  inverse tree factorials) is in the span iff every such a also
  kills w, even if the family is not full.
- Over a general ring, full spanning means the ideal generated
  by the |T|×|T| minors of C is the unit ideal. Equivalently its
  reductions have full rank over every maximal residue field.
  Indeed Cᵀ is onto iff it has a right inverse (the target is
  free); Cauchy–Binet gives necessity of the unit ideal. A linear
  combination of minors equal to 1, applied to their individual
  adjugate right-multipliers, gives a right inverse. A single
  unit minor is sufficient, not necessary. The absence of a
  nonzero R-linear annihilator alone does **not** prove full span:
  a proper ideal ℓR in Z[1/p!] for a prime ℓ>p has zero annihilator.
- Over a field with fewer permitted values, the same exact
  coefficient argument applies to **polynomial functions**:
  reduce in each variable modulo the monic polynomial
  vanishing on its allowed finite set, and extract coefficients
  in this reduced basis. Unreduced formal coefficients would
  overstate the span. Coupled entries must first be expressed in
  their actual independent parameters, with degree bounds recomputed
  for those parameters. This is not an identity
  for the unrestricted edge grid.

## Common diagonal: a graph-only criterion by order

If every diagonal is δ, set A=B+δI. Expanding in the choices of
which tree edges use the diagonal gives, at every stage,

    Φ_t(A)_i = Σ_{U⊆E(t)} δ^|U| Φ_(t/U)(B)_i.

Here t/U contracts the chosen edges. Equal labels on each induced
component correspond bijectively to labels on the resulting rooted
tree, and the uncontracted factors use B. Thus every stage feature
vector is acted on by the **same** triangular transformation
T_δ, with diagonal 1. Contracting in two steps gives
`T_δ T_η = T_(δ+η)`, since each edge in the final contracted set
chooses one parameter. Hence T_−δ is the inverse, over any ring.
Full span, family or fixed matrix, is invariant under this common
shift. For a particular target, shift the target by T_−δ as well.
No cross-stage extrapolation or new stages are part of this fact.

At diagonal zero the coefficient rows therefore separate by total
proper degree q−1 for q-vertex trees. Under the grid hypothesis,
a common-diagonal pattern spans iff for each q≤p the matrix of
counts of strict rooted label maps with prescribed individual edge
multiplicities has full column rank over a field, or its maximal
minor ideal is one over a ring. This is a pattern criterion in
finite integer counts, independent of the chosen common δ.

## Structural sufficient test strictly weaker than all embeddings

A **leaf-folded labelling** of a rooted h assigns its internal
vertices distinct stages, assigns its leaves stages disjoint
from all those internal labels (leaves may coincide), and sends
every parent→child edge to a permitted proper edge. For a leaf
alone choose any stage. If every h∈T has such a labelling, and p!
is a unit, the family spans for **arbitrary fixed diagonal data**.

Proof: fix the chosen root stage and the resulting exponent
vector ν_h from h's proper edges. Its total degree is |h|−1, so
at this degree and tree size a labelling cannot have diagonal
stays. Every nonroot internal-stage label has exactly one incoming
edge-use count, hence one occurrence in any labelling with these
counts. The root label has exactly its specified occurrence.
Each leaf-stage label has no outgoing uses. At each forced
internal occurrence the outgoing exponent counts prescribe which
internal children occur and how many leaf positions have each
leaf label. Thus the unique unordered shape with these counts
is h. For the fixed h the assignments form one orbit under
rooted automorphisms: map each internal occurrence to the unique
same-labelled occurrence and biject leaves of the same label
at each parent. Therefore the coefficient of h in this row is
a nonzero integer dividing |Aut(h)|, hence dividing |h|!; it is
a unit. Tree sizes below |h| cannot contribute. Taking these
coefficient rows for all h gives an upper triangular square
matrix with unit diagonal after ordering by size, even when
larger trees have diagonal stays. This proves full span.

The condition is strictly weaker than literal embeddings, for
all p≥3. For each h of size up to p, make its own allowed DAG
by identifying all leaves to one sink, keeping internal vertices
distinct, and discarding duplicate copies of a proper edge.
Use their disjoint union, including the one-stage leaf pattern.
Each h has a leaf-folded labelling; sibling repetitions are
recorded as powers of the edge variable, not parallel edges.
The greatest outdegree is at most floor(p/2): if a vertex has
I internal children they require ≥2I descendant vertices, and
having any leaf adds one descendant and one edge to the sink.
Thus this pattern cannot even embed the p-vertex bush, which
needs outdegree p−1. Nevertheless its family spans. This is a
support example only, not a polynomial stage bound or a path
classification. Folded labelling is sufficient, not asserted
necessary; the coefficient test is the exact one.
