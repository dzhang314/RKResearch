# When RK terminal stages can be compressed

Suppose a Runge–Kutta method has a fixed prefix and a set of terminal stages, with no couplings between different terminal stages. They share a self-diagonal entry and their rows on the prefix have the form $\mathbf a+x\boldsymbol\delta$. The scalar $x$ may vary by stage. Let $r$ be the least order of a tree $u$ for which $\boldsymbol\delta\cdot\boldsymbol\Phi_u(A)$ is nonzero, where $A$ is the prefix matrix.

[The Lean equivalence](../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/TerminalLineMomentTransport.lean) says that a replacement on this same line preserves order $p$ if and only if it preserves the weighted moments of $x$ through $q=\lfloor(p-1)/r\rfloor$. For sufficiency, the terminal Butcher weight for a tree $t$ is a polynomial in $x$ of degree at most $\lfloor(|t|-1)/r\rfloor$. Each occurrence of the perturbation requires a branch of order at least $r$, even when the terminal self-diagonal is nonzero. For necessity, consider the tree with $j$ copies of $u$ attached to the root. Its terminal coefficient is the $j$th power of an affine function of $x$ with nonzero slope. The resulting equations for $j=0,\ldots,q$ determine the same moments as the powers of $x$.

[The Lean lower bound](../lean/RKCert/ReviewedNative/TerminalTailMomentWidth.lean) is stronger than the equivalence requires. If the original tail has $n$ distinct responses to $u$ and positive weights, any order-preserving replacement with the same prefix and prefix weights needs at least $\min(n,\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1)$ rows. Its rows need not lie on the line: they can be coupled, have arbitrary diagonals, or carry signed weights. Indeed the repeated-child tests still determine the moments of its actual responses to $u$. If it has too few distinct nonzero-weight responses, the square of their node polynomial has zero replacement integral and strictly positive original integral, at a degree where the two must agree.

Over the reals this count is attained on the original line. If $n\le\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1$, keep the original nodes. Otherwise take the $\lfloor q/2\rfloor+1$-node Gaussian quadrature rule for the original positive finite measure in $x$. Its degree of exactness is at least $q$. This last upper-bound argument uses the classical Gaussian quadrature theorem; the new Lean modules certify the order equivalence and the replacement lower bound, not existence of the Gaussian rule. This is a fixed-prefix result, not a global minimum stage count for ERK, DIRK, or SDIRK.
